# THE QUIET TIDE — ACADEMY YEAR PROGRESSION & COHORT STRUCTURE

**Status:** AUTHOR-APPROVED ACADEMY SYSTEM LOCK — ACTIVE 2026-09-26  
**Scope:** Four-year Academy structure, year-to-year competence progression, campus presence, professional exposure, seniority, character-generation requirements, and cohort differentiation.  
**Authority:** Active Academy canon. This document supersedes older proposal language wherever it conflicts with the four-year progression model below. It does not alter the seven-Division model, APR, Esper Rank, Resonance, Contract System, or Arthur's existing role locks.

---

## 1. Four-Year Academy Structure

Ravenscroft Academy is a **four-year specialist Academy program**.

The four years are not simply power tiers. Each year represents a different stage of professional development:

| Year | Institutional Stage | Primary Question | Typical Campus Presence | Experience Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Year 1** | Foundation | Can the cadet perform the fundamentals safely? | Very high | Beginner |
| **Year 2** | Developing Specialization | Can the cadet become reliably useful? | High | Intermediate |
| **Year 3** | Field Readiness | Can the cadet operate under real field conditions? | Moderate | Advanced |
| **Year 4** | Professional Transition | Can the cadet make professional decisions with limited supervision? | Low | Near-professional |

These are **development stages, not Esper Rank categories**.

YEAR ≠ ESPER RANK  
YEAR ≠ POWER LEVEL  
YEAR ≠ APR STANDING  
YEAR ≠ DIVISION  
YEAR ≠ AUTOMATIC FIELD AUTHORITY

---

## 2. The Four Development Axes

Year progression should be felt through four related but distinct axes:

1. **Knowledge** — understanding rules, terminology, procedures, and institutional context.
2. **Skill** — ability to perform a task correctly and repeatedly.
3. **Experience** — ability to apply skill under changing or imperfect conditions.
4. **Responsibility** — ability to make and justify professional decisions that affect other people.

The intended progression is:

**Knowledge → Skill → Experience → Responsibility**

This is not a mandatory chapter formula. It is the institutional logic behind why a fourth-year cadet should feel different from a first-year cadet even when their raw Esper Ranks are similar.

---

## 3. YEAR 1 — FOUNDATION CADETS

### 3.1 Institutional Identity

Year 1 cadets are learning how to function inside a specialist Academy.

They receive substantial shared foundation training covering areas such as:

- Resonance fundamentals;
- physical conditioning;
- weapon safety and basic handling;
- anomaly terminology;
- field safety;
- communication procedures;
- teamwork fundamentals;
- Academy rules and documentation;
- basic observation and practical reasoning.

### 3.2 Competence Standard

The Year 1 question is:

> **Can you perform the task?**

Errors are expected. Cadets may know a procedure intellectually while still failing to execute it under pressure.

High potential does not automatically mean high competence.

### 3.3 Social Position

Year 1 cadets are the most visible student population on campus. They form the primary social environment for Arthur's early Academy life.

Year 1 characters should therefore carry most of the early friendship formation, first rivalries, cohort gossip, APR competition, Division comparisons, and ordinary Academy life.

### 3.4 Character Generation Requirement

Minimum recurring new characters for the Year 1 generation: **5**. Recommended working range: **7–8**.

Useful functions include Combat, Reconnaissance/Observation, Research/Analysis, Medical/Support, ambitious high-performing, ordinary social peer, and international/scholarship students.

Not every Year 1 character needs a rare Ability or a major rivalry with Arthur.

---

## 4. YEAR 2 — DEVELOPING SPECIALISTS

Year 2 cadets have completed the foundation stage and begin developing clearer professional identities and meaningful specialization.

The Year 2 question is:

> **Can you perform the task reliably?**

Typical development includes deeper Division training, repeated practical exercises, supervised field exposure, clearer specialist identity, small leadership or coordination responsibilities, growing awareness of team composition, and development of personal working methods.

Year 2 is the first senior layer Arthur encounters regularly. These cadets can function as benchmarks, informal practical examples, competitors, temporary team leaders, and sources of Academy culture that first-years do not yet understand.

Minimum recurring new characters: **5**. Recommended working range: **6–7**.

Useful functions include Combat specialist, Reconnaissance specialist, Operations Support specialist, Research/Anomaly specialist, high-APR student leader, and developing field operator.

Year 2 characters should not merely be stronger Year 1 characters. Their distinction should come from reliability, specialization, judgment, and accumulated experience.

---

## 5. YEAR 3 — FIELD-READY CADETS

Year 3 is the major transition from **student competence** to **field competence**.

Qualified Year 3 cadets may increasingly participate in supervised external operations, Joint Teams, research deployments, practical surveys, emergency-response exercises, and other field-facing work.

The Year 3 question is:

> **Can you perform the task under field conditions?**

The distinction is operational rather than numerical. A Year 1 cadet may win a controlled sparring exercise; a Year 3 cadet is expected to understand that a real operation also requires extraction, casualty handling, communication, route awareness, equipment recovery, changing objectives, team integrity, documentation, and accountability.

Year 3 cadets should understand that **winning the fight is not necessarily the mission objective**.

Year 1 cadets generally see Year 3 students as strong or intimidating. Year 2 cadets see them as experienced specialists. Instructors increasingly treat qualified Year 3 cadets as near-professional personnel under supervision.

Minimum recurring new characters: **5**. Recommended working range: **5–6**.

Useful functions include elite Combat specialist, Reconnaissance/Investigation specialist, Intelligence specialist, Medical/Rescue specialist, Joint Operations leader, and experienced Research/Anomaly operator.

Year 3 characters should demonstrate **experience**, not merely higher output.

---

## 6. YEAR 4 — PROFESSIONAL TRANSITION

Year 4 is the Academy's transition stage into professional life. Fourth-years remain cadets, but many spend substantial time outside normal campus routines through approved professional placements, internships, supervised missions, research placements, institutional assignments, exchanges, and other external work appropriate to their training.

They are therefore **not expected to be continuously present on campus**.

### 6.1 Campus Presence

The following are structural guide ratios, **not literal canon percentages**:

- Year 1 — approximately 90% Academy / 10% external;
- Year 2 — approximately 75% Academy / 25% external;
- Year 3 — approximately 55% Academy / 45% external;
- Year 4 — approximately 25% Academy / 75% external.

Individual students can differ substantially.

### 6.2 Professional Exposure

Fourth-years may spend time with or inside government institutions, anomaly-response organizations, research laboratories, medical institutions, intelligence organizations, private Esper corporations, licensed field teams, international programs, and supervised long-duration missions.

External placement does not mean the cadet has already graduated.

### 6.3 Competence Standard

The Year 4 question is:

> **Can you make professional decisions with appropriate responsibility?**

A fourth-year should increasingly understand consequences beyond the immediate exercise, institutional obligations, professional accountability, when to defer to a specialist, when to challenge a plan, how decisions affect teammates and civilians, and how field information changes an existing plan.

### 6.4 Character Visibility

Year 4 characters should appear less frequently in ordinary campus scenes, but their appearances can carry greater institutional weight. They may return for joint exercises, Academy evaluations, specialist demonstrations, institutional briefings, international programs, external-team visits, major missions, and graduation-related events.

Their absence should be treated as normal professional scheduling, not unexplained disappearance.

### 6.5 Character Generation Requirement

Minimum recurring new characters: **5**. Recommended working range: **5**.

Useful functions include professional-track cadet, advanced Combat/field specialist, international-placement cadet, Research-track cadet, field leader, and at least one student whose training keeps them primarily Academy-based if campus continuity requires it.

Not all Year 4 characters need to be physically present together. Their external placements are part of their character design.

---

## 7. Year-to-Year Competence Ladder

**Year 1 — Can perform the task.**  
**Year 2 — Can perform the task reliably.**  
**Year 3 — Can perform the task under field conditions.**  
**Year 4 — Can make professional decisions about the task.**

This ladder is descriptive, not a grading formula. It must never become a hidden numerical level system.

---

## 8. Power and Rank Separation

Year progression does not guarantee a specific Esper Rank.

**Esper Rank = institutional classification**  
**APR = accumulated demonstrated performance**  
**Year = stage of Academy development**  
**Actual combat capability = contextual result of ability, training, physiology, technique, experience, equipment, tactics, environment, and team conditions**

No year should be written as a disguised power tier.

---

## 9. Arthur Reed Within the Four-Year Structure

Arthur begins as a **Year 1 Operations Support — Field Support Specialist**.

### Year 1
**Observe → Train → Apply**

Arthur learns foundation skills, especially dagger technique, physical movement, Support procedures, basic Resonance application, and team operations.

### Year 2
**Compare → Adapt**

Arthur develops meaningful secondary options and learns to compare specialist methods against Support requirements.

### Year 3
**Field-test → Become reliable**

Arthur's value increasingly comes from field judgment, flexibility, equipment use, backup capability, and remaining useful when a team's original plan fails.

### Year 4
**Professionalize → Decide**

Arthur's generalist capability can mature into a reliable operational identity, but breadth continues to cost depth. He does not become the best specialist in every domain.

This progression remains compatible with his Rank system: a later annual classification may remain substantially below his practical capability.

## 10. Character Generation Rules

1. Generate **at least five distinct recurring characters per year**.
2. Year 1 emphasizes foundation, social formation, and first competence.
3. Year 2 emphasizes specialization and reliability.
4. Year 3 emphasizes field experience and operational competence.
5. Year 4 emphasizes professional transition and responsibility.
6. Year 4 characters should be less campus-visible because many have external placements or missions.
7. Do not make every senior character stronger solely because of their year.
8. Do not give every character a rare Ability.
9. Do not make every senior a mentor to Arthur.
10. Do not make every year a separate power tier.
11. Character design follows **background → experience → worldview → behavior**.
12. Rank, APR, Division, specialization, and year remain separate concepts.
13. Existing named characters may be assigned a year only after checking age, chapter appearance, and continuity.
14. New characters must not overwrite existing named characters merely to satisfy a quota.
15. Year 4 external placement must create plausible reasons for recurring characters to appear outside campus without making them disappear from the story permanently.

## 13. Narrative Payoff

The intended reader perception is:

**Year 1:** "They are learning."  
**Year 2:** "They are becoming specialists."  
**Year 3:** "They have actually been outside."  
**Year 4:** "They are already partly working in the real world."

The Academy therefore evolves from a school environment into a professional transition environment as the reader moves upward through the cohorts.

---

## 14. Canon Guardrails

- Four academic years are active canon.
- Year 4 has substantially lower routine campus presence because of professional placements, internships, supervised missions, research assignments, exchanges, and related external work.
- Year progression is a developmental structure, not a power ladder.
- APR remains separate from year and Esper Rank.
- Esper Rank remains annual and system-generated with no manual override.
- Arthur's Operations Support role remains separate from Records & Archives.
- Eleanor Price remains a Year 3 Intelligence cadet with Archive Liaison duties.
- Character-generation minimum is five recurring new characters per year.
- Existing characters are not automatically assigned new years without continuity review.

# ARCHIVE CONTACT — ELEANOR PRICE

**Status:** AUTHOR-APPROVED CHARACTER LOCK — ACTIVE 2026-09-26

## Identity

**Name:** Eleanor Price  
**Age:** 20  
**Status:** Third-year Ravenscroft cadet  
**Division:** Intelligence  
**Academy role:** Senior Archive Liaison / Records Assistant  
**Primary workplace:** Records & Archives

Eleanor is a senior cadet assigned several hours each week to the Academy's Records & Archives service as part of an Intelligence-track records rotation. She is not a civilian librarian and not an administrator with authority over Arthur.

She knows how the archive works because she has been using it for years: classification codes, restricted shelves, cross-references, old incident names, transfer records, and the difference between a file that does not exist and a file that cannot be retrieved.

## Function in Arthur's Story

Arthur visits Records & Archives because Field Support sometimes requires historical or operational information before a practical exercise or mission.

Eleanor becomes the person who helps him navigate the archive.

She does not hand him answers. She helps him find the correct place to look.

Her recurring pattern is:

**Arthur brings a question → Eleanor identifies the relevant archive structure → Arthur searches/tests the material → they discover what the records actually support.**

She is therefore a useful bridge between Arthur's field-support role and the Academy's institutional memory.

## Personality

- Calm and observant.
- Dry sense of humor.
- Patient with genuine questions; impatient with lazy ones.
- Likes people who tell her exactly what they need.
- Knows when a student is looking for information for the wrong reason.
- Does not treat Arthur as a celebrity survivor or a mysterious prodigy.
- Respects careful work more than impressive Ability output.

Her recurring correction to Arthur is:

> "Tell me what you need to know. Not what you think the answer is."

## Relationship with Arthur

Eleanor is initially **senior → junior**, not mentor → chosen student.

She has no obligation to solve Arthur's problems. She helps because that is what competent archive support is supposed to do, and because she recognizes that Support cadets often need information other divisions take for granted.

Arthur gradually learns that asking Eleanor the right question is itself a skill.

Their relationship can become friendly, but romance is not assumed or locked by this file.

## Why She Is Useful

Eleanor gives Arthur a believable recurring connection to Records & Archives without turning him into an archive clerk.

She can appear when a story requires:

- an old mission report;
- a prior version of a containment protocol;
- a historical incident comparison;
- a missing cross-reference;
- a restricted record request;
- confirmation that two apparently unrelated cases share an administrative lineage;
- clarification of Academy terminology;
- a record that conflicts with what a field team has just observed.

She cannot bypass security because she likes Arthur. Restricted information remains restricted.

## Character Growth

Eleanor's own arc is institutional rather than romantic by default.

She begins as someone who knows how to navigate the archive better than almost anyone in Arthur's cohort. Over time, she becomes interested in the gap between **records truth** and **field truth**.

Arthur repeatedly brings her cases where the two do not agree.

This creates a natural recurring tension:

**Archive:** what the institution says happened.  
**Field:** what Arthur actually observed.  
**Question:** which part is wrong, and why?

## Hard Rules

1. Eleanor works in Records & Archives; Arthur does not.
2. She is a third-year Intelligence cadet performing a senior archive rotation.
3. She helps Arthur navigate records but does not solve mysteries for him.
4. She cannot casually access restricted files.
5. She is not automatically a romantic interest.
6. Her expertise is institutional memory, classification, and archive navigation—not supernatural omniscience.
7. She should have her own duties and disappear from scenes when those duties call.

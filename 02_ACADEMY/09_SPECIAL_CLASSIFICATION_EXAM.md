### 1.2 Legal basis [PROPOSED]

- The Sorting Board's charter contains a **secondary-evaluation clause** for "sorting failures, exception-path placements, and instructor-flagged misplacements." The Examination is that clause, operationalized.
- **The economic logic** (the sentence that survives every review): the state has already paid to Awaken, register, house, and feed every cadet. An F-rank with a general's brain is still cheaper than a new cadet. The Examination exists because the military **hates wasting talent it has already bought**.
- **Charter firewall [PROPOSED]:** the Examination's charter states explicitly: *"This Examination measures aptitude for service employment. It does not measure, reassess, or revise Esper Rank."* The firewall is load-bearing — see §6 for why the institution accepts it.

### 1.3 Who can trigger it [PROPOSED]

Three paths, in order of frequency:

1. **Automatic — ERROR/UNDEFINED readings.** Any intake reading of ERROR/UNDEFINED triggers the Examination by protocol, within the first month. *Arthur's path* (File 4 §1.4): he does not petition; the machine flagged him, so the system must produce a second opinion for the file. (Design consequence: nobody can resent him for *seeking* it — which defers the envy plot honestly, rather than deleting it.)
2. **Instructor referral.** Any instructor may refer a cadet whose demonstrated aptitude visibly mismatches their placement. Requires written justification; the referring instructor's name goes on the file (accountability cuts both ways — frivolous referrals are remembered at promotion boards).
3. **Cadet petition.** Allowed, rare, stigmatized (see §8 anti-abuse). The petition itself goes on the permanent file.

## §2. STRUCTURE — the nine stations

[PROPOSED] One day, nine stations, no channeling required at any station (an F-rank must be able to *sit* the exam — this is a design constraint, not an accommodation). Each station specifies **what it tests** and **the format**. No prose scenes.

### Station 1 — Analytical reasoning: "the box"

- **Tests:** synthesis of contradictory sources into a coherent account; citation discipline; comfort with unresolvable gaps.
- **Format:** 40 contradictory incident reports, witness statements, and logistics chits from a fictionalized incident; 90 minutes; produce a timeline with sourced citations. **Bonus scored:** naming what *can't* be resolved from the sources (the exam rewards the sentence "the sources do not establish this" — most cadets never write it).

### Station 2 — Tactical reasoning: the sand-table

- **Tests:** planning under incomplete intel; contingency coverage; civilian-risk accounting; casualty-avoidance as a *planning* value.
- **Format:** a Sweeper-adjacent cordon scenario (fictionalized real incident); 60 minutes planning + 20 minutes oral defense before two examiners. The intel packet is deliberately missing one critical fact discoverable only by *asking* (the exam tests whether the cadet interrogates the brief — the "client lies" rule from MISSION_TASK_SYSTEM, adapted).

### Station 3 — Anomaly interpretation: the sealed files

- **Tests:** Rule-extraction under redaction; falsifiable phrasing; epistemic honesty.
- **Format:** three redacted real incidents (Tide-mark photos, witness statements, Seismograph strips). For each: state the phenomenon's Rule **in falsifiable form** ("the Rule needs X; it fails when Y"). Scored on precision and on *acknowledged unknowns* — a confident wrong Rule scores worse than a hedged partial one.

### Station 4 — Pattern recognition: the strips

- **Tests:** signal detection with false-positive discipline.
- **Format:** 200 Seismograph/noise segments; 12 contain real signals. Timed. **False positives are penalized** — the station punishes seeing patterns that aren't there. (The exam's quietest cruelty: most clever cadets fail here by being *too* clever.)

### Station 5 — Information management: the cart

- **Tests:** building retrievable order from disorder; retrieval under time pressure; discrepancy sensitivity.
- **Format:** a disordered archive cart (~2,000 mixed documents); 2 hours to build a cross-index; then timed retrieval trials. **A discrepancy is planted** (two files that don't match — the ch4 seed's institutional cousin). Bonus scored for flagging it *unprompted*; the examiners' log notes the time-to-flag.

### Station 6 — Falsification / hypothesis testing: kill the favorite

- **Tests:** designing the cheapest test that could destroy a favored hypothesis; cost-awareness; willingness to attack an authority's belief.
- **Format:** the staff's favored hypothesis about an incident is given; design the cheapest falsifying test under a budget cap. **Adversarial by design:** the hypothesis is *held* by one of the scoring examiners. The cadet must try to kill the examiner's belief, on the record, politely.

### Station 7 — Decision under uncertainty: the clock

- **Tests:** making a call with missing data under time pressure — and *stating the uncertainty honestly*.
- **Format:** a developing incident, incomplete authority, a 10-minute clock. Make the call; then write the uncertainty statement. **Scored separately:** decision quality *and* the error bars. A confident wrong answer scores worse than a hedged right one. (The doctrine sentence, tested: the exam grades what you do with what you notice — including noticing what you *don't* know.)

### Station 8 — Stress reasoning: the degradation curve

- **Tests:** which cognitive functions degrade first under pressure — not the absolute score.
- **Format:** a compressed Station 1 task, repeated under induced stress: noise, time compression, an authority figure disputing every conclusion. Scored on the **delta from baseline**, and on *what* degrades (analysis? social function? willingness to commit to an answer?).
- **Design note:** this station is why the exam is not gameable by test-prep — you cannot rehearse your own degradation curve.

### Station 9 — Leadership potential: the strangers

- **Tests:** role allocation, information sharing, conflict handling — whether the group *used its members well*.
- **Format:** 6 strangers (mixed-division cadets), a planning task, 45 minutes, **no appointed leader**. Observer notes only — **no peer votes** (peers would just vote rank; the exam knows this and says so in the instructions, which is itself a small lesson in institutional honesty).

## §3. SCORING — the Aptitude profile, decoupled from Rank

[PROPOSED]

- **Output:** a nine-station **Aptitude profile** (percentile bands per station) plus a composite **Operational Aptitude Index (OAI)**. Bands, not scores — the exam speaks in ranges ("Band 1: exceptional; Band 5: below cohort mean"), because false precision would invite rank-thinking.
- **The doctrine sentence** (directive Part IV, adopted verbatim — it is the right sentence): **"Rank measures what the Tide does through you. Aptitude measures what you do with what you notice."** Printed on the Examination's charter page. Quoted by instructors who half-understand it, which is also correct.
- **Filed sealed by default** (§0, Worker 1 §6.7 decided): the full profile goes to the Sorting Board and the SMD liaison desk's quiet file. The examinee receives **band summaries only** ("your analytical reasoning is Band 1; your stress-social function is Band 4") — enough to be useful, not enough to be a weapon. The seal is what makes the *rumor* possible: "they say the Error's Tokurei file is sealed" becomes barracks gossip by ch5 — institutional ambiguity, manufactured by procedure.

## §4. ARTHUR'S EXPECTED PROFILE [PROPOSED]

The exam must *discover* him accurately — including the limits — or the institution looks stupid for the rest of the series. Expected bands:

- **Band 2 (strong):** Tactical reasoning (plan quality high; civilian-risk accounting exceptional for the age; oral defense quiet but precise — he answers the missing-fact question because he *asked* for the missing fact in Station 2's intel packet).
- **Band 3–4 (average to low):** Decision under uncertainty (decisions good; uncertainty statements **over-hedged** — conflict-avoidance in writing); Stress reasoning (analytical functions barely degrade; **social functions collapse** — he stops talking and writes instead; the examiners' note: "subject routes around people under stress"); Leadership potential (**low command presence, high plan quality** — "the group followed the plan, not the man; subject did not contest this").
- **The profile's signature line** (examiners' summary, filed sealed): *"Subject understands phenomena better than people, and knows it."*
- **Physical component:** the Review includes a reaction-and-movement drill with Esper output prohibited. Arthur places **fifth-fastest** among the cadets tested that morning. This is a human physical-competence result, not an Esper result: the exam does not reinterpret his Rank F or zero sustainable Esper output. It establishes that **weak Esper power and strong physical conditioning can coexist**.
- **What the exam does NOT find:** any channeling (there is none to find); any Esper-powered physical enhancement; any ambition to lead (he doesn't want the room; he wants the *answer*).

[INFERENCE] The profile is designed to be *true to the character bible's functional intelligence* (§0): rule-extraction, falsification, information diet, attention budget — all visible; people-reading approximate, conflict-avoidant, bad at asking for help — all visible. An exam that found only strengths would be a wish-fulfillment device; this one finds the exact weaknesses the story will press for 30 chapters.

## §6. THE IRON RULE — the exam MUST NOT raise his Rank

[PROPOSED] Explicit, with the institution's reasons stated — because the reader must understand this isn't authorial mercy, it's *bureaucratic logic*:

1. **Rank is a measurement, not a reward.** Esper Rank measures channelable output; it is interoperable across agencies, guild licenses, hazard-pay law, Erosion-care eligibility, and Compact postings. Revising a measurement because the subject is clever corrupts the instrument — and every institution downstream of the instrument knows it.
2. **The firewall is the point.** The Examination's charter decouples aptitude from rank *so the state can use clever F-ranks without paying B-rank wages*. Stated plainly, the cynical truth: the decoupling lets the institution *extract the value* (his analysis) while *withholding the price* (rank, pay, access). The system is not being fair to him. It is being *efficient*. (This is also why the Intelligence observer's "asset" argument doesn't move the board — the board already gets the asset at F-rank prices.)
3. **Precedent risk.** If aptitude moved rank, every F-rank in every cohort would petition (see §8) — and the rank system's treaty/legal function would collapse into an argument about cleverness. The board will not buy that problem at any price.

[INFERENCE] This is the philosophical core the old design demanded (ARTHUR_CHARACTER_BIBLE §3.2: reversals must *change the terms*, not reveal hidden stats): the exam changes the terms — *what should the institution value?* — without touching the measurement. Every future face-slap beat runs on this track, not on a secret power reveal.

## §7. ANTI-ABUSE — why every F-rank doesn't petition for one

[PROPOSED] Four governors, each load-bearing:

1. **Cost.** One sitting costs ~40 examiner-hours, sealed case materials (real redacted incidents — the supply is finite and classified), and Lantern examiner time. The budget office tracks sittings per cohort. Scarcity is real, not invented.
2. **Quota.** ≤12 sittings per cohort per year, board-approved. The quota is the reason instructor referrals are *spent carefully* — a referral is a favor the instructor can't spend twice.
3. **Stigma.** Petitioning marks you "difficult" — in a military culture, ambition without rank is a black mark, and instructors remember. Failing a petition goes on the permanent file as *documented overreach* — used against you at the Re-sorting Review (File 3 §1.7). The rational cadet does not petition unless an instructor pushes them.
4. **The kicker: passing changes nothing about rank, pay, or placement.** The exam moves *attention*, not *standing*. A cadet who petitions, passes, and stays F-rank has spent social capital for a sealed file. Rational actors don't. Arthur didn't petition — his was automatic — which is why the envy plot is *deferred*, not deleted: when the cohort learns what his sealed file says (and they will — barracks gossip, §3), the question won't be "why did he get one" but "why is his sealed."

## [COORDINATOR AMENDMENT — 2026-09-23 · Organizational Revision] Exam name + five-concept framing

### Formal name [RECOMMENDATION — NEEDS DECISION]

**Exceptional Aptitude Review** (formal). Rationale, from the bible terminology audit (2026-09-23): `00_WORLD_BIBLE/` contains **no existing term** for this instrument — no "classification exam," no "aptitude review," no 特別分類. The name is unconstrained new content. "Exceptional Aptitude Review" is recommended over the working "Special Classification Examination" because, under the five-concept model, **CLASSIFICATION is now a separate concept** — an exam named "Classification Examination" that neither grants nor changes classification (or rank) is a standing confusion engine. "Aptitude Review" names exactly what the instrument does: it *reviews aptitude*. Nothing else.

- "Special Classification Examination" → recorded as the **superseded working name**.
- Cadet slang "Tokurei" (特例, "special exception") [KEEP] — it still fits: the review is the exception path's second opinion.
- File name `09_SPECIAL_CLASSIFICATION_EXAM.md` retained as a working title (rename churn not required); the formal name above is what the institution calls it in-world.

### Five-concept framing [PROPOSED]

The Review measures **aptitude** — and only aptitude. It changes:

- **NOT** Esper Rank (Rank F stays; the slip prints "no rank effect" — unchanged).
- **NOT** Classification (Arthur is stamped Irregular at ch2, *before* the ch3 Review; the Review is the system's second opinion *about* the stamp, not a re-stamping).
- **NOT** Division, Curriculum, or Assignment.

Its product is an **aptitude profile** (the nine aptitudes) that the Sorting Board, instructors, and Joint Mission Command may *consult* — and that the institution, by default, **seals** (sealed-by-default [RECOMMENDED] — the ambiguity engine). What the Review creates is *institutional ambiguity*: a sealed file saying "exceptional aptitude" inside a stamped file saying "Irregular," next to a rank letter saying "F." Three true statements that do not reconcile — that non-reconciliation is a plot engine, not a plot hole.

### Test areas (per the revision) [PROPOSED]

The nine stations are re-described (not rebuilt) around the revision's seven test areas: **analytical/tactical reasoning, anomaly interpretation, pattern recognition, information management, falsification/hypothesis testing, decision-making under uncertainty, leadership potential.** The station designs in this file already test these mechanisms (they were built to measure the functional-intelligence system, not IQ); the amendment is a *labeling* alignment so the Review's charter language matches the revision's. No station mechanics change.

### Arthur's result (unchanged, reframed)

Aptitude: exceptional. Rank: F. Classification: Irregular (provisional). The Review's sealed profile is the reason Joint Mission Command will *later* reach for him — and the reason no one can say so out loud. No instant respect: the seal holds until the story earns its breaking.

## AUTHOR-APPROVED OVERRIDE — ANNUAL SYSTEM CLASSIFICATION

The previous doctrine remains active that aptitude does not itself revise Esper Rank. In addition, Esper Rank is now formally **reassessed once per academic year** by the authorized classification system. No instructor, examiner, administrator, or faculty board may manually assign a different Rank.

A discrepancy can trigger documentation or a formal system reassessment, but the resulting Rank remains whatever the classification system produces. Arthur's later progression therefore does not require an instructor to "recognize" him as a higher Rank. The system itself must eventually return a new classification.

Arthur's ERROR / UNDEFINED state remains a genuine classification anomaly. The private Contract System's relationship to the classification infrastructure is unresolved and must not be explained before the story earns it.

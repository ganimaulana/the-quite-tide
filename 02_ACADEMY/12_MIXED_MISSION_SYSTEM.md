## §4. SELECTION RULES — how names fill slots [PROPOSED]

### §4.1 Rotation quotas (the anti-fixed-party machinery)

Filed academy regulations — violating them in a draft requires naming the regulation broken and who authorized the exception (making drift a *plot event*, never an accident):

1. **No consecutive deployments:** a cadet who deploys on mission N stands down for mission N+1 (rest, debrief, Erosion-hygiene check — the diegetic reason is medical) [PROPOSED].
2. **No identical composition twice per term:** the same six names may not deploy together twice in one term — the board's anti-collusion rule (diegetic: the academy *wants* cadets to work with strangers; cliques are a training failure) [PROPOSED].
3. **Division-spread minimum:** every team carries ≥3 divisions [PROPOSED] — the "mixed" in mixed-division is a filed requirement, not an aspiration.
4. **Stand-down ledger:** quotas are tracked per cadet on the public ledger (§5.5) — cadets *watch* each other's eligibility, which makes the exceptions gossiped-about (the rivalry engine's ambient fuel).

### §4.2 Competence-match (Rank vs Aptitude, operationalized)

Slots are filled by **filed tier + Exceptional Aptitude Review (EAR) aptitude profile**, not by rank alone [PROPOSED — directive Part III: the exam measures aptitudes across A/B/C dimensions; no rank increase; Rank vs Aptitude distinction]. The exam's output is a *placement profile*; the board's matching algorithm weights aptitude-fit above rank for non-command slots. **Sealed-profile rule** [per `02_ACADEMY/07_EAR_DESIGN.md` §5 / LOCK 4 — this file follows it] [REVISED 2026-09-23 — name-only, LOCK 7 J-1]: exam results are sealed by default; the *board* (sorting authority) matches against the sealed profile while the cadet receives band summaries only. The competence-match therefore runs on data Arthur himself cannot see — which is why his inclusion can be *correct* (the board's algorithm) and *unexplained to him* (his slip) at the same time: institutional ambiguity, not instant respect.

This is the legitimate path for an F-rank onto a team: Arthur's aptitude profile (null output, spiked pattern-recognition / rule-inference / records-reconstruction / documentation-fidelity) *matches* the Irregular records-slot on investigation and records-recovery templates — **the system's own rule forces his inclusion**, and the contempt is in *how he's briefed* ("attached observer," never "analyst") [INFERENCE from directive Parts I+III]. The undervaluers are right on their own terms (Rank F truly measures his Esper output: none — FACE_SLAP §1 [KEEP]); the aptitude-match changes the *terms*, never the rank.

### §4.3 Political placement

Instructors and division heads may **attach** a cadet to a mission as a 7th (observer) slot or *swap* a selected cadet with written justification [PROPOSED]. Uses, all story-fuel:
- **Patronage:** a head advancing a favorite (the rivalry engine: "why is *she* on every good mission?").
- **Punishment:** the mop-job as discipline (Irregular-adjacent cadets get these; the insult that becomes the opportunity).
- **Observation:** an instructor placing a cadet to *watch* them (Hayes-class move — the placement is the surveillance).
Every political placement is **filed with its justification** — the justification is reviewable at the after-action review, which means political placements are *auditable insults* (Part IX's "instructor prestige" as a priced currency).

### §4.4 Instructor veto

The mission's supervising instructor may **veto any selection** before deployment — safety, readiness, or "instructor's judgment" (the last must be justified in writing within 48 hours) [PROPOSED]. The veto is filed; a pattern of vetoes against one cadet is itself reviewable (the contempt, made procedural — matrix R1/C1 territory). The veto is also the safety valve that keeps the rotation quotas from reading as authorial cruelty: when the quotas would put a genuinely unready cadet on a Threat Tier T3, the veto fires *on the record*.

### §4.5 Irregular-inclusion triggers — when the system bothers [PROPOSED]

The story beat, designed: **Irregular inclusion is never routine.** Every Irregular deployment carries its justification *on the record*, and the justification is always slightly insulting — and always load-bearing later (the after-action review must reckon with it). Five triggers (any one fires):

1. **Records-flag:** the mission's target zone carries a filing anomaly (misfiled reports, sealed gaps, decommission-year records) — the board's own paperwork needs a records-body. (Diegetic root: the Records & Archives assignment exists because the academy's archive is full of things nobody can file.) → *This is the ch6 trigger in the 30-chapter restructure: Arthur's own ch4 re-filing creates the flag that deploys him.*
2. **Aptitude-match:** the profile's requirements match an Irregular cadet's tested aptitude (the §4.2 machinery — the system's own rule, visibly resented).
3. **Expendability posting:** the mission is low-yield / high-nuisance and no division claims it — Irregular gets the mop jobs. (The insult that becomes the opportunity: **the underbrush is where the Seal hunts** — §6.)
4. **Instructor request:** a named instructor requests the Irregular cadet — political, visible, gossiped-about. Inclusion as *favor* = social cost (the cadet now owes the instructor; the debt is social, therefore unpayable cleanly).
5. **Incident overflow:** a real incident outruns the exercise plan and the board drafts whoever is nearest — chaos inclusion. (No plan survives contact; the trigger most likely to put Irregulars on missions *above* their filed tier — with the instructor veto (§4.4) as the safety valve that usually *doesn't* fire in time.)

**Trigger discipline:** the briefing *states* the trigger aloud ("Irregular attached per records-flag 7-C") — the reader always knows *why* he's there, which keeps inclusion from ever reading as authorial favoritism. A deployment with no statable trigger is a design error — flag at batch planning.

### §5.2 Deployment

Standard movement, instructor overwatch (not intervention — the instructor grades, not saves; a saved team fails the exercise — the stakes are real *because* the safety net is procedural, not physical).

### §5.3 Field command friction — who actually leads [PROPOSED]

The filed doctrine (this is the directive's leadership engine, phases 1–7, given rules):

1. **Formal command:** the highest-ranked cadet of the highest-status division present (Combat > Reconnaissance > Tactical > Research > Intelligence > Medical > Logistics > Irregular) [LOCK 7B 2026-09-23 — name-only fix: formal Division name is **Reconnaissance Division**; "Elite Recon" is not a formal Division name] — the *formal* chain. Never an Irregular cadet while a higher division is present. The insult is structural, therefore inarguable, therefore dramatic.
2. **Functional command:** the board's standing doctrine — **"command follows the problem."** The aptitude-matched cadet directs the *technical* action within their matched domain. A records-matched Irregular cadet directs the documentation action; they do not direct the Combat pair.
3. **The friction (mandatory):** formal and functional command *diverge by design* — the Irregular cadet with the aptitude-match has technical authority without formal authority, so orders get "reinterpreted" in the field. Every mixed mission must stage ≥1 formal-vs-functional conflict (anti-formula rule 12, §8) — this is the unofficial-leadership engine's fuel (directive Part I: "He has to understand what everyone in the room can do" — phases 1–7 are the slow conversion of functional authority into followed authority).
4. **Instructor override:** the supervising instructor may reassign command mid-mission — filed, graded, political (the override itself becomes a Part IX prestige event).
5. **Disputed command:** when formal and functional accounts differ, **both versions are filed**; the after-action review reconciles them (§5.4). The two-versions-on-paper mechanic is a standing story engine (whose log does the board believe?).

### §5.4 After-action review — the undervalue engine's home ground

Every mission ends in a **graded, filed** review [PROPOSED]. Five sections:

1. **Objective outcome** (was the profile's objective met? — 40% of the individual grade).
2. **Method log** (each cadet's per-action record — 30%; Arthur's strategy: *accurately boring* — the cultivated undervaluation, filed).
3. **Command assessment** (formal vs functional reconciliation — 20%; the friction, priced).
4. **Aptitude notes** (did the exam profile predict the performance? — feeds the §4.2 machinery; a note that says "profile unresolved, not low" is a recognition-as-access event).
5. **Remainder / disposition** (what's left, what's chained — the backlog's paperwork; §5.6).

The review is **permanent and readable by cleared staff** — a review that describes his method *accurately* is an intelligence product (mission file §2.1 [KEEP]); reviews that misdescribe him are camouflage. Both are story fuel. The review is also where **political placements are audited** (§4.3's justifications, re-examined) and where **vetoes are patterned** (§4.4).

### §5.5 Reputation ledger [PROPOSED]

Two ledgers, both **public inside the academy** (the scoreboard is the Part IX rivalry's instrument):

- **Individual ledger:** mission scores (weights above), commendations, demerits, stand-down eligibility (§4.1.4), aptitude-note deltas. Posted per mission; cumulative per term.
- **Division ledger:** aggregated standings, posted quarterly. Drives Part IX's material stakes: **training resources, equipment priority, instructor prestige postings, practicum priority, public-record citations**. Irregular starts last — every point it gains is visible, which is exactly why the points are *resented* (the dark-horse engine's economics: progress is public, therefore political).

**Ledger rule:** recognition arrives as **access, never rank** (verdict §5.6 / FACE_SLAP §3.5 [KEEP]) — a deeper archive tier, a requisition approved, a method cited in the manual's appendix, a sealed file opened one page. Any ledger event that moves Arthur's *rank letter* is a design error — flag and rewrite (stress test D, FILE 11).

### §5.6 The backlog — mission thermodynamics at the academy

Ported from mission file §5 [KEEP]: Arthur carries 3–5 open threads; chapters *collide* threads; the academy's **remainder/disposition filings** (§5.4.5) are the institutional form of the backlog — last term's remainder is this term's mission lead. ## §8. ANTI-FORMULA RULES — extending the nine [PROPOSED]

12. **Command friction is mandatory:** every mixed mission stages ≥1 formal-vs-functional command conflict (§5.3.3) — the leadership engine's fuel is a *requirement*, not a garnish.

15. **No clean wins:** every mission leaves a remainder, a debt, or a witness (§5.4.5 filed) — the backlog thermodynamics (§5.6) made a planning rule. A mission that closes everything is a dropped thread wearing a bow.
16. **The curriculum is a suspect:** at least one mission per arc must reveal that the *briefing itself* was the anomaly's vector (misfiled on purpose, filed wrong by design, or filed right and read wrong) — the institution is never a neutral quest-giver.

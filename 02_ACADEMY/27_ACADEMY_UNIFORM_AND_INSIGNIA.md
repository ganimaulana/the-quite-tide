# RAVENHURST ACADEMY — UNIFORM & INSIGNIA SYSTEM

> **STATUS: AUTHOR-LOCKED — 2026-09-24**
> **Scope:** visual institutional language for cadet uniforms.
> **Canon note:** the exact graphic design of each Division emblem remains open for later visual design. This lock establishes the information architecture of the uniform, not the final artwork.

## 1. Core Principle

The Academy uniform should communicate institutional identity visually without resembling an RPG status interface.

The uniform separates:

- **Division** → represented by a dedicated Division emblem/badge.
- **Rank** → represented by a colored shoulder cord.
- **Assignment** → represented through official records, identification, or local posting markers rather than a second large uniform emblem.
- **Classification** → may have a small institutional marker when operationally necessary; it does not receive a competing rank-like color system.

## 2. Division Emblem

Every Division has its own recognizable emblem.

The emblem should be visually distinctive and sufficiently detailed to function as a memorable institutional insignia, not a generic minimalist icon.

The final visual language should feel compatible with a British Academy/institutional setting and may incorporate Ravenhurst's coastal character without turning the emblem into a supernatural symbol.

The Division emblem is independent of Rank. A cadet's Division identity remains visually stable when Rank changes.

### Operations Support

Arthur's current Division is **OPERATIONS SUPPORT**.

Operations Support receives its own dedicated emblem. The exact graphic form is **not yet locked** and must be designed separately.

The emblem should communicate the Division's functional identity — coordination, operational support, information flow, and connection between operational functions — without reducing the design to a generic gear icon.

## 3. Rank — Shoulder Cord

Rank is **not** represented by changing the Division emblem.

Instead, cadets wear a visible **shoulder cord / shoulder-rope marker** whose color identifies Rank.

Current Rank color mapping:

| Rank | Shoulder-cord color |
|---|---|
| **S** | Platinum / white |
| **A** | Gold |
| **B** | Red |
| **C** | Blue |
| **D** | Green |
| **E** | Yellow |
| **F** | Gray |

The shoulder cord should remain an institutional uniform element rather than a game-like status display.

### Arthur

Arthur initially presents:

- **Division:** Operations Support
- **Rank:** F
- **Shoulder cord:** Gray
- **Assignment:** Records & Archives
- **Classification:** Irregular

His gray shoulder cord visibly places him at F Rank without requiring repeated exposition.

## 4. Assignment

Assignment is not represented by a large Division-style emblem.

For Arthur:

**Operations Support** is the Division.

**Records & Archives** is the assignment/posting within that institutional structure.

Assignment may appear on:

- identification;
- duty rosters;
- official paperwork;
- workstation or office designation;
- other practical institutional surfaces.

This distinction must remain clear in prose.

## 5. Classification

**Irregular** is a classification, not a Rank and not a Division.

It should not receive a second full-color hierarchy competing with the Rank shoulder cord.

If a visible uniform marker is required for operational reasons, it should be a restrained classification mark rather than another rank-like color system.

## 6. Storytelling Function

The uniform system should allow readers to understand Academy hierarchy through observation.

For example, a cadet can notice:

**gold shoulder cord → higher Rank**

without a narrator stopping to explain the ranking system.

Likewise, a Division emblem can identify institutional function at a glance.

The visual system should support status contrast while avoiding explicit RPG-style labels such as RANK: F.

## 7. Design Guardrails

- Do not make the uniform resemble an RPG interface.
- Do not make Rank and Division compete for the same visual channel.
- Do not create a unique Rank emblem for every level.
- Do not use a rainbow of decorative colors unrelated to institutional meaning.
- Do not finalize the Operations Support emblem as a generic gear, clipboard, book, or other obvious single-object icon without visual exploration.
- Do not imply that Assignment and Division are interchangeable.
- Do not treat Irregular as a separate Division.

## 8. Author North Star

> **The emblem tells the reader where a cadet belongs.**
>
> **The shoulder cord tells the reader where the cadet stands.**
>
> **The assignment tells the institution where the cadet works.**

*End of Ravenscroft Academy Uniform & Insignia System.*

## 1. Academy Performance Record (APR)

The **Academy Performance Record (APR)** is the official cumulative performance record for cadets. It is a points-based institutional record, not an RPG stat sheet.

APR points may be earned through documented Academy activities including:
- theoretical examinations;
- physical assessments;
- Resonance-control exercises;
- combat exercises;
- field operations;
- teamwork and joint exercises;
- practical assignments;
- inter-Division exercises;
- officially sanctioned competitions; and
- other assessed cadet duties approved by the Academy.

Points represent **demonstrated Academy performance**. They do not directly measure supernatural power.

## 2. Cohort Standing

Each academic year maintains its own **Cohort Standing**. Students commonly discuss who is highest in their year, and posted standings are part of Academy social life.

Standing may be shown as:
- Year 1: #X of cohort;
- Year 2: #X of cohort;
- Year 3: #X of cohort;
- Year 4: #X of cohort;
- overall Academy standing where an event or regulation requires it.

The exact cohort population may vary. A senior cadet is not automatically compared with a first-year cadet as though they had received identical training time.

## 3. What APR can affect

Depending on Academy rules and the specific event, APR standing may influence:
- representative selection;
- team composition;
- exercise seeding;
- advanced practical opportunities;
- recommendations;
- scholarships or institutional opportunities;
- internships and placements; and
- eligibility for sanctioned inter-Academy events.

APR does **not** automatically determine Esper Rank, Division, or future career.

## 4. Esper Rank is separate

**Esper Rank and APR are different institutional records.**

Esper Rank measures the subject's measurable Esper capability through the official classification system. APR measures what a cadet demonstrates through Academy work.

Therefore all of the following are canonically possible:
- high APR + low Esper Rank;
- low APR + high Esper Rank;
- a cadet with strong theory but weak combat performance;
- a physically strong cadet with weak Resonance output; and
- a low-ranked Esper becoming highly effective in field operations.

Arthur Reed is an intentional example of this separation.

## 5. Annual Esper Classification

Esper Classification is formally reassessed **once per academic year** through the authorized classification system.

Classification is **system-generated**. Faculty, instructors, examiners, and Academy administrators may document discrepancies, request review, or submit evidence, but they may not manually assign a different Esper Rank.

A manual statement such as "Arthur is really A-rank" has no authority to change the registry.

The annual assessment may change Arthur's official Rank when the system's measured data crosses its classification criteria. The change is determined by the system, not by a teacher's opinion.

## 6. Arthur's classification anomaly

Arthur's initial Rank F is an **imperfect classification of a genuinely irregular subject**, not a deliberate lie by an individual examiner.

The assessment infrastructure can register Arthur's abnormal response but cannot cleanly map all of it into the conventional Esper taxonomy. His private Contract System is an unresolved contributing factor to the classification interference. This relationship must remain a mystery until earned on-page.

The visible institutional result is therefore allowed to be:
- Rank F;
- ERROR / UNDEFINED;
- Irregular; and
- later, a low annual Rank increase that still fails to represent his full practical capability.

The assessment terminal's ERROR state is an **instrument/system classification failure**, not proof that the Academy software is simply broken. The exact causal relationship between Arthur's private system and the classification infrastructure remains hidden.

## 7. Resonance and Esper combat

Every Awakened Esper can develop some form of **Resonance use**. Resonance has two broad expressions:

**Internal Resonance** â€” channeling through the body to improve physical performance, movement, reaction, durability, coordination, or weapon handling.

**External Resonance** â€” expressing the individual's specific Ability outside the body: flame projection, pressure effects, constructs, elemental manifestations, barriers, and other Ability-specific effects where canon supports them.

Not every Esper has equally strong internal or external expression.

Arthur initially has extremely weak conventional Esper output. His early supernatural combat expression is therefore primarily **Internal Resonance applied to physical movement and weapon handling**, especially the dagger. His physical conditioning, Motion Sense, weapon training, and later Contracts are separate progression axes and must not be collapsed into Rank.

## 8. Rank does not equal combat outcome

Esper Rank is a classification of measurable Esper capability, **not an absolute combat-power ranking**.

A lower-ranked cadet may defeat a higher-ranked cadet when circumstances, technique, physical conditioning, weapon choice, Ability interaction, terrain, tactics, teamwork, or other legitimate factors produce that outcome.

Arthur may eventually defeat Rank A opponents while remaining substantially below them in raw conventional Esper output. Such victories must be earned through established capabilities and circumstances, not through an unexplained hidden Rank.

## 9. Inter-Academy competition

The Academy may select representatives for sanctioned inter-Academy competitions using APR, event-specific requirements, practical assessments, team composition, and institutional judgment.

A representative does **not** have to be the highest Esper Rank in the Academy.

Ravenscroft's lower institutional prestige and limited resources can make representative selection socially controversial. Arthur being selected while carrying a low Esper Rank is therefore plausible and can become a recurring source of rivalry and underestimation.

## 12. Quarterly Merit Distribution and Student Exchange

Every three months, the Academy conducts a **Quarterly Merit Review** for each academic cohort. The review does not replace continuous APR accumulation; it is a scheduled point at which the current cohort standing is formally recognized and certain institutional rewards become available.

The **Top 10 cadets in each cohort** at the quarterly cutoff receive eligibility for the **Quarterly Merit Distribution**. The exact reward pool varies by term and Academy resources and may include:
- skill manuals or instructional materials;
- Resonance or training materials;
- approved recovery consumables;
- equipment-maintenance or acquisition vouchers;
- training-facility vouchers;
- specialist consultation or supervised-training vouchers;
- approved academic or research access; and
- other lawful institutional rewards.

Merit rewards accelerate progression but do not grant automatic mastery, increase Esper Rank directly, or replace training. A skill manual remains a learning resource; a material remains a material; a voucher grants access or purchasing authority only within its stated terms.

The quarterly reward pool also contributes to the Academy's student economy. Cadets may lawfully exchange, sell, or barter eligible personal rewards and other permitted goods or information, subject to Academy rules and general law.

### The Grey Market

Students commonly call the campus's long-standing informal exchange network **the Grey Market**. The Academy's administrative terminology is **Unregulated Student Exchange Activity**.

The Grey Market is not inherently illegal. It functions as an informal student economy for lawful transactions involving permitted items, materials, equipment, vouchers, services, and information. Barter and information exchange are common, and transactions may occur through personal contacts or recurring student-run meeting points.

The Academy is aware that the Grey Market exists and does not attempt to eliminate every unregulated exchange. Its enforcement boundary is based on conduct and legality rather than the mere existence of an informal market.

The following are prohibited and subject to disciplinary action, with serious violations potentially resulting in immediate expulsion:
- theft or trafficking of Academy property;
- restricted or controlled items;
- forged documents or credentials;
- coercion, extortion, or deliberate harm for commercial purposes;
- unauthorized classified information transactions; and
- other conduct prohibited by Academy regulations or applicable law.

This creates a clear institutional understanding among students: lawful informal exchange is tolerated; crossing the prohibited boundary carries severe consequences. The Academy therefore monitors the environment without treating ordinary student barter as a disciplinary offense.

The Grey Market is also a social information network. A cadet may trade an item for information, exchange an unwanted reward for a needed material, or use an institutional voucher as part of a lawful private transaction. Such exchanges may affect social relationships, access to resources, and practical opportunities, but they do not override official Academy records or restricted institutional authority.

### Story Use

The Grey Market should appear only when it serves an existing character objective, resource problem, information need, or social consequence. It must not become a standalone shopping system or RPG-style reward shop.

For Arthur, the Grey Market may become particularly useful because his Records experience, field observations, practical knowledge, and broad Support role can give him information or resources that other cadets value. Any advantage he gains through the market must remain earned through knowledge, judgment, negotiation, or established relationships.

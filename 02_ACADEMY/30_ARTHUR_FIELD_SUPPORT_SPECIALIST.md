# ARTHUR REED — FIELD SUPPORT SPECIALIST

**Status:** AUTHOR-APPROVED CHARACTER / ACADEMY ROLE LOCK — ACTIVE 2026-09-26
**Scope:** Arthur's Division role, field function, support identity, mission participation, weapon flexibility, and Records & Archives relationship.

---

## 1. Current Academy Role

Arthur Reed's initial professional home at Ravenscroft Academy is:

**Division:** Operations Support  
**Specialization:** **Field Support Specialist**

Arthur is a cadet first. He is not assigned to Records & Archives as a primary job, clerkship, or long-term internship.

His normal Academy progression centers on shared foundation training, practical evaluation, Division training, team exercises, and eventual field participation.

---

## 2. Field Support Function

The Field Support Specialist exists to keep a field team functional when conditions change.

Typical responsibilities may include:

- equipment preparation and recovery;
- containment and support equipment handling;
- route and position observation;
- communication support;
- emergency response assistance;
- casualty and extraction support;
- field documentation when needed;
- basic reconnaissance support;
- temporary combat coverage when a team loses a specialist;
- adapting available equipment to the immediate operational need.

Arthur may be attached to practical missions, exercises, surveys, and other field operations when the Academy requires a Support cadet.

He is not initially a frontline specialist. His field participation is earned through training, evaluation, and demonstrated reliability.

---

## 3. Backup Operator Identity

Arthur's distinctive value is **flexibility**, not specialization.

He can develop usable competence across several weapon ranges:

- **Close:** dagger and other short weapons;
- **Medium:** spear, short-to-medium weapons, and selected field tools;
- **Long:** selected ranged weapons when properly trained and authorized.

This does not make Arthur a master of every weapon. A dedicated specialist should normally outperform him inside that specialist's trained domain.

Arthur's value appears when the original plan breaks:

> **A specialist is the best person for a specific job. Arthur is the person who may still be useful when the specific person is no longer available.**

He can therefore function as a **backup operator** inside a joint or field team when a combat, reconnaissance, or ranged role temporarily becomes unavailable.

This role must remain bounded. Arthur cannot instantly substitute for a highly trained specialist, and changing weapons does not erase differences in technique, experience, terrain, or judgment.

### 3A. Generalist Development — ACTIVE CHARACTER DIRECTION

Arthur's long-term development may resemble a **field generalist** rather than a conventional single-weapon specialist. The model is inspired by the narrative function of a versatile combat generalist: breadth creates options, but breadth does not equal equal mastery.

The key principle is:

> **Arthur is not a master of many weapons. He is a generalist who learns when different tools become useful.**

His versatility must be revealed gradually. The story should not repeatedly display a large arsenal in the same fight. At any given stage, Arthur normally commits to **one primary weapon and, at most, one secondary option** for a confrontation or field task. Other learned weapons remain situational capabilities rather than an inventory to be spammed on-page.

The progression is therefore:

**One reliable weapon → two meaningful options → situational choice → controlled switching → broader generalist competence.**

Early in the Academy, the dagger is Arthur's obvious identity. An opponent who studies him may reasonably prepare for a dagger-oriented fighter. A later encounter can then reveal that Arthur has developed a different weapon or approach because the situation calls for it. The surprise comes from **the decision**, not from showing off how many weapons he owns.

Weapon changes must have a causal reason. Arthur does not change weapons merely to surprise an opponent. He changes because distance, terrain, objective, target behaviour, team composition, authorization, or available resources makes another option more useful.

Arthur's resourcefulness is similarly grounded in **use of what is available**, not failure recovery. A correct plan may work exactly as intended and still produce less operational impact than another available option. Arthur's development is learning to recognize the difference between:

**"This works."**

and

**"This gives the team more."**

His progression in judgment is:

**Function → Efficiency → Operational Impact.**

He should increasingly ask what a choice leaves the team able to do next, rather than simply whether the immediate problem can be solved.

### 3B. Information Advantage Through Versatility

Arthur's versatility can become an information advantage without becoming a supernatural or game-like mechanic.

Other cadets and opponents naturally build profiles from observed behaviour: preferred weapon, preferred distance, movement habits, and common responses. Early Arthur can therefore be underestimated or over-classified as **"the dagger user."** As his training broadens, that assumption becomes less reliable.

The intended social progression is:

> **"He's Support."**
>
> **"He's a dagger user."**
>
> **"He can use a spear?"**
>
> **"He can actually shoot?"**
>
> **"Why does he know how to use that?"**
>
> **"Don't prepare for Reed's weapon. Prepare for Reed's decision."**

This progression must happen across separate encounters and training phases, not as a single exposition dump.

Arthur should not become unpredictable by acting randomly. His choices remain explainable in hindsight. The reader should be able to understand why he selected a weapon once the relevant field conditions are visible.

### 3C. Weapon Limits and Depth/Breadth Trade-off

Arthur's versatility has a hard developmental limit: **breadth costs depth**.

- A specialist who trains one weapon continuously can normally achieve greater depth with it.
- Arthur's time is divided across multiple tools and field competencies.
- Learning a new weapon does not erase previous limitations.
- Switching between weapons requires genuine training in grip, balance, distance, timing, movement, and judgment.
- Arthur may know how to use a weapon without being qualified to use it in every operational context.
- Authorization and mission conditions remain separate from personal competence.

Early Arthur should normally fight with one weapon rather than cycle through several. The ability to use a second weapon in a later encounter is a **character reveal**. The ability to transition between options during one encounter should arrive only after the underlying competence has been earned.

The dagger remains Arthur's anchor. Other weapons expand his options; they do not replace the dagger's identity or its connection to his fragmented Ravenhurst memory.

## 4. Primary Weapon

Arthur's primary weapon remains the **dagger**.

The knife used during the Ravenhurst Massacre is part of his fragmented physical memory. At Ravenscroft, he deliberately studies the dagger and develops it through technique, footwork, distance control, repetition, failure, and field experience.

Other weapons are secondary options. They exist because Arthur needs adaptability, not because the story should turn him into an effortless weapon master.

---

## 5. Benkei Constraint

The **Benkei** weapon concept is a limitation that shapes Arthur's training and equipment choices.

It must never be treated as a free source of weapon mastery.

Arthur must first acquire genuine learned competence through Academy training and practical experience. Benkei-related progression may amplify or interact with that learned competence, but it cannot create competence from nothing.

Therefore:

**training → learned competence → Benkei interaction → adaptation**

not:

**Benkei → instant mastery.**

Arthur's unusually broad loadout has an in-world explanation. Other cadets initially assume the weapons are carried for the Support team. The later realization is that Arthur carries many of them because **he may need them himself** when his Benkei-related limitation makes deep specialization unreliable.

This reveal should be gradual rather than explained in one exposition scene.

---

## 6. Social Progression

Arthur's initial reputation should be low:

**Rank F → Irregular → Support → underestimated.**

Other cadets may initially interpret his equipment as evidence that he is merely carrying supplies for stronger teammates.

Through practical exercises and missions, they gradually discover that Arthur can cover gaps across different ranges and operational situations.

The intended reputation progression is:

> "He's Support."

→ "He carries too much equipment."

→ "He can actually use those weapons."

→ "He is not the best specialist."

→ "If the plan breaks, put Reed on the team."

Arthur's status should rise through demonstrated usefulness, not through a sudden rank increase or effortless victory.

---

## 7. Team and Character Interaction

Field Support naturally places Arthur beside stronger cadets from other Divisions.

He may repeatedly work with:

- Combat specialists;
- Reconnaissance specialists;
- Tactical/Command cadets;
- Medical/Support personnel;
- Research or technical specialists;
- temporary Joint Teams.

This is the primary mechanism for Arthur to build relationships with strong recurring characters.

He does not need to become their equal immediately. His role is initially to observe, support, survive, learn, and eventually become someone teams specifically request.

---

## 8. Records & Archives Relationship

Records & Archives is **not Arthur's primary assignment**.

It remains a recurring side location and information channel because Field Support sometimes needs historical, operational, or procedural records before a practical exercise or field assignment.

Arthur's recurring Archive contact is **Eleanor Price**, a third-year Intelligence cadet serving a senior Archive Liaison / Records Assistant rotation. She knows the archive's classification and retrieval system and helps Arthur identify where to look, but she does not simply provide answers or bypass restricted access. Her full character lock is in `02_ACADEMY/31_ARCHIVE_CONTACT_CHARACTER.md`.

Arthur may visit the Archive and occasionally:

- locating an old file;
- checking a record against field reality;
- tracing an incident reference;
- understanding institutional history;
- investigating a discrepancy that affects a current support assignment.

Archive scenes should therefore be **story-driven side episodes**, not daily workplace routine.

The Archive connection may become especially useful when a field assignment produces a discrepancy between the official record and what Arthur actually observed.

---

## 9. Core Character Principle

Arthur's professional identity is not:

> "the clever boy who works in the Archive."

It is:

> **"the underestimated Support cadet who keeps becoming useful when the mission stops going according to plan."**

His progression remains:

**Observe → Train → Apply → Compare → Adapt → Support → Field-test → Become reliable.**

The previous "Fail" emphasis is intentionally replaced. Arthur does not need to fail for every improvement to be earned. A solution can succeed and still teach him that another available choice would have produced greater operational impact.

## 10. Hard Rules

1. Arthur is a **Field Support Specialist**, not an Archive clerk.
2. Records & Archives is a recurring side connection, not his primary job.
3. Arthur may join missions as Support when the Academy assigns him.
4. He can function as backup across close, medium, and selected long ranges only after genuine training.
5. Dagger remains his primary weapon.
6. Multiple weapons do not imply equal mastery.
7. Benkei cannot create weapon competence from nothing.
8. Arthur should not replace a true specialist at that specialist's own peak function without a concrete situational reason.
9. His reputation must grow through demonstrated field usefulness.
10. Support is a professional operational role, not a low-status synonym for weak.
11. Versatility is revealed across encounters, not spammed through large weapon rotations in every fight.
12. Arthur normally commits to one primary weapon and at most one secondary option in a given confrontation during his early development.
13. Weapon changes require a concrete situational reason; surprise alone is not sufficient justification.
14. Breadth never implies equal mastery. Dedicated specialists retain meaningful depth advantages.
15. Resourcefulness means making effective use of available options, not magically producing the perfect tool.
16. A plan may succeed while still being less impactful than another viable option; this distinction is a core part of Arthur's development in judgment.
17. Dagger remains Arthur's primary weapon and emotional/physical anchor even as his generalist competence expands.

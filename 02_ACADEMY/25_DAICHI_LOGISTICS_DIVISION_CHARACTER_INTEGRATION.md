## 3. Learning Opportunities

Mapped to the required loop — **LEARN → OBSERVE → CONNECT → APPLY → FAIL → ADAPT → IMPROVE** — with the firewall that the Division grants **breadth, never mastery**, and **KNOWLEDGE ≠ SKILL** at every step **[PROPOSED]**.

- **LEARN:** maintenance cards, spec sheets, issue procedure — *what things are called*; shallow by design.
- **OBSERVE:** watching depot staff do field repair during exercises — *sequence* (what is checked first) and *triage* (what is fixed, what is written off). Costs only attention.
- **CONNECT:** the Arthur-specific step — linking an observed failure pattern to a procedural cause. The Division supplies the *data*; the connection is his, and it can be wrong.
- **APPLY:** supervised attempts, priced by the world's budget and graded through after-action review (attested **[CANON]** machinery).
- **FAIL:** misdiagnosed faults, fumbled handling, an inventory count that won't reconcile and costs him an evening. Visible and priced — hours, embarrassment, a supervisor's low opinion. A loop without visible failure is a montage.
- **ADAPT:** correcting the map — "the edge-check isn't skipped; it's done at a different station."
- **IMPROVE:** incremental, capped competence — a *reliable* junior detail, not a skilled technician. The ceiling is "the quartermasters stop double-checking his counts." Real, earned, small.

**KNOWLEDGE ≠ SKILL — what he may understand without becoming:**

| He may understand… | …without becoming… | Why the firewall holds |
|---|---|---|
| Weapon construction | An expert fighter | Assembly knowledge is declarative; fighting is embodied. |
| Maintenance principles | A weapons master | Knowing the care of a thing is not command of it. |
| Equipment weaknesses | A tactical genius | A chipped edge is a material fact; exploiting it under fire is a different skill. |
| Logistical constraints | A combat prodigy | Constraints shape planning, not execution. |
| Field procedures | Any of the above | Procedure is choreography for ordinary people — steps, not the dance. |

**The Foster test [PROPOSED]:** Daniel Foster has had this Division's exposure longer, with no weapon-master trajectory. Exposure is common; what Arthur *does* with it differs.

## 6. Instructor Relationship

**[PROPOSED — described by role; no named instructor is created.]**

The plausible figure: the **rotation supervisor**, a career quartermaster-officer grading cadets on two things — reliability and not breaking things.

- **What the instructor sees:** a Rank-F stamped cadet who shows up, doesn't complain, counts correctly, and asks an unusual number of questions about *why*. The read is not "hidden genius" — it is "diligent, a bit odd, probably compensating."

## 7. Weapon Exposure

**[PROPOSED — the mechanism. The Division issues no weapons and teaches no combat.]**

Weapons enter his hands **as maintenance objects**: intake inspection, cleaning, storage, inventory, supervised basic repair. He learns:

- **Construction:** how spears, blades, bows, firearms assemble and disassemble; which parts wear first.
- **Failure modes:** recurring breakage shapes per class under field conditions — the material half of "equipment failure patterns" (§2.12).
- **Handling familiarity:** weight, balance, grip of each class — *familiarity*, not skill. Holding a spear a hundred times teaches what a spear feels like; nothing about fighting with one.
- **Storage and issue doctrine:** who gets what, when, why — weapons as *allocated resources*. A Combat cadet learns the spear; a Logistics cadet learns the spear *system*.

**What he does NOT get:** combat instruction (must be sought across Divisions at the cost of favors, exposure, secrecy **[PROPOSED]**); sparring certification; any personally issued weapon; any scene where handling confers ability.

**Load-bearing sentence:** *Logistics teaches him what weapons are. Nothing in Logistics teaches him what to do with one.* **[PROPOSED]**

## 8. Relationship to Future Master of Weapon

"Master of Weapon" is design shorthand (never an in-world title) for four capacities — **selection**, **adaptation**, **rule exploitation**, **restraint** **[PROPOSED]** — aspiration tracked through decisions, never a threshold crossed.

**Logistics' contribution per capacity — foundation only, never creation:**

- **Selection:** familiarity with many classes' properties (reach, weight, concealability, maintenance burden) — the raw data good selection needs. Not judgment under pressure, not the ruleset knowledge that makes a selection *correct*.
- **Adaptation:** handling familiarity lowers the *novelty* penalty of an unfamiliar class. Not embodied skill — adaptation is trained, not exposed-into.
- **Rule exploitation:** deep knowledge of mundane properties (a vessel's containment, a cord's binding, a blade's reach) — the vocabulary a future ruleset will speak through. Not the ruleset; validity is discovered per-Contract by test.
- **Restraint:** the quartermaster's native virtue — knowing what a tool is *for* and what misuse costs. Not the wisdom to decline, which comes from cost experience, not inventory work.

**The honest chain** (kept modest): Logistics → exposure to many weapon systems → maintenance knowledge → equipment familiarity → practical experimentation (supervised, small) → broader weapon training (sought elsewhere, priced in the world's budget) → Contract-medium adaptation → eventual Master of Weapon. **Every arrow after "familiarity" depends on systems outside the Division**.

**Three guardrails [PROPOSED]:**

1. **Foster guardrail:** same Division, same exposure class, no trajectory. The difference is never the Division.
2. **Decision guardrail:** the reader must point to *decisions* — seeking instruction, paying in hours and injury, choosing breadth when specialization was advised — as the cause, with the Division as setting. Setting is not cause.
3. **Timing guardrail:** nothing in the Logistics chapters may read as "weapon master origin." Payoffs resolve as *competence*, never *prowess* — the reader should feel the training-debt behind any impressive weapon beat.

## 10. Risks of Vocational Subplot

**[PROPOSED]** The central risk — Logistics becoming a long vocational subplot — with failure shapes:

1. **The depot chapter.** A chapter of filing, warehouse work, maintenance procedure, inventory counting. One such chapter teaches skimming; two teach that the Division *is* the story. **Severity: critical.**
2. **The competence mirage.** Long equipment scenes make him *look* like a budding fighter/technician; the eventual reveal that he still can't fight then reads as betrayal.
3. **The cozy-workshop trap.** The depot becomes a warm, low-stakes home base — tension leaks from the Academy arc while the anomaly plot drags him out of somewhere the reader likes.
4. **Deus-ex-warehouse.** A mission solved because he remembered a depot detail. If unearned, equipment knowledge becomes superpower with extra steps — payoffs must be fair-play: planted visibly, dormant long, recalled at cost (§11).
5. **The specialization lecture, misread.** "Pick a station and own it" could read as the story endorsing specialization — or winking that he is above it.
6. **Breadth as quirk.** Curiosity becomes a cute trait ("Arthur asks about everything!") instead of a priced survival strategy; when the true reason surfaces later, it reads as retcon.
7. **Narrative status drift.** Situational-indispensability beats accumulate; "overlooked" erodes one small triumph at a time.

## 11. Pacing Solution

**Governing rule [PROPOSED]: Logistics appears in missions, not in the depot** — shown through *outputs* (a convoy that arrives, a cordon that holds, a weapon that doesn't jam) and *brief references* (reconciliation §4.2), never through sustained workplace narrative.

**Plant → dormancy → payoff**, for every Logistics-derived beat:

1. **Plant (one line, in passing).** A maintenance detail mentioned while the scene is about something else: "the coupling threads on these are reversed — ask me how I know." Visible (fair play), unstressed. Plants live in Arthur's notebook — the story's honest record of what he noticed.
2. **Dormancy (chapters).** Not mentioned again; the reader forgets it. A payoff without dormancy is a setup, and setups are promises; dormancy converts the promise into texture.
3. **Payoff (a mission beat, at cost).** The detail matters during a Joint Team mission or mixed exercise — the coupling reversed *now*, under pressure, with witnesses. It must cost him (time, exposure, a favor, a supervisor's question later) or it reads as free — and it resolves as *competence*, never *prowess*.

**Quantitative guardrails [PROPOSED]:** no chapter centers on the depot, warehouse, or a maintenance procedure — a depot setting gets at most a corridor conversation's worth of space. At most one or two plants per early chapter, a line or two each — the notebook accumulates them, the prose doesn't. The after-action review (attested **[CANON]**) is the preferred visibility surface; mixed practical exercises (reconciliation §6.2 **[PROPOSED]**) are the legitimate stage for applied knowledge under pressure.

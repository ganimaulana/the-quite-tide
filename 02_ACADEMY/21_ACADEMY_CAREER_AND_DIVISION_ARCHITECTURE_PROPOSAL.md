## 1. Core Academy Principle

The Academy is a state-recognized specialist academy whose purpose is to prepare cadets for professional work across the Esper/anomaly ecosystem. Military service is one major post-graduation path, but not the Academy's sole or defining purpose.

Cadets do **not** necessarily spend their entire Academy life separated by Division. Division is primarily a **professional / military career track and practicum placement**. Cadets remain part of a shared cohort and continue to study alongside cadets from other Divisions.

Therefore:

- DIVISION â‰  CLASSROOM COHORT
- DIVISION â‰  SOCIAL CASTE
- DIVISION â‰  PERMANENT MILITARY DESTINATION

Division represents the professional environment in which a cadet receives practical experience during Academy.

---

## 2. Three-Layer Academy Model

The Academy operates through three overlapping layers:

### A. Common Academic Life

Cadets from different Divisions study together in common academic classes.

Illustrative subjects (design direction only, **not a locked curriculum**):

- Military History
- Military Ethics
- Basic Tactics
- Academy Regulations
- Physical Conditioning
- Basic Weapon Safety
- Anomaly Awareness
- Field Safety
- Communication
- Emergency Response

### B. Specialized Academic Study

Some classes are classified according to Curriculum or professional track. These classes provide deeper knowledge relevant to the cadet's chosen or assigned professional direction.

### C. Division Practicum

Cadets periodically work/study within their assigned Division as professional military practicum / internship. The practicum gives practical experience that can later affect military placement.

---

## 3. Division Function

Division should be understood as:

> "Where a cadet learns how a military profession actually works."

It is NOT:

- a reward
- a power ranking
- a permanent caste
- a declaration of future military rank
- an exclusive classroom
- a reason to isolate cadets from other Divisions

A cadet may have a low-status Division but still develop excellent academic and practical performance. A prestigious Division does not automatically guarantee a prestigious post-graduation assignment.

## 5. Low-Status Division Design

The Division should have **low prestige but real military utility**. It must NOT be a "garbage division." It contains other legitimate cadets who were also placed there.

Possible characteristics of the Division's cadets:

- cadets without clear specialization
- ordinary/general cadets
- cadets whose initial assessment does not justify elite placement
- cadets who still have useful military potential

Arthur is therefore **not the only person there**. He becomes part of an existing social group.

The Division has meaningful professional work. Potential practicum functions (examples of FUNCTION, not authorization to create a new Division):

## 9. Academy Story Pacing

The low-status Division must NOT create a slow vocational subplot. Do not write long chapters about filing, repairing weapons, inventory, library work, or routine maintenance â€” unless the activity directly contributes to a later story beat.

The Division appears as **side-story / background material**. Use brief references and payoff scenes:

- *Example:* Arthur previously handled weapon maintenance. Later, a practical exam presents a damaged weapon. Arthur recognizes a maintenance failure other cadets miss. The reader realizes: he learned that from his Division. No flashback necessary.
- *Example:* Arthur previously read archived incident reports. Later, he recognizes a pattern in a current anomaly. The prior Division experience pays off without a dedicated training chapter.

Core pacing principle:

> LEARNING SHOULD BE SHOWN THROUGH PAYOFF, NOT THROUGH EXTENDED TRAINING SEQUENCES.

## 12. Graduation and Military Placement

Division does **not** permanently determine a cadet's post-graduation military career.

At graduation, cadets receive an opportunity to select military fields for which they want to compete. A cadet may attempt a placement test **outside** their current Division.

Conceptual model:

ACADEMY DIVISION â†’ PRACTICUM EXPERIENCE â†’ ACADEMIC RECORD â†’ PRACTICAL RECORD â†’ GRADUATION â†’ MILITARY PLACEMENT EXAM â†’ TARGET MILITARY FIELD â†’ RESULT + RECORDS â†’ FINAL MILITARY ASSIGNMENT

Therefore: a Support/low-status cadet may attempt Intelligence; a Reconnaissance cadet may attempt Intelligence; an Intelligence cadet may attempt another field; a Combat cadet may attempt Engineering â€” but passing is NOT automatic.

## 13. Cadet Agency vs Institutional Constraint

## 20. Post-Academy Career Ecosystem (Proposed)

**Status of this section:** PROPOSED â€” AUTHOR DIRECTION. Not locked. World-design examples only; no organizations, legal systems, or detailed corps are established here.

### 20.1 Core Principle

> ACADEMY IS ONE CAREER GATEWAY, NOT THE ENTIRE WORLD.

Graduating from the Academy does not automatically mean "the character becomes a military soldier." Failing to graduate does NOT mean "the character has no future."

The Academy provides one important route into professional service, but the wider world contains multiple legitimate career paths.

### 20.2 Post-Academy Career Mobility

The world should support multiple career paths, including (illustrative examples only â€” the final list is **not** locked):

- Military service / military placement
- Specialist military or government service
- Government / civil service
- Research or technical organizations
- Private or corporate service
- Private security
- Licensed mercenary / independent contractor work
- Other professional organizations
- Civilian careers

No organizations or legal frameworks are created by listing these.

### 20.3 Military Placement Remains an Option

The existing proposed chain is preserved unchanged:

ACADEMY DIVISION â†’ PRACTICUM EXPERIENCE â†’ ACADEMIC RECORD â†’ PRACTICAL RECORD â†’ GRADUATION â†’ MILITARY PLACEMENT EXAM â†’ TARGET MILITARY FIELD â†’ RESULT + RECORDS â†’ FINAL MILITARY ASSIGNMENT

A cadet may attempt a military field outside their Academy Division. Passing is not guaranteed. Academic and practical performance still matter.

### 20.4 Graduation Is Not a Life Binary

> GRADUATE â‰  AUTOMATIC MILITARY CAREER
> NON-GRADUATE â‰  LIFE OVER

Graduation should open or improve access to certain professional routes, but people who do not graduate can still enter other parts of society. The exact institutional and legal consequences remain undefined.

### 20.5 Mercenary Path (Proposed Possibility)

A cadet or civilian who does not enter conventional military service may potentially pursue licensed mercenary / independent contractor work.

"Mercenary" should NOT automatically mean criminal. The world may distinguish between:

- licensed / legal mercenary work
- independent contracting
- private security
- illegal armed activity

The legal framework is **not** defined here. No licensing authorities, ranks, fees, contracts, jurisdictions, or organizations are invented. These remain future worldbuilding material.

### 20.6 Career Mobility Principle

> ACADEMY DIVISION â‰  PERMANENT CAREER
> ACADEMY GRADUATION â‰  GUARANTEED MILITARY PLACEMENT
> ACADEMY FAILURE â‰  END OF CAREER

The Academy functions as one major institutional gateway among several possible paths.

### 20.7 Story-Pacing Rule

This broader career ecosystem must NOT be explained in detail during the opening Academy chapters. Early chapters focus on: Arthur entering Academy, survival, shared cohort, classes, Division practicum, social hierarchy, examinations, missions, anomalies, character development.

The wider career ecosystem appears only through brief, natural references when relevant. Acceptable early foreshadowing (design examples, not mandatory prose):

- A cadet: "If I fail placement, I'll apply for a mercenary license."
- Another: "My brother never graduated. He's doing contract work now."
- A teacher: "Your Academy record won't be the only thing that determines where you work."

These are not exposition dumps. The wider world is revealed gradually.

### 20.8 Long-Term World Expansion

This framework allows future arcs to leave the Academy without the world feeling like it suddenly expanded. Possible future environments (future possibilities, NOT canon locations):

- military units
- private organizations
- mercenary groups
- government institutions
- research facilities
- anomaly-related organizations
- corporations
- civilian settlements
- independent contractors

### 20.9 Arthur-Specific Implication

Arthur's final career is **not** determined now. His Academy experience should give him options. His eventual career choice should depend on: accumulated knowledge, relationships, reputation, practical experience, military placement results, anomaly/Contract development, personal goals, and circumstances.

Do not predetermine that he becomes an Intelligence officer, military officer, mercenary, government agent, or any other specific profession.

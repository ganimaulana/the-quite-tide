## §0. The load-bearing distinction

**The Five Disciplines are NOT divisions.** Lantern, Tide, Bone, Choir, Hollow are ability *channel-shapes* — how an Esper's Ability manifests [CANON 36_TERMINOLOGY]. A **Division** is what the military institution *does with the cadet* — Combat, Reconnaissance, Tactical/Command, Intelligence, Research, Medical/Support, Logistics, Irregular [PROPOSED]. Every division holds espers of **any** Discipline: a Combat Division cadet may channel Bone or Tide; a Logistics cadet may channel Lantern; a Choir-channeling esper may sort to Medical/Support, Intelligence, *or* Irregular depending on what the Choir output actually does. Confusing the two is the single most common failure mode in Esper-institution fiction; this design forbids it at the bible level.

**Rank (F–S)** measures *conventional Esper capability* — sustainable output, deployability, hazard tolerance. **Division** measures *institutional function*. A B-rank Lantern-channeler with no tactical sense is a Research asset, not a Combat one; an E-rank Bone-channeler with perfect spatial memory is Logistics gold. The sorting pipeline (§9) exists precisely because rank and discipline do not determine function — which is also why the directive's eight divisions are named by *function*, not by power type.

## §2. COMBAT DIVISION — 戦闘科 (Sentō-ka) [PROPOSED working name]

- **Primary function:** direct confrontation with hostile phenomena and hostile actors; the academy's spearhead. When something must be *destroyed, driven off, or physically contained*, Combat goes in first.
- **Typical Ability profile:** high-sustainable-output channelers — Tide (force projection), Bone (durability/weaponization), Lantern (directed discharge) at **C-rank and above** typical; B/A ranks concentrated here. Choir-channelers almost never sort Combat (Choir's output profile rarely sustains direct confrontation [INFERENCE from discipline-as-channel-shape]). Hollow-channelers sort Combat rarely and nervously — their output is real but institutionally frightening.
- **Prestige/status:** **Highest.** The division the public, the media, and most cadets picture when they hear "Esper officer." Its graduation parade leads the academy's.
- **Typical cadet profile:** physically capable at entry (school-athlete tier — *earned further on-page*, not prodigy-tier [standing capability constraint applied to all cadets, not only Arthur]); confident; often from families with service or guild backgrounds; selected as much for aggression-control as for output.
- **Relationship with other divisions:** respectful dependence on Reconnaissance (will not enter blind) and Medical/Support (will not deploy without them); patronizing toward Logistics ("the quartermasters"); openly contemptuous of Irregular ("the filing cabinets") — the contempt is institutional, not personal, which makes it worse.
- **Mission responsibilities:** breach and clear, hostile-phenomenon neutralization, cordon enforcement under Sweeper direction, VIP/asset protection details.
- **Training emphasis:** output control under stress, Erosion-hygiene discipline (Combat has the fastest Erosion curves outside Halcyon's alumni [CANON-adjacent: 15_EDUCATION outcomes]), small-unit tactics, Rules-of-engagement law.
- **Stereotypes:** "meatheads with licenses" (Intelligence's version); "the only division that matters" (their own version); "brave, blunt, and first to die of Erosion" (Medical's version).
- **Institutional advantages:** first equipment priority, highest hazard-pay multipliers, fastest promotion recommendations, the academy's public face — which means the academy's *reputation* is hostage to Combat's conduct.
- **Institutional disadvantages:** highest casualty and Erosion rates; doctrine-bound — Combat's playbook assumes phenomena can be *overpowered*, which fails against Rule-structured anomalies (the exact failure Arthur's method exploits); politically exposed — every Combat excess is a headline the Disclosure-era state cannot afford.

## §3. RECONNAISSANCE DIVISION — 偵察科 (Teisatsu-ka) [PROPOSED working name]

- **Primary function:** see first, be seen never. Pre-mission survey, phenomenon observation, Seep-site monitoring, target acquisition for Combat and Sweeper teams.
- **Typical Ability profile:** sensory and information-channeling espers — **Lantern** (perception, illumination, signal) dominant; Tide (environmental reading); Choir (pattern-listening) in its non-combat register. Ranks skew **D–B**: Recon values *precision and discretion* over output; a D-rank with perfect recall outranks a B-rank with a loud Ability.
- **Prestige/status:** **High — the elite's elite in the quiet sense.** Combat gets the parade; Recon gets the missions that decide whether the parade was necessary. Its top graduates are the most headhunted cadets in the academy (SMD Operations wants them; so does everyone else).
- **Typical cadet profile:** observant, patient, self-effacing; often overlooked in Year 1 because their Abilities don't *do* anything spectacular. The division self-selects for people comfortable being uncredited.
- **Relationship with other divisions:** mutual professional respect with Combat (the spearhead/eyes partnership is the academy's oldest working relationship); quiet rivalry with Intelligence (Recon collects *field* truth; Intelligence collects *file* truth — each thinks the other's truth is incomplete); protective of Irregular in a detached way — Recon cadets are the most likely to notice that an Irregular's "useless" Ability is actually *seeing* something.
- **Mission responsibilities:** site survey, phenomenon behavior logging, early-warning pickets, post-incident verification ("was it really dead? is the site clean?" — the Q277-solution-2 function, institutionalized).
- **Training emphasis:** stealth and Erosion-light operation, observation logging (Still Point's Rule-documentation method [CANON 15_EDUCATION] is taught here first), survival, exfiltration.
- **Stereotypes:** "ghosts" (admiring); "cowards who watch" (Combat's less charitable version); "the division that knows where the bodies are" (Logistics' version, half-joking).
- **Institutional advantages:** lowest casualty rate; highest information leverage per cadet; the division whose graduates the SMD's Ravenhurst field office requests by name.
- **Institutional disadvantages:** invisible work = invisible credit — Recon's mission scores undercount its contribution (a structural grievance that fuels its rivalry with Intelligence); its Abilities are hard to *demonstrate* in inter-division exercises, so it underperforms in the academy's public scoreboard.

## §4. TACTICAL / COMMAND DIVISION — 戦術指揮科 (Senjutsu-shiki-ka) [PROPOSED working name]

- **Primary function:** plan, command, and coordinate. Tactical cadets do not win fights; they make fights winnable. The division produces the academy's mission commanders and staff officers.
- **Typical Ability profile:** the most rank-diverse division: **C–A typical**, but Ability *type* matters less than cognitive profile. Any Discipline sorts here if the aptitude battery shows command potential — including low-output espers whose Ability is a *command asset* (a Lantern-channeler's illumination as battlefield signaling; a Choir-channeler's pattern-sense as threat-board). Notably: the only conventional division where a **D-rank with exceptional tactical aptitude** is not an embarrassment.
- **Prestige/status:** **High — the officer track proper.** Combat has glory; Tactical has *authority*. Its graduates command mixed-division missions, which means they command everyone else's graduates.
- **Typical cadet profile:** analytical, decisive, comfortable with responsibility; often second-generation (officer families); selected for stress reasoning and decision-making under uncertainty — the same battery the Exceptional Aptitude Review draws on (interface: Worker's File 5).
- **Relationship with other divisions:** command authority over all divisions on-mission creates permanent friction — Combat resents being *managed*, Recon resents being *spent*, Irregular resents being *assigned the jobs nobody wants* (which Tactical commanders do, by the book). Tactical's institutional loneliness is structural: everyone needs a commander; nobody loves one.
- **Mission responsibilities:** mission planning, mixed-team command, rules-of-engagement authority, after-action review (the academy's institutional memory of what went wrong).
- **Training emphasis:** wargaming, anomaly-Rule literacy (enough to plan around phenomena without needing to *solve* them), leadership under Erosion-stress, inter-division liaison.
- **Stereotypes:** "armchair generals" (Combat); "the ones who'll order you into the Seep and write the report" (everyone); "the only adults in the building" (their own).
- **Institutional advantages:** command billets are the fastest route to real authority; the division's graduates write the doctrine everyone else trains to — power over the *rules of the game*.
- **Institutional disadvantages:** blamed for every failure, credited for no success (command's ancient bargain); its cadets' Abilities are often *personally* unimpressive, which erodes their authority over high-rank Combat cadets — the rank/authority mismatch is Tactical's permanent wound.

## §5. INTELLIGENCE DIVISION — 情報科 (Jōhō-ka) [PROPOSED working name]

- **Primary function:** know things before they matter. Records analysis, Census-data work, phenomenon-pattern correlation, counter-intelligence, informant handling. The academy's memory and its paranoia.
- **Typical Ability profile:** Lantern (signal, record, illumination-as-analysis) and Choir (pattern-listening) dominant; **D–B typical** — Intelligence values *access and synthesis* over output. A cadet whose Ability is "perfect recall of filed reports" is an Intelligence prize and a Combat joke.
- **Prestige/status:** **Middle — respected, not envied.** Everyone knows Intelligence matters; nobody wants to *be* Intelligence at seventeen. Its status rises sharply after graduation (the SMD's Census Administration and Liaison arms are Intelligence's natural habitat [CANON 06_GOVERNMENTS]).
- **Typical cadet profile:** meticulous, curious, discreet; the cadets who read the footnotes. Often the Year-1 students who asked *why* the drill works.
- **Relationship with other divisions:** the academy's quiet feud with Reconnaissance (§3 — field truth vs file truth); professional contempt for Combat's incuriosity ("they break the phenomenon before we've finished reading it"); **the posting the Irregular stamp most resembles and most threatens** — Intelligence regards stamped cadets as its dumping ground *and* its unacknowledged farm team, a contradiction it has never resolved.
- **Mission responsibilities:** pre-mission intelligence packets, phenomenon-history correlation, informant cultivation (Lantern Bearers, Night Clerks [CANON 12_FACTIONS] — the street sources), counter-infiltration.
- **Training emphasis:** source evaluation, falsification discipline (kill your own hypothesis first), information management, the academy's classification and handling rules.
- **Stereotypes:** "librarians with clearance" (Combat, dismissive); "the ones who knew and didn't tell us" (everyone, after every failure); "spooks" (affectionate, from Recon).
- **Institutional advantages:** information is leverage — Intelligence trades in the one currency every division needs; its graduates are hard to replace, which is job security.
- **Institutional disadvantages:** its victories are invisible and its failures are catastrophic (one missed pattern = one dead team); chronic under-crediting in the academy's public scoreboard; the permanent temptation to hoard rather than share — which makes it the division most likely to *cause* the disasters it exists to prevent.

## §6. RESEARCH DIVISION — 研究科 (Kenkyū-ka) [PROPOSED working name]

- **Primary function:** understand phenomena *as phenomena*. Rule extraction, Erosion science, artifact study, the academy's laboratory arm. Research does not deploy to *win*; it deploys to *learn*.
- **Typical Ability profile:** the widest spread — any Discipline, **E–A**, because Research values *instrumental* Abilities: a Bone-channeler's material sense, a Hollow-channeler's containment affinity, a Choir-channeler's resonance detection. Low-rank espers with precise, narrow Abilities are Research's backbone.
- **Prestige/status:** **Middle — the academy's conscience and its embarrassment.** Respected for Erosion science (White Reed's clinical lineage [CANON 15_EDUCATION] runs through here); mocked for caring more about the phenomenon than the mission.
- **Typical cadet profile:** obsessive, precise, often socially marginal — the cadets who disassembled the training equipment to see how it worked. The division with the highest Irregular-adjacency: many Research cadets were one bad Awakening reading away from Irregular.
- **Relationship with other divisions:** Combat treats Research as dead weight on real missions ("don't bring a clipboard to a breach"); Medical/Support is Research's closest ally (Erosion science is shared ground); **Research is the conventional division most likely to poach Irregular cadets** — an Irregular whose "useless" Ability turns out to be a precise instrument gets a Research transfer offer, which the stamped cohort's faculty advisor experiences as talent-stripping.
- **Mission responsibilities:** field-sample collection, Rule documentation (Still Point's Year-1 method [CANON], adapted), Erosion-field measurement, artifact handling protocols.
- **Training emphasis:** laboratory discipline, Rule-hypothesis method, Erosion-hygiene science, the ethics and law of human-subject study (the disclosed state's bright line — no visible laboratories, per 14_ACADEMY_RESTRUCTURE.md §3.2).
- **Stereotypes:** "clipboards" (Combat); "the ones who'll get us all killed taking notes" (Tactical, exasperated); "the only people here doing actual science" (their own, accurate).
- **Institutional advantages:** the division that *learns* — its knowledge compounds across cohorts; Erosion science gives it a genuine life-saving function nobody can dismiss.
- **Institutional disadvantages:** slowest mission tempo (Research wants a third sample; the phenomenon wants a third victim); its findings are routinely ignored by commanders under time pressure — the academy's tragedy in miniature.

## §7. MEDICAL / SUPPORT DIVISION — 医療支援科 (Iryō-shien-ka) [PROPOSED working name]

- **Primary function:** keep espers alive and functional. Field medicine, Erosion triage and care, Ability-burnout rehabilitation, psychological support. Nobody deploys without Medical/Support's sign-off — which is quiet power.
- **Typical Ability profile:** Bone (tissue, structure) and Tide (flow, circulation) channelers dominant; Lantern (diagnostic illumination) valued; **D–B typical**. Choir-channelers sort here when their output profile is *soothing/regulatory* rather than informational — one of the sorting pipeline's hardest calls.
- **Prestige/status:** **Middle — universally needed, rarely celebrated.** The division everyone thanks in speeches and forgets in budgets. Its internal culture is the academy's healthiest; its external standing is the academy's most undervalued.
- **Typical cadet profile:** steady, compassionate, unflappable; often the cadets who chose service over glory deliberately — or whose Abilities chose for them.
- **Relationship with other divisions:** loved in the abstract, bullied in the particular — Combat treats medics as *equipment* ("bring two"); the division's quiet revenge is the **fitness sign-off**: no sign-off, no deployment, no glory. Deep alliance with Research (Erosion science); protective warmth toward Irregular (Medical sees Irregular's casualties — the bullying's physical receipts — more clearly than any other division).
- **Mission responsibilities:** field triage, Erosion-exposure management, post-mission debrief care, the academy's standing authority to *ground* a cadet (medical hold — the one order a Tactical commander cannot override).
- **Training emphasis:** trauma medicine under phenomenon conditions, Erosion-hygiene practice, triage ethics, the medical-hold authority and its politics.
- **Stereotypes:** "band-aids" (Combat, affectionate-contemptuous); "the division that decides whether you graduate" (accurate, and resented); "saints" (Irregular's version, sincere).
- **Institutional advantages:** the medical-hold veto — the single strongest *negative* power in the cadet hierarchy; genuine indispensability (no other division can do its job).
- **Institutional disadvantages:** systematically under-resourced (glory buys budgets; triage doesn't); its cadets absorb everyone else's trauma — the division with the highest quiet-attrition; chronically outranked in the public scoreboard by divisions whose work photographs better.

## §8. LOGISTICS DIVISION — 兵站科 (Heitan-ka) [PROPOSED working name]

- **Primary function:** the academy's circulatory system. Supply, transport, containment-equipment maintenance, Seep-site cordon engineering, quartermaster functions. Wars — and academies — run on Logistics.
- **Typical Ability profile:** the most *practically* sorted: Tide (movement, transport), Bone (material, construction), Lantern (inventory, illumination-as-organization) at **E–C typical** — Logistics is where reliable mid-rank espers with *useful-shaped* Abilities go. A C-rank whose Ability is "perfect spatial packing" is a Logistics star.
- **Prestige/status:** **Middle-low — the academy's working class.** Respected by everyone who has ever needed ammunition, transport, or a working cordon *right now*; invisible the rest of the time. Its cadets have the driest humor in the academy.
- **Typical cadet profile:** pragmatic, unromantic, competent; often from ordinary backgrounds — the division with the least legacy-family concentration and the most first-generation cadets.
- **Relationship with other divisions:** cordial contempt for everyone ("you'd all be dead without us, and you know it"); particular friction with Combat over equipment priority (Combat gets first pick — Logistics decides what "first pick" *means* in practice); **the division Irregular relies on most** — Irregular gets last equipment priority by the book, and Logistics quartermasters are the ones who quietly route the *usable* castoffs instead of the broken ones.
- **Mission responsibilities:** mission supply chains, containment-cordon construction, equipment maintenance and field repair, casualty evacuation transport.
- **Training emphasis:** quartermaster craft, field engineering, transport operation under phenomenon conditions, the *real* curriculum: making do with what the priority list left you.
- **Stereotypes:** "quartermasters" (neutral); "the reason anything works" (their own, accurate); "thieves" (Combat, when the good gear goes missing — it went to someone who needed it).
- **Institutional advantages:** controls the *material* reality every division operates in — soft power of the most concrete kind; the division whose skills transfer directly to civilian life (its graduates are never unemployed).
- **Institutional disadvantages:** lowest public prestige of the conventional divisions; blamed for every shortage, credited for no sufficiency; its competence is *assumed* — which means it is only ever noticed failing.

## §9. IRREGULAR DIVISION — 特異科 (Tokui-ka, "the special ones" — the name is half official designation, half slur) [PROPOSED working name]

> **[SUPERSEDED — Organizational Revision, 2026-09-23.]** This section described the old division model and is retained as worker history. Current doctrine: Irregular is a **cross-division classification** (proposed formal name 特別分類), not a division — see `03_IRREGULAR_CLASSIFICATION.md` and the coordinator amendment appended to this file. Do not cite §9's divisional language as current.

- **Primary function:** the division for cadets whom the sorting cannot place. **Records-Intelligence**: archive work, incident documentation, cold-case review, Seep-site monitoring logs, unclassified-incident paperwork — low-risk, high-paperwork, real. The charter line: "the system does not know what to do with them."
- **Typical Ability profile:** deliberately unspecifiable — that is the point. **F-rank cadets** (including ERROR/UNDEFINED readings like Arthur's); unstable or strange Abilities; failed/abnormal Awakening cases; highly specialized Abilities with no military application *yet*; unusual compatibility problems (the Ability works but the cadet can't be deployed with others); special cases the Sorting Board declines to defend. Disciplines: all five, in their most awkward registers. Ranks: **F–E typical, D rare and suspicious** (a D-rank in Irregular means the sorting failed twice, or the Ability is *genuinely* frightening).
- **Prestige/status:** **Lowest. Explicitly.** "Garbage Division" is the common name; the faculty's official euphemism ("Special Classification Division") is used by nobody under thirty. The directive requires this to be visible and unambiguous (objectives 11–12).
- **Typical cadet profile:** the academy's misfits — not the romantic kind, the *administrative* kind. Defensive, prickly, or withdrawn; some bitter, some weirdly cheerful; all of them have been told, in writing, that the system has no use for them. (Full roster is the Irregular-Division worker's file; interface only here.)
- **Relationship with other divisions:** the academy's designated inferior — every division's contempt needs an address, and Irregular is it. Combat's open contempt; Intelligence's proprietary unease (see §5); Research's poaching; Medical's protective warmth; Logistics' quiet material solidarity; Recon's detached noticing; Tactical's bureaucratic assignment of the unwanted jobs. **No division is Irregular's ally in public.** This isolation is structural, not accidental — and it is what makes the division cohere internally.
- **Mission responsibilities:** records and documentation support to mixed-division missions (the lawful reason Irregular cadets are *present* at real incidents); Seep-site monitoring; cold-case paperwork; the academy's unclassified-incident archive. On paper, never the sharp end. In practice — the directive's engine — the ones filing the records are the ones who *read* them first.
- **Training emphasis:** records craft (the Still Point documentation method [CANON], taught without the prestige), Erosion-hygiene basics, the Exceptional Aptitude Review's aptitude battery (analytical reasoning, anomaly interpretation, pattern recognition — the aptitudes the conventional divisions don't test for), and — unofficially, inevitably — **making do**: the Logistics castoffs, the unwanted jobs, the missions nobody else wanted, done anyway.
- **Stereotypes:** "garbage division" (universal); "the filing cabinets" (Combat); "a rounding error with uniforms" (Tactical, in its crueler moods); "the ones who'll surprise you" (Recon, quietly, later).
- **Institutional advantages:** **invisibility** — nobody watches the records office, which means the records office sees everything; **low expectations** — every success is a surprise, every failure is priced in; **the archive itself** — the academy's institutional memory sits in Irregular's hands, and memory is leverage (interface: Arthur's Records→Analyst ladder).
- **Institutional disadvantages:** last equipment priority, last mission pick, first blame; instructor authority bias (the social-hierarchy worker's file); the service bond binds them to six years of being the academy's designated inferior — exit is failure, endurance is humiliation, *unless the division itself changes what it is* (the dark-horse arc worker's file).

**Design guardrail [PROPOSED, directive objective 9]:** Irregular is NOT secretly all overpowered. Its members are *individually* limited — the division's eventual strength is **unusual combinations, complementary weaknesses, adaptability, and (later) Arthur's leadership** (directive objective 10). Any Irregular cadet who turns out to be individually formidable must be the *exception that the sorting genuinely missed*, not the rule — and the story must show the sorting's miss as a system failure, not a secret plan.

## §10. The sorting mechanism — how the Academy assigns cadets to divisions

[PROPOSED] The Sorting Board (chaired by the Deputy Commandant (Sorting); see 14_ACADEMY_RESTRUCTURE.md §3.3) runs every Year-1 cadet through a four-stage pipeline. The design goal: it must feel like a **real military bureaucracy** — rule-bound, defensible, appealable on paper and unappealable in practice, and wrong in the specific ways bureaucracies are wrong.

### Stage 1 — Awakening reading (arrival week)

Lantern examiners [CANON] perform the formal reading: Ability classification (or ERROR/UNDEFINED), Rank determination (F–S), Discipline channel-shape identification. The reading is filed to the Census [CANON 06_GOVERNMENTS] and becomes the cadet's permanent institutional identity. **Arthur's reading: Ability ERROR/UNDEFINED, Rank F** — a true reading of his conventional Esper output (nil), filed as *broken* rather than interesting (per the standing capability rule and PACING premises §1.1–2). The reading triggers the charter's ERROR protocol (14_ACADEMY_RESTRUCTURE.md §3.2).

### Stage 2 — Examiner classification (Month 1)

The examiner corps converts the reading into an **institutional recommendation**: which divisions could *use* this cadet. The recommendation is a vector, not a verdict — e.g., "Combat-possible (output sufficient, temperament untested); Recon-possible (perception adequate); Logistics-probable." For unclassifiable readings the recommendation is the honest bureaucratic sentence: **"No conventional division recommends acceptance."** That sentence is Irregular's founding document, repeated per cadet.

### Stage 3 — Aptitude battery (Months 2–6, alongside foundation training)

Standardized testing across: physical fitness and Erosion tolerance; analytical reasoning; tactical reasoning; stress decision-making; teamwork compatibility; and — critically — **anomaly interpretation and pattern recognition** (the aptitudes the conventional divisions underweight and the Exceptional Aptitude Review measures in depth). The battery is where a D-rank with command potential sorts Tactical (§4), where a quiet E-rank sorts Recon (§3), and where Arthur's **exceptional analytical aptitude against a Rank-F reading** first appears as an institutional anomaly: the file says *broken*, the battery says *remarkable*, and the system has no box for both at once (directive Part IV — the exam worker owns the mechanism; the ambiguity is established here).

### Stage 4 — Institutional needs (end of Year 1)

The Sorting Board reconciles Stages 1–3 against **the academy's manpower requirements**: each division has a quota (Combat needs bodies; Logistics always needs more; Irregular has no quota — it has *capacity*). Borderline cadets sort by need, not merit — the pipeline's open secret, and the source of half the academy's grievances ("I tested Tactical and got Logistics because Logistics was short"). Appeals exist, are rarely granted, and are remembered against the appellant.

### Arthur's sort, worked example [INFERENCE — the directive's required outcome, shown as the pipeline's deterministic output]

1. Reading: ERROR/UNDEFINED, Rank F → Stage-2 recommendation: "No conventional division recommends acceptance."
2. Aptitude battery: physical — ordinary (school-sports baseline [standing constraint]); analytical/anomaly-interpretation — exceptional (flagged for the Exceptional Aptitude Review).
3. Institutional needs: no conventional division has a use for an unclassifiable F-rank; Irregular has capacity.
4. **Assignment: Intelligence Division, Records & Archives.** Not his choice (directive objective 6). Bureaucratically logical: unclassifiable Ability + F-rank + no combat function + ordinary fitness = the lowest-risk placement the system can defend on paper. Socially read as failure (directive Part III). The Exceptional Aptitude Review (exam worker's file) runs in parallel — it measures *aptitude*, changes nothing about *rank* or *classification*, and deepens the institutional ambiguity rather than resolving it.

## §11. Division interdependencies in the field — who needs whom and resents it

[PROPOSED] Mixed-division missions (the mission-system worker's file) are the academy's normal operating mode because **no division is self-sufficient**. The dependency graph — and its resentments — is a primary story engine (directive Part VIII, objective 14):

| Division | Cannot function without | Resents it because |
|---|---|---|
| Combat | Recon (won't enter blind), Medical (won't deploy unsigned), Logistics (no supply, no fight), Tactical (needs orders to be legal) | needing permission feels like weakness; the spearhead hates the hand that steadies it |
| Recon | Combat (findings need someone to act on them), Intelligence (field truth needs file truth to mean anything) | its work is credited to whoever acts on it |
| Tactical | Everyone (command without troops is a seminar) | command's ancient bargain — blamed for all, credited for none |
| Intelligence | Recon (files without field are fiction), Irregular's records function (the archive it pretends not to need) | Irregular holds the records Intelligence claims as its domain |
| Research | Combat/Logistics (samples don't collect themselves), Medical (Erosion science is shared) | its method is treated as delay by everyone under time pressure |
| Medical | Combat (casualties are its caseload — the dependency it hates naming), Research (Erosion science) | its veto power makes it feared rather than loved |
| Logistics | Everyone (customers), no one (it is the only division that could, theoretically, walk away) | assumed competence = invisible competence |

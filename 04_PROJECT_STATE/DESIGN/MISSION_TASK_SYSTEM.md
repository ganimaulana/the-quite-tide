## 1. Design principles

1. **Arthur is not a quest hub.** He is a night-shift records clerk whom work *finds* — because he is Loud (pressure finds Loud people — 29.B.3's anchor physics [CANON]), because his day job *is* the intake (the night archive sees what the day shift files), and because the Seal points (Use-8 ported). He does not hang a shingle; the world knocks.
2. **The mission economy obeys 02.I–II [KEEP].** Payment is denominated differently — never just money. Information is the scarce resource — so *investigation* is the billable service, not contracts (impressions are non-transferable — Q014 [ADAPT] — so there is no contract market; there is a *solving* market).
3. **The inadmissibility ceiling.** Impression-derived knowledge is inadmissible without corroboration (ANOMALY_DESIGN.md §2). This *caps his market value*: he can solve what he cannot prove — which makes him cheap, deniable, and perfect for clients who don't want proof. The economy prices his exact limitation.
4. **No mission is only a mission** (the three-thread rule, §6.7): every assignment carries at least one of — a faction thread, a family thread, an origin thread. Missions are never filler; they are the delivery mechanism for the engines.

## 2. The eleven encounter sources

For each: **who** · **what they pay** (incentives) · **what it costs him** · **why they can't solve it themselves** (structural, never "they're lazy") · **the source's failure mode** (exploit closure).

### 2.1 Academy assignments
- **Who:** the Academy's field-practicum board [PROPOSED institution — institutional design is another worker's scope; this file specifies only the mission-interface]. Arthur is attached as a *mature auditor* (records-clerk secondment — the cover his filing needs).
- **Pay:** practicum credit, archive access (the Academy's library is the *real* wage — firsthand-adjacent records, Q344), and *cover* (a student ID explains his presence at phenomena).
- **Cost:** the assignments are *curriculum-safe* — which means they're chosen to be solvable by students, which means the Academy has *already decided what they are* (the client-lies rule, §6.2: the brief is the first hypothesis to kill). The cost is *being wrong in public*: Academy missions are witnessed, graded, filed — no tether-mask.
- **Why they can't:** they *can* — that's the point. Assignments are training. The structural reason he gets *his* assignments: the board assigns by *filed tier*, and his Laggard filing (MV-T1/C-D [CANON], 29.B.12) sorts him into the underbrush practicum — exactly where the Seal hunts. The system misfiles him into opportunity.
- **Failure mode:** the Academy's grading *is* a filing — a practicum report that describes his method accurately is an intelligence product. He must solve *while being seen to be ordinary* (the undervalue engine's home ground).

### 2.2 Government missions
- **Who:** the municipal government (harbor office, records office), the Classification Office's local desk — small, unfiled, deniable commissions.
- **Pay:** money (modest), *jurisdiction* (a city work-order opens doors a clerk can't), and *disposal* (the government takes the settled phenomenon's paperwork — the filing burden lifts).
- **Cost:** the 72-hour clock starts *officially* (05.2 [KEEP]) — government missions are the fastest route to the auditors' attention. Also: the work-order *names* him — the paper trail is the price.
- **Why they can't:** **attention economics** (05.5 [CANON]): the Classification Office can't spare auditors for T1/T2 underbrush — the Seismograph doesn't see it, the auditors are busy with T3+. They hire the underbrush-solver *because* he's beneath their threshold. Also: the government can't *officially* do what he does unofficially (settle without decommissioning — Q336: the Compact's doctrine is containment-or-decommission; *settlement* is his unlicensed third option).
- **Failure mode:** the commission that *succeeds too visibly* — a settled phenomenon with a city work-order attached is a re-audit trigger with his name on it (05.4 [ADAPT]).

### 2.3 Hunter missions
- **Who:** licensed hunters [PROPOSED: the Compact licenses freelance hunters for decommission work — institutional detail is another worker's scope] — subcontracting the part they can't do.
- **Pay:** hunter money (the best-paying source — danger pay), *protection* (hunters owe debts; a hunter who owes Arthur is a bodyguard who can't be bought), and *intelligence* (hunters see the underbrush's teeth).
- **Cost:** hunters *decommission* (05.4 [CANON]) — every hunter mission carries the **kill-pressure**: the client's default solution is destruction, and Arthur's method (settle, don't kill — Q053's mechanical forfeit) is *incompatible* with the hunter's contract. The cost is *conflict*: he must solve it before they kill it, or solve it *despite* them.
- **Why they can't:** **doctrinal**: hunters are licensed to *end* Rules (decommission by substrate destruction — 05.4); the client wants the phenomenon *kept* (a useful anomaly, a civic one — 28's catalog precedent [CANON]: the Bell, the Spoons, the Salt Garden are *kept*). Settlement is not in the hunter's license. He is hired for the third option the law doesn't name.
- **Failure mode:** the hunter who decides the settlement *is* the threat — "you're protecting it" — and reclassifies *him* as the mission. The kill-pressure inverts.

### 2.4 Research requests
- **Who:** the Cartographers, the Archivists (SUP-008), university-adjacent researchers — the people who *study* rather than *contain*.
- **Pay:** *records* (the real currency — 02.II [KEEP]: information is scarce; the Archivists' holdings are the wage), *equipment* (ward-tuned instruments, firsthand-adjacent), and *legitimacy* (a researcher's letterhead explains his presence better than a student ID).
- **Cost:** researchers *publish* (in closed journals — but closed journals leak, Q348: secrecy is work [CANON]). Every research mission risks his method becoming *literature*. Also: researchers want *data*, not *settlement* — their incentive is to *prolong* the phenomenon (a settled anomaly is a closed paper). The cost is *misaligned incentives*: they pay him to understand it and hope he doesn't *finish* it.
- **Why they can't:** **firsthand** (Q344 [ADAPT]): the Veil edits their instruments and their retention — his tether-witness is the instrument (29.B.5 [CANON]: records, not knowledge). He's Loud; he can *be there* and *remember*. Also: researchers are *quiet* — the phenomena don't manifest for them the way they do for him (29.B.3's anchor physics: Loud people attract pressure).
- **Failure mode:** the paper that describes his method *accurately enough* — the closed journal becomes the Red Ledger's shopping list (Q086's footprint logic: attention is the cost of being useful).

### 2.5 Family records
- **Who:** no one — the drawer assigns. Eleanor's *protective charm* drawer, the grandmother's papers, the 1987 appraisal, the salt-customs nobody explained.
- **Pay:** *origin threads* (ARTIFACT_DESIGN.md §3–§4: the custody history is the origin mystery's paper trail), *customs* (the folk heuristics are data — 03.2 [CANON]: "we don't sell grandmother's thing" is a Rule-condition's folk memory), and *cover* (family business explains everything).
- **Cost:** the family *notices* (Q096 [ADAPT]: his silence protects them; the records make silence harder — every drawer he opens is a question Eleanor will ask). The cost is *intimacy*: the missions are in his mother's handwriting.
- **Why no one else can:** no one else *reads* them — the drawer is his by custody (ARTIFACT_DESIGN.md §4). Also: the records are *pre-Compact* in places (the grandmother's papers predate the filing system — single-letter notation, 05.1) — the Classification Office *can't read its own history* without him.
- **Failure mode:** the record that *names* something the family was keeping *from* him — the drawer assigns missions whose client is dead and whose *real* client is the past.

### 2.6 Random incidents
- **Who:** no one — the Tide assigns. He's Loud; pressure finds Loud people (29.B.3 [CANON]). The night shift, the waterfront, the 03:33 — the city *happens* to him.
- **Pay:** *novelty* (Q057: the gauge pays for new understanding — random incidents are the highest-yield source because they're *unsorted*), *first claim* (no client means no paper trail — the cleanest missions), and *cover* (being in the wrong place is his job — he's a night clerk; the night is full).
- **Cost:** *no brief* (the client-lies rule's inverse: no brief means no hypothesis — he starts from zero every time), *no backup* (no one knows he's there), and *the Seal's appetite* (random incidents are where the dowsing hunts — §2.8 — and the stone doesn't care about his schedule).
- **Why no one else can:** there's no *client* — the incident hasn't been *noticed* yet by anyone with jurisdiction. He's first because he's *there* (night shift) and *Loud* (pressure finds him). The structural reason is *temporal*: he arrives before the filing exists.
- **Failure mode:** the incident that's *bait* — something that manifests *for* Loud people (the Quiet learns voices — ANOMALY_DESIGN.md §7.8). Random is never random twice.

### 2.7 Black market
- **Who:** the Factor's buyers, Gray Market appraisers, the Red Ledger's cutouts [CANON factions per audit/task] — post-activation approaches (Q086's footprint: the 1987 appraisal is the cold file they *find*).
- **Pay:** *real money* (the only source that pays what the work is worth — which is the tell), *intelligence* (the market knows what's *priced* — prices are information, 02.II), and *access* (the market reaches places the Compact doesn't).
- **Cost:** *the appraisal* — every black-market mission *examines* him (the Factor's buyers appraise the solver the way the 1987 appraiser appraised the stone). The cost is *being priced*: once the market knows what he is, he's inventory. Also: the market wants *the Seal*, not the solving (Q014/Q084: they can't use it — so they hire the *holder*: "bring the stone and your hands").
- **Why they can't:** **the void** (ARTIFACT_DESIGN.md §11): impressions void in unlawful hands; the Seal goes dormant for thieves. They can't *take* it — so they must *rent* him. The structural reason is the custody Rule-condition: the market's entire toolkit (theft, purchase, coercion) is *mechanically* void. They need him *willing* — which is why the money is real.
- **Failure mode:** the job that's *an appraisal* — the mission is the interview, the phenomenon is the test, and the *real* client is deciding whether to *acquire* him. Every black-market mission might be a tryout.

### 2.8 Artifact resonance
- **Who:** the Seal — the dowsing (Use-8 ported from 29.B.11 [ADAPT]): the stone points at pressure. No client, no brief, no pay.
- **Pay:** *first claim* (like random incidents, but *directed* — the stone points at what it *wants*), and *gauge-yield* (the Seal points at what will *feed* it — the missions it assigns are the ones that pay its invoice).
- **Cost:** *no veto* (the stone doesn't explain; the counterparty-echo means the pointed-at phenomenon *notices* being pointed at), *the appetite* (the dowsing inverts at high fill — ARTIFACT_DESIGN.md §12.4: the fuller the Seal, the more the world comes to *it* — the missions escalate *because* he's succeeding), and *schedule* (the stone points at 3 AM; the stone doesn't care about his shift).
- **Why no one else can:** no one else *holds* it. The structural reason is *custody* (ARTIFACT_DESIGN.md §4) — the dowsing is a property of the holder-stone pair, not a service.
- **Failure mode:** the pointing that's *a lure* — at high fill the Seal attracts phenomena (the inversion). The mission the stone assigns might be *the stone feeding itself* — and Arthur has to decide whether the pointer is still *his*.

### 2.9 Contract clues
- **Who:** the impressions — residues and relations (§4 of ANOMALY_DESIGN.md: each impression yields leads, never answers). Solved Rules *point* — the Bellwether's route crosses the Quiet's edge; the Letters name the Lantern's unmourned.
- **Pay:** *chain logic* — each solved phenomenon is a *key to one room* (scope-lock), and rooms *connect* (the chain is the real investigation — the serial engine).
- **Cost:** *the chain doesn't end* (every answer is a lead — the five-fold brake [§4] means closure is *procedural*, never *total*), and *the counterparty-echo* (invoking for clues notifies the source — asking the Letters about the Lantern tells the Drowned he's *linking* them).
- **Why no one else can:** the clues are *impression-native* — they exist only in the holder's understanding (firsthand rule, Q344). No one else can follow a chain whose links are *his* memories (Veil-edited, Q413 — he must transcribe before they rot).
- **Failure mode:** the chain that *loops* — a clue that points back at the Seal (the Locker Bank's seam-work pointing at Theory A — the origin mystery surfacing *inside* a mission chain). The chain becomes self-referential — and the question becomes whether he's investigating the world or the *stone*.

### 2.10 Hidden organizations
- **Who:** the Red Ledger (acquisition), the Pale Court (bloodline pretexts — EVENT-098 [CANON]), and the unnamed others [PROPOSED: the class is designed as an interface pattern; new orgs are the institutional worker's scope].
- **Pay:** *the highest pay* (money, protection, *answers* — the Ledger knows things the Compact doesn't), and *the devil's legitimacy* (a hidden org's cover is better than the Academy's — no one audits what doesn't exist).
- **Cost:** *the real client* (the cutout rule: he never knows who's paying — the mission's *stated* objective is never the *real* one), *the debt* (hidden orgs don't pay in money — they pay in *obligation*; the Pale Court's pretext is *kinship* — "we're family" is a more expensive currency than cash), and *the exposure* (working for the Ledger *is* the Ledger's intelligence on him — the job is the appraisal, §2.7's failure mode, professionalized).
- **Why they can't:** **visibility**: they can't be *seen* hiring (the Ledger acquiring openly is a Compact incident; the Court acting openly breaks its own bloodline theater). They need a *cutout* — a deniable solver with no visible affiliation (his technically-true filing is the *product* they're buying). Also: **the void** (§2.7) — they can't take the Seal, so they rent the holder through layers.
- **Failure mode:** the mission whose *real* objective is *him* — the Pale Court's bloodline pretext isn't a pretext *for the mission*; the mission is the pretext *for the bloodline* (EVENT-098's logic: they're collecting the wrong thing for the right reason — and the mission is how they get close enough to *check*).

### 2.11 Personal investigation
- **Who:** himself — the origin mystery (ARTIFACT_DESIGN.md §3), the Letters' "why the night shift," the Locker Bank's seam-work, the 9/18 quarantine's *edges* (not the event — the fence around it), his mother's drawer.
- **Pay:** *the only pay that matters* (the series' questions are his questions — the engines' fuel), and *no clock* (no 72-hour rule on his own curiosity — the one mission class with no legal bomb).
- **Cost:** *everything else* (personal investigation has no client, no pay, no cover — it's the mission class that *spends* the other classes' earnings), and *the fence* (Q100 [KEEP]: the 9/18 UNDETERMINED and Saitō's glance UNRESOLVED are *fenced* — his personal investigation must *approach* the fence without *crossing* it — the discipline *is* the cost).
- **Why no one else can:** no one else *cares* — the origin mystery is his custody, his stone, his invoice. The structural reason is *standing*: only the holder can ask the stone what it is.
- **Failure mode:** the investigation that *succeeds* — deciding the origin (§3's decision procedures) *spends* mystery capital (33's rules [CANON] — new intentional mysteries need CHANGELOG + coordinator approval). The personal missions are budgeted against the series' mystery reserves.

## 4. The mission economy — pricing and costs

**[PROPOSED]**

- **What's billable:** *investigation* (his time, his method, his witness) — the service is *solving*, not *contracts* (Q014: impressions are non-transferable — there is no contract market). *Records* are the product (29.B.5 [CANON]: records, not knowledge — he sells the *file*, not the *understanding*).
- **What's not billable:** impressions (can't be sold); *proof* (can't be provided — inadmissibility, §1.3); *certainty* (fallibility, 29.B.5 [CANON]).
- **The price list (denominated differently — 02.I [KEEP]):**
  - Academy: credit + archive access (paid in *future capability*).
  - Government: money + jurisdiction (paid in *access*).
  - Hunters: money + debts (paid in *obligation* — the most expensive currency).
  - Researchers: records + equipment (paid in *information* — the scarcest resource, 02.II).
  - Black market: real money (paid in *money* — which is the tell: money is the *cheapest* currency in this economy, and the market paying in it means they're buying something *else*).
  - Hidden orgs: obligation (paid in *belonging* — the Pale Court's kinship pretext).
- **What every mission costs him** (the universal price, non-negotiable):
  - *Resonance* per invocation (ANOMALY_DESIGN.md §5: the budget).
  - *Attention* — two-way (the counterparty-echo; the client's appraisal; the paper trail).
  - *The clock* — 72 hours from first official notice (05.2), ticking per mission.
  - *The Veil* — his testimony is inadmissible (he can solve what he can't prove — the ceiling on his value and the floor of his deniability).
- **Exploit closure:** the economy can't be *arbitraged* (no contract market — Q014; no proof premium — inadmissibility; no salary — novelty-gating, Q057/Q066). The only appreciating asset is *method* (firsthand understanding — Q344), which can't be transferred.

## 5. Why "one quest per chapter" never happens — the backlog

**[PROPOSED]** The directive forbids "one quest per chapter." The machinery:

- **The backlog:** he carries **3–5 open threads** at all times (the intake's six channels guarantee it). Chapters *collide* threads — they don't *complete* them. A chapter is a *collision*, not a quest.
- **The chain logic** (§2.9): solved phenomena yield *leads*, not closures — every mission *opens* at least as much as it closes (the five-fold brake: residues and relations). The backlog is *thermodynamic*: it grows by working.
- **The tide schedule:** phenomena are *tidal and seasonal* (the 03:33 is nightly; the Lantern is Obon; the Quiet is low-tide) — missions have *windows*, not deadlines. Missing a window *defers*, not *fails* — the backlog is a *calendar*, not a queue.
- **The escalation rule:** the Seal's dowsing inverts at high fill (ARTIFACT_DESIGN.md §12.4) — the *better* he does, the *more* missions arrive. Success is the intake. There is no "clearing the board."

## 6. Anti-formula rules

**[PROPOSED]** — the explicit machinery that keeps the mission system from becoming a formula:

1. **No intake repeats** (§3): six channels, mutually uncoordinated — no two missions arrive the same way twice in a row.
2. **The client-lies rule:** every mission brief is *wrong* in at least one load-bearing way. The brief is the first hypothesis the threefold test kills (ANOMALY_DESIGN.md §2). (Random incidents and the stone's pointing invert this: *no* brief — starting from zero is its own discipline.)
3. **The price-is-denominated-differently rule** (§4): no mission pays in just money — and the *real* price is never the stated one (the Salt Law's ledger logic, applied to the economy).
4. **Cold cases:** the drawer (§2.5) and the archive assign *old* work — the past is a client with no statute of limitations. At least one open thread is always *decades* old.
5. **The stone assigns** (§2.8): the dowsing gives missions no one asked for — the anti-formula engine. He doesn't choose; the stone points.
6. **Mission collision:** two sources' missions *interfere* — the hunter's target is the researcher's subject is the black market's lot (the three-thread rule, §1.4, operationalized). Threads don't run parallel; they *collide*.
7. **The three-thread rule** (§1.4): every mission carries a faction thread, a family thread, or an origin thread. No filler — missions are the engines' delivery mechanism.
8. **The fence rule** (Q100 [KEEP]): personal investigation (§2.11) may *approach* the 9/18 quarantine and Saitō's glance but never *cross* — the discipline is load-bearing. A mission that would resolve a fenced unknown is *refused by the design* (not by the character — by the coordinator).
9. **The refusal precedent** (ANOMALY_DESIGN.md §7.9): the Seal *refuses* some missions (the Unsaid Thing). The mission system includes *unplayable* missions — and the refusal is always the scene's climax, never its absence.

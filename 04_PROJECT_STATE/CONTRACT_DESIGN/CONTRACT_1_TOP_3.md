## Finalist ranking

### #1 — Candidate 7: THE POLITE KNOCK (rule-fragment / exception-clause finder)

**Why it ranks first.** The First Mooring is the *calibration* contract (Q196): it must teach Arthur — and the reader — the system's grammar in the fewest scenes. The Knock teaches the single most load-bearing sentence in the canon: **powers have conditions, and conditions can be attacked** (04 §4.7 [KEEP] — "the protagonist's entire survival strategy"). Every future survival scene runs on knocked terms; every future contract cites them as precedent. No other candidate puts the bible's core mechanic in the reader's hands this early.

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Investigation utility | Highest in the set. Converts a week of observation into one confirmed sentence of terms — not the solve, but the *shape* of the solve. Directly feeds Rule interference, his canonical survival strategy. |
| Anti-auto-solve safety | Strong by construction (demonstrated-only, one knock per phenomenon, personally-observed, presence-only), with one sharp edge: the felt-certainty failure mode — the knock can *confirm a misreading* with full conviction. This is the candidate's main risk (see trade-offs). |
| Future-plot connectivity | Highest mechanical feed-forward: knocked terms become the archive's most reliable records and the precedent library for all later interference work. The "bound" vocabulary hook ties the Artifact mystery to a T1 buoy — the small thing speaking the big language. |
| Chapter-ending satisfaction | Repeatable chapter buttons: the knock's answer as a closing sentence (*"it cannot cross what is bound"*) is a perfect scene-ender, producible once per phenomenon without repetition fatigue because each answer is different. |
| Abuse resistance | Strong: knock-for-hire requires Arthur's own fieldwork per phenomenon (he cannot knock what he hasn't stood in front of); Depth scales with tier, pricing curiosity about dangerous things; Sapient exclusion keeps it from becoming interrogation. |
| Originality vs shadows | Not a system-grant (Solo Leveling), not a vow (HxH — it's a *receipt*, the inverse direction: the world states terms, he doesn't), not containment paperwork. The demonstrated-only constraint — the tool refuses to tell you what you haven't earned by watching — has no shadow analog. |

**Trade-offs / risks (honest):**
- The felt-certainty failure mode is the sharpest blade in the set: if the Knock confirms a misreading early, readers may distrust the tool *as a tool* rather than learning the intended lesson (distrust *unearned* certainty). The prose must dramatize the distinction — the failure must read as *his* error, legible in hindsight, never as the system lying.
- Highest utility means highest temptation to overuse: the coordinator should note the Depth-scaling-by-tier and one-tap rules are doing real load-bearing work here; if drafts soften them, the Knock becomes a Rule-extraction service and E7/E8-class exploits reopen.
- Its Artifact hook ("bound" vocabulary) is subtler than the Ledger's or Brine's — a reader-attention risk: the mystery beat could pass unnoticed where the Ledger's INSUFFICIENT DEPTH cannot be missed.

### #2 — Candidate 9: THE LEDGER OF SMALL DEBTS (truth-fragment / archive)

**Why it ranks second.** It is the safest, most original, and most future-loaded candidate — and the weakest *first impression*. The Ledger is the endgame archive (Q187's ARCHIVE shape) in seed form: every later contract feeds it, and its value compounds monotonically across the series. Its Artifact hook — *INSUFFICIENT DEPTH — THE TERMS OF THIS QUESTION EXCEED THE SOLVE* — is the single best chapter-ending sentence among all ten candidates. It ranks below the Knock only because the calibration contract must also *move chapters now*, and the early Ledger's most common answer is INSUFFICIENT RECORD — a hard sell for directive §14's "frequent explicit satisfaction" in the opening arc.

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Investigation utility | High but delayed: the archive starts small and earns its power. Its unique value is anti-misreading audit — the only defense against Arthur's own fallibility (29 §B.5 [KEEP]) — which matters more the deeper the series goes. |
| Anti-auto-solve safety | Highest in the set. Archive-bounded absolutely: it cannot answer about the unsolved, the future, or other people (Q439 option 1). Daily cap + per-question Depth pricing. The disclosed misreading-laundering failure mode (it certifies tests, not truth) is dramatically productive rather than dangerous. |
| Future-plot connectivity | Highest long-term: it *is* the archive the endgame requires. Every solve, knock, and carried impression flows into it. No other candidate grows in value this reliably. |
| Chapter-ending satisfaction | The Artifact question's answer is the set's peak single beat. Repeatable satisfaction is weaker than the Knock's (daily cap limits scene frequency by design). |
| Abuse resistance | Highest in the set: the oracle that cannot see beyond his own notes. Faction-intelligence abuse fails at the archive boundary. |
| Originality vs shadows | The strongest differentiation: Solo Leveling's system *tells* the protagonist things; the Ledger *refuses to*. HxH has no analog. Containment-fiction-adjacent in form (documentation) but inverted in horror — the dread is what the archive *won't* say. |

**Trade-offs / risks (honest):**
- Slow start is the real cost: as Contract #1, its first dozen invocations mostly return INSUFFICIENT RECORD. If the opening arc needs visible wins, the Ledger under-delivers where the Knock over-delivers. (Mitigation available: pair with the natural-sequel structure — but the First Mooring holds one contract, so the coordinator must weigh pacing honestly.)
- The misreading-laundering failure mode, while disclosed, requires sustained prose discipline: the reader must *watch* him learn not to trust TRUE — if drafts let TRUE function as certainty, the candidate silently becomes the oracle its design forbids.
- Meta-position risk: a contract whose Grantor is the medium itself (not a phenomenon) teaches the *archive* before the *world*. The calibration contract arguably should teach phenomena first, systems second.

### #3 — Candidate 4: BRINE ON THE TONGUE (sensory-echo / Rule-family taxonomy)

**Why it ranks third.** It is the most sensorially original candidate and the purest Artifact-mystery hook: *the family artifact tastes of nothing* — every phenomenon has a flavor; the one object at the center of his life has none. Is it empty, or is it the baseline everything else is measured against? That question is unresolvable at this stage by design, which makes it the longest-burning hook in the set. It ranks third because it is the least mechanically load-bearing: it accelerates the *start* of every investigation (family-classification in seconds) but feeds fewer downstream mechanics than the Knock's terms or the Ledger's records.

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Investigation utility | High at the triage layer: instant family-classification turns "unknown phenomenon" into "unknown phenomenon of a known family." Becomes the filing system (flavor-index) the whole later portfolio uses. Below the Knock in raw decisive power (family ≠ terms). |
| Anti-auto-solve safety | Extremely safe: family-level granularity *cannot reach* what auto-solve needs (identity, Rule, specifics); novel phenomena are opaque until earned; it accelerates investigation's start, never its end. |
| Future-plot connectivity | Strong as infrastructure (the flavor-index keys later detection contracts), strongest as mystery (the tasteless artifact). Weaker than 7/9 in mechanical feed-forward. |
| Chapter-ending satisfaction | The artifact-tastes-of-nothing reveal is a superb single beat; repeatable endings are subtler (a *strange new flavor* as a closing line works, but is quieter than a knocked term or a ledger refusal). |
| Abuse resistance | Strong: proximity + active-only + ward-dampening + compounding per-scene drain. Tasting faction wards yields genera, never specifics — the design refuses the data the abuse needs. |
| Originality vs shadows | The most sensorially original: gustatory taxonomy exists in none of the shadow systems. Solo Leveling detects via system windows (explicit, labeled); Brine gives *sensation without labels* — the taxonomy must be built by the protagonist, which inverts the shadow dynamic. |

**Trade-offs / risks (honest):**
- Passivity risk: tasting is the least *active* of the three finalists' verbs (knocking, asking, tasting). In a progression fantasy, the signature tool should feel like an action; the coordinator should weigh whether "he tastes the air" carries a first-contract reveal scene.
- The flavor taxonomy is authorial labor: every new phenomenon family needs a distinct, memorable, non-silly flavor, sustained across hundreds of chapters. The design is only as good as the menu.
- Its Artifact hook, while the purest, is also the most *static*: "tastes of nothing" is a fact that doesn't evolve until very late. The Knock's "bound" and the Ledger's INSUFFICIENT DEPTH both imply *next questions*; tastelessness implies waiting.

## Why the other seven did not make the top 3

- **(1) The Last Ninety Seconds:** excellent cold-open clue generator, but the pre-manifestation-only window makes it the *narrowest* utility of the ten; its Artifact hook (counting knots) is strong yet thinner than the finalists'. Loses to the Knock on mechanical feed-forward.
- **(2) Silt-Silhouette:** the best *dataset* candidate (feeds future identification contracts), but as Contract #1 it is pure setup with minimal payoff — ten seconds of blurry shadow is a hard first-power sell. Loses to Brine, which does the same triage job faster and more originally.
- **(3) The Unfinished Sentence:** the most *literary* candidate (interruption as data), but its output is the least actionable (half-thoughts, raw feel, hours to live) and its abuse surface (lifting from operatives' gear) needs the most prose-level policing. Loses to the Ledger, which does the anti-fallibility job with harder walls.
- **(5) The Room Remembers:** superb triage instrument, but non-propositional by ontology means it can never *confirm* anything — as a first contract it teaches feeling before fact, which inverts the calibration order the Knock gets right. Its domestic Artifact hook (relief in the childhood home) is outstanding but is a *plot* beat more than a *system* beat.
- **(6) Secondhand Heartbeat:** the most *dramatic* candidate (the compromised courier), but it is designed as a sequel (gated behind the imprint discipline, DEEP WATER solve) — promoting it to Contract #1 would skip the grammar it depends on. Correctly placed as a later contract, not the first.
- **(8) What the Water Took:** the most *cinematic* candidate (the tobacco tin, seven knots, the rotting face-turn), but 3 seconds of object-perspective smear is the lowest signal-to-noise ratio in the set; its Artifact hook, while the most visual, overlaps the Knock's territory (bound hands) without the Knock's mechanical payload.
- **(10) Flotsam Bell:** the best *triage* candidate and the natural seed of all later detection — but binary-and-directionless is deliberately the weakest useful signal, which makes it the weakest *satisfying* first power. Its apartment-rings hook is the best ambient engine in the set and deserves a later slot, where "the bell won't stop" can run as a background thread rather than carrying the premiere.

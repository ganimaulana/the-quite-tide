## Candidate 1 — THE LAST NINETY SECONDS (residual-sound family)

- **Name:** *The Last Ninety Seconds* ("Sisa Sembilan Puluh Detik" — working title; final naming is prose-side)
- **Authority:** Non-sapient acoustic-residue phenomenon: the settling echo a Seep leaves in enclosed air. Grantor class: Reactive residue-field. [PROPOSED]
- **Condition:** At any indoor location where a Seep manifested, Arthur may replay the last 90 seconds of *sound* from **before** the manifestation began — never during, never after. One replay per location, ever (one-tap). Invocation requires him to stand in the room and listen in real time (90 seconds, unskippable).
- **Cost:** Signing: FIRST WATER solve + standard Depth price + 1 toll-mark (a faint high-pitched tone only he hears before rain). Per use: Depth drain per replay; the replay is Lantern-audible at close range (use reads at T1 tier — stealth table).
- **Limitation:** Sound only — no speakers identified, no volume normalization, no translation of non-human acoustics. The 90-second window is fixed and cannot be scrubbed. Cannot replay outdoor Seeps (no enclosed air to hold the residue). Residue older than one lunar cycle is gone.
- **Failure condition:** If the "Seep" was staged (mundane trick), the replay returns room tone — the contract doesn't lie, it just has nothing to settle on. If Arthur misidentifies the manifestation moment, the window lands wrong and the Depth is spent anyway (misreading priced, 29 §B.5 [KEEP]).
- **Why it is weak:** Zero combat application. Ninety seconds of decontextualized audio, heard once, cannot be paused or searched. In a fight it is a 90-second distraction.
- **Why it is useful:** It answers the investigation's cheapest question — *what happened right before?* — without answering *what it means*. A slammed door, a name spoken once, a kettle's whistle stopping mid-note: the classic cold-open clue.
- **How it helps solve future anomalies:** Trains the "before-state" discipline: every later solve starts with establishing the pre-manifestation baseline. The replays become the archive's earliest dated records (feeds Candidate 9's ledger directly).
- **How it connects to the long-term plot:** One replay — at a site the family insists was always quiet — contains, under the room tone, a voice counting knots: *"…five, six, seven."* The Artifact mystery: who counted the Repository's knots before Arthur was born? [PROPOSED hook; does not resolve anything]
- **How it can be abused:** Crime-scene replay as routine procedure — infinite cold-case clearance; replaying faction meetings' rooms for intelligence.
- **How to prevent abuse:** One-tap per location kills farming; the pre-manifestation-only window means it never captures the event itself (leads, not answers); outdoor exclusion + lunar-cycle decay bound the supply; per-use Depth drain + Lantern-audibility price each replay; replaying a *person's* room for their conversations fails the Addressable test if no Seep occurred — the Grantor is the residue-field, not the room, and mundane rooms have no Grantor to settle with.

## Candidate 2 — SILT-SILHOUETTE (silhouette family)

- **Name:** *Silt-Silhouette*
- **Authority:** Non-sapient impression-phenomenon: the shape an entity's passage presses into a room's "still air," like silt holding a footprint. Grantor class: INTEL None trace-field. [PROPOSED]
- **Condition:** In a room an anomaly has passed through, Arthur may call up a single still silhouette — rough size, posture, gait-suggestion — of the last entity to pass. The silhouette hangs for ten seconds, visible only to him. One calling per room, ever.
- **Limitation:** No detail finer than silhouette: no face, no color, no identity, no species certainty. Cannot distinguish between two similar-sized entities. Fails entirely on living persons (minds are not Addressable — §1.1.1 [KEEP 02.I]); attempting it on a person's passage returns nothing and still spends Depth.
- **Cost:** Signing: FIRST WATER + Depth + 1 toll-mark (his own shadow lags half a beat in peripheral vision — noticed only by him). Per use: Depth drain; the calling is Seismograph-quiet but Lantern-visible at short range.
- **Failure condition:** If the "passage" was mundane (a person, an animal), nothing settles — Depth spent, lesson learned. If two entities passed, only the *last* is shown; the earlier is silently omitted (the witness-rule: incomplete testimony presented without disclaimer is still incomplete — Arthur must learn to distrust completeness).
- **Why it is weak:** A ten-second blurry shadow. It cannot fight, restrain, or identify. Against a fast entity it is a souvenir.
- **Why it is useful:** It answers *was something here, and roughly what shape* — the triage question that decides whether a cold room is worth a week of investigation. Size-and-gait narrows the phenomenon family before any solve begins.
- **How it helps solve future anomalies:** Builds the silhouette archive: across arcs, Arthur accumulates a private bestiary of shapes. A later contract (entity-identification class) can be *fed* these silhouettes as its training set — the candidate is literally the dataset future contracts learn from.
- **How it connects to the long-term plot:** One silhouette — in the Reed family storehouse, a room sealed since his childhood — shows a small figure with a corded object wound seven times around one wrist. The Artifact mystery: who carried the Repository before the family myth says anyone did? [PROPOSED hook]
- **How it can be abused:** Tracking specific entities across the city by chaining room-callings; using silhouettes as evidence against Sapient persons.
- **How to prevent abuse:** One-tap per room + last-only + ten-second duration make chaining impractical (Depth cost per room, no persistence); living persons excluded by ontology (attempts fail loudly in the Depth ledger — after-the-fact visibility means Arthur *sees* the wasted spend, which disciplines him); silhouettes are inadmissible as identification (no finer detail — the design refuses the data the abuse needs).

## Candidate 3 — THE UNFINISHED SENTENCE (memory-fragment family)

- **Name:** *The Unfinished Sentence*
- **Authority:** Non-sapient handling-residue: the last uncompleted thought-pattern pressed into an object handled during a Seep. Grantor class: Reactive object-residue. Records, not knowledge (29 §B.5 [KEEP]). [PROPOSED]
- **Condition:** Arthur may lift from a handled object the final *unfinished* impression left during Seep exposure — a fragment of intent, never a complete thought: *"the key is under the—"*, *"don't let them see the—"*. One lifting per object, ever. The impression arrives as raw feel, not words — Arthur's interpretation is his own risk.
- **Cost:** Signing: FIRST WATER + Depth + 1 toll-mark (occasional déjà vu of other people's half-thoughts when touching old objects). Per use: Depth drain; lifted impressions **decay within hours** (rot boundary — Q159 option 1, Q442 option 1): transcribe immediately or lose it.
- **Limitation:** Unfinished only — completed thoughts leave no residue (the mind filed them; only the interrupted persists). Cannot lift from objects handled outside Seep exposure. Cannot lift the dead's thoughts (echoes un-addressable — §1.1.2); cannot lift a living person's current thoughts (minds un-addressable — §1.1.1).
- **Failure condition:** If the handler's last impression was mundane ("*need milk—*"), that's what he gets. If Arthur misreads the raw feel, the error compounds into every downstream deduction — the design *wants* this failure mode (Q160: wrong fragments are the price of the records-not-knowledge rule).
- **Why it is weak:** Half-thoughts, hours to live, often banal. No combat use whatsoever.
- **Why it is useful:** Victims and witnesses of Seeps are Veil-edited; their objects are not. The unfinished sentence is the only testimony that survives the Veil Reflex — *what were they reaching for when it happened?*
- **How it helps solve future anomalies:** Establishes the object-witness methodology that later perception contracts (Candidate 8's family) formalize. Teaches Arthur that *interruption* is data — what the phenomenon stopped is as diagnostic as what it did.
- **How it connects to the long-term plot:** Lifted from his grandmother's sewing box (handled nightly, one night during a minor Seep decades ago): *"the eighth knot is—"*. There are seven knots. The Artifact mystery: what is the eighth knot, and who interrupted her? [PROPOSED hook]
- **How it can be abused:** Lifting from faction operatives' gear for intelligence; building a interrogation-by-object pipeline.
- **How to prevent abuse:** Seep-exposure requirement bounds the supply (no Seep, no residue — and staging Seeps to farm impressions is Oslo-risk behavior); hours-decay prevents stockpiling; unfinished-only + raw-feel (uninterpreted) means the intel is never actionable without Arthur's own corroborating work; lifting from a *person's* held object for their secrets fails unless a Seep interrupted them — the ontology, not Arthur's restraint, is the wall.

## Candidate 4 — BRINE ON THE TONGUE (sensory-echo family)

- **Name:** *Brine on the Tongue*
- **Authority:** Non-sapient Rule-residue phenomenon: every stable Rule-family leaves a distinct taste/scent signature on the air around its active phenomena — the Undertow's grammar rendered as flavor. Grantor class: Reactive ambient-field. [PROPOSED]
- **Condition:** Within arm's reach of an *active* phenomenon, Arthur can taste its Rule-family: Tide-phenomena taste of brine and copper; Hollow-adjacent of cold ash; Choir-adjacent of honeyed rot; and so on (a private taxonomy he must build himself — the contract gives sensation, never labels). Passive once signed; each tasting costs a sip of Depth.
- **Cost:** Signing: FIRST WATER + Depth + 1 toll-mark (his saliva tastes faintly of brine for an hour after each signing-grade event — a tell he must hide). Per use: small Depth drain per tasting; continuous tasting across a scene compounds the drain (anti-loitering math).
- **Limitation:** **Family-level only, never identity, never the Rule itself** (leads, not answers). A novel phenomenon tastes *strange* without mapping — the taxonomy must be earned case by case. Requires proximity to an active phenomenon; dormant sites are tasteless. Wards dampen the flavor exactly as they dampen Lantern senses (Q444 option 1).
- **Failure condition:** Two related-but-distinct families can taste maddeningly similar (brine vs. brackish) — misclassification is always possible, and the design refuses to disambiguate (the taxonomy is *his* instrument; instruments need calibration, and his early calibration is wrong in instructive ways).
- **Why it is weak:** Tasting things has no combat value. It is, literally, standing near danger and licking the air.
- **Why it is useful:** Instant family-classification of the unknown — the first cut every investigation needs (*is this the same kind of thing as the harbor case?*). It turns "unknown phenomenon" into "unknown phenomenon of a known family" in seconds, which is where every solve begins.
- **How it helps solve future anomalies:** The flavor archive becomes the index key for all later contracts: when a future detection contract (phenomenon-specific, Q443) needs its target class, Arthur *names the flavor*. This candidate is the filing system the whole portfolio later uses.
- **How it connects to the long-term plot:** The family artifact — the Repository itself — **tastes of nothing**. Every phenomenon has a flavor; the one object at the center of his life has none. The Artifact mystery in its purest form: *what has no Rule-taste?* Is it flavorless because it is empty — or because its flavor is the baseline everything else is measured against? [PROPOSED hook; unresolvable at this stage by design]
- **How it can be abused:** Instant classification short-circuits investigation; tasting faction facilities' wards to map their defenses.
- **How to prevent abuse:** Family-level granularity means the abuse *can't reach* what it wants (defenses need specifics; the tongue gives genera); novel-phenomena opacity forces earning; proximity requirement + active-only + ward-dampening keep it a field instrument, not a remote sensor; compounding per-scene drain punishes loitering-tasting. It accelerates the *start* of investigation, never the *end*.

## Candidate 5 — THE ROOM REMEMBERS (location-imprint / emotional-residue family)

- **Name:** *The Room Remembers* ("Hearth-Imprint")
- **Authority:** Non-sapient emotional-residue field: the dominant *feeling* present during a location's last Seep, held in the site like heat in stone. Grantor class: INTEL None site-field. [PROPOSED]
- **Condition:** Once per location, ever, Arthur may ask a Seep site for its strongest emotional residue — **the feeling only, never the facts**: dread, grief, relief, amusement, hunger. The residue arrives as a wave he physically experiences for up to a minute. He may not ask *whose* feeling or *why*.
- **Cost:** Signing: FIRST WATER + Depth + 1 toll-mark (he sometimes wakes with a stranger's grief already fading — residue-bleed on nights after heavy use). Per use: Depth drain **plus** the minute of experienced emotion (physiological price — the old 29 §B.6 currencies echoed, not copied).
- **Limitation:** Non-propositional by ontology: emotions are not statements (the WITNESS RULE). One reading per location ever (one-tap). Cannot be directed ("show me fear" is refused — the site offers its strongest, or nothing). Residue fades with site remediation (a cleansed site goes quiet).
- **Failure condition:** The strongest feeling may belong to a bystander, not the phenomenon (a janitor's relief at shift-end, layered over a T2's dread) — the design *wants* this misattribution risk; triage by feeling is fallible triage.
- **Why it is weak:** Feelings aren't evidence, can't be entered anywhere, can't be shown to anyone. In combat it is a minute of someone else's dread.
- **Why it is useful:** Triage at scale: which of twelve cold sites *matters*? Grief where the file says "minor disturbance" is a flag no paperwork raises. It tells him where to spend his weeks (investigation is priced by the world — Q133 — so triage is survival).
- **How it helps solve future anomalies:** Calibrates his emotional literacy as an instrument: later emotional-residue contracts (carrying, comparing, layering feelings) all build on the discipline learned here — *read the feeling, distrust the feeling, corroborate anyway*.
- **How it connects to the long-term plot:** His childhood home — where the family record says nothing ever happened — reads **relief**. Profound, specific, unmistakable relief, on a night no Seep was ever filed. The Artifact mystery goes domestic: *what was his family relieved about, and why is there no file?* [PROPOSED hook]
- **How it can be abused:** Emotion-reading as lie detection ("ask the interrogation room how the suspect felt"); screening locations for faction activity by feeling.
- **How to prevent abuse:** Non-propositional ontology (feelings aren't statements — the design refuses the data lie-detection needs); one-tap per location; bystander-attribution noise; site remediation erases (factions that clean their sites are immune — competence is distributed, 02.IV); the experienced-emotion price makes casual screening personally expensive.

## Candidate 6 — SECONDHAND HEARTBEAT (emotional-residue carrying / evidence-transport family)

- **Name:** *Secondhand Heartbeat*
- **Authority:** Non-sapient carrier-phenomenon: Arthur's own pattern as a temporary vessel for a Tide-mark-grade impression, settled from a site-field Grantor. [PROPOSED]
- **Condition:** After experiencing a site's emotional residue (Candidate 5's family — designed as its natural sequel, signable only if the imprint discipline is demonstrated), Arthur may *carry* one impression — a feeling plus its sensory fragments — for up to one hour, during which a Lantern-sensitive can read *him* as the carrier. He becomes the evidence transport past static rot.
- **Cost:** Signing: requires DEEP WATER solve (this is the one candidate gated above FIRST WATER — the price of its power) + Depth + 1 toll-mark (his resting heartbeat occasionally syncs to a rhythm that isn't his). Per use: Depth drain **plus accelerated decay** — carrying *spends* the impression (it fades in the hour whether read or not) — plus he experiences the carried emotion continuously while carrying it.
- **Limitation:** **One impression at a time.** Cannot carry facts — only the feeling and its sensory fragments (impressions, not video — Q419/Q442 option 1). The carrier cannot edit, pause, or replay; the Lantern reads what the hour gives. He cannot carry his *own* memories (minds un-addressable).
- **Failure condition:** If no Lantern is available within the hour, the impression dies unread and the Depth is gone — logistics as a failure mode (the design prices infrastructure, not just power). If the carried feeling overwhelms him (grief, terror), his judgment degrades for the rest of the scene — the courier pays in composure.
- **Why it is weak:** He is a courier, not a combatant. For one hour he is *weaker* — distracted, emotionally compromised, carrying something every faction would kill to intercept, with no way to defend it.
- **Why it is useful:** Static rot (02.III [KEEP]) kills Seep evidence; the Veil kills witnesses' memories. The carrier bridges the gap: a feeling that would rot on sensors and fade from minds can be walked, physically, to a Lantern who can read it. It makes Arthur *infrastructure* — the thing factions need but can't replicate.
- **How it helps solve future anomalies:** Foundational for the evidence-chain methodology: later contracts formalize chain-of-custody for impressions (who carried what, when, at what decay). It also teaches the series' hardest lesson early — *evidence has a half-life, and so does he*.
- **How it connects to the long-term plot:** Carrying one harbor-site impression, mid-transport, he hears — inside the feeling, where no words should be — his own name, spoken with *relief*. The Artifact mystery turns personal: something in the Undertow knows his name and is *glad*. He cannot carry words; he carried one anyway. The impossibility is the hook. [PROPOSED hook]
- **How it can be abused:** Courier-for-hire for factions (evidence smuggling); carrying impressions *out* of warded sites (exfiltration).
- **How to prevent abuse:** One-at-a-time + one-hour + accelerated decay make bulk smuggling uneconomical; wards dampen the settling (Q444 — impressions don't settle cleanly inside wards, so exfiltration from hardened sites fails at the source); the carrier is Lantern-readable while carrying (he can't smuggle secretly — the cargo is *legible on him*); the emotional price makes him a reluctant courier, which is a character-level brake no rule needs to enforce.

## Candidate 7 — THE POLITE KNOCK (rule-fragment family)

- **Name:** *The Polite Knock*
- **Authority:** Non-sapient Rule-fragment phenomenon: the *stated condition* of an anomaly's Rule, as the Rule itself "announces" it — phenomena with stable Rules broadcast their terms the way a door announces it is locked (by resisting). Grantor class: Reactive Rule-field. [PROPOSED]
- **Condition:** For a phenomenon Arthur has **personally observed in active manifestation**, he may knock — and the Rule answers with **one condition it has already demonstrated in his presence**. Only demonstrated conditions; never unobserved ones; one knock per phenomenon, ever. The answer arrives as a felt certainty: *"it cannot enter where the bell has rung"* — and he knows, the way he knows a door is locked, that this was shown, not told.
- **Cost:** Signing: FIRST WATER + Depth + 1 toll-mark (his knuckles ache before it rains — the knock remembered in his hands). Per use: Depth drain scaled to the phenomenon's tier (knocking on a T3 costs real Depth — the design prices curiosity by danger).
- **Limitation:** **Demonstrated-only** (the hard wall): the knock cannot reveal what he hasn't seen. One knock per phenomenon ever (one-tap). The condition arrives without context — he learns the *term*, not the *reason*. It cannot knock on living persons, Sapient Grantors' undisclosed terms (Sapients negotiate; they are not knocked on), or phenomena he hasn't personally observed.
- **Failure condition:** If he misobserved the demonstration (thought the bell mattered, but it was the *silence after* the bell), the knock confirms his misreading with full felt certainty — **the most dangerous failure mode in the set**: true-feeling false confirmation. The design owns this (Q146 option 2's snap logic at micro-scale).
- **Why it is weak:** It tells him what he half-saw. It grants no force, no protection, no action — only a sentence of terms. In combat, knowing the condition and *exploiting* it are different skills, and the knock grants only the first.
- **Why it is useful:** It is the **exception-clause finder** — and the exception clause is the key to Rule interference (04 §4.7 [KEEP]: the weak defeat the strong by attacking conditions). Every survival scene Arthur will ever have runs on knowing the exact terms. The knock converts a week of observation into a confirmed sentence — not the solve, but the *shape of the solve*.
- **How it helps solve future anomalies:** Foundational for the interference methodology: all later interference work (his signature survival strategy, per 04 §4.7) cites knocked conditions as precedent. It also feeds the archive (Candidate 9): knocked terms are the most reliable records he owns — *demonstrated* terms, not inferred ones.
- **How it connects to the long-term plot:** Knocked on a minor harbor phenomenon — a bell-buoy Seep, barely T1 — the answered condition comes back in vocabulary that doesn't belong to buoys: *"it cannot cross what is bound."* Bound. The Repository binds. The Artifact mystery: why does a harbor buoy's Rule speak the artifact's language? [PROPOSED hook]
- **How it can be abused:** Rule-extraction as a service (knock for hire); knocking on faction-held phenomena for their containment terms.
- **How to prevent abuse:** Demonstrated-only + personally-observed + one-tap make extraction require Arthur's own fieldwork per phenomenon (he can't knock on what he hasn't stood in front of — presence-only, Q422 option 1); Depth scaling by tier prices curiosity about dangerous things; Sapient exclusion keeps it from becoming interrogation; the felt-certainty failure mode (confirming misreadings) means the knock is *less* trustworthy the less careful he was — the abuse vector degrades its own product.

## Candidate 8 — WHAT THE WATER TOOK (last-perception / object-witness family)

- **Name:** *What the Water Took*
- **Authority:** Non-sapient object-impression phenomenon: the last ≤3 seconds *perceived* by an object present at a Seep, held as a Tide-mark-grade impression. Grantor class: INTEL None object-field. [PROPOSED]
- **Condition:** Arthur may ask an object that was present at a Seep for its last perception — **≤3 seconds, visual only, impressions not video** (Q419 option 1 [ADAPT]): a smear of motion, a shape, a light-change — the object's "sight," which is nothing like human sight. One asking per object, ever. The object must have been present; he must hold it.
- **Cost:** Signing: FIRST WATER + Depth + 1 toll-mark (his peripheral vision occasionally strobes at 3-second intervals — the object's frame rate ghosting his). Per use: Depth drain; the impression follows the rot boundary (T4/≤3h-equivalent — Q442 option 1): impressions from high-pressure Seeps degrade *as he watches*.
- **Limitation:** 3 seconds, object-perspective, no sound, no identity resolution, no replay. The object "perceived" only what its material registered (a knife saw the swing; a window saw the street). Cannot ask objects about events without a Seep (no Grantor to settle with). Cannot ask about the past beyond the Seep moment (the past is view-only and impression-capped — 02.I [KEEP]).
- **Failure condition:** Object-perspective is alien: a coin on a table "saw" the ceiling. Misreading the perspective is the standard failure — and the design prices it (Depth spent on a ceiling). High-pressure impressions rot mid-viewing, which can look like a lie but is physics.
- **Why it is weak:** Three seconds of blurry, possibly ceiling-facing, possibly rotting visual smear. No combat use; barely a clue on its own.
- **Why it is useful:** The murder weapon's last sight. The dropped phone's last three seconds. Objects don't get Veil-edited (the Veil edits *memory*, not physics — 03 §3.1 [KEEP]) — the object's testimony is the one witness the masquerade can't touch.
- **How it helps solve future anomalies:** Formalizes the object-witness methodology: later perception contracts (longer windows, other senses, comparison across objects) all generalize this grammar. It teaches *perspective discipline* — every witness, even a knife, has a standpoint.
- **How it connects to the long-term plot:** Asked of a Reed family object — his grandfather's tobacco tin, present at *something* in the winter of the year the family stopped talking about the harbor — the three seconds show hands tying knots in a cord. Seven knots. The hands are old, and almost familiar, and the impression rots before the face turns. The Artifact mystery, shown, not told: *who bound the Repository, and why did the family stop talking about it?* [PROPOSED hook]
- **How it can be abused:** Omni-witness via object networks (ask every object at a scene); using objects as surveillance (plant objects, trigger Seeps, ask later).
- **How to prevent abuse:** 3-second cap + one-tap per object + Seep-presence requirement make networks impractical (Depth per object, no persistence); planted-object surveillance requires *staging Seeps* — Oslo-risk behavior the world punishes; rot boundary degrades high-value impressions; object-perspective alienness means the intel needs Arthur's interpretation labor per object — the bottleneck is *him*, which is the correct bottleneck.

## Candidate 9 — THE LEDGER OF SMALL DEBTS (truth-fragment / archive family)

- **Name:** *The Ledger of Small Debts*
- **Authority:** The Repository itself, in its notary function — **the only candidate whose Grantor is the medium, not a phenomenon**. Authority: the artifact's own record-keeping. This is the meta-contract: it doesn't investigate the world; it audits Arthur's investigations. [PROPOSED]
- **Condition:** **Once per day**, Arthur may ask the Repository one falsifiable question **answerable solely from its own stored records** — his solves, his knocked terms, his carried impressions, his predictions and their outcomes. The Repository answers TRUE / FALSE / INSUFFICIENT RECORD. It never answers about the unsolved, the future, or the world beyond his archive. The question and answer are themselves recorded (the audit trail audits itself).
- **Cost:** Signing: FIRST WATER + Depth + 1 toll-mark (he dreams in ledger columns on nights after asking — the archive settling). Per use: Depth drain per question (the oracle has a meter — Q181 option 2); the daily cap is hard (a second question is refused, politely, by silence).
- **Limitation:** **Archive-bounded absolutely**: no record, no answer. It cannot verify what he hasn't solved, cannot predict, cannot identify unknowns, cannot answer about other people's holdings. INSUFFICIENT RECORD is the most common answer in year one — the design *wants* the early ledger to feel small.
- **Failure condition:** The ledger answers from records — and records include his *misreadings* (29 §B.5 [KEEP]): ask "was my harbor solve correct?" and it answers TRUE *if the recorded prediction held*, even if the understanding underneath was wrong (Q146's false-premise logic — the ledger certifies the test, not the truth). The anti-misreading tool can launder misreadings that passed their tests. The design owns this openly: the ledger is a mirror, and mirrors don't correct posture.
- **Why it is weak:** It knows only what he knows, minus what he's forgotten, filtered through what he got wrong. In combat it is a voice saying INSUFFICIENT RECORD.
- **Why it is useful:** It is the **only defense against his own fallibility**: the archive cross-checks his deductions against his *recorded* evidence, catching the contradictions his tired brain misses at 3 AM. For a protagonist whose power is understanding, an auditable memory of what he actually established is the difference between a detective and a fabulist.
- **How it helps solve future anomalies:** It *is* the archive the endgame shape requires (Q187's ARCHIVE): every later contract's records flow into it; every later verification cites it. It compounds — the most "boring" candidate is the one whose value grows monotonically with the series.
- **How it connects to the long-term plot:** Ask it about the Repository itself — *"is the artifact's Rule stored in this ledger?"* — and for the first time it does not answer TRUE, FALSE, or INSUFFICIENT RECORD. It answers: **INSUFFICIENT DEPTH — THE TERMS OF THIS QUESTION EXCEED THE SOLVE.** The ledger has a floor he hasn't reached. The Artifact mystery as a chapter-ending sentence: the one thing his perfect archive cannot answer is the archive itself. [PROPOSED hook]
- **How it can be abused:** Daily oracle for faction intelligence ("is the Red Ledger's offer truthful?"); laundering deductions as certainty.
- **How to prevent abuse:** Archive-boundedness is the wall — it cannot answer about anything he hasn't solved, which excludes faction intentions, the future, and other people (Q439 option 1: phenomena know their own Rule, not faction plans — and the ledger knows only his records); daily cap + per-question Depth price the oracle; the misreading-laundering failure mode is disclosed, not hidden — the reader watches him learn not to trust TRUE the way he wants to.

## Candidate 10 — FLOTSAM BELL (anomaly-trace / triage family)

- **Name:** *Flotsam Bell*
- **Authority:** Non-sapient threshold-phenomenon: the Repository's own sensitivity to Addressable-class Rules settling nearby — the medium humming when something *solvable* is near. Grantor: the artifact's notary function (like Candidate 9, the Grantor is the medium). [PROPOSED]
- **Condition:** Whenever Arthur enters a location where an **Addressable-class** phenomenon has settled within the last **72 hours**, a small bell-tone sounds — audible only to him, directionless, binary: *present / absent*. No identity, no direction, no distance. It rings once per site per entry.
- **Cost:** Signing: FIRST WATER + Depth + 1 toll-mark (a permanent faint tinnitus at the bell's pitch — he will never hear silence again; the design prices this one in *quiet*). Per use: passive; each ring costs a sip of Depth (anti-loitering: pacing a site to farm rings drains him).
- **Limitation:** **Binary and directionless** — the weakest useful signal. 72-hour window only. Addressable-class only (not all anomalies — a T4 horror with no stable Rule doesn't ring; the bell is *not* a danger sense, and the design is explicit that treating it as one gets him killed). Wards dampen it (Q444 option 1); the Seismograph-quiet formation rule means nobody else hears it.
- **Failure condition:** The bell rings for the Addressable — which includes phenomena that are *settled and harmless* (a benign tide-clock Seep rings the same as a predator's lure). False-positive triage is the standard failure: he spends a week on a ringing site that was never a case. The design wants this — triage instruments produce *leads*, and leads include dead ends.
- **Why it is weak:** A directionless ding. It cannot find anything, identify anything, or protect him. As a "detection contract" it is deliberately the worst one the system permits — which is why it's safe to grant first.
- **Why it is useful:** It answers the *zeroth* question: *is there anything here worth my weeks?* Investigation is priced by the world (Q133) — his scarcest resource is attention. The bell rations it. Cold cases sort themselves into *warm* and *cold* as he walks past.
- **How it helps solve future anomalies:** It is the seed of all later detection work: future phenomenon-specific detection contracts (Q443 option 1) are *narrowings* of the bell — the bell says "something Addressable"; the later contracts say "something Addressable *of this family*, *that way*." The progression from binary to directional is the visible growth of his instrument set.
- **How it connects to the long-term plot:** The bell rings **in his own apartment**. Something Addressable settled where he lives, within 72 hours, and he cannot find it — it's directionless, and his home is small, and the ringing won't stop. The Artifact mystery goes ambient: is it the Repository itself settling? Is something *visiting* the artifact? The safest place he owns becomes the longest-running question. [PROPOSED hook]
- **How it can be abused:** Grid-searching the city for phenomena (systematic sweeping); using the bell as a danger sense; selling "is this place haunted?" as a service.
- **How to prevent abuse:** Binary + directionless + 72h window make grid-searching Depth-expensive and information-poor (each ring says *something was here*, nothing more); Addressable-only means the most dangerous things don't ring — danger-sense use is a documented way to die; wards dampen (prepared sites are silent); the tinnitus toll-mark is a permanent, personal, non-transferable price that makes the "service" un-sellable (he can't lend his ears).

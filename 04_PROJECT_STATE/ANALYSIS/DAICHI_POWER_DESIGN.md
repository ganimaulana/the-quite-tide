## 0. The governing constraints (read first)

1. **No output.** `[PROPOSED]` `[REWRITE]` (rewrites 29's "almost never new capability" restraint into the contract-era ceiling): Arthur's contracts never grant raw destructive output, direct damage, passive defense, regeneration, stat enhancement, or ability immunity. His body is trained-human; his power is positional. This is the line the stress test's refusal list (Q427) and the combat ceiling (Q204) both point to, stated once here as the design's load-bearing wall.
2. **Intelligence is demonstrated, not declared.** `[RECOMMENDATION]` Every "he's extremely intelligent" beat must be a visible chain — observation → alternatives → falsifiable test → correction — and he must sometimes be wrong (Q238 solution 1; Q203's governor). A Arthur who is never wrong is a different genre's protagonist.
3. **The absence must stay meaningful.** `[RECOMMENDATION]` At every milestone, name what still kills him: bullets, ambushes, poison, ranked abilities used competently, exposure. "A bullet still works" (29.B.12 `[CANON]`) is the sentence the whole design hangs from — if a contract ever stops a bullet by *being armor*, the design has failed.
4. **Every contract is priced and visible.** `[RECOMMENDATION]` Activation tolls, Recognition/Attention accumulation, slot interference — the costs from the stress test apply to Arthur first and hardest. His advantages compound; so do his debts.

## 1. Zero contracts — the baseline

**What he is.** `[PROPOSED]` `[REWRITE]` (rewrites 29 Part A's "physically untrained" body): Extremely intelligent (with the verification protocol: he tests his own reads, keeps a falsification ledger, and is wrong on-page), physically capable at the **trained-human ceiling** — coordination, evasion, endurance, basic defensive skill (Q230 solution 1's shape: the audit's recommended compromise between the old "physically untrained" and the brief's "physically capable"). He is Loud (02.VII `[CANON]` — the one old-canon trait that ports cleanly and explains why instruments misread him). He keeps the clerk's eye: perfect recall of *what he has actually seen* — not photographic super-memory (Q236's bound: the audit warns against upgrading recall into a power).

**What he can do with zero contracts:** investigate, document, prepare, evade, endure, spend *other people's* strength (call the Sweepers, file the report, aim the faction at the problem). His zero-contract victories are all mechanism 5 and 7 victories (information advantage, social/legal constraints — 30.3 `[CANON]`).

**What still kills him:** everything. `[CANON]` 29.B.12: "a bullet still works." No contract has changed this yet, and the design must keep a visible ledger of his exposures — the moments he survives by luck or by others' intervention, so the reader never mistakes "competent" for "safe."

**What this milestone must establish** `[RECOMMENDATION]`: the method (observe → hypothesize → test small → pay), the Loudness (why the system misfiles him), and the first entry in the exposure ledger. The reader must believe he *needs* the artifact — not for power, but for reach.

## 2. Contract #1 — the design brief

`[INFERENCE]` Q003, Q196, and Q377 converge on the spec; the audit's ten-candidate exercise is a separate mechanism, so this file states the **brief**, not the pick:

- **Weak in combat, vital for investigation** (Q003 `[PROPOSED]`): the contract must be useless in a fair fight and indispensable in an unfair investigation.
- **Must not auto-solve** (Q377): it supplies *residue*, never answers — the Use-10 pattern from 29.B.11 `[CANON]` is the template: "impressions, not video; fallible; depth-capped; the question of *who set it* is never answered by the impression."
- **Must feed later contracts** (Q196): its output (traces, contradictions, Rule-failures) is the *input* to future solves — it is an instrument, not an arsenal.
- **Narrow, priced, visible** (Q377 solution 1): a contradiction/residual-trace effect with a named boundary — e.g., it shows where an observed Rule *failed*, never the cause, never the culprit, never the complete answer; every use accrues Attention (the old B.6.3 cost, generalized).

**Example shapes (illustrative, `[PROPOSED]`, not canon):** a Tide-mark residue reader (fragment-grade impressions of recent phenomena, domain-bounded to the site); a Rule-failure highlighter (marks where the local Rule broke, not why); a contradiction ledger (records mutually exclusive observations for later falsification). Any of these obeys the brief; the brief is the canon, not the example.

**What Contract #1 must not do** `[RECOMMENDATION]`: grant detection-at-range, identify persons, predict, or defend. The first contract is a *magnifying glass*, and the story must show him almost dying with it in his pocket — otherwise the "weak" premise is theater.

## 3. Contract #2 — the escalation

> **[IX-AD — AUTHOR DECISION 2026-09-23: NON-COMMITTAL ROADMAP / NOT CANON]** Everything in this section is a **planning hypothesis only** — not author-locked, not current canon. Contract #2 remains completely **UNDESIGNED / UNDECIDED** per AD-12-1 [AUTHOR-LOCKED]; no Contract #2 identity, ability, condition, timing, outcome, or slot requirement is committed by anything here. The example shapes below are illustrative design deliberation, not committed design. Do not read this section as a Contract #2 design.

**Design role.** `[INFERENCE]` Contract #2 must *complement* #1, not upgrade it — the audit's progression logic (Q360–Q364) demands that growth be lateral before it is vertical. If #1 is the magnifying glass, #2 is the notebook: a bounded threshold/positioning utility (Q377's "second contract" shape) — e.g., a ward-noise sense (know when you're being sensed — Q443's detection-announces-itself, turned inward), or a recognition ledger (track which phenomena have "noticed" him — the Attention cost made legible).

**What it must not be** `[RECOMMENDATION]`: a damage upgrade, a defense, or a second detection source that stacks with #1 into omniscience. The two contracts together should make him *harder to fool*, not *harder to kill*.

## 4. First true combat contract

**Design role.** `[INFERENCE]` The ceiling-defining moment (Q204): the first contract usable *in* a fight sets the reader's expectation for every fight after. The audit's recommended restraint (Q204 solution 1) is adopted here as `[RECOMMENDED]`: **information, access, and conditional triggers only** — never direct damage, never defense, never stat-boosts.

**The shape.** `[PROPOSED]` A brief Rule-interference opportunity, *conditional on Arthur correctly identifying a condition*: e.g., a contract that can *hold* a single ward-anchor for seconds, or *borrow* Tide-noise to break an opponent's targeting condition — but only after he has named the condition correctly, on-page, under fire. The contract does not hit; it *opens a window* that his preparation, allies, or the law must exploit (mechanisms 1, 2, and 7 — 30.3 `[CANON]`).

**What it must cost** `[RECOMMENDATION]`: the activation toll at its highest so far, plus a visible Attention spike — the reader must feel that using it *in combat* is spending something he can't afford twice.

## 5. First defensive contract

**Design role.** `[INFERENCE]` Evasion-shaped, never armor (the §0 wall). The audit's defense discussion (Q204/Q405-adjacent) supports: early warning, misdirection, escape.

**The shape.** `[PROPOSED]` A narrow, conditional interruption/mitigation — e.g., a half-second of "the phenomenon looks away" (attention-deflection, priced per use), or a pre-paid escape condition (a threshold he set yesterday, triggered today — preparation made contract). It buys *one* beat. It never tanks a hit.

**What it must not do** `[RECOMMENDATION]`: become reliable. A defensive contract the reader trusts is armor with extra steps; the design must show it *failing* or *failing to trigger* at least once, so the exposure ledger stays honest.

---

## 6. First information contract

**Design role.** `[INFERENCE]` The most dangerous milestone in this file — information is the scarce resource (02.IV `[CANON]`), and an unpriced information contract ends the mystery architecture. The guardrails are Q181's: domain-bounded, records-not-answers.

**The shape.** `[PROPOSED]` A contract that returns *records* within the source phenomenon's own domain — the entity knows only its own nature (Q181 solution 1) — and the records are fallible, fragment-grade, and per-use priced. It answers "what happened here *in the phenomenon's terms*," never "who did it" or "what do I do."

**What it must not do** `[RECOMMENDATION]`: touch the protected unknowns (the L5 layer, Q036/Q483), identify persons at range, or predict. The first information contract should *deepen* a mystery at least once — returning a record that contradicts his working theory — so the reader learns that information-contracts are instruments, not oracles.

## 7. First mobility contract

**Design role.** `[INFERENCE]` Movement without breaking the anchor doctrine (02.V `[CANON]`: slipping needs living-memory anchors; wards remain decisive — Q421).

**The shape.** `[PROPOSED]` Movement along a **personally traversed, recognized route** — the anchor rule imported into the contract system (Q421 solution 2): he can only go where he has *been*, on foot, with attention. Walls and wards remain decisive; the contract shortens the *walk*, it doesn't grant flight or teleportation.

**What it must not do** `[RECOMMENDATION]`: bypass wards, cross the Membrane, or reach the Undertow (Q421/Q424 — the refusal list). The first mobility use should be *evasion-shaped* (leaving a scene he couldn't survive), not *assault-shaped* (arriving where he couldn't be stopped).

## 8. First major breakthrough

**Design role.** `[INFERENCE]` The brief promises "GREATER BREAKTHROUGH" as a mechanic (premise 12); the audit warns it must be *defined* or it becomes a level-up with better marketing (Q435, Q472, Q482).

**The shape.** `[PROPOSED]` A breakthrough is a **gauge-threshold event with a named, irreversible price**: e.g., the second slot unlocks — but the unlock *is* the Attention event (the old B.6.3 "attention flows both ways," generalized): something on the other side of the medium now has his shape on file. Or: the first *permanent* contract — but permanence means the phenomenon's Rule now includes *him* (Q175's cap logic: permanents are counted against the hard slot maximum, and the slot is gone until release).

**What it must not be** `[RECOMMENDATION]`: a stat increase, a power multiplier, or a free slot. The breakthrough's price must exceed its gift — that asymmetry is what keeps "outside classification" from becoming S+.

## 9. First defeat of a higher-ranked opponent

**Design role.** `[INFERENCE]` The undervalue→reversal engine's proof-of-concept (Q382–Q386). The audit is strict here: the victory must be auditable against 30.3's mechanisms, and the old "documentation-shaped victory" (Q398) is the template.

**The shape.** `[PROPOSED]` Preparation + Contract #1 + Rule interference + terrain/legal response — *not* overpowering. He wins by making the opponent's win too expensive (mechanism 6), or by moving the fight into a jurisdiction where the opponent's rank is a leash (mechanism 7), or by breaking one condition the opponent's ability needs (mechanism 1). The victory's *form* is documentation, exposure, or withdrawal — the opponent leaves, stands down, or is arrested; they are not *beaten down*.

**What it must cost** `[RECOMMENDATION]`: the exposure ledger's biggest entry so far — the victory must *reveal* him (to a faction, to the Seismograph, to the counterparty), because secret victories are free victories, and free victories are the genre the brief forbids.

## 10. First genuine loss

**Design role.** `[INFERENCE]` Q203's governor demands it: the story must let him lose, or the intelligence is decorative. The loss must cost something *real* — not a setback he narrates away.

**The shape.** `[PROPOSED]` His understanding is **correct but insufficient**: he names the Rule, predicts the behavior, executes the plan — and the objective still fails, or someone still gets hurt. The loss is designed to teach the brief's own limit: *understanding is not control.* Candidates: a solved phenomenon whose solution required a price he wouldn't pay (and someone else paid it); a contract that worked exactly as designed and still wasn't enough output (the §0 wall, demonstrated); an Attention debt called in at the worst moment.

**What it must establish** `[RECOMMENDATION]`: that the system's prices are real. If the first loss is softened, every later stake is softened with it.

---

## 11. First failed Contract

**Design role.** `[INFERENCE]` The failure-pricing milestone (Q140–Q150; stress test §9): failure must be priced, never profitable.

**The shape.** `[PROPOSED]` He solves the *symptom* rather than the Rule — the contract signs, then fails certification on first real use. The price: the slot locks until the debt is worked off; the gauge scars (that phenomenon's family now "knows" him — re-solving is barred or penalized, Q472's release-cost logic); and the counterparty *remembers* (reputation cost — the failed entity, or its kin, refuses him later).

**What it must teach** `[RECOMMENDATION]`: the difference between *solving* and *signing*. The failure should be *his* error — a rushed read, a skipped falsification step (29.B.11's generative principle `[CANON]`: "a Use 13+ that skips a step is a continuity error, not a power-up") — so the lesson is methodological, not moral.

## 12. First Contract that scares him

**Design role.** `[INFERENCE]` The Attention cost made personal — the old 29.B.6.3 horror ("attention flows both ways"), ported to the contract era. This is the milestone where the *price* stops being abstract.

**The shape.** `[PROPOSED]` A contract whose use reveals **reciprocal observation**: when he looks through it, something indexes *him* — a residue-reader that leaves his own residue readable; a recognition ledger that shows his name already entered, in a handwriting that isn't his; an entity-counterparty that greets him by a name he hasn't given it. The scare is not the effect — it's the *implication*: the medium keeps books, and he is in them.

**What it must change** `[RECOMMENDATION]`: his risk calculus, visibly. After this contract, he hesitates before uses he once spent freely — and the story must show the hesitation *costing* him something, or the scare is cosmetic.

## 13. The Contract that changes his worldview

**Design role.** `[INFERENCE]` The thematic milestone — the contract-era equivalent of 29.B.8's "not chosen, present." It must rewire what he believes understanding *is*.

**The shape.** `[PROPOSED]` A contract whose solve requires recognizing a **sapient anomaly's autonomy** — proving that understanding is not ownership (Q460/Q461's ethic, made mechanical): e.g., an echo-contract (Q408) where the "solution" is realizing the residue is *someone*, and signing would spend them — so the correct move is *not to sign*, and the gauge honors the refusal (the anti-farming ethic: restraint as progression). Or: a phenomenon that can only be solved by accepting that some things *shouldn't* be solved — the contract he earns by walking away is the understanding that the pipeline has limits.

**What it must establish** `[RECOMMENDATION]`: the novel's thesis sentence for the contract era. The old thesis was "he was not chosen; he was present." The new one is unwritten — `[UNKNOWN]` until the author writes it — but this milestone is where it must be *earned*, not declared. Candidates the design leaves open: "understanding is not ownership"; "some doors stay closed because he closed them"; "the books balance both ways."

## 14. Keeping the absence meaningful — the standing checklist

`[RECOMMENDATION]` At every milestone, present and future, the design must be able to answer yes to all five:

1. **Can a bullet still kill him?** If any contract would stop it by *being armor*, the contract is redesigned or refused.
2. **Can he lose a fair fight to a D-rank?** If not, the power has drifted past the trained-human ceiling.
3. **Is he ever wrong on-page?** If three consecutive solves are clean, the verification protocol is decorative.
4. **Does every contract show its price?** Activation toll, Attention, interference — if a use is free, the economy is leaking.
5. **Does the world survive without him?** (Q475/Q298 `[CANON]`-adjacent test.) If the contract system only works in his hands *and* the world's causal structure now depends on it, the Arthur-free test fails — the system must be *his* affordance, not the world's keystone.

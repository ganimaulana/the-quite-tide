## 0. The design decision this ladder assumes

**The measurand.** `[RECOMMENDATION]` Rank measures **certified safe deployable output** — output × control, tested under controlled conditions (Q027 solution 1; Q396 solution 1). This is `[PROPOSED]` `[REWRITE]` (rewrites 04.1's Awakening/rank mechanics): it unifies the reboot's public F–S ranks with the old T0–T7 Meridian Vector by a single mapping table, instead of creating a third scale (the audit's recommended solution to Q396). `[CANON]` 30.1: "Tier measures sustainable output, not skill, not danger, not destiny." The F–S ladder inherits that sentence verbatim — it is the old doctrine with a public filing number.

**What rank is not.** `[INFERENCE]` Rank is not combat power, not worth, not danger, not destiny — 30.1's doctrine, kept. A B-rank accountant and a B-rank duelist hold the same *certificate* and radically different *threat profiles*. Every "but he's only C-rank" scene must survive this distinction or the ladder becomes a power level with extra steps.

**Tiers above S.** `[PROPOSED]` `[ADAPT]` (adapts 30's T7 doctrine): T7 ("we don't know what that was") is not a rank anyone holds — it is the drawer for the unclassifiable (Q480 solution 1). S ≈ T6. Nothing in the ladder goes above S as a *held rank*.

## 1. The ladder, F to S (qualitative)

Each rank: what it can do, what it cannot do, and its footprint at individual / building / city / nation / anomaly scale. All capabilities below are `[PROPOSED]` working definitions for the reboot — new canon for the F–S layer, i.e. `[REWRITE]` of 04.1's rank mechanics; the *doctrine* sentences are `[CANON]` from 30.

### F — Uncertified / ERROR

- **Definition:** `[PROPOSED]` `[REWRITE]` (rewrites the covert-only filing doctrine of 05/15): No certifiable conventional ability. This is where 'Ability: ERROR / UNDEFINED' files *by regulation* — a known-wrong filing (Q007 solution 1): the instrument correctly reports "no pattern," and the bureaucracy files the report under F rather than admitting the instrument's limits.
- **Can do:** Live a civilian life. Hold ordinary employment. Be underestimated on paper — which is itself an asset (29.B.12's `[CANON]` irony: "certified weak").
- **Cannot do:** Be deployed. Hold field licenses. Enter restricted zones. Draw rank pay, rank insurance, or rank legal standing. `[PROPOSED]` `[REWRITE]` (gives F written denial-content per Q229): F is not a power tier; it is a *denial of institutional access* (Q229 solution 1 — the audit's recommended definition of what F denies).
- **Footprint:** Individual: none supernatural. Building / city / nation / anomaly: none.
- **The social fact:** `[INFERENCE]` F is where the reboot's undervalue engine lives — but only if F's *content* (what it denies) is written with teeth. An F-rank who can walk into a restricted Seep site is not undervalued; he's just mislabeled.

### E — ≈ T1 — Licensed curiosity

- **Can do:** Minor utility under supervision — conjuration-contracting's licensed trade (02.II `[CANON]`), household/industrial cantrips, registered civilian work. Hold a civilian practitioner license.
- **Cannot do:** Anything decisive. An E-rank cannot decide a fight, a negotiation, or an investigation.
- **Footprint:** Individual: convenience. Building: nuisance. City / nation / anomaly: none.

### D — ≈ T2 — Professional grade

- **Can do:** The working class of the Esper economy. Hold field licenses. Be hired for containment support, ward maintenance, survey work. Decide a room.
- **Cannot do:** Operate unsupervised on live phenomena. Outlast Erosion on long deployments.
- **Footprint:** Individual: decisive advantage. Building: can clear or hold a floor. City: none. Anomaly: support role only.

### C — ≈ T3 — Registered, watched

- **Can do:** Decide a firefight or a boardroom. Hold independent field command. Every use is Seismograph-visible (04.5 `[CANON]`).
- **Cannot do:** Act without the file following. A C-rank's career is a ledger: every deployment is recorded, reviewed, and priced.
- **Footprint:** Individual: overwhelming. Building: can take or hold one. City: incident-scale (one bad day, contained). Nation: none. Anomaly: can work a live Seep with backup.

### B — ≈ T4 — National asset

- **Can do:** Hold theater command. Be the reason a faction wins a quiet war. Every deployment is a policy decision.
- **Cannot do:** Deploy casually — `[CANON]` 30.4's doctrine: at this tier, use *is* politics. Erosion-priced: sustained B-tier output shortens the career by years (04.2 `[CANON]`).
- **Footprint:** Individual: irrelevant opposition. Building: trivial. City: can break one open (and will answer for it). Nation: strategic reserve. Anomaly: can close mid-tier Seeps.

### A — ≈ T5 — Geopolitical event

- **Can do:** End a standoff by arriving. Hold deterrence posture. Be briefed to heads of state.
- **Cannot do:** Have a private life, a long career, or a quiet deployment. `[CANON]` At T5+, "retirement" is a medical term (30.2's profiles).
- **Footprint:** Individual / building: not a meaningful scale. City: can unmake the functioning of one. Nation: a line item in other nations' war plans. Anomaly: can engage high-tier phenomena — and the phenomena engage back.

### S — ≈ T6 — Living deterrent

- **Can do:** Be the reason wars don't start. Hold permanent observation detail — the watchers are part of the rank.
- **Cannot do:** Be deployed without a coalition-level decision. Be *used* without being *spent*: `[CANON]` Erosion at this tier is measured in months, not years (04.2).
- **Footprint:** City: can erase the question. Nation: strategic fact. Anomaly: the only tier that treats apex phenomena as peers — briefly, and at terminal cost.

## 2. When lower beats higher — and why

`[CANON]` 30.3's seven mechanisms, kept verbatim as the reboot's answer to every "how did F beat C" scene. A lower rank defeats a higher one **only** through these, never through hidden output:

1. **Rule interference** — the higher rank's ability has a condition, and the lower rank breaks the condition (ward, anchor, line-of-sight, Tide-noise).
2. **Preparation** — the fight was decided before it started; the higher rank walked into a prepared position.
3. **Terrain** — the environment taxes the higher output more than the lower (confined space vs. area effects, wards, the Undertow's edge).
4. **Tide-noise** — `[CANON]` 04.6: high Tide attenuates channeled power; the lower rank fights in the noise.
5. **Information advantage** — `[CANON]` "information is scarcer than raw power" (02.IV): the lower rank knows the Rule, the higher rank doesn't know they're in one.
6. **Erosion attrition** — the higher rank cannot afford to win; the lower rank's victory is making the win too expensive.
7. **Social/legal constraints** — the higher rank *could* win and would be destroyed for it (Choir law, Accords, exposure). The leash is the weapon.

`[RECOMMENDATION]` Every upset scene should be *auditable* against this list — ideally on-page, in Arthur's own after-action accounting. An upset that can't name its mechanism is a power-level violation, not a triumph.

## 3. Why ranks remain socially and institutionally important

`[CANON]` 30.4's doctrine — "the file is the power" — ports to the public era with interest. Rank matters because it is the key to:

- **Deployment rights and licenses** (who may legally use power, where, on whom).
- **Insurance and liability** (who pays when a ward fails; unranked use voids coverage).
- **Restricted-zone access** (Seep sites, anchor facilities, the academies' inner courts).
- **Legal force powers** (ranked Espers hold defined use-of-power authority; F-ranks hold none).
- **Pay, procurement, and guild standing** (the Esper economy's wage ladder).
- **Social capital** (marriage market, school admissions, corporate boards — the vulgar, honest part).

`[PROPOSED]` `[REWRITE]` (new canon for the public-rank era) — two guardrails the audit demands:
- **Rank decay** (Q311 solution 1): rank is re-certified; it can be lost. Without decay, the ladder is an aristocracy and the story's social mobility dies.
- **Rank ≠ command** (Q312's warning): high rank does not confer rank over people — the military learned this; the academies must too. Otherwise every S-rank is a warlord and the setting's politics collapse into duels.

## 4. Where "outside classification" sits

**It does not sit above S.** `[RECOMMENDATION]` This is the single most important sentence in this file.

`[INFERENCE]` The ladder measures **certified deployable output**. Arthur has no output to certify — the instrument reports ERROR, and the report is *correct*. His power, if the contract system works, lives on a different axis: **information position** (Q429 solution 2; Q397 solution 1) — near-zero output, compounding knowledge of Rules, conditions, and prices.

So: the ladder is a ladder of output; **Arthur stands next to the ladder, not on it** (Q187 solution 1; Q480 solution 1). "Outside classification" is orthogonal, conditional, and composite — not S+, not a secret tier, not a higher number. He cannot defeat an S-rank by raw output, and he never becomes "SSS" — the brief's own prohibition, kept as law.

`[RECOMMENDATION]` The story must *show* the orthogonality, not just file it: scenes where his information position beats rank (mechanisms 1, 5, and 7 above), and scenes where it doesn't matter at all — where output is the only currency and he loses, runs, or pays someone else's price. If every scene is winnable by cleverness, "outside classification" becomes S+ with better branding.

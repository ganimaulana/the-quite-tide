## 1. Anomaly / Contract / Resonance farming

**The exploit.** Arthur solves low-stakes phenomena on repeat — staged kitchen hauntings, minor Tide-marks, every filed curiosity in the Meridian library — and banks contracts or Resonance per solve, turning investigation into a treadmill that outpaces the entire progression curve. `[INFERENCE]` Related findings: Q057 (trivial-solve farming), Q066 (firsthand-presence requirement), Q191 (what counts as a solve), Q193 (Resonance as Pokédex check-ins), Q257 (grinding low-tier Seeps), Q162 (copy-of-a-copy rot).

**Severity:** `CRITICAL` — unpriced farming collapses both progression integrity and the mystery economy; the "understands things" identity becomes "visits things."

**Closing rule.** `[RECOMMENDATION]` Three interlocking rules, all required:
1. **Novelty-gated, difficulty-scaled yield:** only *genuinely new* understandings pay. Re-solving a filed phenomenon, re-visiting a solved one, or solving below one's demonstrated grade yields zero Resonance and no contract (Q057 solution 1; Q191 solution 1). The gauge is a Pokédex, not an odometer (Q193 solution 1) — one tap per anomaly.
2. **Firsthand investigation, personal risk:** secondhand reports, library solutions, and guided walkthroughs do not count (Q066 solution 1; Q191 solution 2). The understanding must be *earned in the room*.
3. **Empirical certification:** a solve is real only when the predicted behavior holds in reality (Q192 solution 1; 02.IV's method rule). Self-declaration pays nothing; faked confirmation pays nothing.

**What the closure accidentally breaks.** It punishes legitimate re-engagement — Arthur cannot grind, but he also cannot profitably revisit an old phenomenon for a deeper read unless the "upgrade path" (Q156 solution 1: same anomaly pays again only for genuinely deeper understanding grades) is kept open, which re-opens a narrow farming vector the author must police by grade-rigor. Variety-forcing can also feel gamey to readers ("why won't he just check the warehouse again?") unless the novelty rule is shown, not told, early.

## 2. Fake anomalies

**The exploit.** Arthur (or a faction) stages a phenomenon — a conjured haunting, a faked Seep signature, a hired poltergeist — then "solves" it for a contract. The pipeline rewards theater, not understanding.

**Severity:** `MEDIUM` — scene-level fraud, but if it works once it teaches readers the pipeline is gullible.

**Closing rule.** `[RECOMMENDATION]` The empirical-certification rule from §1 does the work: a staged solve fails certification because the "prediction" was scripted, not discovered — and 02.IV's `[CANON]` "the universe has an outside" means a conjured phenomenon is a *conjuration*, not a Seep, and conjurations have no independent Rule to contract (Q010 solution 3's shape). Additionally: the artifact records the *understanding*, and there is nothing to understand in a stage play.

**What the closure accidentally breaks.** It slows the loop — every solve needs a falsifiable-test beat (Q190 solution 1), which costs pages. It also creates a false-positive risk: a *real* phenomenon that looks staged (the old "instrument-blind" class, Q090) gets dismissed by Arthur himself, which is actually story fuel if the author remembers to use it, and a plot hole if they don't.

## 3. Trivial solves

**The exploit.** Same as §1 at the low end: phenomena so minor they carry no risk get solved en masse for incremental Resonance — the "check in at every haunted vending machine" strategy.

**Severity:** `CRITICAL` — this is the volume form of farming and it defeats difficulty-scaling by sheer count.

**Closing rule.** `[RECOMMENDATION]` Difficulty-scaled yield with a floor of zero: below-grade phenomena pay nothing (Q057 solution 1); the gauge measures *depth of understanding*, not count of visits. Firsthand presence without risk still fails the certification bar (Q066/Q191).

**What the closure accidentally breaks.** The author must then define "grade" with enough rigor that readers can feel when a solve *should* pay — otherwise every low-stakes investigation scene reads as wasted time, and the episodic texture the old world did well (ch1–80's quiet cases) loses its reward logic.

## 4. Forced entities (coercion)

**The exploit.** Sapient phenomena are *made* to sign: hostages, threats, pain, leverage over loved ones, engineered desperation. "Understand, don't kill" becomes "understand, then squeeze." Related: Q151 (coercion-by-proxy), Q460 (the coercion rule), Q446 (farming him), Q109 (faction farming).

**Severity:** `HIGH` — mechanical (corrupts the pipeline) and thematic (destroys the premise-3 ethic). If coercion works, every faction does it on page one.

**Closing rule.** `[RECOMMENDATION]` The coercion-lock: a coerced solve yields fragment-grade output at best, or the contract degrades/corrupts, or the artifact refuses — pick one mechanism and state it (Q460 solution 1; Q151 solutions). `[CANON]` precedent: 29.B.8's anti-engineering rule (staged deaths void the tether) shows the old world already priced engineered outcomes at zero. The cleanest statement: *understanding extracted under threat is not understanding; the medium knows the difference.*

**What the closure accidentally breaks.** It removes a dark plot avenue (the "morally gray Arthur" or villain-farmer arc) unless the author keeps *attempted* coercion as a story beat — which is fine, but every attempt must then visibly fail or corrupt, or readers will ask why the villains don't just try harder. It also requires the artifact to *judge* coercion, which grows the artifact's black box (Q454's entity-ontology pressure).

## 5. Duplicated / stolen / copied Contracts

**The exploit.** Contracts are copied (the power-sponge shape), stolen (stripped from the repository), or free-ridden (a faction reverse-engineers the contract from its effects). Arthur becomes a mint (Q467) or a victim (Q465). Related: Q110 (`[CANON]` — old canon answers the sponge question: no), Q162 (copy-of-a-copy rot), Q305 (stealing documentation), Q344 (free-riding on published solves), Q463 (fungibility), Q467 (the mint loophole).

**Severity:** `CRITICAL` — the mint loophole is the economic form of the sponge problem; if transfer bypasses the solve-requirement, premise 3 ("stronger by understanding") becomes Arthur's private ethic while everyone downstream gets power without understanding.

**Closing rule.** `[RECOMMENDATION]` A three-part lock:
1. **Understanding is personal and firsthand** (Q344 solution 1; Q467 solution 2): transfer requires *re-solving* — Arthur can teach, not give. The recipient must independently understand the phenomenon.
2. **Copies degrade** (Q162 solution 3; Q305 solution 3): documentation-derived use is fragment-grade and rots per remove — secondhand contracts never reach the original's reliability.
3. **Non-fungibility ported** (Q463 solution 1/3): contracts are not units; "slots" count *relationships*, not inventory. The understanding is Arthur-bound; invocation rights, if ever lent, are licenses, not assets (Q466 solution 2).

**What the closure accidentally breaks.** It kills the fragment market — a genuine worldbuilding loss (the Red Ledger auctioning *understandings* was a good villain economy, Q289–Q294). It also limits team combat: Arthur cannot hand a contract to an ally, which constrains the ensemble's tactical ceiling. And "the artifact knows it's a copy" is more black-box discernment.

## 6. Stolen / copied Artifact

**The exploit.** Kill Arthur, take the artifact, strip the contracts. Every competent faction (02.IV: "competence is distributed") sees this on page one. Related: Q105 (theft), Q220 (government seizure), Q255 (custody), Q291 (black-market artifact), Q468 (engineered succession), Q320 (inheritance).

**Severity:** `CRITICAL` — an unpatched assassination economy makes the setting's competent factions behave irrationally if they *don't* try it.

**Closing rule.** `[RECOMMENDATION]` Port 29.B.8's anti-engineering rule in the Q468-solution-3 shape: **engineered succession hollows the prize** — the artifact may pass, but the contracts dissipate; the murderer inherits an empty medium. `[CANON]` Old rule: "staged deaths void the tether — the tether dissipates." The reboot needs the sentence: *the repository is Arthur-bound; the artifact without its understandings is a box.* (Artifact binding/transfer itself stays `[UNKNOWN]` — the closure works without resolving the metaphysics: whatever the binding *is*, engineered transfer breaks it.)

**What the closure accidentally breaks.** Murder-proofing the artifact requires it to *discriminate* engineered from natural succession — more black-box discernment (Q454 again). It also removes dynastic-horror plots (the family that breeds holders) unless failed attempts are shown. The author must show at least one failed attempt early, or the rule reads as authorial protection.

## 7. Forced revelation (detection / prediction / memory contracts)

**The exploit.** Information-contracts are aimed at the setting's protected unknowns: detection contracts that find anything (Q443), prediction contracts that collapse the mystery economy (Q449), memory contracts that interrogate the dead (Q408, Q412, Q413). Each is a mystery-killer if unpriced.

**Severity:** `HIGH` — individually each breaks one protected system; collectively they break 33's architecture.

**Closing rules.** `[RECOMMENDATION]`
- **Detection:** contracts detect *phenomena*, not people; detection announces itself (ward-noise, the sensed phenomenon reacts — Q443 solution 3); precision is domain-bounded (Q443 solution 1).
- **Prediction:** precognitive phenomena are self-defeating — solving collapses what made them precognitive (Q449 solution 1; the old "attention modulates pressure" finding weaponized); and the Tribunal declares oracle-Seeps strategic assets (Q449 solution 2).
- **Memory/echoes:** contracts store *residues*, not souls (Q408 solution 3); echoes are fallible recordings (Q412 solution 1); interrogating the dead spends the residue (Q408 solution 2) — use is consumption.
- **General:** information yields *records*, not answers (Q181 solution 3); the entity knows only its own nature (Q181 solution 1); and `[CANON]` 04.4's rule survives: **detection does not grant comprehension.**

**What the closures accidentally break.** They discipline every detection scene — the author can never again write "and then he knew." The "omniscient detective" fantasy is capped by construction, which some readers will experience as the system being stingy. The self-defeating-oracle rule also removes the most valuable prize in the setting, which must be replaced with something (the *sabotage* of oracle-farming becomes the prize — Q449 solution 3).

## 8. Temporary / conditional / partial contract abuse

**The exploit.** Temporary contracts are cycled (sign, burn, re-sign) to dodge permanence costs; conditional contracts are triggered in safe rehearsal to bank the effect; partials/fragments are stacked to approximate a full contract without paying for one. Related: Q153 (one-tap), Q165 (temporary cooldown), Q169 (conditional triggers), Q140–Q150 (partial economics).

**Severity:** `HIGH` — cycling converts every priced contract into a free trial.

**Closing rules.** `[RECOMMENDATION]`
- **One-tap:** a solved phenomenon yields one contract-event; you cannot re-sign what you burned (Q153 solution 1).
- **Cooldown + degradation:** temporaries have cooldowns; cycling the same source degrades reliability per cycle (Q165 solution 2).
- **Conditionals are real triggers:** a conditional contract's condition must be genuine — rehearsed triggers fail certification (Q169 solution 1); the artifact enforces sincerity the way it enforces non-coercion.
- **72-hour discipline:** `[CANON]` borrowed matter reverts within 72 hours — temporary-contract *effects* obey the same reversion (Q153 solution 3's shape).

**What the closures accidentally break.** The "creative temporary use" design space shrinks — some of the most delightful contract ideas live in temporaries, and cooldowns make them feel rationed. The author must budget: temporaries are the *spice*, not the meal.

## 9. Intentional-failure benefits

**The exploit.** Arthur deliberately fails a contract's terms to trigger the failure-state benefit — the "fail upward" shape (Q144): if breaking a contract refunds the slot, teaches the Rule for free, or angers the entity into revealing itself, failure becomes a strategy.

**Severity:** `MEDIUM` — slow-burn; it teaches readers that the system's punishments are negotiable.

**Closing rule.** `[RECOMMENDATION]` Failure yields nothing positive (Q144 solution 2): a failed contract locks the slot until the price is paid, scars the gauge, or is remembered by the counterparty (reputation cost — entities refuse the known defaulter). The old ratchet's logic ports: **failure is priced, never profitable.**

**What the closure accidentally breaks.** Purely punitive failure flattens the interesting middle — the *partial* and *fragment* grades must carry all the texture of "not quite succeeding," and the author must resist making every failure a catastrophe (catastrophe-fatigue) or a slap on the wrist (toothlessness).

## 10. Infinite slots

**The exploit.** The slot count grows without bound — via gauge breakthroughs, purchases, or premise drift — until Arthur holds dozens of contracts and the "weak" premise is a filing error. Related: Q175 (permanent-contract cap), Q188 (slot cap), Q435 (the sponge problem), Q472 (overflow).

**Severity:** `CRITICAL` — uncapped accumulation is godhood with extra steps; it is the failure mode the entire reboot is trying to avoid.

**Closing rules.** `[RECOMMENDATION]` A layered cap, all stated:
1. **Hard slot maximum, low** (Q175 solution 1 / Q188 solution 1): a single-digit cap, named in canon. Seven is the audit's recurring shape; the number matters less than its hardness.
2. **Slot interference** (Q435 solution 1): reliability degrades with breadth — each filled slot makes every contract slightly less reliable. Breadth is its own tax.
3. **Maintenance cost** (Q435 solution 2): contracts must be re-derived or they degrade — the portfolio is a garden, not a vault.
4. **Forced curation at capacity** (Q472 solution 1): a full-slot solve forces a *release* — and release has a cost (the understanding fades; re-solving is barred or penalized). Every new contract is a sacrifice.
5. **Concurrency pricing:** only one active contract initially; later concurrency, if allowed, carries geometric interference (Q149 solution 3; Q183 solution 1).

**What the closures accidentally break.** The collection fantasy — a major webnovel selling point — is capped by construction. Forced curation is story-rich (every new contract is a goodbye) but emotionally expensive; the author must make releases feel like *choices*, not taxes, or readers will resent the system. The interference math must stay qualitative (02.II's `[CANON]` "we do not publish what we cannot afford to be wrong about") or it becomes a spreadsheet.

## 11. Emergent combination powers

**The exploit.** Two individually-priced contracts combine into an unpriced third effect — the interaction the designers never costed. Related: Q043 (05.7 underweights interaction effects), Q149 (contract vs. contract), Q183 (permanents evolve/merge).

**Severity:** `HIGH` — emergence is where every "clever combo" fantasy lives, which means it's also where the price schedule dies.

**Closing rule.** `[RECOMMENDATION]` Extend 05.7's composite-vector doctrine to contracts: **interactions are audited, not assumed.** State the rule: combined effects inherit the *worst* reliability of their components and pay *both* activation tolls (Q149 solution 2/3); some combinations are refused by the artifact (the refusal list, Q427 solution 1); genuinely novel emergences are treated as new phenomena — they must be *solved* before they can be *used* (Q183 solution 3). Emergence is therefore never free: it is a discovery with its own price.

**What the closure accidentally breaks.** The "clever combo" fantasy — the reader's favorite kind of payoff — is taxed. The author must let combinations *work* often enough to reward cleverness while pricing them enough to preserve the economy. This is the hardest balance in the file, and it cannot be solved by rule alone; it needs the Q203 governor (the story must sometimes let the combo fail expensively).

## 12. Death / time / causality / rank bypasses

**The exploit.** A contract is aimed at a CANON-LOCKED wall: resurrection-adjacent effects (Q405), Erosion reversal (Q410), direct mind control (Q415/Q416), past alteration or time travel (Q418), anchorless relocation (Q421), Undertow access (Q424), immortality (Q430), living-pattern storage / ability copying (Q433), rank-ceiling evasion (Q427/Q429). Each bypass breaks one of 02's hard rules.

**Severity:** `CRITICAL` — each successful bypass retires a load-bearing rule; the setting's causal structure is the casualty.

**Closing rule.** `[RECOMMENDATION]` The **refusal list**, stated in canon (Q427 solution 1): the artifact refuses — resurrection-adjacent effects, Erosion-reversal, direct mind control (brake #4 restated: "no *reliable* mass influence, by any medium" — Q416 solution 1), past alteration, living-pattern storage, anchorless relocation, Undertow access, immortality-shaped bargains. The refusals must be *principled*, not convenient: the recommended principle is **"the artifact cannot store what has no fixed Rule, and cannot store a price it cannot name"** (Q427 solution 2; Q433 solution 2) — resurrection has no fixed Rule (the dead are gone; `[CANON]` no true resurrection), Erosion's price is named and irreversible (`[CANON]`), mind control's unreliability is geometric (`[CANON]` brake #4). The list is therefore not authorial fiat; it is the hard rules restated for the new medium.

**What the closure accidentally breaks.** Every refusal is a foregone story avenue — and some of the best old-canon horror lived *at* these walls (the Undertow, the Choir). The author must keep the walls *visible* (characters should push against them and be refused on-page at least once) or the refusal list reads as a line in a bible nobody enforces.

## 13. Organizational abuse: governments, black markets, research orgs, and faction-farming of Arthur

**The exploit.** Competent institutions do the rational thing: the Quiet Desk *tasks* him (golden handcuffs with renewable output — Q446); the government licenses or seizes the pipeline (Q215–Q220); research orgs industrialize solving (Q283–Q288, Q286); the Red Ledger auctions contracts or strips them from him (Q289–Q294, Q465); rivals farm competing solvers (Q298); someone kidnaps his family as leverage (Q296). `[INFERENCE]` The old threat model ("they want to study or contain him") becomes "they want to rob or farm him" the moment contracts are separable or renewable.

**Severity:** `CRITICAL` — 02.IV's `[CANON]` "competence is distributed" means the factions *will* do this; a setting where they don't, without explanation, insults the reader.

**Closing rules.** `[RECOMMENDATION]` A doctrine, not a single rule:
1. **Understanding is irreducibly personal** (Q109 solution 1; Q460 solution 1): coerced or delegated solving yields garbage — fragment-grade, unreliable, or refused. Farming him at gunpoint produces nothing worth having. This is the system's built-in defense: *understanding cannot be extracted.*
2. **Solve-farms fail on novelty and firsthand rules** (§1): an institution cannot mass-produce solves because each solve needs a *new* phenomenon and *personal* risk. The pipeline does not industrialize.
3. **The escrow ports to the pipeline** (Q446 solution 1): the dead-man's switch now covers the *method* — if he disappears, how to solve phenomena goes public. Mutually assured disclosure of the pipeline, not the product.
4. **Golden handcuffs are allowed as arcs, not as equilibrium** (Q446 solution 3): the Desk *will* try to task him; the story is his negotiation of it. But the exit must be earned on-page, and the arrangement must cost him visibly, or it becomes comfortable employment.
5. **Family protection is doctrine, not luck** (Q296 solution 1): write the countermeasure (decoys, dead drops, the affidavit network) *before* the kidnapping arc, not during it.

**What the closures accidentally break.** If overused, the "understanding can't be coerced" defense makes institutions look toothless — the author must let them be threatening in *other* currencies (legal, financial, social, violent) rather than the one the system immunizes. The escrow's upgrade (publishing the *method*) is a bigger masquerade threat than the original, which raises the stakes appropriately — but it also means the deterrence, if ever triggered, ends the world as the characters know it. That trigger must never be pulled cheaply.

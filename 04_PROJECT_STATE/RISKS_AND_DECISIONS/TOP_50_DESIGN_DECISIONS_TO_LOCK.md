### D-01 — Cost currency: what does Arthur pay?
**Decision:** Name the single currency (or currency-set) in which contract prices are denominated, given [CANON] "Fathom count stays zero — permanently" (AUDIT/03).
**Options:** (a) Ratchet/attention port — unifies old and new, but every contract lengthens the lag toward the 10s unknown (a hard series clock). (b) New currency (memory, time, resonance debt) — clean break, but NEEDS REDESIGN-level invention: must be visible, finite, dramatically legible. (c) Restrictions-as-price (HxH vows) — no currency needed; behavior is the price; elegant but hard to audit for fairness across 1000 chapters.
**RECOMMENDED:** (a)+(c) hybrid — the *base* price is ratchet/attention (ports 29 §B.6's five currencies: social, ratchet, attention, physiological, exposure [ADAPT]), and Arthur may *additionally* vow restrictions to discount or afford bigger contracts (Q127-3). Fathoms stay zero; the lock's perimeter is stated (R-19).
**Why lock early:** every price, vow, and trade-off in the directive is currently denominated in nothing (R-02); no contract can be designed until prices have units.
**What breaks if open:** all cost answers float; "earned, not granted" is unenforceable; drafts improvise incompatible prices per scene.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — AUDIT/03 brake (5) gains a positive statement of what he *does* pay.

### D-02 — Intervention redefinition: is a signed contract "intervention"?
**Decision:** Redefine [CANON] brake #2 ("the entity never intervenes on Arthur's behalf") for a world with entity-derived contracts, or retire it.
**Options:** (a) Surgical redefinition: the brake forbade *unilateral, unpriced, unasked* action *for* him; a contract is *bilateral, priced, asked* — a bargain, not a favor; brake survives as "no *free* intervention." (b) Keep absolute; redesign contracts as non-entity-derived (artifact stores *his understandings*, not favors) — dissolves the clash but guts premise 6's "Entities." (c) Drop the brake openly with a changelog — honest, but retires ARC XII's "whether it answers" hinge.
**RECOMMENDED:** (a) — with a published bright-line test (the "bargain vs favor" test: was it asked, priced, and bilateral? all three, or it's intervention) enforced in the chapter engine. Q461-1, Q108-2.
**Why lock early:** Q461 calls this "the single most load-bearing clash in the reboot… binary"; every contract scene is either legal or a DEM violation — there is no neutral draft.
**What breaks if open:** the old endgame hinge is spent by accident in chapter one, or every contract scene fails the story audit.
**Canon impact:** [REWRITE] — AUDIT/03's brake #2 must be redefined explicitly; silent coexistence is forbidden.

### D-03 — Recognition mechanics: what distinguishes "recognition" from chosenness?
**Decision:** Give "the artifact recognizes Arthur" a mechanical definition that survives a hostile reader.
**Options:** (a) Reproducible lock-and-key: stated criteria (Loud + specific cognitive profile + a solved test the artifact administers); anyone meeting them would be recognized — Arthur is first, not fated. (b) Contingency: it didn't choose; he was the first mind that could *hold* what it stores (proximity + Loudness, the tether's own physics — 29 §B.8); "recognition" is the family's romantic word. (c) Election, owned: the reboot's theme *is* chosenness examined — can you refuse it? what does it obligate?
**RECOMMENDED:** (a) with (b) as the backstory physics — the criteria are mechanical and falsifiable (a second candidate *could* appear: a loaded gun for later arcs — Q476-1); the family's "choosing" language is an in-world misconception (02.V allows these).
**Why lock early:** without a transfer rule the anti-cliché rule is a slogan (R-13); theft, succession, and rejection plots are all unplottable.
**What breaks if open:** every reader collapses "recognition" into chosen-one on page one; the RED_TEAM tradition (AUDIT/04) will do it first.
**Canon impact:** [REWRITE] of premise 4's wording ("recognizes," never "chooses").

### D-04 — Counterparty ontology: what *is* an "Entity" that can sign?

**RECOMMENDED:** (b) as the opening rule, with (a) as the metaphysical gloss — counterparties must present a stable, testable Rule; the artifact cannot bind what has no pattern-continuity. (c) is reserved as a late-series discovery, never an opening mechanic.
**Why lock early:** Q454: "every downstream question (consent, ownership, death, ethics) inherits this hole" — it is the foundation under the entire system.

**Canon impact:** [REWRITE] — premise 6's counterparty list gains an ontology; "Entities" as currently used is retired as a placeholder.

### D-08 — Solve-certification authority: who says "solved"?

### D-09 — Artifact transferability and binding

**RECOMMENDED:** (a) for the *binding* (soul-bound while he lives), with Q105-2's nuance for *handling*: anyone can physically hold it, but only his understanding activates contracts — theft still hurts (denies him the tool); heist tension survives without chosen-one mechanics.

# POWER COMBINATION

The team exercise began without anyone asking Arthur to be the leader.

That was the first thing he noticed.

The instructor divided the class into groups of five.

Arthur ended up with Evelyn Hart, Mara Bennett, Owen Carter, and Simon Clarke.

The five of them stood in front of a sealed training chamber.

Inside was a metal case.

The objective was printed on a board.

**RETRIEVE THE CASE.**

No time limit was given.

No combat score was displayed.

The instructor explained the rules.

"The chamber contains moving obstacles, false signals, and a locking mechanism. You are not required to defeat every obstacle. You are required to retrieve the case."

Mara raised a hand.

"Can we break the chamber?"

"No."

"I wanted to establish the boundary."

Evelyn looked at the chamber.

"We should identify the movement pattern first."

Arthur nodded.

"I can tell you what is moving."

Evelyn looked at him.

"Then I'll decide where we move."

Owen adjusted his gloves.

"I'll watch the signals."

Clarke examined the case through the glass.

"I'll inspect the objective once we reach it."

Mara raised her shield.

"I'll cover whoever goes in."

Arthur looked at the four of them.

Nobody had asked who was in charge.

They had simply divided the problem.

The door opened.

Arthur stepped inside first.

Movement immediately registered around him.

Three mechanical arms.

Two sliding panels.

Something small moving behind the left wall.

He raised a hand.

"Wait."

The others stopped.

"Three arms ahead. The first moves left. Second follows two seconds later. Third reverses."

Evelyn watched the mechanism.

"Can you tell which one reaches the centre?"

"Yes."

"Then we move after the second reversal."

Arthur counted silently.

The arm moved.

Another.

Reversal.

"Now."

The team crossed.

They reached the first junction without triggering anything.

Owen stopped.

"Something is wrong."

Arthur looked at him.

"What?"

"The sensor signal."

Owen pointed to a small indicator.

"It flickers every seven seconds."

Arthur watched the area.

"I don't see movement there."

"Exactly."

Clarke crouched beside the wall.

"The signal is not from the obstacle."

Owen looked at him.

"How do you know?"

Clarke touched a maintenance plate.

"Because this panel was opened recently."

Arthur felt a strange satisfaction.

Everyone was seeing a different part of the same problem.

They moved forward.

The next obstacle was faster.

A training projectile crossed the corridor.

Arthur detected it.

"Down."

Mara moved before anyone else.

Her shield caught the projectile.

The impact was heavy enough to force her one step backward.

She gritted her teeth.

"I'm fine."

Evelyn looked toward the launcher.

"Again."

Another projectile fired.

Arthur sensed it.

"Right."

Evelyn raised her hand.

The projectile changed direction in mid-flight and struck the padded wall.

"Good," Mara said.

"Don't praise me yet," Evelyn replied. "There are four launchers."

Arthur felt the next movement.

"Five."

Evelyn's eyes narrowed.

"Five?"

"One is behind us."

Mara turned.

The fifth projectile launched.

She caught the impact on her shield.

The team moved.

No one shouted instructions over everyone else.

Arthur reported movement.

Evelyn redirected what she could.

Mara absorbed what could not be redirected.

Owen watched for abnormal signals.

Clarke examined anything that looked deliberate rather than mechanical.

At the final chamber, they found the case.

It had no obvious lock.

Clarke ran his fingers along the metal.

"There is a secondary seal."

Mara looked at him.

"Can you open it?"

"No."

Arthur expected disappointment.

Instead Clarke continued.

"But I can tell which part was installed last."

Evelyn understood first.

"Then we don't open the original mechanism. We open the replacement."

Clarke nodded.

He pointed.

Arthur watched for movement.

Mara covered the group.

Evelyn shifted a locking pin.

The case opened.

Inside was a sealed training marker.

Arthur picked it up.

The chamber lights changed.

The instructor's voice came through the speaker.

"Exercise complete."

The door opened.

The five students stepped outside.

Mara lowered her shield.

"That was easier than expected."

Owen stared at her.

"You got hit three times."

"And I survived three times."

The instructor approached.

She looked at the group, then at the retrieved marker.

"Good team."

Arthur waited for another comment.

There wasn't one.

She did not say he had carried them.

She did not say Evelyn had solved everything.

She did not praise one Power above the others.

"Your individual abilities were useful," the instructor said. "Your success came from knowing when not to use them."

Arthur looked at the others.

For the first time, the five of them stood together without being assigned to the same line.

It felt different.

Not friendship yet.

Not loyalty.

Something more practical.

Reliability.

The instructor handed them their next schedule.

"Tomorrow, the exercise gets harder."

Arthur read the heading.

**APPLIED ESPER COMBAT.**

Mara looked over his shoulder.

"That sounds threatening."

Arthur remembered her saying the same thing the day before.

This time, nobody laughed.


They gathered outside the chamber while the instructors reset the exercise.

Mara leaned against the wall.

"So," she said, "are we a team now?"

Owen shook his head.

"We were assigned together twice."

"That's practically friendship at this Academy."

Clarke looked at the schedule.

"We still don't know whether our methods work under real combat pressure."

Evelyn nodded.

"He's right."

Mara looked betrayed.

"You could at least pretend to enjoy the moment."

Arthur smiled.

"We worked well."

Mara pointed at him.

"See? Reed understands."

Evelyn folded her arms.

"The next exercise will change the problem."

"How?" Owen asked.

"The first test rewarded observation. The next one will punish slow decisions."

Arthur looked at her.

"You sound certain."

"Because that's what the Academy has been building toward."

She counted on her fingers.

"We learned what our Powers do. Then what our bodies can do. Then what happens when those things conflict. Then we learned to choose methods. Now they want to know whether the pieces work together."

Clarke looked toward the closed field gate.

"Which means individual strength is no longer the entire problem."

Arthur nodded.

Their weaknesses did not overlap completely.

Arthur could detect movement but could not interpret everything.

Evelyn could alter trajectories but could not control every object.

Mara could absorb impact but could not erase it.

Owen could detect abnormal signals but tired quickly.

Clarke could read physical history but could not safely reach every objective.

The group was not powerful.

It was useful.

There was a difference.

The next schedule sheet contained one final line.

**Thirty-minute objective. No instructor intervention.**

Arthur folded the paper.

For the first time, the thought of fighting beside these four people did not feel like another classroom exercise.

It felt like preparation for something the instructors were not yet telling them about.

The exercise also revealed something Arthur had not expected.

He trusted Mara to move into danger.

He trusted Evelyn to change the path of an attack when she said she could.

He trusted Owen when he said a signal was wrong, even when Arthur's own senses showed nothing unusual.

And he trusted Clarke's strange habit of examining everything before touching it.

None of that had existed yesterday.

Trust had not appeared because they had become friends.

It had appeared because each person had done exactly what they said they would do.

That was more useful than friendship in a dangerous situation.

After the chamber closed, Evelyn handed Arthur the retrieved case.

"You were first inside."

Arthur looked at her.

"And?"

"Next time, don't assume that means you should remain first."

Arthur understood.

He had been unconsciously taking the front position because Motion Sense made him good at detecting danger.

But detection did not mean he should always be the one closest to it.

Mara could absorb a hit better.

Evelyn could redirect a moving threat.

Owen could identify signals Arthur could not.

Clarke could solve physical details Arthur might overlook.

The team would become stronger when Arthur stopped treating his own Power as the centre of every decision.

He nodded.

"Understood."

Evelyn gave him a small smile.

"Good."

The next exercise would test whether he remembered.

Arthur thought about the difference on the way back.

A strong individual could solve a problem quickly.

A reliable team could solve a problem when the first solution failed.

That was what had happened inside the chamber.

When Arthur's information was incomplete, Owen supplied another observation. When a projectile came too fast to redirect, Mara absorbed it. When the mechanism did not make sense to Arthur, Clarke found the physical clue. When the path itself became dangerous, Evelyn changed the trajectory.

Nobody replaced anyone else.

They covered the gap.

Arthur suspected that was what the instructors actually wanted them to learn.

Not how to combine Powers into one enormous attack.

How to keep a plan alive when reality damaged it.

He looked at the four students walking beside him.

Mara was already complaining about the next exercise.

Owen was explaining why the sensor had produced a false signal.

Clarke was taking notes.

Evelyn was silently studying the training schedule.

Arthur smiled.

They were not friends yet.

But if tomorrow went badly, he knew who would still be standing beside him.

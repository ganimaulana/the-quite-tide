# RIVAL CONTEMPT

**Scene one â€” the mission assignment.**

The real mission came from the Institute. A Seep in the industrial zone was moving tools into a single pile. Standard teams had failed to contain it because every time they built a frame, the tools moved again.

Mercer was assigned as lead. Arthur was assigned as records support. The roster was the Academy's way of testing both of them: put the D-rank in command, put the F-rank in support, and see whether the formula held on a real site.

"In a real mission, filing is not a method," Mercer said at the briefing. "It is a record of the method. I will solve the Seep. You will write down what I did."

"Yes," Arthur said.

"Then stay out of my way."

Arthur stayed out of his way. He stood at the edge of the site and watched Mercer's team build a perimeter, set up a sensor net, and wait for the Seep to behave predictably.

It did not.

---

**Scene two â€” the wrong rule.**

Mercer's team classified the Seep as object-class, low-mobility, returning tools to their last used location. They built the frame around the last place a tool had been seen.

The tools went around the frame.

"It's learning," Mercer said.

"It's not learning," Arthur said. "You are feeding it."

Mercer turned. "What?"

"Every time you mark a location, it remembers the marker. The marker is a new witness."

"Then what do we do?"

"Stop marking. Stop watching. Let it return to the place it was before any of us arrived."

"That is a guess."

"That is a different guess from yours."

Mercer's jaw tightened. "Fine. Try it. If you fail, you leave the mission."

The team stood down the sensor net. They pulled the frame back. Arthur asked Owen to log the flicker from the building's edge, and Clarke to read the wear on the floor. Clarke found a spot in the northeast corner where the concrete was smoother than the rest.

"This is where tools were first left," Clarke said. "Before the building."

Arthur placed a single custody card there. No frame. No witnesses. Just the record.

The tools returned.

Not to the card. To the smooth spot beneath it.

"It follows the first memory," Arthur said. "Not the last."

Mercer watched from the doorway. He did not clap.

"Is it safe to frame?" the Logistics student asked.

"Not yet. We frame the smooth spot, not the card." Arthur knelt and marked the edge of the smooth patch with chalk. "The Seep returns to the memory. The card is just where I recorded that the memory exists. If we frame the card, the tools will go around the frame. If we frame the memory, the tools have nowhere else to go."

He drew the frame himself, slowly, one line at a time. When it closed, the tools stayed inside it. The Seep settled.

"It's done," Owen said.

"The rule is done. The source is not." Arthur looked at the northeast corner. "Something made this place the first memory. Someone left tools here before the building, which means the building was built on top of a habit. That is a person, not a phenomenon."

Mercer stepped into the room. "You always go one step too far."

"It is the step that matters."

"No. It is the step that gets you suspended." Mercer looked at the chalk frame. "The mission is contained. The report is written. The source is a rumor. That is where a D-rank stops."

"Then that is why you are still D-rank," Clarke said, from the edge of the room.

Mercer turned. The two of them looked at each other. It was the first time anyone on the team had said something like that to the benchmark, and the room went very still.

---

**Scene three â€” the challenge.**

"It follows the first memory," Mercer repeated. "That is your rule."

"Yes."

"Prove it can be wrong."

Arthur looked at him. "What?"

"Every rule I have seen you state has been right. That is not a rule. That is a talent." Mercer stepped into the room. "A rule can be wrong. A rule can be tested. A talent cannot. If you cannot be wrong, then you are not solving anything. You are just winning."

The team was silent. The Medical student looked at the floor. Owen stopped writing.

"Then test it," Arthur said.

"Gladly." Mercer took a tool from the pile and set it on the far side of the room, in a place where no one had ever stood. "If the Seep follows the first memory, that tool goes back to the smooth spot. If it follows the last witness, it goes to me."

They waited. The tool moved, but it did not go to Mercer and it did not go to the smooth spot. It went to the door, where the Logistics student had been standing, and stopped at the threshold.

"That is a third answer," Owen said.

"It is the last witness answer," Mercer said. "The student was the most recent person to move."

"She moved before I set the tool down," Arthur said. "The tool was last seen by me. If the Seep followed the last witness, it would follow the tool's last witness, not the room's."

The room was quiet. Mercer looked at the tool, then at the threshold, then at Arthur, and something in his face changed. He had expected to prove a null set. Instead, the Seep had produced a result neither of them had predicted, which meant the rule was more complicated than either of them had said.

"Then the rule is incomplete," Mercer said.

"Yes," Arthur said. "That is a better result than either of our guesses."

Mercer looked at him for a long moment. Then he wrote something down himself, in his own notebook, which was the first time Arthur had ever seen him take a note at a scene.

"What is the point?"

"That you win on the rule, and you lose on the report." Mercer put the tool back. "Because the report is going to say that the benchmark identified the first-memory rule and the support clerk confirmed it. That is the version that fits the formula."

Arthur looked at the tool in Mercer's hand. Mercer was not lying. The report would say exactly that, and the formula would score it, and the score would favor the D-rank lead no matter who had found the rule.

"You are not claiming credit," Arthur said.

"No. I am claiming the formula." Mercer set the tool down. "You beat me in the demonstration. You will not beat me in the record. That is the same fight, and it is the one that matters."

---

**Scene four â€” the answer Mercer could not unhear.**

They finished the mission. The Seep was contained at the smooth spot. The report was filed the way Mercer had predicted: the benchmark identified the rule, the support clerk confirmed it.

Arthur wrote the report truthfully, because the truth was that Mercer had tested the rule and the test had shown where the rule applied. That was a contribution. It was not the contribution the formula would record.

At the end of the debrief, Mercer stopped him.

"One more question," Mercer said. "How did you know the tools would return to the smooth spot?"

"Because the worker who first left tools there would have set them down standing. The wear on the concrete shows where feet were. A person standing there looks at the pile. The pile becomes the memory."

"That is a guess."

"Yes. But it was a guess I could check with the wear, and the wear agreed." Arthur looked at him. "That is the difference. You asked me to prove I could be wrong. The answer is that I can. The wear could have disagreed. If it had, I would have filed the failure."

Mercer was quiet for a moment. Then he said, "I have never filed a failure."

"I know."

"That is why I am still D-rank."

He said it without anger, which was the strangest part. Then he walked away, and Arthur wrote the exchange down in the parallel file, under RIVAL, second entry, because it was the first time Mercer had admitted that something was wrong with his own record.

Clarke read the entry over his shoulder.

"He is not a bad man," Clarke said.

"No."

"He is a bad system with a good face."

"He is what the formula produces," Arthur said. "If I had his rank, I would be him. The formula does not reward doubt, so he does not doubt."

"That is generous."

"It is accurate. Generous would be saying he could change."

Clarke considered that. "Could he?"

Arthur thought about the mission, the null set, and the way Mercer had tested the rule without claiming to have won. Then he thought about the report, and the formula, and the fact that Mercer would file it exactly as he had promised.

"Only if the formula punishes him for it," Arthur said. "And the formula does not punish D-ranks. That is the whole point of being one."

# THE CONTRACT

Arthur waited until the weapons hall emptied.

The other students left in groups. Their voices faded down the corridor until only the distant sounds of the Academy remained.

Arthur sat alone on the edge of the practice floor.

He placed the training dagger in front of him.

Nothing happened.

He picked it up.

The private interface appeared.

```text
CONTRACT CONDITION DETECTED

Weapon Compatibility: CONFIRMED
```

He waited.

The message changed.

```text
NEW CONTRACT SKILL AVAILABLE

LUGH

A weapon mastery Contract.

Integration parameters are incomplete.

WARNING:

Benefits, restrictions, and consequences
may not be fully known before acceptance.

Unknown consequences may exist.

Accept the Contract?

[ ACCEPT ]   [ DECLINE ]
```

Arthur stared at the name.

"Lugh."

The interface did not respond.

He tried saying it again.

"Who are you?"

Nothing.

He looked at the two choices.

Declining would be safer.

That was obvious.

But the message had not appeared because he had asked for it.

It had appeared because something had recognized compatibility between him and a weapon.

That raised more questions than it answered.

Why the dagger?

Why him?

Why now?

And what exactly did a Contract mean?

Arthur looked at the warning again.

**Unknown consequences may exist.**

The system was admitting that it did not tell him everything.

That made the choice more frightening.

It also made it more believable.

Arthur took a slow breath.

"Accept."

The word vanished.

New text appeared.

```text
CONTRACT ACCEPTED

LUGH

Integration: PARTIAL

Core Function:
Weapon compatibility and temporary mastery.

Known amplification:
Approximately 2x existing effective capability.

Known active window:
Approximately 10 minutes for the current weapon state.

Known restriction:
Forcing the active state beyond its normal limit
causes escalating physical strain.

Additional consequences:
UNKNOWN
```

Arthur read it twice.

No level.

No experience.

No strength statistic.

No damage number.

Just rules.

That was strangely reassuring.

He picked up the training sword.

Nothing happened.

He selected the dagger.

The interface changed.

```text
LUGH

WEAPON STATE:
DAGGER

Activation:
AVAILABLE
```

Arthur hesitated.

Then he deliberately activated it.

For one instant, his grip felt different.

Not stronger.

More complete.

The weight of the dagger became obvious.

The balance.

The angle of the wrist.

The distance at which the point would become difficult to control.

The recovery after a missed strike.

Arthur moved.

The first cut was cleaner than anything he had performed during training.

He stopped.

He tried another.

The movement came naturally.

Not magically.

There was no voice teaching him.

No invisible instructor.

His body simply seemed to understand what the weapon wanted.

Arthur stared at his hand.

"Temporary mastery."

He looked at the clock.

He began a controlled sequence.

Footwork.

Cut.

Recovery.

Turn.

Again.

He had practiced similar movements for less than an hour.

Yet his execution had become dramatically cleaner.

That was the temptation.

The dangerous part.

Arthur understood immediately why someone could become dependent on this.

He increased the pace.

Then stopped.

Pain flashed through his wrist.

Not severe.

But real.

Arthur lowered the dagger.

The Contract had not promised him a stronger body.

It had promised a temporary weapon state.

The body carrying that state still belonged to him.

He deactivated the Contract.

The strange clarity faded.

Arthur rotated his wrist.

"So that's the price."

He looked at the interface.

The final line remained.

**Additional consequences: UNKNOWN.**

That bothered him more than the pain.

Because if the system had shown him every consequence, he could calculate the risk.

It had not.

Arthur put the dagger away.

The next morning, he returned to the weapons hall before class.

He tested the sword.

Then the spear.

Then the dagger.

Each weapon produced a different sensation when Lugh was invoked.

The Contract was not making every weapon identical.

It was meeting each one differently.

Arthur began to understand the real advantage.

Breadth.

Not infinite power.

Breadth.

He could not become the best swordsman in Ravenscroft overnight.

But if the situation changed, he might not be trapped by the weapon in his hand.

That was valuable.

And potentially terrifying.

Arthur closed the interface.

The Academy still believed he was Rank F.

That had not changed.

His Esper Power was still Motion Sense.

That had not changed.

Nothing about his institutional record had changed.

But privately, something new had entered his life.

It had a name.

Lugh.

And it had rules he did not fully understand.


Arthur returned to the weapons hall the next morning before the first bell.

He had slept badly.

Not because Lugh had frightened him.

Because the Contract had made too much sense.

The Academy measured his Rank. His Power explained his perception. His Classification described the difficulty of fitting him into existing categories.

Lugh did none of those things.

It did not change his Rank.

It did not make Motion Sense stronger.

It did not tell the Academy that Arthur was suddenly exceptional.

It simply gave him another way to interact with a weapon.

Arthur tested the sword again.

He invoked Lugh.

The strange clarity returned.

He moved through a basic sequence.

The improvement was obvious, but so was the limit. When he ignored fundamentals, his footwork still became sloppy.

Lugh did not correct every mistake automatically.

It amplified a capability. It did not replace training.

Arthur changed his grip halfway through the sequence.

The clarity remained, but the movement became less reliable.

He stopped.

That was important.

The Contract had not transformed him into a master of every possible technique. It had created a temporary relationship with the weapon that exceeded his ordinary familiarity.

At the bottom of the interface, another line appeared.

```text
Development:
No confirmed path available.
```

Arthur stared at it.

No confirmed path.

So there could be a path.

But the system would not tell him until something had been discovered.

Arthur smiled despite himself.

The Academy tested students by giving them problems.

Lugh seemed to do the same thing.

Only the Contract did not explain what counted as the correct answer.

Arthur closed the interface.

He made a rule for himself.

Never trust a Contract merely because its first effect is useful.

Test it.

Observe it.

Find the boundary.

And never assume the information displayed is the whole truth.

The first bell rang.

Arthur left the hall.

Behind him, the dagger remained on the rack.

Its blade caught the morning light.

For a moment, the private interface flickered by itself.

Then it disappeared.

Arthur spent the next hour testing the Contract carefully.

He did not attempt anything dramatic.

He changed stance.

Changed grip.

Changed distance.

Repeated the same movement without Lugh, then with it.

The difference was consistent.

His ordinary skill remained his baseline.

Lugh raised the quality of execution above that baseline for a limited period.

That distinction mattered.

If he had no understanding of a weapon at all, the Contract could not simply place knowledge into his head and turn him into a perfect fighter. It was responding to something he had learned.

Arthur tested the spear next.

The weapon state felt different from the dagger.

Longer reach.

Different balance.

Different timing.

He understood the relationship faster than before.

That made him wonder whether the Contract was measuring knowledge, physical familiarity, or something deeper.

He did not know.

He wrote the question in his notebook.

**What exactly does Lugh recognize?**

Under it, he wrote another.

**Why did the dagger trigger it first?**

He underlined the second question twice.

The answers would have to come through testing.

That was acceptable.

Arthur preferred a mystery with rules to a miracle without them.

When he finally left the weapons hall, the Contract was quiet.

No new message.

No explanation.

No promise.

Only the knowledge that something had opened a door for him.

Arthur had no intention of walking through it blindly.

The word **Lugh** remained in his thoughts as he walked back to the dormitory.

Arthur had heard enough history at Ravenscroft to know that names mattered. A name attached to a Contract was unlikely to be decorative.

But he did not yet know what the name meant to the system.

He refused to invent an answer.

That was another rule.

Do not fill an unknown with a convenient explanation.

If Lugh was a Contract of weapon mastery, then he would test weapon mastery.

If the Contract had another rule, the rule would reveal itself through evidence.

Arthur looked at his hand.

The pain had already faded.

That made the warning easier to underestimate.

He knew better.

A cost did not become harmless because it disappeared quickly.

Ten minutes was not an invitation to use all ten.

Two times ordinary capability did not mean two times ordinary safety.

And UNKNOWN did not mean harmless.

Arthur opened his notebook.

Under the name **LUGH**, he wrote:

**Useful. Limited. Incomplete. Test carefully.**

Then he closed it.

That was enough for tonight.

For now, that was enough.

He had a weapon Contract.

He did not yet have mastery of its rules.
He would learn the rest through use.

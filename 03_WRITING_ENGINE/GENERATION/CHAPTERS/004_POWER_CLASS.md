# UNDERSTANDING POWERS

Arthur had expected another test.

Instead, the instructor walked to the front of the classroom and wrote a single letter on the board.

**B**

She turned around.

"Who thinks a Rank B Esper would beat a Rank C Esper?"

Almost every hand went up.

Arthur kept his down.

The instructor noticed.

"Mr. Reed. You disagree?"

"I don't know."

A few students laughed.

"Why not?"

Arthur looked at the letter.

"Because I don't know what either person can do."

The laughter stopped.

The instructor nodded once.

"Good. That's where we start."

She erased the letter and wrote three words.

**RANK. POWER. CLASSIFICATION.**

"These three terms will follow you through the Academy. You need to understand the difference before you start comparing yourselves to one another."

She tapped **RANK**.

"Rank is a measurement. It gives the Academy a common way to describe an Esper's conventional output and overall capacity. It is useful for assessment, training, and administration."

She tapped **POWER**.

"Power is the ability itself. What can you actually do? What changes when you use it? What does it allow you to affect?"

Then **CLASSIFICATION**.

"Classification is the Academy's current way of describing and organizing that ability. Some abilities fit existing categories neatly. Some do not."

She looked around the room.

"Those three things are related. They are not interchangeable."

Arthur wrote that down.

The instructor continued.

"There is another rule you should remember. No Power is a complete solution. Every ability has conditions. Every ability has situations where it is useful, situations where it is less useful, and situations where it can fail."

That sounded more important than the three definitions.

Arthur underlined it.

The instructor picked up a wooden ball.

"You can understand the theory by watching what happens when we apply it."

She placed two blocks on the floor.

"Evelyn Hart."

Evelyn stood.

"Rank C. Vector Shift."

She entered the marked circle.

The instructor rolled the ball toward the first block.

Evelyn raised two fingers.

The ball curved.

It avoided the block and continued toward the second.

The instructor nodded.

"A Power does not need to destroy something to be useful. Sometimes changing a result by a small amount is enough."

Arthur watched the ball.

The instructor rolled another one.

Faster.

Evelyn shifted it again.

The ball changed direction, but not quickly enough.

It struck the wall.

The instructor let the result stand.

"And that is the other half of the theory. A Power does what it does within its own conditions. More force, more speed, greater distance, a different environment—any of these can change the result."

Evelyn glanced at the wall and returned to her seat.

Arthur wrote:

**The ability stays the same. The situation changes what the ability can accomplish.**

"Mara Bennett."

Mara stood with a sigh.

"I knew there was a reason I sat near the back."

"There is no safe seat in this Academy."

"That's disappointing."

The room laughed.

Mara stepped into the circle.

The instructor lifted a weighted block.

"Impact Dampening."

She dropped it.

Mara raised one hand.

The block struck the padded platform with a dull thump.

Mara flexed her fingers afterward.

"Still hurts."

The instructor nodded.

"Another principle. A successful use of a Power does not mean there is no cost to the user."

Arthur added another note.

**Result and cost can exist at the same time.**

The instructor looked toward the next row.

"Iris Vale."

Iris stood.

"Rank B. Afterimage."

She entered the marked area.

The instructor pointed to the opposite side of the room.

"Cross to the other marker."

Iris moved.

Arthur saw her take the first step.

Then his vision betrayed him.

One Iris became two.

Then three.

The figures seemed to move through different parts of the room.

Arthur's eyes tried to follow all of them at once.

He failed.

Iris reached the marker.

The extra images disappeared.

The instructor did not explain what Arthur had just seen.

She only said,

"That was the demonstration."

Arthur looked at the floor.

One set of footsteps had made a different sound.

He wasn't certain what that meant yet.

He wrote one word.

**Misleading.**

Iris returned to her seat without saying anything to him.

The instructor turned back to the board.

"Notice what just happened. We gave you a Rank, a named Power, and a demonstration. You still don't know everything that person can do."

Arthur looked up.

That was the point.

The labels described the ability.

The demonstration showed only one application of it.

The actual boundary would have to be discovered through training and experience.

The instructor continued.

"Do not assume that Rank alone tells you who wins a fight. Do not assume that a rare Power is automatically stronger. And do not assume that knowing a Power's name means you understand how its user will apply it."

Arthur wrote the sentence down.

That was much closer to what he had expected from the Academy.

Not answers.

A framework for asking better questions.

Then the instructor looked toward him.

"Arthur Reed."

Arthur stood.

The whispers began before he reached the centre.

"Rank F."

Someone said it loudly enough for everyone to hear.

Arthur ignored him.

The instructor held a tennis ball between her fingers.

"Tell me when it moves."

Arthur focused.

The ball remained still.

Then something changed.

"Now."

The ball dropped.

She caught it.

"Again."

Her hand moved as if she were going to release it.

Arthur said nothing.

Her fingers closed again.

The ball had never moved.

Arthur waited.

The third attempt came differently.

"Now."

The ball dropped.

The instructor caught it near her knee.

She looked at him.

"What did you notice?"

Arthur considered the question.

"A change."

"In what?"

"Motion."

She nodded.

"That's enough."

She turned toward the board.

"Rank F. Motion Sense. Classification: Irregular."

The word settled over the room.

Arthur felt the same uncomfortable weight he had felt during the assessment.

The instructor did not explain it.

She did not need to.

The word itself was the Academy admitting that the existing description was incomplete.

Arthur wrote it down.

**Irregular = not neatly understood.**

He stared at the sentence for a moment, then crossed out the equals sign.

Too certain.

He changed it.

**Irregular = the Academy does not have a simple answer yet.**

That felt safer.

The instructor looked at the clock.

"One final principle."

The room quieted.

"A Power is a capability. Mastery is what you build through training. Do not confuse the two."

Arthur looked around the room.

Evelyn had demonstrated control, but the faster ball had still beaten her correction.

Mara had reduced an impact, but she had still felt it.

Iris had shown something Arthur could not yet explain.

And Arthur himself had only demonstrated that he could notice movement.

None of them had demonstrated mastery.

Not yet.

The instructor picked up her clipboard.

"Tomorrow, we remove your Powers from the test."

The room erupted.

"What?"

"Seriously?"

"For how long?"

The instructor waited for the noise to settle.

"Speed. Reaction. Balance. Coordination. Strength. Endurance."

She counted them on her fingers.

"No Power."

Arthur looked through the window.

The Academy track stretched beyond the training hall.

For the first time, the lesson felt finished.

Not because he had learned everything.

Because he finally understood what he was supposed to do next.

Observe.

Test.

Train.

Then find out where the theory stopped matching reality.

Arthur closed his notebook.

Tomorrow would begin there.

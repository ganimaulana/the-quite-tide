# APPLIED ESPER COMBAT

The training field looked different when the Academy stopped pretending it was a classroom.

There were no desks.

No demonstration circles.

No instructor standing close enough to intervene immediately.

The field was divided by walls, ruined structures, narrow passages, elevated platforms, and artificial terrain.

Arthur stood with Evelyn, Mara, Owen, and Clarke at the starting line.

An instructor addressed them from the observation platform.

"Your objective is simple."

A marker appeared on the far side of the field.

"Retrieve the Signal Marker."

The marker was inside a sealed structure.

"You have thirty minutes."

The timer began.

The gate opened.

Arthur moved first.

Motion Sense spread through the environment as a stream of changes rather than a map.

Something moved behind the first wall.

Two things.

One high.

One low.

He raised his hand.

"Two training constructs."

Mara lifted her shield.

Evelyn stepped slightly to the side.

"Distance?"

"Twenty metres. Moving toward us."

The first construct emerged.

Arthur saw its attack begin.

He moved.

Mara intercepted.

The impact pushed her backward.

Evelyn redirected the construct's second strike into the wall.

Arthur moved around the opening.

The team passed.

No one celebrated.

They were still inside the first phase.

Owen stopped suddenly.

"Wait."

Arthur turned.

"Flicker."

Owen pointed toward a dark corridor.

"The signal is repeating, but the pattern is wrong."

Arthur watched.

Nothing moved.

He almost dismissed it.

Then he remembered his own lesson.

Motion Sense was information, not interpretation.

He looked again.

"You're right."

Clarke examined the wall.

"There is a maintenance panel."

They opened it.

Inside was a second route.

The visible corridor had been designed to waste time.

The hidden route led closer to the objective.

They moved.

At the next chamber, the Signal Marker was visible through reinforced glass.

The case around it had a mechanical seal.

Clarke crouched.

"This isn't the original seal."

Arthur looked at him.

"Meaning?"

"Someone changed it after the original installation."

Owen looked toward the observation cameras.

"Is that allowed?"

"Probably."

Mara sighed.

"I dislike the word probably."

Clarke traced the edge.

"The replacement has a weakness here."

Evelyn examined it.

"I can move the locking pin."

"Only once," Clarke said. "If it slips, the mechanism resets."

Arthur looked toward the corridor.

Movement.

Fast.

"We have company."

The construct entered.

Mara moved between it and the others.

The first strike landed against her shield.

Her Impact Dampening reduced the force, but the blow still drove her backward.

"Open the seal!"

Evelyn reached for the mechanism.

Arthur stepped forward.

The construct changed direction.

Motion Sense caught the shift.

Arthur raised the training sword.

He could fight it normally.

He could survive for a while.

But the construct was stronger.

His body remembered the heavy student from the combat hall.

Seeing an attack did not guarantee that he could answer it.

Arthur looked at the sword.

Then at the interface.

Lugh was available.

He had tested it.

He knew the cost.

He did not know every consequence.

The construct attacked.

Arthur moved.

The sword caught the first strike.

The impact still forced him backward.

He had another option.

Arthur deliberately activated Lugh.

The change was immediate.

The weapon became an extension of his movement.

Not a miracle.

Not invulnerability.

Precision.

Control.

Timing.

Arthur slipped beneath the next strike and cut across the construct's exposed joint.

The training weapon struck the marked weak point.

The construct staggered.

"Now!"

Evelyn moved the locking pin.

Clarke guided her hand.

The seal released.

The Signal Marker came free.

The timer stopped.

**OBJECTIVE COMPLETE.**

For a second, nobody moved.

Then Arthur felt pain in his wrist.

He looked down.

The Lugh state was still active.

The timer on the private interface showed that several minutes remained.

But the pain had started early.

Arthur deactivated it.

The strange weapon clarity faded.

Mara lowered her shield.

"You all right?"

"Yes."

She looked at his hand.

"That didn't sound convincing."

Arthur flexed his fingers.

"It is."

The observation platform opened.

The instructor descended.

She looked at the marker first.

Then at Mara's shield.

Then at Evelyn.

Owen.

Clarke.

Finally, Arthur.

"Explain your decision."

Arthur knew exactly what she meant.

"Normal technique wasn't enough to stop the construct quickly. The objective mattered more than winning the fight. I used an available option to create an opening."

The instructor nodded.

"And the cost?"

Arthur paused.

"Manageable."

"For now."

Arthur said nothing.

The instructor looked at the group.

"This is what applied combat means. Your Powers did not solve the exercise. Your bodies did not solve it. Your weapons did not solve it. Your Contract did not solve it."

She pointed at the marker.

"You solved it by combining what each layer could actually do."

Arthur looked at his hand.

Motion Sense had told him where the danger was.

Training had allowed him to move.

The sword had given him a method.

Lugh had temporarily raised that method beyond his ordinary level.

Mara had bought him time.

Evelyn had changed the geometry of the fight.

Owen had noticed the false signal.

Clarke had understood the lock.

None of them had been enough alone.

Together, they had been.

The instructor dismissed them.

The five students began walking back toward the Academy.

Arthur fell slightly behind.

He opened the private interface.

Lugh remained listed.

But beneath the Contract name, a new line had appeared.

```text
CONTRACT OBSERVATION

Weapon State: SWORD

Integration remains PARTIAL.

Further interaction may reveal additional conditions.
```

Arthur stopped walking.

Additional conditions.

He had expected the Contract to become clearer through use.

He had not expected the system to acknowledge that it was still watching.

Then another line appeared.

```text
NEW CONDITION DETECTED

Weapon compatibility is not limited to conventional forms.

Requirement for confirmation:
UNKNOWN
```

Arthur stared at the message.

He looked toward the training field.

Then toward the ordinary objects carried by the students around him.

A pen.

A belt.

A metal coin.

A piece of broken wood.

His eyes narrowed.

Lugh had not given him an answer.

It had given him a question.

And for the first time, Arthur understood what made the Contract dangerous.

It was not simply a source of power.

It was a set of rules waiting to be understood.

He closed the interface.

"Arthur?"

Iris's voice came from ahead.

He looked up.

The others had stopped.

"Are you coming?"

Arthur looked once more at his hand.

Then he smiled faintly.

"Yeah."

He caught up with them.

Behind him, the training field remained silent.

But somewhere inside Arthur's private interface, the word **UNKNOWN** remained.

And this time, he wanted to know what it meant.


The five reached the Academy corridor.

Mara was the first to speak.

"You could have told us about that Contract."

Arthur stopped.

Evelyn looked at him.

Owen looked at him.

Even Clarke closed the notebook he had been carrying.

Arthur's stomach tightened.

"What makes you think I have a Contract?"

Mara pointed at his hand.

"You moved differently."

Evelyn nodded.

"Not like a normal improvement."

Owen added, "The field sensors registered a change in your movement pattern."

Arthur glanced at Clarke.

"And you?"

Clarke shrugged.

"I don't know what you have. I know you hid it."

Silence.

Arthur had assumed the private interface meant absolute secrecy.

He realized his mistake.

The interface was private.

The consequences were not.

If he used something in front of people, they could still observe what happened.

Mara lowered her voice.

"We're not asking you to explain everything."

Evelyn looked toward the empty corridor.

"But if we are going to work together, don't let a hidden limitation become our problem without warning."

Arthur considered that.

It was fair.

"I don't know everything about it either."

Owen frowned.

"Then what do you know?"

Arthur answered honestly.

"It has rules. It has a cost. And I don't know all of the consequences yet."

Nobody laughed.

Mara nodded.

"That's enough for now."

They continued walking.

Arthur looked back at the training field.

The instructors had already reset the constructs.

The place looked ordinary again.

But the exercise had changed something.

Not just the team.

Arthur's understanding of himself.

Motion Sense was no longer the whole answer.

Lugh was not the whole answer either.

His body was still limited.

His Rank was still F.

His Academy record was still strange.

And the private system had just shown him a rule it had never mentioned before.

**Weapon compatibility is not limited to conventional forms.**

Arthur thought about the pen in his pocket.

Then the coin.

Then the broken piece of wood on the training field.

If Lugh's definition of a weapon depended on learned competence rather than shape, the possibilities were much wider than he had assumed.

But the interface had also said something more important.

**Requirement for confirmation: UNKNOWN.**

Arthur smiled.

That was a problem for another day.

He had survived his first real test.

He had not mastered Lugh.

He had not mastered combat.

He had barely begun.

But he now had five things he could trust more than a Rank.

His own observation.

His own training.

His own caution.

Four people who had fought beside him.

And a Contract whose rules were still waiting to be discovered.

Arthur walked toward the dormitory.

Behind the closed door of the training field, something moved.

Arthur did not see it.

The private interface did not react.

And that was the first thing he should have learned from Motion Sense.

Not everything important moved.

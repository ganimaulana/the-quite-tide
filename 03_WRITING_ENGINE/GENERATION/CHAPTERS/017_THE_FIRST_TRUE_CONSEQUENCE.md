# TWELVE PLACES

Arthur had counted the hollows before.

He had simply never known what question they were waiting for.

The stone sat beneath a reading lamp in the empty annex.

Twelve hollows.

Eleven pale.

One darker.

He drew the lens closer.

The temptation was immediate: twelve hollows meant twelve Contracts.

Arthur wrote the sentence.

Then crossed it out.

No.

Twelve hollows meant twelve hollows.

That was the evidence.

He inspected each one.

Depth.

Edge.

Surface.

Colour.

The first eleven were smooth and shallow.

The twelfth was different.

Its bottom carried a darker residue that did not match the others.

Arthur checked the notes from the binding.

He checked the date.

He checked the first invocation.

He checked the mark.

The sequence aligned.

Not perfectly.

Enough to justify one conclusion.

The stone recorded something when the Contract formed.

It did not prove that the stone created the Contract.

Arthur wrote that distinction twice.

Then he stopped.

The Academy had taught him to classify people.

Rank.

Division.

Ability.

Classification.

The stone did something stranger.

It kept a physical record of a system that the Academy could not see.

That reminded him of something he had noticed during weapons training.

A weapon could be described by weight, length, balance and material.

None of those descriptions explained why one person used it well.

The description was not the capability.

The record was not the thing.

Arthur looked at the twelve hollows.

He wondered who had made the first mistake of assuming otherwise.

---

The library gave him the next piece.

His access card was not special.

It was simply useful.

That made it valuable.

He requested the older holdings for carved-stone appraisals from 1987.

The clerk returned with a box.

Arthur worked through it without looking for a miracle.

Dates first.

Names second.

Object descriptions third.

Cross-references last.

Near the bottom of the third folder, he found the word.

USELESS.

The appraisal concerned carved stones.

The date matched his family record.

Arthur read it twice.

The Academy copy had been made from an older document.

The next page was a duplicate.

Almost.

Someone had blacked out the signature line.

Not a correction.

An erasure.

Arthur held the page under the light.

The paper showed a shallow impression beneath the ink.

A name had once been there.

He could not recover it.

He did not try to invent one.

He copied only what he could verify.

1987.

Carved stones.

Verdict: useless.

Signature line removed in Academy copy.

Then he added:

Relationship to current artifact: not established.

That sentence was the most important one on the page.

He wanted the connection.

He did not yet have it.

The difference mattered.

---

That night, Arthur returned to the stone.

He placed the library copy beside it.

The two documents did not prove the same thing.

They did not even describe the same object.

But they shared a detail that was difficult to dismiss.

Someone had taken care to erase part of the old record.

And someone had cared enough about the stone to preserve twelve physical places in it.

Arthur touched the edge of the artifact.

"Who had you before me?"

The stone gave him nothing.

He almost laughed.

He had asked a question of an object that had never promised to answer.

He opened the Contract interface.

CONTRACT CAPACITY

Active Contracts: 1

He stared at the line.

The interface did not show twelve active Contracts.

It did not need to.

The artifact had twelve physical markers.

The system had one active Contract.

Those were two different facts.

He wrote them separately.

Physical markers: twelve.

Active Contract occupancy: one.

Then he added:

Meaning of remaining markers: unknown.

That was enough.

He closed the interface.

For once, the unknown did not feel like a failure.

It felt like fair play.

The library had given him a document.

The artifact had given him a physical change.

Neither had given him the answer.

He had to earn that part.

Arthur put the redacted appraisal back into its sleeve.

Before leaving, he wrote one final line:

The artifact records something the Academy has not yet explained.

That one stayed.

When he returned the stone to its case, the darker hollow caught the light.

Twelve places.

One occupied.

And somewhere behind the erased line of a thirty-seven-year-old appraisal was a name he did not have.

Arthur closed the case.

The question could wait.

The record could not.

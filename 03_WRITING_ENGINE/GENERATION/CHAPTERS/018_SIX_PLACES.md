# THE SECOND INVESTIGATION

The second investigation began with Arthur refusing to use the Contract.

That was the first difference.

The phenomenon had already cost him once.

He would not spend another answer merely because the interface could provide one.

The mixed-mission roster was posted at eight.

MEDICAL — ONE.

LOGISTICS — ONE.

TACTICAL — ONE.

COMBAT — ONE.

OPERATIONS SUPPORT — ONE.

The final slot belonged to Arthur.

The Tactical cadet looked at the roster, then at him.

"You are Reed?"

"Yes."

"I read your correction."

Arthur paused.

"My correction?"

"The one from the previous file."

The formal Combat lead closed the briefing folder.

"We are here to investigate an object, not debate a clerk's theory."

Arthur said nothing.

The Tactical cadet did.

"Then we investigate both."

The Combat lead looked annoyed.

"Fine. Reed supplies the records. Tactical evaluates the procedure."

Arthur nodded.

That was enough.

He had not been given command.

He had been given a place.

He intended to use it.

---

The object was a small brass thimble.

It sat in a numbered returns tray inside a controlled annex.

The first intake record placed it in Tray Four.

Later records placed it elsewhere.

Every time, it returned to Tray Four.

Arthur had already seen that pattern.

This time, he wanted witnesses.

Logistics photographed the tray.

Medical recorded the room conditions.

Tactical sealed the custody sheet.

Combat stood at the door.

Arthur wrote the prediction before the test.

If the return follows the current handler, changing the handler will change the return location.

He signed it.

The Tactical cadet read it.

"You are willing to let the page prove you wrong."

"That is what the page is for."

The cadet nodded.

They moved the thimble to Tray One.

A new custodian handled it.

The transfer was witnessed.

The room was sealed.

When they returned, the thimble was in Tray Four.

The Combat lead stepped forward.

Arthur raised one hand.

"Do not touch it."

The lead stopped.

Arthur looked at the camera.

The thimble had disappeared from Tray One and reappeared in Tray Four.

The camera did not show the movement.

That was not enough.

One unexplained transition could still be a failure of the instrument.

So they repeated the test.

New handler.

New destination.

Same original intake.

Again, the thimble returned to Tray Four.

Arthur looked at his prediction.

It was wrong.

He crossed it out.

Then wrote the corrected version beneath it.

The return is tied to the first recorded intake location, not the current custodian.

The Tactical cadet read it.

"What changed?"

"The Rule."

"No. Your model changed."

Arthur looked at them.

"Yes."

The formal lead gave a short laugh.

"That is a very convenient distinction."

"It is the distinction that matters."

The Medical cadet glanced at the mark on Arthur's hand.

"You learned that recently?"

Arthur pulled his sleeve lower.

"Recently enough."

No one asked again.

---

They performed one final test.

The original intake record remained Tray Four.

The thimble was transferred to Tray Two.

Different handler.

Different destination.

Same record.

They waited.

The thimble returned to Tray Four.

This time, Arthur did not feel the temptation to call the result obvious.

He wrote the observation.

Then the interpretation.

Then the limits.

The Rule was strong enough to use.

It was not strong enough to explain why the object had been in Tray Four before the first recorded intake.

That question remained.

The Tactical cadet read the final page.

"You have a second problem."

Arthur looked up.

"The object was already here."

"Yes."

"Before the first record."

"Yes."

"Then the first recorded intake is not the beginning."

Arthur stared at the thimble.

"No."

The formal lead frowned.

"That is speculation."

Arthur shook his head.

"No. The existence of an earlier state is supported by the record. The cause is speculation."

The Tactical cadet smiled.

"Good."

Arthur looked at them.

"Good?"

"You corrected yourself before I had to."

It was the closest thing to praise he had received all day.

He did not know what to do with it.

---

Back at the Academy, the mission was technically complete.

The report would state:

The phenomenon returns to its first recorded intake location despite later custody transfers.

The formal lead signed it.

The Tactical cadet signed beneath.

Arthur added an appendix.

Not a conclusion.

A lead.

The original storage box had entered the records block years earlier from an older storage location.

The manifest contained a cross-reference.

A docket number.

A dormant file.

T3-ADJACENT SEEP — DORMANT.

Arthur read the line twice.

The status was institutional.

It was not proof.

The memorandum attached to the docket contained one sentence that caught his attention:

Retain the original intake record when testing return behaviour.

A previous holder had written it.

No name.

No explanation.

Just the method.

Arthur closed the file.

He had not solved the dormant case.

He had not even proved that the previous holder had been connected to the thimble.

But the lead existed.

The Tactical cadet looked at him.

"You want to follow it."

"Eventually."

"Why not now?"

"Because the mission is over."

The cadet nodded.

For the first time, the distinction between curiosity and procedure felt useful.

Arthur filed the lead.

Then he looked at the sealed thimble.

The Contract remained untouched.

That mattered.

The second phenomenon had been solved without it.

The first lesson after his failure was therefore simple.

A Contract could confirm a condition.

It could not replace a method.

And if Arthur wanted to become useful, he would need both.

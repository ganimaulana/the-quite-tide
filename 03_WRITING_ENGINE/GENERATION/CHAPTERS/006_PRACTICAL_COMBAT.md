# POWER IN COMBAT

The combat hall was louder than the Power classroom.

Wood struck padded guards. Shoes scraped across the floor. Somewhere behind the dividing screens, someone shouted after taking a hit.

Arthur stood inside one of the marked circles with a wooden sword in his hand.

Across from him stood Iris Vale.

They had barely spoken since the first Power class.

Arthur preferred it that way.

If he wanted to understand her Power, he would rather find out by himself.

The instructor stepped between them.

"Three rules. No live weapons. No intentional strikes to the head. Reach the marked zone behind your opponent to score."

She pointed to the blue line behind Iris.

"You don't need to defeat your opponent."

She stepped away.

"Begin."

Iris moved.

Arthur reacted immediately.

Motion Sense caught the change.

Weight shifted.

Direction changed.

Acceleration followed.

His body started moving before his eyes had finished processing what happened.

Then his vision split.

One Iris became two.

Arthur chose the nearer movement.

Wrong.

The wooden sword struck his shoulder.

"Point."

Iris stepped back.

Arthur reset.

The information had been there.

He had still followed the wrong one.

The instructor gave no explanation.

"Again."

Iris moved.

This time Arthur waited.

He ignored the first visual trace.

Motion Sense gave him another change.

He turned toward it.

Iris changed direction again.

Arthur followed.

Too late.

The wooden sword struck his ribs.

"Point."

Arthur stepped back.

His first mistake had been trusting his eyes.

His second had been assuming that refusing to trust his eyes was enough.

He watched Iris return to position.

She had not said anything.

Good.

He didn't want an answer.

He wanted a pattern.

"Again."

The third exchange began.

Arthur stopped trying to identify Iris.

He watched the floor.

One foot pressed down.

The body shifted.

Something else crossed his vision without producing the same physical change.

Arthur moved.

Iris changed direction.

He followed the transfer of weight instead of the picture his eyes were giving him.

She accelerated toward the marked zone.

Arthur cut across the shortest path.

The wooden sword moved into the space she needed.

Iris stopped before crossing the line.

Arthur stepped over it.

"Point, Reed."

Arthur lowered his sword.

He had not solved it completely.

But he had found something.

The images could occupy a space. They could show movement. But only one of them had forced the floor to answer.

That difference was small.

Small was enough.

The next exercise began before he could think further.

The instructor paired him with a heavier student named Marcus.

Marcus stepped into the circle and raised his wooden weapon.

"Ready?" Marcus asked.

Arthur adjusted his grip.

"No."

Marcus blinked.

"Good answer."

No strange Power.

No visual tricks.

Just a larger body and a longer reach.

"Begin."

Marcus moved.

Arthur felt the change before the strike came.

Shoulder.

Hip.

Weight.

He knew where the attack was going.

He stepped away.

Marcus recovered faster than Arthur expected.

The next movement came almost immediately.

Arthur saw it.

He understood it.

His body was simply too slow.

The padded weapon struck his ribs.

The impact knocked the air from his lungs.

Arthur stumbled outside the circle.

"Stop."

He lowered the sword and breathed through the pain.

Nobody explained what had gone wrong.

He didn't need them to.

The answer was already in front of him.

He had known.

He had still been hit.

Arthur looked at his feet.

Motion Sense had given him an earlier warning.

It had not moved his legs for him.

That distinction suddenly felt much more important than it had yesterday.

He had spent the first two days thinking about what his Power could detect.

He had not spent enough time thinking about the gap between detection and action.

He tested that idea again before the next rotation.

He stood at the edge of the mat and watched a pair of students spar.

One of them moved quickly enough that the first step was almost invisible to the eye. Arthur noticed it anyway. His attention caught the shift of weight, the angle of the hip, the change in the opponent's centre of balance.

But the student defending against it had already committed to the wrong direction.

The attack landed.

Arthur frowned.

The defender had not lacked information. He had received enough information to react.

He had simply reacted badly.

The next exchange proved it again.

This time the defender waited a fraction longer. The attacker changed direction. The defender followed and escaped the strike.

Arthur looked down at his own hands.

That fraction mattered.

Motion Sense might give him the same fraction. But if he wasted it by panicking, choosing badly, or moving too slowly, the advantage disappeared.

He wrote another note beneath the first.

**More information does not mean fewer decisions.**

If anything, it meant the opposite.

The more Arthur could perceive, the more carefully he would have to decide what deserved his attention.

He tested the idea during the next exchange.

Marcus stepped forward.

Arthur moved earlier.

Not much earlier.

Enough.

The weapon missed by a few centimetres.

Arthur countered.

Marcus blocked.

Arthur changed direction.

The larger student recovered and forced him back again.

Arthur lost the exchange.

But this time he understood why.

His Power had not failed.

His timing had.

The difference mattered.

Marcus lowered his weapon.

"You're quick to notice things."

Arthur caught his breath.

"You're quick to hit them."

Marcus laughed.

"Fair."

He stepped back into position.

Arthur tried to picture the same exchange without Motion Sense.

He would have noticed Marcus moving only after the movement became obvious. With Motion Sense, the warning arrived earlier.

That meant the Power was not useless.

It meant the advantage was smaller and more practical than he had imagined.

It gave him a chance.

Whether he could turn that chance into distance, a block, a counter, or an escape was still up to him.

For the first time, Arthur stopped thinking of Motion Sense as something that should win fights for him.

It was a tool.

The value of the tool depended on the person holding it.

Later, when the class moved to the next station, Arthur sat at the edge of the mat and watched.

He stopped taking notes about other people's Powers.

Instead, he watched what happened between perception and movement.

A student with a strong attack still missed when he committed too early.

A defensive student still lost when he allowed himself to be pushed out of the objective area.

A faster student still made mistakes when he changed direction without balance.

The same pattern appeared again and again.

Having an advantage was not the same as using it well.

Arthur opened his notebook.

He wrote:

**See. Decide. Act.**

He stared at the words.

Motion Sense helped with the first.

The second belonged to him.

The third depended on his body.

That was the gap.

Not between Power and no Power.

Between knowing and doing.

Across the hall, Iris entered another practice circle.

She moved.

Her afterimages appeared.

Her opponent reacted to one of them.

Wrong.

Iris crossed behind him and touched the objective marker.

Arthur watched the floor.

Again, the same detail.

The real body had to obey balance, weight, contact, and distance.

The false image did not.

He still did not know everything about Afterimage.

He didn't need to.

He only needed to know enough to survive the next encounter.

The instructor called the class together.

"Weapons selection is next."

Students began putting away the training weapons.

Arthur looked at the wooden sword in his hand.

He had learned two things today.

First, Motion Sense was useful.

Second, usefulness had nothing to do with winning by itself.

If he could detect a movement half a second early but could not move in time, the advantage was wasted.

If he could read an attack but chose the wrong response, the information became another way to make a mistake.

And if he chose a weapon that required more speed or strength than he possessed, the problem would only become worse.

Arthur looked toward the weapons hall.

That was the next problem.

He would have to find something that fit the way he actually fought.

He considered the sword in his hand.

It had reach.

It also required him to commit his arms and shoulders to every attack. Against Marcus, that commitment had felt slow. Against Iris, chasing a target with a longer weapon had nearly made him chase the wrong movement again.

Arthur did not know yet whether the sword was wrong for him.

He only knew that he needed to test it properly.

That was enough.

Not the Rank on his assessment.

Not what other people expected.

What he could make work.

Arthur followed the others toward the weapons hall.

Behind him, the combat hall filled with the sound of another match beginning.

He didn't look back.

For the first time since arriving at Ravenscroft, he had a problem he could solve through practice rather than explanation.

And that was exactly the kind of problem he preferred.

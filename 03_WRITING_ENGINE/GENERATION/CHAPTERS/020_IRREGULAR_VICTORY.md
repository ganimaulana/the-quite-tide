# QUIET AFTER THE WIN

The congratulations were worse than the exercise.

Arthur learned that before breakfast.

A Combat cadet passed him in the corridor.

"Good work yesterday."

The words sounded friendly.

The tone was not.

"You were useful."

Arthur nodded.

"Thank you."

The cadet smiled.

"Don't get used to it."

Then he left.

Arthur continued walking.

Simon joined him.

"That was almost affectionate."

"Was it?"

"No. It was worse."

Arthur glanced at him.

Simon shrugged.

"People are very creative when they want to congratulate someone without admitting they were impressed."

Arthur almost smiled.

Almost.

---

The cafeteria was louder than usual.

The exercise had spread through the cohort.

Not the details.

The result.

Irregular cadets had performed well.

Again.

That was the dangerous part.

One success could be dismissed.

Two became a pattern.

Arthur sat with Owen, Simon, Daniel and Iris.

Daniel looked uncomfortable.

"I don't like this."

"What?"

"The attention."

Simon lifted his cup.

"Welcome to being almost respected."

Iris laughed.

Owen looked at Arthur.

"They are going to put us in another exercise."

"Probably."

"Does that bother you?"

Arthur considered it.

"No."

Simon raised an eyebrow.

"Why?"

"Because the next exercise will reveal whether yesterday was useful or accidental."

Daniel nodded.

"That is the correct answer."

Across the room, a Combat student looked over.

Then another.

Arthur noticed.

He did not react.

He had learned that being watched did not require a response.

---

Pram arrived ten minutes later.

He stopped beside the table.

Arthur looked up.

"Pram."

"Reed."

Pram's eyes moved across the group.

Then to Simon.

Then Owen.

Then back to Arthur.

"I heard about the exercise."

Arthur waited.

"You filed the method."

"Yes."

"Of course you did."

Simon took another drink.

Pram's expression tightened.

"You think paperwork makes you important."

Arthur shook his head.

"No."

"Then what does?"

Arthur looked at the table.

"Useful work."

Pram laughed once.

"Paperwork isn't power."

Arthur looked at him.

"I know."

That answer seemed to annoy him more than an argument would have.

Pram leaned closer.

"You keep winning without fighting. Eventually someone will decide that is not a coincidence."

Arthur said nothing.

Pram straightened.

"I intend to be that someone."

Then he left.

Owen watched him go.

"Was that a threat?"

Simon considered it.

"No."

"What was it?"

Simon looked at Arthur.

"A promise."

Arthur did not answer.

---

Later that afternoon, the instructor returned the exercise reports.

Arthur received his copy.

At the bottom, beneath the grade, was a second note.

APTITUDE MAPPING — EFFECTIVE.

He read it once.

Then again.

The skill had acquired a name.

That made it more dangerous.

A technique could be taught.

A technique could be evaluated.

A technique could be requested.

And if the Academy began requesting it, Arthur would no longer control where it took him.

He folded the paper.

Across the room, Simon was arguing with a clerk over a document that had been filed in the wrong cabinet.

Arthur watched him for a moment.

Simon noticed.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You are staring."

"I was thinking."

"That's usually when trouble starts."

Arthur smiled.

"You were useful yesterday."

Simon blinked.

Then looked away.

"I know."

A moment later, he added quietly:

"Thanks for saying it."

Arthur understood that this was the real result of the exercise.

Not the board.

Not the instructor's note.

Not Pram.

Trust.

Small enough that nobody else would notice.

Real enough that Arthur did.

---

At the end of the day, a revised roster appeared on the Academy board.

NEXT MIXED EXERCISE — IRREGULAR REPRESENTATION REQUESTED.

Underneath, the request had been filed by the Combat division.

Arthur read it twice.

The roster had not yet been assigned.

The reason was written in formal language.

TO PROVIDE A MORE APPROPRIATE PERFORMANCE BENCHMARK.

Simon read over his shoulder.

"That is a polite way of saying they want another chance."

Arthur folded the notice.

"Then they can have one."

Owen looked at him.

"Are you worried?"

Arthur looked at the board.

"No."

He paused.

"Interested."

The distinction mattered.

Pram had not become an ally.

Hayes had not changed his opinion.

The Academy had not changed Arthur's Rank.

The Contract had not grown stronger.

But the room around him had changed.

Yesterday, people had called the Irregular cohort a problem.

Today, they were asking for another match.

Arthur walked away from the board.

Behind him, Simon stayed for another minute, reading the request.

Then he followed.

The rivalry had begun.

And this time, it had a name.

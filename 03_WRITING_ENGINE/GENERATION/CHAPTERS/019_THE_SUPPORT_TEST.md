# IRREGULAR VICTORY

The exercise was supposed to be routine.

That was why Arthur distrusted it.

Five cadets stood at the edge of the east training lane while an instructor explained the objective.

"Retrieve the marker. Complete the route. File your method."

No tournament.

No bracket.

No audience.

Just a graded field exercise.

Arthur looked at the team.

Simon Clarke was holding the assignment sheet.

Owen Carter had already opened his flicker-log.

Daniel Foster stood with his shield lowered, waiting.

Iris Bennett rolled one shoulder and watched the lane.

Arthur was the fifth.

"Who leads?" Daniel asked.

The instructor answered before anyone else.

"Formal command belongs to the designated Combat cadet."

Daniel looked at Arthur.

Arthur shrugged.

That was fine.

The team did not need a hero.

It needed a method.

---

The first obstacle was a moving barrier.

Daniel could break it.

Arthur told him not to.

The second was a false signal.

Owen caught it.

The third was a sealed equipment case.

Simon crouched beside it.

"That is not the original lock."

Iris looked at him.

"How can you tell?"

Simon tapped the paper tag.

"The ink is newer than the seal."

Arthur understood immediately.

The exercise had been assembled from several layers.

The team had to identify which layer mattered.

They moved.

Daniel protected the route.

Iris redirected a training projectile.

Owen watched for flickers.

Simon read the documentation.

Arthur mapped their abilities against the problem.

It felt less like combat than solving a machine.

That was the part Arthur liked.

Every ability had a condition.

Every condition created a useful range.

Every useful range had a blind spot.

He did not need everyone to be strong.

He needed to stop asking them to do things they were badly suited for.

---

Then Owen stopped.

"That is wrong."

Arthur looked toward him.

"What?"

"The signal."

A low pulse sounded from the east lane.

The instructor had said the exercise was entirely simulated.

Owen's face had gone pale.

"It isn't part of the exercise."

Daniel raised his shield.

"How sure?"

"I don't know."

That answer mattered.

Arthur looked at the ground.

The training marker was still where it should be.

The barrier was still moving.

The false signal was still repeating.

But something had changed beyond the marked lane.

Grass bent in a straight line.

Not from wind.

From movement.

Arthur raised his hand.

"Everyone stop."

The team stopped.

The instructor on the observation platform was already moving toward the radio.

The exercise had become something else.

Not because the rules changed.

Because the world outside the rules had entered the exercise.

Arthur looked at the team.

"Daniel, protect the group."

Daniel nodded.

"Simon, tell me what changed."

Simon was already reading the equipment labels.

"Owen?"

"Flicker is stronger near the marker."

"Iris?"

"I can redirect the training projectiles, but not whatever is outside the lane."

"Good."

Arthur looked at the grass.

"We do not chase it."

The movement came closer.

Then stopped.

Owen pointed.

"There."

Arthur saw it.

A small object had crossed the boundary.

A piece of old survey equipment.

Not dangerous by itself.

But the training sensor had begun reacting to it.

The instructor called from the platform.

"Do not touch it."

Arthur did not.

Instead, he asked Simon to read the equipment record.

Simon held the tag.

"Wrong storage code."

Arthur understood.

The marker was not the anomaly.

The training equipment had been placed beside something that belonged to a different exercise set.

Owen's flicker had caught the mismatch.

Daniel had the physical control to keep everyone back.

Iris could redirect the automated response.

Simon could identify the documentation error.

Arthur had seen how the pieces fit.

"Move the sensor, not the object."

The instructor hesitated.

Then gave the order.

The team changed the sensor position.

The flicker dropped.

The moving barrier settled.

The exercise lane returned to normal.

Nobody had defeated anything.

They had corrected the conditions.

---

Back in the classroom, the instructor read the method log.

She read it twice.

"Why did you move the sensor?"

Arthur answered.

"Because the object was not behaving like the source. The sensor was reacting to its presence."

"How did you know?"

"Owen's flicker-log gave us the timing. Simon identified the equipment mismatch. Daniel kept the group outside the unsafe area. Iris controlled the automated response."

The instructor looked at each of them.

"Who solved it?"

Arthur glanced at the team.

"The team."

"That is not what I asked."

Arthur considered the wording.

"Then the method solved it."

The instructor smiled faintly.

"Better."

She held up the report.

"Your aptitude map is unusually precise."

Arthur said nothing.

"You did not tell people what to do because you wanted to lead them."

"No."

"You told them what they were already good at."

Arthur nodded.

"That was faster."

The instructor looked down at the report again.

"That is a skill."

Arthur almost disagreed.

Then he stopped.

Maybe it was.

Not power.

Not Rank.

Not Contract.

A way of seeing where people fit.

---

The result board changed that evening.

The stamped cadets had moved upward.

Not first.

Not by much.

But enough.

Owen stared at the board.

"We won."

Simon corrected him.

"We completed the objective."

Daniel looked at them.

"That is what winning means in an exercise."

Iris laughed.

Arthur did not.

He was looking at the instructor's handwritten note beneath the score.

METHOD LOG: UNUSUALLY COMPLETE.

That was not praise.

It was attention.

And attention was never free.

Arthur folded the copy and put it away.

The Contract had not been used.

That mattered.

The victory belonged to the team.

The method belonged to all of them.

For the first time, the Academy had seen something Arthur could do that had nothing to do with what his Rank said.

It was not enough to change his classification.

It was enough to change how one instructor looked at him.

Tomorrow, someone else would look too.

# THE FIRST FAILURE

The first practical lesson began with a question Arthur did not like.

"What do you think your ability does?" the instructor asked.

Arthur stood on the marked floor with a padded baton in his hand. Across from him, Iris Vale waited behind the opposite line.

"It detects movement," Arthur said.

"That is the description. I asked what it does."

Arthur glanced at Iris.

"It gives me information before movement becomes obvious."

"And what does it not give you?"

"Intent."

"Good."

The instructor pointed at Iris.

"And her Power?"

"Afterimage."

"Then remember this: Motion Sense is not prediction. Afterimage is not teleportation. Neither of you gets to ignore fundamentals."

Iris smiled.

"Ready?"

Arthur raised the baton.

"Ready."

The instructor raised one hand.

"Begin."

Iris moved.

Arthur felt the change immediately.

Shoulder.

Hip.

Foot.

Then there were two movement traces.

Arthur committed to the first.

The baton struck his ribs.

He stumbled.

Iris stopped behind him.

"That one was real," she said.

Arthur turned.

"I know."

"Then why did you block the other one?"

He looked at the floor.

"Because I treated information as an answer."

The instructor nodded.

"Exactly. Your Power did not fail. Your decision did."

Arthur reset.

"Again."

This time he waited.

Iris moved left.

Two traces appeared.

Arthur did not chase either.

The real movement accelerated.

He reacted late.

The baton stopped an inch from his shoulder.

"Better," Iris said.

"Still late."

"Yes."

The instructor stopped them.

"That is the lesson. Motion Sense can tell you that something has started. It cannot tell you when you should commit. And Iris can give you multiple movement traces without giving you multiple bodies."

Arthur nodded.

"Then I need technique."

"You need your body to become as reliable as your perception."

Arthur looked toward the weapon rack.

The instructor followed his eyes.

"Weapon familiarization is later."

"I know."

"Good. Fail here first."

Iris laughed.

Arthur almost smiled.

He had expected his first combat lesson to prove whether Motion Sense was useful.

Instead, it had proved something more important.

The Power was useful.

He was not.

Not yet.

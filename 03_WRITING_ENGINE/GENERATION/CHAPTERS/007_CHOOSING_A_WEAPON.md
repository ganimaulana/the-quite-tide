# WEAPON COMPATIBILITY

The weapons hall contained more choices than Arthur expected.

Sword.

Dagger.

Spear.

Axe.

Bow.

Staff.

Training shields occupied an entire rack along one wall.

The instructor stood in front of them with her arms folded.

"You are not choosing a weapon for life," she said. "You are choosing the first weapon you will learn properly."

That distinction immediately made the room less intimidating.

Evelyn picked up a short sword.

"Vector Shift benefits from a weapon that already has momentum."

The instructor nodded.

"Reasonable."

Mara selected a shield and short sword.

"Impact Dampening needs me close enough to protect someone."

She raised the shield.

"And preferably alive enough to do it."

Iris walked directly to the sword rack.

She chose one without testing anything else.

Arthur watched her.

"You already knew?"

"I knew before I walked in."

"Why?"

Iris looked at the sword in her hand.

"Because it does what I need."

That answer was simple enough to be irritating.

Owen chose a staff.

Clarke stood in front of the racks for almost a minute.

"Does Document Reading suggest one?" Arthur asked.

"No."

"Then why are you still looking?"

"Because I was hoping it would change its mind."

The instructor finally handed him a basic short sword.

"Learn the fundamentals first."

Clarke accepted it as if he had been handed an unwanted legal document.

Arthur moved to the dagger rack.

The first thing he noticed was the size.

Small.

Light.

Close.

The second thing was how little movement it required.

He rotated the handle in his palm.

His wrist adjusted naturally.

Arthur made a short forward motion.

The dagger stopped exactly where he wanted.

He tried again.

Fast correction.

Small commitment.

Useful at close range.

Then he tested the spear.

The difference was immediate.

Distance became part of every decision.

The shaft changed the geometry of the fight.

Arthur could control space without entering it.

But when he stepped too close, the weapon became awkward.

He tried the axe.

Its weight carried momentum through every movement.

That was useful when correct.

Punishing when wrong.

He understood why the instructors had warned them about commitment.

The bow was another problem entirely.

The weapon demanded timing he had not learned.

Motion Sense could tell him how a target moved.

It could not teach his fingers how to release a string correctly.

He returned to the sword.

The balance was familiar.

The range was manageable.

The fundamentals were obvious enough to practice repeatedly.

Arthur made a slow cut.

His wrist turned too early.

The blade drifted.

He corrected.

Again.

Better.

Again.

Better still.

The sword made his movement easy to read.

That mattered.

Motion Sense was strongest when Arthur understood what his body was doing at the same time as he understood what other bodies were doing.

The instructor walked past.

"Sword?"

Arthur looked at the weapon.

"Maybe."

"That means no."

"It means I'm testing."

The instructor continued walking.

Iris glanced over.

"Dagger."

Arthur looked at her.

"You sound certain."

"You keep returning to it."

Arthur had not noticed that.

He put the sword down.

The dagger was still on the table.

He picked it up.

The moment his fingers closed around the handle, the private interface appeared.

Arthur froze.

The text was clean and familiar.

```text
CONTRACT SYSTEM

Natural Ability
Motion Sense: ACTIVE

Contract Status:
NO ACTIVE CONTRACT
```

Arthur stared at the final line.

Nothing happened.

He lowered the dagger.

The interface disappeared.

He picked up the sword.

Nothing.

He put it down.

He picked up the dagger again.

For a moment, his hand felt too familiar with it.

A memory flashed.

Ravenhurst.

A knife.

A monster.

Blood.

His own hand moving before thought.

The memory vanished.

The interface returned.

```text
CONTRACT CONDITION DETECTED

Weapon Compatibility: CONFIRMED

Further information unavailable.
```

Arthur's breathing stopped for half a second.

He looked around.

Nobody had reacted.

Evelyn was practicing a controlled strike.

Mara was arguing with the instructor about shield weight.

Iris was working through a sword sequence.

Owen was trying to stop his staff from hitting the floor.

No one could see the message.

Arthur looked down at the dagger.

The words disappeared.

He tried the sword again.

Nothing.

The dagger.

Nothing.

Then, after several seconds, the message returned.

**WEAPON COMPATIBILITY: CONFIRMED.**

Arthur slowly closed his hand around the dagger.

The Academy had taught him that Rank, Power, and Classification were three different things.

Apparently, there was a fourth layer.

And whatever it was, the Academy had not mentioned it.

He looked toward the instructor.

For the first time, Arthur considered lying about something he had not yet understood.

He slipped the dagger back onto the rack.

He said nothing.

That night, he intended to find out what the message meant.


Arthur did not leave immediately.

He walked the weapon racks again after the other students had gone.

The sword encouraged measured movement. It gave him enough reach to avoid being trapped while remaining simple enough to train repeatedly.

The spear created distance. Arthur liked that more than he expected. Distance gave Motion Sense room to work. If he could detect movement early and keep an opponent outside immediate reach, he would have more time to respond.

But the spear demanded space.

The dagger demanded courage.

It brought every decision closer.

Arthur remembered the fight before the Academy, the weapon moving in his hand before thought had fully caught up. The memory should have made him avoid the dagger.

Instead, it made him curious.

He picked it up again.

Nothing happened.

He waited.

Nothing.

Arthur turned the handle over in his palm. There was no symbol. No unusual material. Nothing that could explain the interface.

He opened his hand.

Closed it.

The message returned.

**WEAPON COMPATIBILITY: CONFIRMED.**

"Confirmed by what?" he whispered.

Silence.

He tried the sword.

Nothing.

The spear.

Nothing.

The dagger.

The message.

Arthur began to suspect that the system was not choosing the weapon for him.

It was recognizing something he had already done.

That was more interesting.

And more dangerous.

He placed the dagger back on the rack.

The Academy had asked him to choose his first weapon.

Whatever had appeared inside the private interface seemed to be asking a different question.

Not **Which weapon do you choose?**

But **Why does this weapon recognize you?**

Arthur left the hall without an answer.

For once, he was glad.

An answer that arrived too easily would have been harder to trust.

The next morning, Arthur asked the instructor a question.

"How long do we have to choose?"

"Until the end of the week."

"And if none feels right?"

"Then you choose the one you dislike least and learn why you dislike it."

Arthur considered that answer.

"That's not very encouraging."

"Weapon training rarely is."

She pointed at the racks.

"A weapon is a method. It changes distance, timing, posture, commitment, and recovery. Your Power does not decide those things for you."

Arthur looked at the dagger.

That was exactly what he had been feeling.

Motion Sense could tell him that a shoulder moved. A weapon determined what he could do with the information.

A sword could intercept.

A spear could maintain distance.

A dagger could exploit a narrow opening.

An axe could punish a committed movement.

A bow could turn information into action before an opponent entered range.

There was no universal answer.

Arthur picked up the sword.

Then the dagger.

He finally understood why the instructor had refused to tell them which weapon was best.

The question was incomplete.

Best for whom?

Best for what distance?

Best against what opponent?

Best for what Power?

Best under what physical limitation?

He looked down at his hand.

The strange message from yesterday returned in his memory.

**Weapon Compatibility: CONFIRMED.**

Whatever had created it seemed to be asking the same question from another direction.

What weapon was Arthur actually compatible with?

He had a feeling the answer would be more complicated than choosing a sword.

Arthur spent several minutes imagining the same fight with different weapons.

With a sword, he could meet an attack and recover into another position.

With a spear, he could keep the opponent farther away, but a close-range mistake could become difficult to correct.

With a dagger, every opening would be smaller and every decision would become more committed.

The choice was not cosmetic.

It changed the shape of the fight.

That was exactly what he needed to understand before accepting any private message from a system he did not trust yet.

He returned to the rack and placed his palm around the dagger's handle one final time.

Nothing appeared.

Arthur frowned.

Then he realized something.

The message had not triggered simply because he held it.

It had triggered after he had tested it repeatedly, after he had fought with it, after he had demonstrated a relationship with the weapon that the system could recognize.

That meant compatibility might be earned.

Arthur's eyes narrowed.

If that was true, then the Contract was not a gift waiting to be collected.

It was a consequence of learning.

He left the weapon hall with that hypothesis.

He would test it later.

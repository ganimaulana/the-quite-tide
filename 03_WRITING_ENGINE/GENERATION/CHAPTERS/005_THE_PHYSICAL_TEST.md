# BODY BEFORE POWER

The next morning, the first-years stood on the Academy track beneath a pale sky.

Arthur knew immediately that the instructors had kept their promise.

There were no Power assessment machines.

No demonstration circles.

No classification board.

Only lanes, timers, padded platforms, weights, climbing frames, and instructors carrying clipboards.

The physical instructor faced the class.

"Today," she said, "we find out what your body can do before your Power gets to take credit for it."

Mara looked at Arthur.

"That sounds threatening."

"It is a physical test."

"Exactly. Threatening."

The instructor pointed toward the sprint lanes.

"Speed first."

Arthur took his position beside Evelyn, Mara, and Iris.

He lowered himself slightly.

The starter raised a hand.

"Ready."

Arthur heard his own breathing.

The signal sounded.

Iris exploded forward.

There was no visible hesitation in her first step. She moved as if the distance had already been measured and the answer was obvious.

Evelyn followed close behind.

Arthur ran hard.

He crossed the line in the upper half of the group.

Not first.

Not close to first.

But comfortably away from the bottom.

Mara crossed several seconds later and bent over with her hands on her knees.

"That was terrible."

Evelyn glanced at the result board.

"You finished ahead of twelve people."

"I can be terrible and still beat twelve people."

Arthur almost smiled.

The next station tested reaction.

Lights appeared at different points along a wall. Students had to touch the correct marker as soon as it activated, then return to the centre line before the next signal.

Arthur watched the first light.

The instant before it activated, something changed in the mechanism.

He moved.

His hand touched the marker.

Another light appeared.

He turned.

Another.

Then another.

By the end of the test, his breathing had barely changed.

The instructor checked the sheet.

Then checked it again.

"Reed."

Arthur looked up.

"No deliberate Esper output?"

"None."

That was technically true.

Motion Sense was helping him notice the beginning of each mechanical movement, but the test still required physical action. His feet had to move. His balance had to hold. His muscles still had to obey.

The instructor made another note.

Iris watched him from the next lane.

"You look far too serious for someone running in a straight line."

Arthur glanced at her.

"I'm trying not to fall."

"Reasonable strategy."

Her result appeared beside his.

Faster.

Arthur looked at the difference.

"That's annoying."

Iris looked at the board.

"The board?"

"The person next to me."

She gave him a brief, unreadable look before moving to the next station.

The strength station came next.

Arthur discovered that average strength felt different when it was recorded next to people who trained regularly.

He could grip the bar.

He could lift the assigned weight.

He could maintain control.

But there was nothing remarkable about it.

Evelyn's result was consistently high.

Iris was higher still.

Mara landed somewhere in the middle.

Owen performed well enough until the final repetitions.

Clarke looked personally offended by the existence of the equipment.

"Document Reading," he muttered, lowering the weight. "Does not contain a strength component."

"You could start reading exercise manuals," Owen suggested.

Clarke stared at him.

"I dislike you already."

Owen grinned.

The balance and coordination course was more difficult than Arthur expected.

Thin platforms.

Uneven surfaces.

Sudden changes in direction.

Objects that had to be moved without stepping outside the marked area.

Arthur's Motion Sense helped when something moved.

It did nothing when the problem was his own ankle rolling slightly on an unstable platform.

He nearly fell.

He recovered.

The instructor marked a small error.

Iris made none.

Evelyn made one.

Mara made three and complained about all of them.

"The platform moved."

"That is the purpose of the platform."

"Then the platform is badly designed."

The instructor continued writing.

Mara looked at Arthur.

"If I fall, tell everyone it was deliberate."

"Why?"

"It sounds more professional."

Arthur laughed.

The endurance test came last.

That was where the differences became obvious.

Iris maintained an aggressive pace almost from the beginning.

Evelyn kept a steady rhythm and never appeared to panic about the distance.

Mara's breathing became ragged halfway through.

She did not stop.

Owen slowed significantly near the final circuit.

Arthur found a pace he could sustain.

It was not impressive.

It was simply enough.

Clarke stopped before the final circuit and sat on the grass.

"I'm done."

Arthur handed him water.

"You still have one circuit."

"The Academy has failed to establish why this is necessary."

"Because you're exhausted."

"That is precisely why I object."

Arthur laughed before he could stop himself.

At the end of the morning, the instructor posted the results.

Students gathered around the board.

Arthur waited until the crowd thinned.

He found his name.

He was not first.

He was not last.

His reaction time was unusually good.

His speed was respectable.

His endurance was decent.

His strength was ordinary.

His balance was good but imperfect.

Overall, he was simply competent.

That mattered more than he expected.

Rank F had not made him weak.

Motion Sense had not made him strong.

His body was simply his body.

The physical instructor collected the sheets.

Arthur looked at the results again.

Everyone had something they could rely on.

Everyone had something they could not.

The instructor collected the sheets.

"Tomorrow, you'll use everything you have."

Arthur looked toward the combat hall.

The wooden doors were closed.

He could hear impacts on the other side.

Someone was already training.

Tomorrow, his body would have to answer faster than his thoughts.


Arthur stayed on the track after most of the class had left.

The reaction wall was empty now. No lights flashed. No instructor watched. Without a test sheet in front of him, the equipment looked almost ordinary.

Arthur tossed a training ball against the wall.

It returned.

He caught it.

Again.

Again.

Then he changed the angle.

The return changed with it.

Arthur adjusted.

Caught it.

The exercise was simple enough to feel childish. That was exactly why he kept doing it.

There was no opponent to blame and no complicated Power interaction. If he saw the movement but missed the catch, the problem belonged entirely to him.

He increased the pace.

The ball struck his palm harder.

He missed once.

He picked it up.

"Again."

The next catch was clean.

Then another.

His breathing became heavier.

Arthur stopped before exhaustion turned useful practice into bad practice.

The physical test had given him something more valuable than a position on a board.

It had given him a boundary.

Motion Sense could tell him when something began to move.

It could not make his muscles answer instantly.

It could not give him more endurance.

It could not repair a mistake in balance.

But those were things he could train.

Arthur looked toward the combat hall.

Someone struck a padded target inside.

The sound echoed across the track.

Tomorrow, someone would try to hit him.

That would be a much less forgiving test.

Before leaving, Arthur checked the results one more time.

Iris had been excellent almost everywhere. Evelyn was consistently strong. Mara's endurance was better than her sprint suggested. Owen's reaction time was good, but his endurance dropped sharply. Clarke had struggled with nearly every purely physical station.

Arthur's result sat between them.

That bothered him less than it might have before.

He had spent years thinking of ability as something that had to be proven all at once. The Academy was teaching him a different idea.

A body was a collection of separate problems.

Speed could improve without strength improving at the same rate. Reaction could be excellent while endurance remained ordinary. Balance could fail even when perception was sharp.

The same would probably be true in combat.

One advantage would not erase every weakness.

Arthur folded the result sheet.

He looked at the individual results instead of the overall position.

That was where the useful information was.

His reaction time stood out, but only when there was something to react to. His sprint was good enough to keep him out of trouble, but not good enough to create much distance. His strength was ordinary. His endurance was better than his strength suggested, but only because he had learned to keep a steady pace instead of spending everything at the beginning.

The pattern reminded him of yesterday.

One advantage never solved every problem.

Motion Sense gave him information before a movement became obvious. His legs still had to carry him there. His lungs still had to keep working. His balance still had to survive the turn.

Arthur had always thought of those things separately.

The Academy was forcing him to see them as parts of the same system.

He folded the sheet again and put it away.

If Motion Sense gave him an early warning, then physical training would determine what he could do with that warning.

That was not a spectacular revelation.

It was better.

It was something he could work on.

Arthur left the track with one conclusion.

He was not weak.

He was not exceptional either.

That was a useful place to begin.
That was enough for today.

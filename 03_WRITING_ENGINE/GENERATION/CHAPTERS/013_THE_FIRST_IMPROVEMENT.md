# MULTIPLE WEAPONS

Arthur changed the order on the third day.

Dagger first.

Then spear.

Then sword.

Then axe.

Then bow.

He wanted to remove the easiest explanation.

Maybe the dagger had only felt different because his hands remembered the knife from Ravenhurst.

He needed to separate memory from evidence.

The spear disagreed.

Its balance was obvious. Its center of mass could be felt through the hands. Motion Sense helped him read another person's grip and shoulder line, but the long shaft made every mistake larger.

The dagger disagreed differently.

It rewarded economy. The smallest unnecessary movement became visible. Its short reach forced Arthur to solve distance with his feet rather than pretending the blade could solve it for him.

The axe made him respect momentum.

The bow made him respect preparation.

Then he returned to the dagger.

The difference was still there.

The dagger did not make him skilled.

It made every mistake immediate.

He repeated the same movement twenty times.

Twenty-one.

Twenty-two.

On the twenty-third, his right foot landed half a fraction too early.

The blade stopped short.

Arthur froze.

Not because the mistake was unusual.

Because he had felt it before the blade finished moving.

He reset.

Again.

This time the foot was right.

The shoulder was wrong.

The sword pulled the error into the open.

He tried the same drill with the spear.

The spear showed him the distance.

He tried it with the dagger.

The dagger showed him the wrist and the distance.

He tried the same drill with the sword.

The sword showed him the connection between footwork, body rotation, and weapon path.

Arthur stopped before turning that observation into a conclusion.

He had made that mistake before.

He wrote:

*Observation: sword exposes errors between footwork, body rotation, and weapon path more clearly than the dagger.*

Then underneath:

*Hypothesis: familiarity may make the dagger easier to study, but compatibility still has to be demonstrated.*

He did not write *chosen weapon*.

That would be too much.

He did not write *special weapon*.

That would be worse.

The sword had not spoken.

The System had not reacted.

There was no reason to pretend otherwise.

When he opened the interface, it remained unchanged.

________________________________
CONTRACT SYSTEM

Status: NO CONTRACT

No new condition detected.
________________________________

Arthur looked at it for a moment.

Then he closed it.

Good.

If something was going to happen, he wanted it to happen because he had actually done something to deserve it.

He put the sword back on the rack.

The sword was useful.

The dagger was personal.

That distinction mattered.

He returned to the dagger.

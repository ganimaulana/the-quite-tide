# SECOND FAILURE

The old worker returned with a box.

He had worked in the ferry district decades earlier.

Inside the box were three things.

A ferry token.

A photograph.

A transfer slip.

The transfer slip carried the same family name Arthur had found in the old records.

Reed.

Arthur read the date.

The worker watched him.

"My brother kept that."

"Was he the previous holder?"

The old man shook his head.

"No. He worked for one."

Arthur stopped.

"Who?"

"I never knew."

The worker turned the photograph over.

On the back was another line.

**Return to the person who recognizes the object.**

Arthur looked at the handwriting.

It matched the warning from the Institute photograph.

The two records were connected.

But they did not explain who had written them.

The worker gave Arthur the transfer slip.

"My brother said the person disappeared after the warehouse closed."

"Disappeared how?"

"He stopped coming back."

That was all.

Arthur did not invent the rest.

He wrote the facts down.

The previous holder had existed.

Someone had worked for them.

The artifact had a recognition condition.

The Institute knew more than it had disclosed.

And the warning about separation was old enough to predate Arthur by decades.

The chain had become a real investigation.

Not because Arthur had solved it.

Because someone had finally given him a living witness.

Before Arthur could ask another question, the witness added one sentence.

"The person you are looking for was not the last holder."

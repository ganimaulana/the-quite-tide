# THE PRICE OF INFORMATION

Kessler offered Arthur one answer.

Not for free.

They met after the mission.

"The previous holder was real," she said.

Arthur waited.

"The Institute has a record of the person."

"Where?"

"Restricted archive."

Arthur almost laughed.

"Then you do not actually have the answer."

"I have enough to know that the person was not an ordinary researcher."

She placed a photograph on the table.

The face had been removed from the image.

Not blurred.

Cut away.

Arthur examined the background.

The artifact was there.

The same stone markers.

The same binding faces.

"Why show me this?"

"Because the Institute wants the artifact appraised again."

"And the previous holder?"

"Their file becomes available if the appraisal is authorized."

Arthur understood the exchange.

Information for access.

He did not like it.

But he could use it.

"I will consider the appraisal."

Kessler nodded.

"That is more than a refusal."

"It is not an acceptance."

"I know."

Arthur took the photograph.

Before leaving, he turned it over.

There was handwriting on the back.

One line.

**Do not separate the holder from the object before confirming recognition.**

Arthur read it twice.

The warning was old.

The Institute had just proposed exactly the experiment it warned against.

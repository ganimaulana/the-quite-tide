# THE D-RANK

Mercer introduced himself by asking Arthur to lose.

"D-rank."

Arthur looked at the badge.

"I know."

"And you are F-rank."

"That is also on the badge."

Mercer smiled.

He was not mocking him yet.

That came later.

"The Academy wants a benchmark," Mercer said. "I have been assigned to your next exercise."

"Why?"

"Because your results are becoming inconvenient."

Arthur waited.

"You keep performing above what the classification predicts. I keep performing where my classification says I should. The Academy wants a comparison."

Arthur considered the wording.

"You do not like that."

Mercer laughed.

"I dislike bad comparisons."

That was more interesting than contempt.

The exercise roster arrived that afternoon.

Arthur and Mercer were placed on opposite sides.

The phenomenon would be selected on the day.

The judging criteria were reproducibility, safety, and operational result.

Mercer read the sheet.

"You requested reproducibility."

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because one successful trick proves very little."

Mercer looked at him for a moment.

"Good."

He walked away.

Arthur watched him go.

The rival was not trying to insult him.

He was trying to measure him.

That could become much more dangerous.

The benchmark schedule arrived before lunch.

Arthur read the final line.

**First test: live phenomenon.**

**Second test: direct comparison.**

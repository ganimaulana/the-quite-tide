# THE SURVIVOR

The monster stopped moving.

Arthur kept the knife in its throat.

He waited.

Nothing.

No twitch. No breath.

Only his own breathing, too loud inside the ruined building.

He pulled the blade free.

Blood ran over his hand.

Arthur looked at the thing beneath him.

He knew what it was.

He knew he had killed it.

He did not know how.

Around him, the room was almost silent.

Bodies lay between overturned tables, broken equipment, and pieces of the ceiling that had come down during the fight. Some belonged to people Arthur had known. Some belonged to people he could no longer place.

The memory before the kill was broken.

A door.

A scream.

Something moving behind him.

His hand closing around the knife.

Then the monster's weight against him.

Then this.

Arthur looked toward the corridor.

Another sound came from somewhere deeper inside the building.

Boots.

More than one person.

Arthur tried to stand.

His legs almost gave way.

The first soldier appeared through the smoke with a rifle raised.

"Don't move."

Arthur stopped.

The soldier looked at him, then at the dead monster.

His expression changed.

"Ward!"

Another figure entered behind him.

Major Elias Ward moved with the controlled speed of someone who had already seen too much that night to waste movement. His uniform was dark with dust. One sleeve was torn. He looked at the body first, then at Arthur.

"Clear?" he asked.

"One confirmed hostile dead. No movement from the room."

Ward stepped closer.

He crouched beside the monster without touching it.

The wound was obvious.

So was the direction of the blade.

Ward looked at Arthur.

"You did this?"

Arthur looked at the knife in his hand.

"I remember doing it."

Ward's eyes stayed on him.

"That's not what I asked."

Arthur swallowed.

"Yes."

Ward looked back at the body.

He had spent enough years around anomaly incidents to know what an ordinary civilian could do. This was not ordinary.

"Medic," he called. "He's alive."

The soldier behind him moved.

Arthur tried to look past Ward.

There was a body near the wall.

Then another.

Then another.

He recognized the coat on one of them.

His father's.

Arthur stared.

He knew the coat.

He knew the person wearing it.

His father was dead.

The fact reached him cleanly.

The grief did not.

He waited for something to happen inside him.

Nothing did.

Ward noticed.

He did not ask why Arthur was not crying.

He only looked toward the corridor.

"Family?" he asked quietly.

Arthur nodded.

"My mother. My father. My sister."

Ward's expression tightened.

He had seen families die before.

That did not make this one easier to look at.

"I'm sorry," he said.

Arthur looked at him.

He knew he was supposed to answer.

"Thank you."

The words sounded correct.

They did not feel large enough.

---

Arthur woke in Ravenhurst General three days later.

The hospital gave him the rest of the facts.

By then, the incident had a name.

The Ravenhurst Massacre.

The official paperwork used something longer: the Ravenhurst Industrial District Anomalous Incident.

Several hostile entities had entered the site. The response team had arrived after most of the casualties had already occurred.

Arthur was the only surviving civilian found inside.

His family had not survived.

One monster had been confirmed dead before the response team completed the operation.

The field report attributed the kill to Arthur.

Major Elias Ward had signed that section himself.

Arthur read the report twice.

He remembered the monster.

He remembered killing it.

He remembered the bodies.

He did not remember everything between those points.

When he tried to reconstruct the sequence, his memory became unreliable.

He could remember a sound without remembering its source.

He could remember a movement without remembering what had caused it.

He could remember the knife entering the monster without remembering why he had known where to put it.

The gaps frightened him less than they should have.

That was the part he did not understand.

His family was dead.

He knew that.

He could remember his mother's voice. His father's habit of checking the front door twice. His sister leaving cups beside the sink and forgetting them there.

The memories were real.

The feelings attached to them seemed farther away.

Arthur did not know whether that was shock, memory damage, or something else.

Neither did the doctors.

---

Major Ward visited once before Arthur was discharged.

He stood beside the hospital window with his hands behind his back.

"You remember the incident?" he asked.

"Parts of it."

"The monster?"

"Yes."

"Killing it?"

Arthur looked down at his hands.

"Yes."

Ward nodded.

"Do you remember how?"

Arthur shook his head.

"No."

Ward did not press.

"The report says you were untrained."

"I was."

"It also says you killed a monster."

Arthur waited.

"That combination is unusual," Ward said.

"I know."

"The district wants you assessed properly."

Arthur looked toward him.

"Why?"

Ward's answer was simple.

"Because ordinary people don't kill these things."

He paused.

"At least, they aren't supposed to."

Arthur almost smiled.

"That's reassuring."

Ward's mouth moved slightly.

"It wasn't meant to be."

---

The weeks after the massacre passed without giving Arthur much to do except recover.

The hospital released him. The investigation kept his family home sealed. The social worker handled forms, temporary arrangements, and questions Arthur was too tired to care about.

During that time, he noticed something else.

Movement.

Not the future.

Not intent.

Only movement that had already begun.

A chair shifting before he looked at it.

A hand reaching for a door.

The change in balance before someone stepped forward.

At first, Arthur thought he was simply paying closer attention.

So he tested it.

He watched people cross rooms without looking directly at them. He listened for the small changes that came just before movement. He tried to predict what would happen and paid attention to the times he was wrong.

The pattern remained.

He could not see the future.

He could sense movement once it had already started.

Eventually, he gave the ability a name.

Motion Sense.

He did not know whether that was the correct term. It was simply the name that made the most sense to him.

By the time the formal assessment was scheduled, Arthur had been using the name for several days.

The assessment came before the Academy placement.

Arthur was seventeen. His immediate family was gone. There was no ordinary home placement available.

The social worker gave him several options.

Residential care.

General education.

Specialist education.

Ravenscroft Academy was the only option that had both the facilities and the institutional reason to accept him.

Arthur looked at the folder.

"Because of the Ravenhurst Massacre?"

"Partly," the social worker said.

"And the monster?"

She hesitated.

"Major Ward included a recommendation for specialist assessment. The Academy agreed to review you because there is a possibility that you have an ability that has not been properly classified."

Arthur closed the folder.

That explanation made sense.

Someone had seen him do something that should have been impossible.

Someone wanted to know why.

The formal assessment might answer that.

At least, that was what everyone believed.

---

The hospital gave Arthur a small suitcase.

His family home had been sealed as part of the investigation, and the remaining personal effects would be processed later. For now, the suitcase contained clothes, toiletries, his identification, and a folder of official documents.

One page carried Major Ward's signature.

Arthur looked at it once before putting it away.

The field officer had not promised him anything.

He had only written what he saw.

**One confirmed hostile killed. Survivor located alive.**

Arthur closed the folder.

Outside, Ravenhurst continued as if nothing had happened.

Buses passed.

People crossed the street.

Shops opened.

The city did not know him.

Somewhere in that city, newspapers had already begun telling the story of the boy who survived.

Arthur had not read them yet.

He would.

Soon.

For now, he picked up his suitcase and walked toward the car waiting to take him to Ravenscroft Academy.

He had survived the massacre.

He had killed a monster.

He still did not know how.

And the institution that was about to take him in had decided that finding out might be worth the trouble.

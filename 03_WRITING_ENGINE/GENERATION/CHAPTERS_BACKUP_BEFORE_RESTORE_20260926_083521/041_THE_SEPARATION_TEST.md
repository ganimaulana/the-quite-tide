# THE SEPARATION TEST

The visitor was an Institute official.

He did not introduce himself as an appraiser.

He introduced himself as the person responsible for the test.

"We want to separate you from the artifact for twenty minutes."

Arthur sat down.

"No."

The official did not react.

"You have not heard the procedure."

"I have heard the important part."

The official placed the old warning on the table.

Arthur recognized it.

**Do not separate the holder from the object before confirming recognition.**

"You know what this means," the official said.

"I know someone wrote it."

"We believe the artifact recognizes its holder."

"Then you should test recognition without separation."

The official smiled.

"That is not the question we need answered."

Arthur looked at him.

"Then you are asking the wrong question."

Silence.

The official finally stood.

"The Academy has not rejected the appraisal."

"The Academy has not approved separation."

"Not yet."

That was the pressure.

Arthur understood it.

The Institute did not need to force him.

It only needed the Academy to decide that the test was reasonable.

Arthur took the warning back.

He did not know who had written it.

But whoever had done so had understood the danger decades before the Institute proposed the experiment.

The midpoint was no longer about discovering what the artifact was.

It was about deciding who was allowed to test it.

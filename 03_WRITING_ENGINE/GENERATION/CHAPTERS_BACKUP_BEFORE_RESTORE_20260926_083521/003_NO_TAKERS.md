# NO TAKERS

The first-year cohort performance board went up before lunch.

Arthur found his name near the bottom.

Not last. Not close to the top.

The heading explained why.

**ACADEMY PERFORMANCE RECORD — CURRENT COHORT POINTS**

Beside it, in smaller print:

**Esper Rank is assessed separately once per academic year.**

Someone beside him laughed. "F-rank and still near the bottom."

Arthur looked at the speaker. "Those are cohort points."

"You know what I mean."

Arthur did.

Rank F was visible. The reasons behind it were not.

Later that morning, the foundation session began: sprint work, archery, supervised firearms behind a safety barrier, and paired combat with padded weapons. The instructors were not training soldiers. They were measuring movement, reaction, coordination, procedure, and behaviour under pressure.

Arthur watched the first rotation. He did not yet know enough to participate safely in every station.

At the end of the session, the Support coordinator called him over.

"Operations Support. Field Support track."

Arthur looked at the assignment sheet.

Equipment preparation. Containment support. Route observation. Communication. Casualty and extraction support.

"Not Records?"

"No. You may use Records later when a field problem requires historical information. That is different."

Arthur folded the sheet.

The distinction was small on paper.

It would become important later.

For now, he had a division, a role, a Rank F classification, and almost no reputation.

Then the instructor posted the first practical assessment roster.

Arthur's name was beside a task marked **FIELD SUPPORT**.

The student who had laughed at his Rank F looked at the sheet.

"You are actually going out?"

Arthur folded the notice.

"Apparently."

The student stopped smiling.

Arthur had his first assignment.

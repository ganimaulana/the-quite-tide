# FIRST ATTEMPT

Arthur proposed a test.

The phenomenon appeared to follow recognition rather than physical location.

If that was true, the team could move the recognition point.

They chose a new marker in the centre of the warehouse.

Everyone stood around it.

No one looked at the original return point.

For twenty minutes, nothing happened.

Then the tools moved.

Not toward the new marker.

Back to the door.

The test failed.

Mercer was not there yet.

The team simply watched the tools settle.

Arthur stared at the result.

"Your hypothesis was wrong," Owen said.

"Partly."

"Which part?"

Arthur pointed to the old door.

"We all knew the door was the old return point."

"So memory mattered."

"Yes. But recognition was not enough to overwrite it."

The team had learned something.

It had also spent forty minutes proving a hypothesis that had failed.

The instructor marked the exercise as incomplete.

Arthur accepted the result.

Failure was useful when the test had actually separated possibilities.

That night, Kessler sent him a message.

**Your test was cited by the Institute.**

Arthur read it twice.

The Institute was no longer simply watching the artifact.

It was watching his method.

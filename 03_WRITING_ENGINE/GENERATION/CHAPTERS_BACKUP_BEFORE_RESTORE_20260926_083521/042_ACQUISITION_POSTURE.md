# ACQUISITION POSTURE

The Institute changed tactics.

The next letter was polite.

The one after it was legal.

The third cited public-interest provisions.

Arthur filed all three.

Then he stopped filing.

He read them as a sequence.

The first asked for cooperation.

The second questioned whether a seventeen-year-old cadet could demonstrate sufficient control over an anomalous object.

The third suggested that continued private possession might require review.

The argument was no longer about research.

It was about authority.

Arthur took the letters to the Academy administration.

"What does this mean?" he asked.

The administrator read them.

"It means the Institute is preparing a formal request."

"For the artifact?"

"For access first. Custody if access is denied."

Arthur understood.

The Academy had a student.

The Institute had resources.

Neither side wanted a public conflict.

Arthur was the object between them.

He asked one question.

"Can they take it?"

"Not simply because they ask."

"Can the Academy stop them?"

The administrator hesitated.

"We can protect your student status. The artifact is more complicated."

That was honest.

Arthur returned to his room.

He placed the artifact on the desk.

The six Contract slots remained mostly empty.

The old warning lay beside them.

He had one Contract.

He had one confirmed sentence.

He had a growing list of questions.

He did not need another mystery.

He needed a decision.

If the Institute wanted the artifact, Arthur would make them explain why.

Not because he was stronger.

Because whoever wanted custody had to carry the burden of proof.

The administrator slid one final page across the desk.

**RESPONSE REQUIRED BEFORE THE NEXT REVIEW SESSION.**

Arthur looked at the deadline.

He had one night to decide how much of the artifact's behaviour he was willing to disclose.

# THE GAP BETWEEN RANK AND BODY

The practical assessment was held in the training hall, not the assessment room.

That was the first thing Arthur noticed.

The second was the board.

It displayed three columns.

**ESPER RANK.**

**COHORT PERFORMANCE.**

**PRACTICAL RESULT.**

Arthur read the headings twice.

The instructor noticed.

"Different records," she said. "Do not mix them."

Arthur nodded.

The exercise began with a sprint.

He ran.

The result was ordinary for a student with a strong body and little formal training. He was not the fastest. He was not close to the bottom either.

The second test was reaction and movement.

Lights appeared across the floor. He had to cross the marked points without stepping outside the safe lane.

Motion Sense helped.

Not by showing him where the next light would appear. It did not do that.

It told him when something had already moved.

That was enough to make some decisions earlier.

The third test was controlled Resonance.

Arthur drew power through his body.

The result was poor.

The instrument barely moved.

The instructor looked at the reading, then at Arthur.

"Again."

He tried.

The result improved by a fraction.

Still poor.

"That is your conventional output," she said. "Do not force it."

Arthur released the Resonance.

The next test was a padded-weapon drill.

His opponent was faster and more experienced.

Arthur lost the first exchange.

He lost the second more cleanly.

On the third, he read the shoulder movement correctly and still stepped into the wrong distance.

The padded weapon touched his ribs.

"Stop."

Arthur stepped back.

His body was not the problem.

His technique was.

The instructor wrote something on the tablet.

"Your physical result is ahead of your combat result," she said. "Your Resonance output is below the normal range for your cohort. Your movement awareness is unusual. Your technique is inexperienced."

Arthur looked at the three columns again.

The numbers did not agree with one another.

That was not an error.

They were measuring different things.

At the end of the session, the instructor pointed to the board.

"Your Esper Rank remains F."

"Because of the output?"

"Because the classification system records what it can classify."

Arthur waited.

"The practical assessment does not manually change Rank. It contributes to your Academy Performance Record. Your annual Esper classification is separate."

That distinction was more useful than the result.

Arthur wrote it down.

**Rank F: classification.**

**Cohort Points: demonstrated performance.**

**Combat result: depends on conditions.**

The instructor glanced at his notes.

"You write everything down."

"It prevents me from remembering the wrong thing."

She considered that.

"Keep doing it. Just remember that notes are not conclusions."

Arthur looked at the padded weapon in his hand.

He had learned something more important than whether he had passed.

Rank F did not mean his body was weak.

It meant the Academy's classification of his Esper output was low.

The difference mattered.

He left the hall with his first Cohort Performance points and the same Rank F he had entered with.

Nothing had been fixed.

Nothing had been broken.

But for the first time, Arthur understood exactly what the number meant.

And what it did not.

The instructor stopped him at the door.

"Reed. Your next assessment is not physical."

Arthur turned.

"What is it?"

"Operational judgment."

# THE BENCHMARK

The exercise began with a tool on a table.

Every time a student looked for it, the tool returned to the place they expected to find it.

Mercer tested the phenomenon first.

He deliberately looked at the wrong place.

The tool moved there.

He repeated the test.

Same result.

Arthur watched.

Mercer had learned from the previous reports.

He was not treating the phenomenon as an obstacle.

He was forcing it to reveal a condition.

Arthur respected that.

Then Mercer changed the test.

He told the team to stop looking at the tool entirely.

The tool stayed where it was.

"Recognition," Mercer said.

Arthur shook his head.

"Not enough."

Mercer looked at him.

Arthur pointed to the table.

"Everyone already knows the original position."

Mercer smiled.

"So you think memory is stronger than attention."

"I think we have not separated them yet."

The exercise continued.

Mercer pushed the team hard.

Arthur adjusted the observation protocol.

Neither solved it quickly.

That was the point.

For the first time, Arthur faced someone who could adapt at the same speed he did.

The exercise ended without a clean winner.

The board published both reports.

Arthur's report identified the memory condition.

Mercer's report identified the attention condition.

Neither explanation was complete.

Mercer found Arthur after the review.

"You are not weak."

Arthur waited.

"Your Rank is weak," Mercer continued. "That is different."

Arthur nodded.

"Agreed."

Mercer looked almost disappointed.

"I wanted you to argue."

"Why?"

"Because then I could measure you emotionally as well."

Arthur walked away.

The rivalry had begun.

Then the Academy board posted the next benchmark.

**MERCER vs REED — PUBLIC EVALUATION.**

This time, the judges would be watching.

# THE RIVAL'S GAMBIT

Mercer posted the challenge before breakfast.

**PUBLIC BENCHMARK EXERCISE**

**D-RANK vs F-RANK**

**Random phenomenon. Reproducible conditions. Independent judging.**

Arthur read it once.

Owen read it twice.

"He wants to beat you publicly."

Arthur shook his head.

"He wants a clean measurement."

Mercer appeared behind them.

"Both can be true."

Arthur turned.

Mercer held out the final protocol.

"You asked for reproducibility. I built the challenge around it."

Arthur read the rules.

The phenomenon would be selected by lot.

Neither student could choose the environment.

The judges would not know the students' previous reports until after the exercise.

No Contract invocation was prohibited, but any use had to be declared.

The result would be judged by operational success, safety, and reproducibility.

It was fair.

That made it dangerous.

Arthur signed.

"Why accept?" Owen asked afterward.

Arthur folded the protocol.

"Because if I keep avoiding public tests, other people will decide what my results mean."

The lot was drawn.

Object-class Seep.

Storage room.

Return behaviour tied to recognition.

Mercer looked at Arthur.

Arthur looked back.

Neither smiled.

The exercise began.

Mercer moved first.

Arthur watched the room.

The phenomenon moved the tool.

Mercer adapted.

Arthur adapted.

For several minutes, neither gained an advantage.

Then Arthur saw the smallest change.

The tool was not following where people looked.

It was following the point where they expected the tool to be.

Arthur changed his method.

Mercer saw it.

He changed too.

The phenomenon shifted.

The judges watched.

The room became a contest between two hypotheses rather than two ranks.

Arthur did not know who would produce the better result.

He did know one thing.

For the first time, nobody in the room could dismiss him merely because the badge on his chest said F.

The test would have to do the talking.

The starting signal sounded.

Mercer moved.

Arthur's Motion Sense caught it.

Then Arthur saw something else.

Mercer had anticipated him.

For the first time, Arthur was not the only one adapting.

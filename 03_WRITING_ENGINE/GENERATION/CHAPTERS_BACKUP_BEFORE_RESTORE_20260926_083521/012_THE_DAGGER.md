# THE DAGGER

Arthur requested controlled access to the weapon room.

The instructor looked at his Support assignment.

"You want the full rack?"

"Comparative practice."

"Why?"

"I failed."

That answer earned permission.

Arthur started with the dagger, then spear, sword, axe, and bow.

The differences were immediate.

Dagger: distance and economy.

Spear: reach and control.

Sword: connection between footwork and weapon path.

Axe: momentum.

Bow: preparation and tension.

None gave him a Contract. None gave him mastery.

The dagger simply made his errors easiest to see.

Its short reach forced him to move his feet correctly.

Arthur returned to it.

The choice was not supernatural.

It was practical.

And personal.

He had killed a monster with a knife in Ravenhurst.

He still did not know how.

The dagger gave him a way to investigate that memory through training rather than imagination.

The instructor pointed toward the practice floor.

"If you want to use it in the field, prove you can control it first."

Arthur raised the dagger.

The next drill began.

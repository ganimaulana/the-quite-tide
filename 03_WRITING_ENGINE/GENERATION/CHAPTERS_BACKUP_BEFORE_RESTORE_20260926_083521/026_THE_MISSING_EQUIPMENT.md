# THE MISSING EQUIPMENT

The ferry exercise changed three hours before deployment.

The containment team lost its portable frame.

The frame had been assigned to another Division.

Their request had priority.

Arthur's team had one hour to find another solution.

The Combat cadet swore.

The Tactical cadet opened the equipment catalogue.

"There is another frame."

"Locked in the old storage wing," Owen said.

"Can we get it?"

"Not with our authorization."

Arthur looked at the mission packet.

The old ferry district.

Historical anomaly density.

Tool displacement.

No reproducible rule.

He looked at the equipment list again.

"We do not need the frame."

Everyone looked at him.

"We need something that prevents the tools from leaving the test area."

"That is what the frame does."

"The frame contains space. It does not explain the phenomenon."

The Tactical cadet frowned.

Arthur pointed to the support kit.

Communication beacons.

Containment straps.

Portable markers.

"We can mark the last known position, record every movement, and keep the team outside the return path."

"That is not containment."

"No. It is observation with boundaries."

The team commander considered the proposal.

"We have forty minutes. Do it."

They entered the warehouse without the frame.

The first tool moved.

Arthur marked its position.

The second moved.

He marked that one too.

The pattern appeared after six movements.

The tools were not returning to their last physical location.

They were returning to the last location a person had looked for them.

Arthur stopped.

The old case had said something similar.

Not enough to call it the same Rule.

Enough to change the investigation.

"Nobody look for the next tool," he said.

The Combat cadet stared at him.

"What?"

"We test the memory condition."

They did.

The next tool moved toward the place everyone had expected it to be.

Arthur had been right.

The equipment shortage had not been a setback.

It had forced them to remove a piece of equipment that would have hidden the phenomenon's behaviour.

The team earned the exercise points.

Arthur's report included one sentence that mattered more than the result:

**The missing frame exposed a distinction the standard procedure would have obscured.**

The report was sent to review.

Arthur expected questions.

He got an audit request instead.

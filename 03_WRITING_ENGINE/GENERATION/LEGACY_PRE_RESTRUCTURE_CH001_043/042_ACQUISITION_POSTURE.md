**Scene one â€” the new letters.**

The Institute's letters changed their tone after the failed separation. They no longer asked politely. They pointed out, in careful language, that the artifact was of public interest. They noted, in plainer language, that Arthur was still a student. They suggested, in language so thin it was almost transparent, that ownership could be re-examined if the holder could not demonstrate control.

Arthur filed each letter under REED ARTIFACT â€” INSTITUTE CORRESPONDENCE. He numbered them. He noticed the gaps between letters shortened from one week to three days.

He also noticed that each letter was signed by a different official, but written in the same cadence. The same clauses. The same ordering of concerns. The Institute was not writing to him with different voices. It was printing one letter with different names on it, which meant the campaign was designed, not emotional.

He compared the letters to the appraisal terms and found the same architecture. Concern, condition, consequence. It was the structure of a form, and it told him that the Institute did not distinguish between offering him a study and demanding his property. To them it was all the same document, filed under the same heading, with the heading itself being the dining hallage: **subject of interest**.

He copied the third letter and put the copy in the parallel file. Then he wrote one line: **The Institute does not argue. It files. Argue back in the same language.**

Dr. Kajiwara did not visit.

**Scene two â€” Hayes's warning.**

Hayes found him in the archive after midnight. The building was empty except for the two of them and the hum of the lights.

"They will offer you rank," Hayes said. "Then they will offer you protection. Then they will offer to make the problem go away."

"I know."

"The order is important. Rank first, so you feel seen. Protection second, so you feel afraid. Removal of the problem last, so you feel grateful." Hayes put a hand on the desk. "When they offer to remove the problem, they mean you."

"I will not let them take the artifact."

"They do not want the artifact. They want it documented. If you are the only person who can hold it, then you are the document." Hayes straightened. "Refuse the third offer. Whatever it is."

"I have refused all of them."

"Then you are still ahead. For now."

Hayes did not leave. He stood by the desk, which was unusual, because Hayes never stayed longer than a warning required.

"There is a fourth offer," he said. "They will not send it in writing."

"What is it?"

"They will offer to make you their liaison. Not a student with a study attached â€” a man with an office in the Institute, a salary, and the artifact filed under his own name. It looks like promotion. It is capture." Hayes looked at him. "I have seen it before. Not with an artifact. With a researcher who was too good at something they could not explain."

"What happened to him?"

"He filed his own work under their name for eleven years. Then they retired him, and the work was theirs." Hayes turned to go. "Nobody took anything from him. He signed it away one form at a time."

Arthur sat with that for a while after Hayes left. One form at a time. He thought about the deferral form, and the counter-offer, and the site-access trade. Every one of them had been a form, and every form had given the Institute a little more surface area. He had thought he was negotiating. Hayes was telling him he had been expanding the board.

He opened the parallel file and looked at the page where he had listed the four postures. Rank, protection, removal, liaison. He added a fifth, in Hayes's words: **the slow file.** The slow file was the offer you accepted one clause at a time until the original thing â€” the artifact, the privacy, the name â€” was standing on somebody else's paperwork.

That was the danger. Not the demand. The convenience.

**Scene three â€” the counter-offer.**

Arthur wrote a letter. He stated that he would continue to cooperate with research on Academy grounds, with Academy supervision, and with his own records retained. He did not mention the artifact. He did not mention ownership. He only defined the conditions under which he would answer questions.

Then he added a paragraph Hayes had not suggested. He offered the Institute something: the right to attend his next three field missions as observers, with full access to the sites and the reports, on the condition that any publication cite the Academy's method appendix and leave his personal file untouched.

It was a trade. Site access in exchange for file privacy.

He read the letter three times. Then he sent it.

The Institute's reply came in six hours.

They accepted the conditions. All of them. They even accepted the citation clause, which he had expected them to fight, because a citation clause meant the Academy's name would appear wherever the Institute published.

Dr. Kajiwara appeared at the Academy the next morning with a folder and a pen.

"You made them bend," she said.

"I made them negotiate," Arthur said. "Bending comes later."

"You gave them site access. That is the thing they wanted."

"No. They wanted my notes. Site access gives them the phenomena, not the cross-index. They will learn the method without learning the file." He looked at her. "That is the difference between a study and a purchase."

She wrote something down. For the first time, Arthur noticed that she wrote in the same leftward hook as the 1934 margin. He did not say anything. He filed it under HANDS, third entry, and waited.

**Scene four â€” the posture holds.**

For three weeks, the Institute observed. They sent two researchers to each mission, and the researchers took notes, and the notes were filed, and nothing was taken from Arthur's desk. The campaign of letters stopped. The deadline passed without a courier.

Clarke called it a victory.

"It is not a victory," Arthur said. "It is a posture."

"What is the difference?"

"A victory ends the conflict. A posture changes the shape of it." Arthur looked at the observation schedule on the board. "They are not fighting me now. They are building a baseline, so that the next time they ask, they will know exactly what I did with the site and what I kept. They are learning my filing habits from the outside."

"And you let them."

"I invited them. That is the price of the trade." Arthur closed his notebook. "I gave up three missions of privacy to protect one file. If the file is worth more than the missions, the trade is good. If it is not, I made a mistake."

"How will you know?"

"The same way I know everything. Someone will file something."

That night, Hayes's fourth-offer warning came true in a form Arthur had not expected. The Institute did not offer him a liaison post. It offered him a *review*: a formal evaluation of his fitness to hold a bound object, to be conducted by the Institute and the Academy jointly, with the artifact present.

It was not a promotion. It was a test with a name.

Arthur read the notice twice, then took it to Hayes.

"This is the fourth offer," he said.

"Yes."

"It is not an offer at all."

"No. It is a review." Hayes read the notice and set it down. "A review has three outcomes. Pass, and you keep the artifact with an Institute record attached. Conditional, and you keep it with supervision. Fail, and the artifact is taken for safekeeping while you are re-evaluated."

"And who decides which outcome?"

"The joint panel. Two Institute, two Academy." Hayes looked at him. "The Academy will not fight for you. The Academy will fight for the method, and the method can be filed by anyone."

"Then the review is decided by the Institute."

"The review is decided by whoever writes the definitions."

Arthur went back to the records block and did what he always did with a form: he read the definitions. The notice defined *fitness to hold* in four sentences. Three of them were ordinary. The fourth said that the holder must demonstrate that the bound object *responds to the holder's authority independent of external supervision.*

That was the trap. In the separation test, the artifact had responded to Arthur only when he was present. The Institute had recorded that as a failure of the artifact to separate. Now they had written a fitness definition that turned the same fact into a failure of the holder to command.

The same behavior. Two readings. One of them now had a form number.

Arthur wrote the correction in the margin of his copy: **The artifact responds to presence, not authority. The definition confuses the two. Correct the definition, not the holder.**

Then he filed the notice under REVIEW, first entry, and began to prepare.

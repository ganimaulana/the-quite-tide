# THE REMAINDER

They came back on the fifteenth, and the first thing Arthur noticed was that the annex had not gone quiet.

The file said the recurrences were irregular. It did not say they stopped. But a building that had been shut for years and used by no one should have had nothing left to say, and the annex had something left to say, and it said it at 0930, three seconds, two voices, from the stair.

The Recon student went up onto the roof again. The Medical student set her kit on the tailgate again. The Tactical student started a fresh log with the date at the top. The senior stood in the service road with the second pass's copy of the file, read the first pass's findings aloud, which took eleven seconds, and said, "It is in the building. "Then where is it?"

The senior sent the two Combat students a hundred meters down the road each way, and put the Recon student on the roof line again, and none of it found anything, because there was nothing to find. Whatever answered was not a thing a person could walk to. It was a sound, and the sound was wherever the sound was, and a plan built for a thing in a building had nothing to hold.

On the fifteenth, with the first pass's times set beside it, the shape was not nothing.

The annex had gone quiet for years with a roomful of last-day sound still in it, and each time something made a noise in front of it since, it answered with the sound it had, which was the old one.

He read it twice more to be sure the order held.

The order held.

"It is not the wrong day," he said.

The senior had come back to the tailgate for water. He stopped with the bottle halfway up. "What?"

"The file says it plays back the wrong day. It does not. It plays back the last sound it heard. The wrong day is just the last day anyone was in here talking." Arthur put a finger on the log. It held.

They tested it the way a rule gets tested when nobody wants to be the one who trusts it: cheaply, once, in front of witnesses. The Medical student walked the service road a hundred meters out, saying a line over and over in a level voice, and the annex gave the line back from where she was, not from the stair. She moved, and the sound moved. She stopped, and it stopped. She started again, and it started again, three seconds behind her.

The Tactical student wrote it down, and this time he wrote a location, and the location was a person.

The senior did not say the filing clerk had been right. He did something more useful. He asked him a question. "The log. The fifth of August. What does it say."

"Six-forty in the evening," Arthur said. "A cleaning crew. Two hours. Four recurrences."

"And the crew left when?"

"Nine."

"And the recurrences."

"Seven-forty. Seven-fifty. Eight-oh-five. Eight-forty."

The senior nodded once and went back to the road with the answer, and gave his next three orders out of a page Arthur had read to him. The Tactical student began writing down the questions along with the answers. Nobody said what was happening, because in the field nothing says what is happening; the group simply moved toward the person who had stopped guessing.

Arthur kept reading. A records body reads.

He went into the annex once, near the end of the day, because there was one number in the file he could not check from a tailgate.

The file said the sound was localized to one annex. It had said that before the fifteenth, and the fifteenth had disagreed with it, and if the file was wrong about the day it could be wrong about the shape. He wanted to hear it from inside.

The stair ran down at the back, concrete, into the dark. He waited until it came. Three seconds, two voices, from below him, close enough to feel in the rail.

He did not understand it. He had one part of it now, the part the log had given him, and the part was short in a way he could feel. Something that answered the last sound it heard should have stayed where the last sound was made. This one had answered a woman a hundred meters up the road. The file was wrong about the day. It was wrong about the place too. One wrong thing in a file was a filing error. Two was a rule he did not have yet.

He stood in front of it while it did what it did. He watched it do it. None of it meant anything to him yet, except that the file would have to be corrected twice.

He went back up the stairs, and did not notice, at the time, that he had done anything at all.

The sixteenth and the seventeenth went the same way, which was the point of them.

The senior took Arthur's reading as the working account and ran the mission from it: make a sound, mark where the answer comes from, make the sound again. The sound kept answering. It answered a student at the door and a student on the roof and the Medical student walking the road with her kit on her shoulder, and each time it answered from where the sound had been, three seconds behind, in the two voices from the last loud day, which were the only two voices it had.

By the seventeenth Arthur had stopped calling it a file.

A file is a thing with a rule in it. This had a rule and half of another. The log half fit Ã¢â‚¬â€ sound in, sound back. He wrote down the half he had, underlined it twice, and left the other half blank, and put the pages in the folder with the rest.

The senior asked him for the numbers four more times. He answered four times. Nobody on the team called him an analyst, and nobody called him a clerk. They called him by his name, which in the field is the smallest promotion there is and the only one that means anything yet.

On the evening of the seventeenth the senior set the next board date at the top of the sheet and read it out loud, so that everyone heard where the day stopped.

"The eighteenth," he said. "We go back in the morning."

The sound did not stop. It was still there when the vehicles pulled out of the service road, two voices behind a wall, waiting for someone to say something to it.

They would be back on the eighteenth.

Arthur had one rule now, written twice in his notebook: **do not call a pattern a rule until the world survives the test.** The annex had given him a pattern. Tomorrow would have to decide whether it was a rule.

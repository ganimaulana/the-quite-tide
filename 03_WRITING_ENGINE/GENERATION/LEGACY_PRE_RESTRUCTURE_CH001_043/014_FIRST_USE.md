# THE DAGGER TEST

The next test was not a fight.

Arthur knew better than to learn a weapon only through someone trying to hit him with it.

He built a sequence instead.

Draw.

Step.

Turn.

Stop.

Return.

Repeat.

He changed one variable at a time.

First, stance.

Then grip.

Then foot placement.

Then speed.

The result was uncomfortable.

The dagger did not become easier.

It became more demanding as he became more precise.

At low speed, his errors were obvious. At higher speed, the same errors became dangerous.

He slowed down again.

That was the first lesson.

Speed could hide a bad movement from the person making it.

The dagger could not.

He repeated the drill until the sequence stopped feeling like five separate actions.

Not mastery.

Just continuity.

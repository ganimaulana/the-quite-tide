# THE FIRST FAILURE

The failure happened in twelve seconds.

Arthur remembered all twelve.

The exercise was simple: close distance, disarm the training opponent, step back, reset. No anomaly. No live weapon.

The first mistake was his.

The opponent's right shoulder moved. Motion Sense caught it immediately. Weight shifted to the rear foot. The hand began to come across.

Arthur moved before the strike was complete.

Too early.

The shoulder movement had been a feint.

The real attack came from the other side.

He turned late, caught the padded baton against his ribs, and lost his balance. His body recovered before he hit the ground. His feet found the floor. His hands came up.

Then the opponent stepped inside his reach.

Second mistake.

Arthur had read the movement correctly and still used the information badly.

Not because Motion Sense had failed. Because Arthur had trusted a movement without understanding the position around it.

Afterward he stood alone beside the training rack and replayed the sequence.

Shoulder shift: real.

Weight transfer: real.

First attack: false commitment.

Second attack: concealed by the first.

He had perceived the movement.

He had not understood the weapon distance.

That distinction bothered him more than the loss.

His body was strong enough to recover. His balance was good. His grip had held. None of those things mattered when he did not know what to do with them.

Arthur closed it.

The system had not failed him.

It simply had nothing to give him.

He looked at the weapon rack.

That was the problem.

He needed to learn what his body could actually do.

He stayed until the hall emptied.

He picked up a practice dagger.

The shape stopped him for a moment.

Not because it was unfamiliar.

Because it wasn't.

His fingers settled around the grip before he consciously adjusted them. A brief fragment returned: a knife in his hand, a monster's weight against him, and the certainty that the blade had to go somewhere specific.

Nothing else came with the memory.

No explanation.

Arthur looked down at the practice dagger.

He did not call it a memory of technique. He had no proof of that.

He only knew that, when he had to choose one weapon to understand first, his hand had chosen this one.

Before leaving, he wrote one line:

*Perception without technique is only better information about my own mistakes.*

Then he went back to the residential block.

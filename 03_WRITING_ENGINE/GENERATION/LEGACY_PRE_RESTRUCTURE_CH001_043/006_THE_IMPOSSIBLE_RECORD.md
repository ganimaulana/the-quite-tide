# THE IMPOSSIBLE RECORD

A case closed with the stamp in the wrong field. Clerical weather. He shelved them one by one and did not think about any of them for longer than it took to read.

He had shelved a dozen of those. He read the four lines the way he read all of them, in order, because the order was the point.

**Intake: 10 August.**
**Response: 12 August.**
**Closure: 9 August.**

He read them again.

A case cannot be closed before it is opened. It cannot be closed before it is answered. The closure sat on the slip the way a roof sits over a wall, and the date on it was one day earlier than the intake that had created the case and three days earlier than the response that had finished it. He turned the page. The compliance desk kept its own count on a separate sheet, in its own hand, and the date there was the same. The ninth of August. Twice. Two sheets, two hands, one impossible morning.

He looked, then, for the thing he was already sure he would not find: a correction slip, a re-date, a note in the margin explaining that the date was a mistake and had been fixed. There was nothing in the margin. The file had never been corrected. It had never needed to be, because no one had ever read it closely enough to see that it needed anything at all.

He put the tray aside and sat with the file for a while.

There were only four ways a date like that happened, and he tried each of them against the paper instead of against his hope. A typo: possible in one field, not in two, not on two sheets in two hands. A backfilled file: files were sometimes assembled long after the fact, the dates copied in at the end Ã¢â‚¬â€ but a backfilled file copied the dates that existed; it did not invent a morning that came before the case. A reused catalog number: he checked the index, and twenty-two had been issued once. A misfile: he checked the leaves, and they were in order, and the number matched on every page, and the closure carried the right desk's stamp.

He was left with the file. The file said the case had been closed on a morning when it had not yet been opened, by a procedure that runs only after it has been answered.

Motion Sense remained quiet in the archive. There was no movement to track, only paper and the ventilation system.

---

Margaret read it standing up, which she did not usually do.

She read the four lines twice. She turned the closure sheet over and back again. Then she set the file on the shelf between them and looked at it instead of at him.

"A date," she said.

"Two dates," he said. "Two sheets."

She was quiet for a while. Down the aisle, the low pile waited where it always waited, patient and endless, and neither of them looked at it.

"Response closes these," she said at last. "Compliance counts them. Both of those are done by people who have no reason to make a date that comes before a case. They would have to want a date that was wrong."

"Yes," he said.

"Then I don't know," she said. "And it is closed."

The word did what it always did. A closed case went to the archive and stayed there. It did not get opened again by a student; it did not get a site visit; it did not get a second look from anyone with the standing to take one. The file had already said what it was going to say. Everything after that was the institution agreeing with it, and the institution was very good at agreeing with itself.

He put the file back in the tray and finished the batch and did not mention it again that day. He thought about it, though, all afternoon, in the way he thought about everything Ã¢â‚¬â€ not by deciding it mattered, but by failing to stop turning it over.

The second notice arrived on the last day of that week.

It did not say *analyst*. It did not say *investigator*, or *specialist*, or anything that would have cost the board a decision to write. It said *attached observer*, which was a records body, which was him. He read the notice twice more, folded it, and put it in his notebook.

He did not know what was wrong with the twenty-second file. He did not know what had happened at the service stair in the west annex, or who had closed a case three mornings before it was opened, or why that impossible morning had been written down twice, in two hands, as though someone had wanted it to be found.

He knew it had been written twice.

That was enough to keep. He wrote it in his notebook and went to find the first brief for the survey, because a records body was expected to know the file before the file was read aloud.

The impossible part was not that the Academy had made a mistake. Mistakes happened. The impossible part was that two independent records agreed on the same mistake before the case existed.

The next morning he trained before breakfast.

He ran the east path, worked through bodyweight exercises, then repeated the basic movement drills from the Academy.

His body was not the problem.

He recovered quickly. His balance was good. His strength was better than his Rank F paperwork suggested.

Technique was different.

When the drill required a real fighting sequence, his feet were late and his hands were uncertain. Motion Sense told him what was moving. It did not tell his body how to answer.

Arthur wrote that distinction down.

**Strong body. Weak technique.**

Then he ran the drill again.

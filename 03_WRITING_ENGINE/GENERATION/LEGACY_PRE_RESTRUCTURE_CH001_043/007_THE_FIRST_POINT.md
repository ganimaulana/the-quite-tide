They went out at 0900 in two vehicles, north along the coast road, past the last of the campus and onto the service road that ran behind the old waterfront. He stood at the door and held the file.

Motion Sense had been active since they left the campus, not as a warning but as a second layer of awareness. Vehicles moved. Recon moved on the roof. Combat moved along the perimeter. Arthur could separate those movements from one another without looking directly at each source. None belonged to the phenomenon. He read the file again. It still said stationary.

The first hour was the brief's hour, and it was spent on the wrong thing.

The senior walked the moving perimeter himself, twice, the map folded into his coat, and both times he came back to the vehicles with the same line: nothing on the road, nothing on the water side, and the annex sitting there the way it had been sitting there for forty years. The Recon student called down from the roof that the building read as a building. The Medical student set her kit on the tailgate and read the file over Arthur's shoulder and asked him, once, why the survey board had sent six people to a building that did not move. Arthur said he did not know. It was true. He did not say the other thing, which was that the six people were there because a curriculum had guessed and a filing clerk had been attached.

He walked the east wall while the others walked the road.

It was brick, with a basement window low in it, barred, clouded over with old paint. The stair the file named ran down behind it, inside. He put his hand flat on the brick and held it there the way he put his hand on paper at the counter, to feel whether it was cold, and the brick was brick, and there was nothing in it but the morning's warmth and the sound of the road.

He checked the file against the wall. The file's zone was the annex; the annex was the annex; the coordinates on the intake sheet landed on the south corner, six meters around from where he stood. He marked the corner and walked to it and looked through the barred window at the top three steps of a concrete stair and nothing else.

Behind him, the senior told the flanks to widen again, because the thing wandered, and the thing did not wander, and so far the only true thing anyone had done that morning was stand in the right place with the wrong plan.

The Tactical student had been counting the recurrences aloud, the way the brief told him to, and at 1102 he finished the count and said, in his ordinary voice, to no one, at the vehicles, fifty meters from the wall:

"That is the fourth one. Two and a bit minutes apart. It is not moving."

Three seconds later, the annex said it back.

It was not the two voices from the wrong day. It was the Tactical student's voice, in his own flat tone, out from behind the east wall, saying *It is not moving*, exactly the way he had said it, and then stopping.

Something changed while he stood there.

It had been warm all morning, evenly warm, the way it was always warm. Somewhere in the last minutes it stopped being evenly warm. It had a side. One face of it, the face laid against his hip, had gone hotter than the rest, and the hotter face was turned toward the east wall, toward the stair, and when he shifted his weight the heat did not stay where it was; it stayed where the stair was.

He turned a quarter turn, testing it, the way he tested a date. The hot side swung round with him and settled back against his hip, still facing the stair, the way a needle settles when you set it down ÃƒÂ¢Ã¢â€šÂ¬Ã¢â‚¬Â a moment of turning, and then one direction, and no argument.

He turned the other way. The hot side came round again. Always the stair. Always the same few degrees of the same wall.

He stood very still and looked at the wall and let it happen.

The contradiction in the file had finally become physical. The record said stationary. The recurrence had answered a man fifty meters away. Arthur noticed the mismatch and wrote it down before deciding what it meant.

For less than a second, the wall disappeared. He saw the concrete stair as if from above, and at the bottom of it stood a person-shaped shadow facing away from him. Then the wall returned.

Arthur blinked. Pressure gathered behind his eyes. A thin ringing filled his left ear, and for one brief moment the empty stair still seemed occupied. He looked down toward the basement entrance. Nothing. He waited. The afterimage faded.

His first interpretation was immediate: someone had been standing at the bottom of the stair.

He wrote that down, then stopped. It was an interpretation, not an observation. The observation was narrower: **movement detected at the stair; source not yet identified.**

He did not trust the movement yet. The file said the annex played back a conversation from the wrong day. The file said nothing about the stair being anything other than a stair. Motion Sense had caught movement before he consciously saw the figure, but movement alone was not an explanation.

He wrote down the time of that, too: 1118. He wrote what Motion Sense had actually given him: **movement detected at stair; direction downward; duration less than one second.** He did not write what it meant. He put the notebook away and kept watching the stair.

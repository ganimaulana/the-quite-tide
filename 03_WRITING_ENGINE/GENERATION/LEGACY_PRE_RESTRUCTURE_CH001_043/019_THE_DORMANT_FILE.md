# THE DORMANT FILE

**Scene one â€” the second pass.**

The request arrived on a Tuesday with a different header from the one Arthur had expected.

He had expected a records correction. The thimble was still in his drawer, and the returns tray still showed an impossible pattern: three removals, three returns, then faster returns after the term had made him certain and the certainty had failed. He had copied the observations and requested a controlled room with two witnesses.

The header said **MIXED MISSION â€” ROTATION QUOTA 2**.

Under it were the names of the divisions assigned to the new pass. Combat provided the formal lead. Tactical provided the reading lead. Medical provided one student. Logistics provided one student. Operations Support provided an attached records observer.

His name was at the bottom.

The formal lead read the page in the briefing room and stopped at that line.

"This is an anomaly investigation," he said.

"Yes," Arthur said.

"Not an archive inspection."

"The object is in the archive."

The formal lead looked at him, then at the page, as though one had been placed there by mistake. He was a Combat student with a worn field patch and a habit of planting both hands on the table. He had led the waterfront pass and disliked Arthur's four-column account, though he had used it when the building answered the road.

The Tactical student sat opposite with the log open, an instrument case at one boot.

"Rotation quota is met," the Tactical student said. "Medical and Logistics have not been on this template before. We are not changing the case because the clerk wants a private test. We are changing the team because the roster says we change the team."

The formal lead's eyes went to the word *clerk* and stayed there for half a second.

"The attached observer does not command," he said.

"I know," Arthur said.

"You read the file. You do not redesign the mission."

"I have not redesigned it."

"Then tell us what we are investigating."

The Tactical student asked without ceremony. Previous teams had asked what the file said. This asked what Arthur thought it meant.

He opened his folder.

"It returns to the tray where its first intake was recorded," he said. "I thought it followed the hand that took it. That failed. My correction is that later custody does not change the first recorded location. I need to test whether a new, properly witnessed transfer changes that."

The formal lead tapped the table once.

"That is a theory."

"Yes."

"And if it fails?"

"The theory fails."

The Tactical student wrote that down.


The formal lead glanced from the Medical student, who had called Arthur's mark a contact burn, to the Logistics student and their compartmented kit.

"We do the test in the returns annex," the formal lead said. "No contract use. No unscheduled contact with the object. The observer supplies the file and nothing else."

The Tactical student closed the log.

"You will give us the read before we set the room," they said to Arthur.

The formal lead turned his head.

"We will review the read."

"That is not what I said."

The formal lead's hands flattened on the table.

Arthur put the prediction sheet on top of the folder. He had written and signed it before the test: if a valid transfer changed the return point, his reading failed. He would not call it true because it felt complete.

They left at 1400.

**Scene two â€” the return address.**

The returns annex stood behind the records block. Its trays had numbered places, paper labels, and a camera with a blind angle under the lowest shelf. The Logistics student checked the camera and copied the tray numbers before anyone touched the thimble.

"The first recorded location is tray four," Arthur said.

He placed the thimble in the tray from a sealed pouch. The procedure kept a hand from becoming an explanation.

The formal lead stood at the loading door, the Medical student in the corridor. Logistics marked the tray, shelf, and threshold with chalk. Tactical kept the log beside Arthur.

"Initial state," Arthur said. "Original intake: tray four. No later transfer recorded."

The Tactical student wrote it.

The Logistics student lifted the thimble with tongs, placed it in tray two, and entered the transfer on the custody card. The card was time-stamped and signed by both students. They left the corridor.

When they returned, the thimble was back in tray four.

The formal lead moved first, but Arthur raised a hand.

"Do not touch it."

The formal lead stopped.

No one had crossed the corridor. Frame by frame, the camera showed the thimble in tray two after the transfer and in tray four when they returned. The camera did not show it moving between them.

"It returned to the first recorded position," Arthur said.

The formal lead looked at him.

"The original intake?"

"The later entry says tray two. The thimble is back in tray four."

"It could have been moved outside the camera's view."

"Then the test is not yet sufficient."

The Tactical student wrote that down.

They reset the room. Logistics placed the thimble in tray four again. This time they entered a transfer to tray one, with the Medical student witnessing and a different custodian signing. They sealed the card before moving the thimble.

The formal lead said, "Change the recorded destination and the person handling it. Keep the original intake fixed."

"The original and new locations have to disagree," Arthur said. "Otherwise we are testing the handler, not the record."

"The first transfer changed both."

"This separates them."

"He is right," they said. "We need one variable moved."

The formal lead did not answer. They put the thimble in tray one under the valid new entry, with the Medical student witnessing.

When they checked again, it was in tray four. The formal lead did not accept a conclusion from one return.

"My first reading failed," Arthur said. "I treated the current hand as the important one. The record keeps pointing to the first intake."

"That is not in your prediction."

"No. It is the reason to keep the result separate."

"What stays constant across the returns?"

Arthur opened the returns register. Its sensor logged each move. Every prior return in his notes ended at tray four, the first recorded intake. Different hands, different destinations, same return address.

The stable fact was the original intake location. Later entries changed custody but not the point to which the thimble returned.

"One more transfer," Arthur said. "Same destination, new handler. Keep the original intake unchanged."

The formal lead signed the custody card. The Tactical student witnessed it. Logistics moved the thimble to tray two, and the new handler locked the room.

When they unlocked the room, the thimble was in tray four.

The Tactical student said, "It returns to the first recorded location, even after valid transfers."

"No more transfers," Arthur said. "If it remains or returns to tray four, the first location remains the best-supported Rule."

The formal lead's head came up. "Do not improvise another test."

They left the thimble in tray two and stopped making entries. From outside the camera's blind angle they watched it return to tray four and stay there. The earlier returns, two valid transfers, and final observation pointed to the original intake, not the last hand or latest destination.

The Tactical student closed the log.

"Rule understood," they said.

It was a filing status, not praise. Arthur preferred it that way.

The formal lead picked up the mission sheet. "We report two returns to the original tray."

"And the stable condition is the original intake," the Tactical student said.

"You may write your opinion in the appendix. The lead report will state what was observed."

The lead report would be true and incomplete. A file could leave the important part outside its margins.

The Medical student broke the silence.

"There is another question. Why was this object in the returns tray before any of us brought it there?"

Nobody answered.

The question followed them back to the records block.

**Scene three â€” the file beside the file.**

Arthur filed the thimble in the controlled locker, attaching the custody record. The formal lead signed the chain-of-custody line. Tactical added a page to the mission log: *returns to its first recorded intake location despite later custody transfers*.

The Medical and Logistics students left with the equipment. Their rotation quota was met. The mission would dissolve with its paperwork; only the record would carry forward.

The Tactical student stayed.

"You said the first return was already in the tray," they said.

"Yes."

"Before your controlled test."

"Yes."

"Then the source is not the tray."

Arthur looked at the annex log. The first entry came from a records box transferred from the east storage block. The box number had been corrected twice. The correction marks were old enough that the ink had browned.

He pulled the transfer register. The box had arrived with a cross-index clipped to its manifest: a reference to a separate anomaly record, transferred out of the east storage block years earlier. The reference pointed to a docket, not a box.

The docket line read:

**T3-adjacent Seep â€” Docket 41-B â€” DORMANT.**

The formal lead had said no archive inspection. The archive had put the file beside the object anyway.

Arthur requested the docket. The summary gave nothing beyond the filed tier and the status. An attached memorandum disputed the DORMANT filing and cited a method note from a **previous holder**: keep the original intake record, then compare it with later returns. The note established no identity and no cause. It was a lead, not a solution.

The Tactical student read over his shoulder.

"That is a lead," they said.

"It is a source implication."

"You are going to call it a lead in the report."

Arthur looked at the docket number, then at the memorandum.

"I will call it what the page supports."

The formal lead returned to the room. He saw the open docket and stopped.

"We are done."

"The mission is done," Arthur said. "The dormant file is not resolved."

"That file is dormant."

"The status is a filing, not a finding. The memorandum disputes it."

The formal lead stepped closer. "You are attached to this mission as a records observer. You do not reopen a case because a neighboring slip uses a similar term."

"I did not reopen it. I found the docket beside the current file. The method note belongs in the handoff because it gives us a comparison to make."

The Tactical student closed the docket.

"Put it in the handoff," they said. "Not as a conclusion. As a chained lead."

The formal lead's resentment remained. Now he also knew the lead would enter the record whether he liked it or not.

"Appendix only," he said.

"Appendix is still a record," Arthur said.

The formal lead's hands flattened on the desk.

Arthur did not identify the source or treat the disputed filing as fact. He recorded the observation, test, corrected Rule, and docket number in separate sections.

At the bottom he wrote:

*The second phenomenon returns to its first recorded intake location despite later custody transfers. An adjacent T3-adjacent Seep docket is filed DORMANT; its memorandum disputes that classification and cites a previous holder's method. Identity and causal relationship not established. Follow-up required.*

The Tactical student signed beneath it; the formal lead signed the mission report above.

Arthur returned the thimble to the controlled locker and closed the door. The adjacent dormant docket remained open to challenge but closed to inspection, enough to direct the next request and not enough to answer it.

He had not solved the dormant case. He had found the direction in which the next question would have to travel.

That was not an answer.

It was better than one.

Permission came with rules: no live edge, no unsupervised combat, return every weapon to its marked place, report every injury.

Arthur accepted all of them.

He started with the dagger.

The dagger was fast. It punished hesitation but rewarded small movements. Motion Sense gave him information about wrist rotation and changes in grip, but the range was short enough that a correct read could still arrive too late.

The grip felt wrong in several small ways. Arthur corrected them one at a time rather than trusting the familiarity in his hands.

He had used a knife at Ravenhurst.

That did not mean he knew how to use a dagger.

It meant only that he had a reason to test the connection.

The spear was different.

Its movement began farther from the body. A small adjustment at the rear hand changed the point at the other end. Arthur's strength mattered more here, but strength without distance control created its own problem.

The axe punished another mistake.

It carried weight through the swing. Stopping it required more than stopping a hand. Arthur learned that by letting the practice head pull his shoulders forward and nearly stepping over his own foot.

The bow was the strangest.

There was almost no useful comparison between drawing a bow and reading a strike. The movement happened through tension rather than a traveling weapon. His body understood the pull quickly. His timing did not.

He recorded everything.

Not scores.

Not levels.

Not a ranking.

Just observations.

**Dagger:** short range, fast correction; familiar hand position, unproven technique.

**Spear:** distance dominates.

**Axe:** momentum dominates.

**Bow:** tension dominates.

Then he reached the sword.

He picked it up.

Nothing happened.

No voice.

No Contract offer.

No sudden knowledge.

The sword was simply a sword.

Arthur almost laughed.

That was exactly what he needed.

He began with the same drills.

The first ten minutes were ordinary.

Then he noticed something he had not noticed with the other weapons.

The sword did not merely extend his reach.

It made his own movement visible to him.

When his wrist turned too early, the blade drifted.

When his hips moved late, the blade lagged.

When his weight settled correctly, the weapon seemed to arrive where his body had already decided to be.

That was not mastery.

It was a useful relationship.

Arthur put the sword down.

He would test it again tomorrow.

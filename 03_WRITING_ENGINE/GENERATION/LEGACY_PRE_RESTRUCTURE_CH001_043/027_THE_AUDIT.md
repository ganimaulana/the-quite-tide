# THE AUDIT

**Scene one â€” prove the artifact does anything.**

The auditor sat across from Arthur in a room with no windows. He was a civilian in a grey coat, and he had a list of questions that began with the artifact and ended with Arthur's sanity. Between the two, there were forty minutes of silence, because the auditor believed that silence was a question too.

"The Academy claims you have an object that returns records," the auditor said.

"I have an object that returns to recorded locations," Arthur said.

"Prove it."

Arthur took the thimble from its pouch. He placed it on the table. He took a blank custody card, filled it out for tray one, and sealed it. Then he slid the thimble to the far end of the table.

They waited.

The thimble returned to the center, where the card had been placed.

The auditor wrote something down. "That is one demonstration."

"That is the only demonstration I can give you without exposing someone."

"Expose who?"

"Anyone whose hand touches it after me."

The auditor's pen stopped. He looked at the thimble, then at Arthur, then at the thimble again. The room was very quiet. Somewhere beyond the wall, a cart went past, and its wheels made a sound like a door closing.

"Explain," he said.

"The object returns to the last accepted record. If I write a card, the card becomes the record, and the object returns to it. If another student writes a card, the object returns to theirs â€” but only if their card is accepted by the annex." Arthur tapped the sealed card. "Acceptance requires an authorized custodian. I am the authorized custodian for this object. So the only cards that count are mine."

"Then the object is bound to you."

"No. It is bound to the record. I am simply the only person authorized to make one."

The auditor wrote that down, and this time he wrote for longer.

"Show me the acceptance stamp," he said.

Arthur turned the card over. On the back was a small square signature block, initialed by Margaret, dated the previous week.

"The senior clerk signs these," the auditor said.

"She signs them because she is the annex authority. I write the card. She accepts it. The object follows the accepted record, not the written one."

"Then the clerk controls the object."

"No. The clerk controls the acceptance. If she stopped signing, I could not make a new record, and the object would return to the last one she had already accepted." Arthur slid the card back into the file. "That is the limit. I do not control the object. I control a channel to it. The channel can be closed."

The auditor looked at him for a long moment. "You are describing a bureaucracy."

"I am describing the object. It happens to be organized like one."

"That is the strangest sentence I have heard this year."

"You should read the annex's filing manual. It is stranger."

**Scene two â€” sideways proof.**

"Your report says the object is bound to your records." The auditor flipped a page. "Prove it is bound to yours and not to the object itself."

Arthur reached into his file and pulled a transfer slip from a student who had handled the thimble three days earlier. The slip was ordinary. The thimble had not returned to that student once.

"This person filed it under their name," Arthur said. "The object did not return to them. It returned to me."

"Because you kept it."

"Because I filed the first accepted intake." Arthur placed the slip on the table. "The record is what matters. The hand does not."

The auditor read the slip. He read Arthur's file. He read the custodian's file. The two versions did not match, because they could not both be true.

"There is a lie somewhere," the auditor said.

"No. There is a wrong file and a right file. The wrong file says the object follows hands. The right file says it follows records."

"Prove the right file is right."

"I just did. The student handled it. The object ignored him. The only difference between him and me is the acceptance stamp."

The auditor was quiet for a moment. Then he said, "You are asking me to believe that a filing rule governs a supernatural object."

"I am asking you to believe the object obeys the same rule as the Academy. It treats an unstamped record as irrelevant. So does the Academy. So does the audit you are holding." Arthur nodded at the auditor's folder. "You are the same kind of thing as the thimble. You both ignore what is not filed."

The auditor did not smile, but he wrote again, and the writing took a while.

---

**Scene three â€” watchful neutrality.**

The auditor closed his folder. He did not smile. He did not accuse. He wrote one sentence in the margin of his report: **Subject's record-keeping is accurate. The object's behavior is unresolved. Recommend continued observation without restriction.**

Arthur read it.

"You did not say it is supernatural," Arthur said.

"I did not say it is not."

"Then what is it?"

"It is a file," the auditor said. "The Academy understands files."

He stood, put on his coat, and stopped at the door.

"One more thing," he said. "The Combat section lead who requested this audit wanted a different result. She wanted me to find that the object does nothing, so that the Irregular cohort could be returned to the bottom of the ledger. I am telling you this because it will be in her file, not mine, and she will know you know."

"Why tell me?"

"Because you will file it correctly," the auditor said. "Most people would file it as an insult. You will file it as a fact. That is why the recommendation is observation without restriction."

He left.

Arthur sat in the windowless room for a minute after the door closed. The auditor had been the first person from outside the annex to ask him a question that was not a test. He had wanted to know how the object worked, not whether Arthur was dangerous. That was a different kind of audit, and it had a different kind of cost. It had required him to say the method out loud.

He wrote the method down, once, in full, in the plainest words he could manage. Then he read it back and crossed out every sentence that sounded like a claim. What remained was short: *the object returns to the last accepted record; acceptance requires the annex authority; the object ignores hands and follows cards.* Four lines. It had taken him three months to be able to write them, and one afternoon to be asked to.

**Scene four â€” the folder.**

Arthur sat alone with the thimble and the folder. The audit had found nothing. The appendix had been read. The institution was now watching him with the same watchfulness Hayes had shown, except this watchfulness had a form number.

He filed the auditor's report under his own name. The folder was thin. The folder was enough.

He wrote a note on the cover: **AUDIT 2 â€” RESULT: NO FINDING. ACTION: OBSERVATION.** Then, underneath, in smaller letters: **The auditor was honest. The section lead was not. Both are now in the record.**

He put the folder in the case and closed it. Outside, it had begun to rain. The thimble sat on the table, where he had left it, because he had learned that it did not matter where he left it â€” it would return to the record, and the record was wherever he had decided it should be. That was the whole of it. The whole of it was a filing rule, and the academy had spent an afternoon trying to prove that a filing rule could not be true.

He picked up the thimble and put it back in its pouch. Then he went to dinner, because the dining hall closed at seven, and the record of a missed dinner would have been the only thing anyone remembered.

At the dining hall, Clarke had saved him a seat. Owen had saved him a cup of tea.

"How did it go?" Owen asked.

"He filed me as unresolved."

"That is good?"

"It is better than resolved." Arthur drank the tea. "A resolved file is closed. An unresolved file is read every quarter. I would rather be read than closed."

Clarke pushed his folder across the table. "The next exercise is posted."

Arthur opened it. The exercise was a chained mission, which meant it followed from the last one: the waterfront phenomenon had left a second phenomenon in its wake, and the second phenomenon was harder. He read the page twice.

"Pram is on the roster," he said.

"Naturally," Clarke said.

Arthur closed the folder. The tea was warm. Outside, the rain had stopped. Somewhere in the Academy, an auditor was writing a report, and a section lead was reading it, and neither of them knew what he was about to write next.

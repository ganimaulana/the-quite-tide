# NARRATIVE FLOW / READABILITY AUDIT — CHAPTERS 001–004

> **Mode:** Audit only. No chapter, baseline, canon, lock, or engine file was modified.
> **Scope:** Continuous-novel flow, chronology, spatial/causal bridges, natural English, sentence rhythm, TNE-transfer load, and reader orientation.

## Executive Diagnosis

The primary problem is **multiple layers**:

1. **Scene-transition layer:** several hard cuts are technically understandable but omit one orienting bridge (date, elapsed time, reason for arrival, or what Arthur carries forward).
2. **Chapter-to-chapter continuity layer:** 001→002 and 002→003 rely too heavily on headers and reader reconstruction. 003→004 contains a concrete time-count inconsistency.
3. **Sentence-level natural-English layer:** recurring compressed or translated-sounding constructions sometimes make the reader parse the sentence before receiving its meaning.
4. **Generation-protocol layer:** the current workflow validates chapter-local function and canon but lacks a mandatory **date/location/causal bridge ledger** and a natural-English pass.

This is not a recommendation to slow the novel. The needed change is: **skip unnecessary events, not necessary causality.**

### Chronology Finding CF-01 — MAJOR FLOW DEFECT
- **Location:** Chapter 004, opening paragraph.
- **Current:** `Arthur had been working the low pile for six days`.
- **Problem:** It conflicts with the chapter's 5 September date and the Ch002/Ch003 continuity state. At most approximately four calendar days have elapsed since 1 September, and approximately three since Records work began on 2 September.
- **Impact:** The reader cannot build a reliable calendar without overriding the prose with metadata.
- **Action:** Existing-chapter bounded correction required before treating the four-chapter run as fully continuous. Do not invent a replacement duration during this audit.

## G. Sentence Flow / Rhythm

### Positive flow
- Ch001 assessment scene uses short procedural units effectively: `The printer clicked.` / `He noticed.`
- Ch002's social beat uses sentence length to reveal the hierarchy rather than explain it.
- Ch003's station sequence moves quickly without becoming a list.
- Ch004's compliance dialogue has clean turn-taking and good pressure.

### Over-compression patterns
- Repeated constructions of `He ... He ... He ...` are sometimes effective, but clusters in Ch004's directional test (`He closed...`, `He took...`, `He stood...`, `He took...`) can feel procedural rather than naturally flowing.
- Ch002 frequently chains clauses with `and`, especially in the mess and gym scenes. The rhythm is readable but occasionally sounds translated.
- Ch003 uses multiple one-line result statements (`Arthur read it.` / `No rank effect.`) effectively once, but the broader style risks becoming a template if repeated.
- Ch004's final three-sentence closure (`The correction had been made. The correction had been ignored. And the stone...`) is clear, but deliberately modular rather than naturally narrative.

### Sentence-level diagnosis
Short sentences are not inherently a problem. The issue is when a short sentence removes the causal connector the reader needs, forcing reconstruction. The strongest candidates are the transitions and idiomatic phrases listed above, not the entire prose rhythm.

## H. TNE Transfer Audit

### Good adaptation
- Information-first openings that begin inside a task.
- Tactical cognition shown through selection, filing, and testing.
- Hard cuts used to skip irrelevant travel and waiting.
- Functional dialogue carrying status.
- Withheld institutional information used as mystery structure.

### Over-application
- **Hard cuts without a time marker:** 001→002, 002→003, and 002 Scene 2→3.
- **Compressed transitions:** the prose often assumes the reader will use the chapter header to bridge a day or work shift.
- **Staccato modularity:** repeated short declarative units sometimes prioritize impact over natural English connection.
- **Information-first phrasing:** several sentences turn a human action into an abstract file/process statement before giving the concrete action.

The remedy is not more travel or school-life filler. Add one orienting clause where causality changes; smooth only sentences that make readers reconstruct meaning.

## K. Primary Diagnosis

The quality problem is **multiple layers**, ordered by urgency:

1. **Generation protocol / continuity ledger:** no mandatory scene-level date, time, location, and bridge field; this produced CF-01 and the header-dependent jumps.
2. **Scene-transition layer:** necessary causal bridges are sometimes omitted to preserve speed.
3. **Sentence-level natural English:** compressed idioms and abstract substitutions recur across all four chapters.
4. **Writing-engine transfer guidance:** hard cuts and short declaratives need a natural-English guardrail, not removal.

The chapter functions themselves are generally sound. The dominant defect is that chapter-local QA can pass while the four chapters do not always read as one continuous novel.

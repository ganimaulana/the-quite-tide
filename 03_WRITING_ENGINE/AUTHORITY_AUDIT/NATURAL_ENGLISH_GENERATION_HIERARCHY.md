## PART V — PROPOSED REPLACEMENT AUTHORITY HIERARCHY

**Current problem:** the engine's priority chain ("NATURAL ENGLISH > CLEAR MEANING > …") exists inside Module 01, but the mechanism modules (03/04/09/13) read as executable defaults, so generation optimizes mechanism density first.

**Proposed generation-time hierarchy (highest wins; lower levels may not override higher):**

| Rank | Level | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **NATURAL ENGLISH** | The sentence must read as naturally written English. Nothing below may justify unnatural phrasing. |
| **2** | **QUIET TIDE NARRATIVE IDENTITY** | The prose must feel like Quiet Tide (restraint, institutional neutrality, observation, priced consequence) — not a generic novel and not translated prose. |
| **3** | **QUIET TIDE CANON / POV / MYSTERY / CHARACTER RULES** | Locks, POV discipline, mystery fences, character truth, terminology. |
| **4** | **SCENE FUNCTION AND INFORMATION MOVEMENT** | The scene must have a reason to exist and must move information/state. |
| **5** | **SELECTIVE TNE STRUCTURAL MECHANISMS** | Only the **A**-class mechanisms (see reclassification) may shape structure; **B** may apply only if Level 1 survives. |
| **6** | **TNE QUANTITATIVE DATA = CALIBRATION ONLY** | Numbers are never targets (8.7 words, 34.2%, hook %, question %, em-dash %, scene counts). |

**Conflict rule:** when a Level-5 mechanism would force a Level-1 violation, **Level 1 wins** and the mechanism is dropped for that sentence. **Concision never outranks natural flow; fragment and staccato are never required; metaphor is never required; one-line paragraphs are never required.**

## PART VII — NATURAL ENGLISH AS A PRIMARY GENERATION GATE

**Move naturalness from downstream QA into a primary generation constraint.** Before accepting **every paragraph**, the generator asks the five questions below. If any answer is **NO**, the paragraph is rewritten **before continuing** (not patched later).

| Gate | Question |
|---|---|
| **NATURALNESS** | Would a competent native English novelist naturally write this? |
| **VOICE** | Does it sound like Quiet Tide rather than translated prose? |
| **FUNCTION** | Does the sentence have a reason to exist? |
| **CHARACTER** | Would Arthur actually think / say / notice this? |
| **FLOW** | Does the paragraph read naturally as a whole? |

**Explicit non-requirements (must NOT be imposed):** short sentences · fragments · staccato · metaphors · one-line paragraphs.

**Numeric de-targeting (required):** restate that 8.7 words/sentence, 34.2% short sentences, hook percentages, question-per-chapter quotas, em-dash frequencies, and scene/word counts are **calibration/QA data only**, never generation targets. Reframe the anti-drift "<10 words" cue as a diagnostic with a "natural variation" qualifier.

## PART VIII — DESCRIPTION REPAIR PRINCIPLES

**Author-directed principles (recorded):**

1. **Description follows perception.** Detail enters because Arthur perceives it, not because the author wants atmosphere.
2. **Perception follows POV.** Only what this POV could notice, in the order a person would notice it.
3. **Description must have informational or emotional function** (clue, positioning, characterization, cost, or feeling) — else cut.
4. **Concrete nouns and ordinary verbs are preferred** over abstractions and Latinate nominals.
5. **Natural comparisons are allowed but not required.** A simile must be what *this* character would naturally reach for.
6. **No automatic "the way X…" construction.** Treated as a tic; ration hard.
7. **No decorative metaphor merely to create atmosphere.**
8. **No compression merely to imitate TNE.**
9. **No sentence fragmentation unless natural in context.**
10. **Description should sound like a person noticing something, not an author demonstrating style.**

**Four-step filter:** *(1) what he notices → (2) why he notices it → (3) what it tells him → (4) what changes because of it.*

### Description calibration examples (BEFORE / AFTER)
> **Calibration only. These "after" lines are examples; no Chapter 001 file is modified.** "Before" lines are quoted from the current/legacy Chapter 001 as evidence.

**D1**
- **BEFORE:** "Whatever the instruments did, they did it the same way every time, the way a punch clock takes its picture."
- **AFTER (calibration):** "The instruments never varied. Whatever they did, they did it the same way every time."

**D2**
- **BEFORE:** "No one looked pleased and no one looked afraid. … The room allowed no doubt."
- **AFTER (calibration):** "No one in the room looked pleased or afraid. They waited their turn, and nothing about the procedure suggested it might go wrong."

**D3**
- **BEFORE:** "…like a man who had reached for a drawer and found it empty."
- **AFTER (calibration):** "Something moved across his face and was gone before Arthur could read it."

**D4**
- **BEFORE:** "He had stopped noticing that months ago, the way a person stops noticing a clock that runs fast."
- **AFTER (calibration):** "He had stopped noticing it months ago."

**D5**
- **BEFORE:** "…warm and quiet and entirely unexplained."
- **AFTER (calibration):** "The stone in his pocket was warm, the way it always was."

## PART IX — DIALOGUE REPAIR PRINCIPLES

**Author-directed principles (recorded):**

1. **Dialogue responds to the previous line** (or conspicuously evades it — evasion is still a response).
2. **Speakers have a reason to speak** — never because the author must explain something.
3. **Characters may be incomplete, indirect, hesitant, repetitive, casual, formal, or blunt where appropriate.**
4. **Dialogue is not a database.**
5. **Dialogue is not an exposition dump.**
6. **Characters do not announce information they would naturally assume the other person already knows.**
7. **Institutional speech may be formal without becoming robotic** (formal ≠ unnatural).
8. **Each recurring character has a recognizable speech pattern** (vocabulary, rhythm, concerns, assumptions — not catchphrases).
9. **Do not compress dialogue simply to increase pace.**
10. **Natural conversational rhythm overrides TNE rhythm.**

**Register separation:** *readout register* (forms/procedures — permitted, e.g., a clerk processing a sheet) · *institutional speech* (formal but connective) · *ordinary speech* (Arthur, peers, family). **Never let one bleed into another.**

### Dialogue calibration examples (BEFORE / AFTER)
> **Calibration only. No Chapter 001 file is modified.**

**X1**
- **BEFORE:** "Family keepsake," he said. "Stone seal, uncarved. Appraised 1987 — the record's attached."
- **AFTER (calibration):** "It's a family thing," he said. "My grandmother's. There's an old appraisal with it, if you need to see it."

**X2**
- **BEFORE:** "Subject, age seventeen. Classification, Irregular."
- **AFTER (calibration):** The clerk read from the sheet without looking up. "Seventeen. Classification, Irregular." *(keeps the readout register but frames it as a person speaking)*

**X3**
- **BEFORE:** "Annex four," the supervisor said. "Records and Archives. You report there, not to the barracks. Quarters are in the east block."
- **AFTER (calibration):** "You're at Annex four," the supervisor said. "Someone from the records office will meet you. You report to the annex, not the barracks, and your quarters are in the east block."

**X4**
- **BEFORE (ch3 reference):** Margaret's explanation of the review and the sealed file.
- **AFTER (calibration):** "Nobody tells you what it's for," Arthur said. "Nobody tells anyone," she said. "You'll work it out." *(responds, withholds, no manual)*

**X5**
- **BEFORE:** "Is the ERROR re-measured?" / "Annual re-examination. It's on the sheet."
- **AFTER (calibration):** "Do they test it again?" Arthur asked. / "Once a year," the clerk said. "It's on the sheet."

## PART X — ARTHUR INTERNAL NARRATION

**Purpose:** his thoughts are analytical **because that is his character**, not because TNE requires "calculation prose." **Analytical ≠ fragmented.**

### The five thought types (each has a different job)

| Type | Job | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **TACTICAL THOUGHT** | Options, timing, distances, fallback during movement | Fast, but in **connected** English; compress steps freely; no slogan lines. |
| **OBSERVATIONAL THOUGHT** | Name what he sees | Plain, concrete; one clause may suffice; no thesis. |
| **EMOTIONAL THOUGHT** | Register feeling | One flat line maximum (canon M63); behavior before explanation; no antithesis flourish. |
| **INFERENCE** | Draw a conclusion from evidence | Must trace to observation/test/document (provenance); state plainly; **no "clever" aphorism**. |
| **DECISION** | Commit to an action | Explicit and brief; may be its own short sentence; never a punchline. |

### Rules
1. Every thought beat must **do work** (hypothesis, interpretation, self-correction, decision).
2. Thought may run **longer than a fragment** when the reasoning is genuinely stepwise; methodical pacing is allowed (e.g., a multi-hour analysis beat).
3. **No slogan construction.** A thought may not be written for the shape of the paragraph ending.
4. **No echo-declaratives** ("It had measured what it could measure. It had not pretended to measure more.") and **no contrast templates** ("not X, but Y"; "X was not the problem. Y was.").
5. Emotional peaks carry **no questions** (reads as evasion) — canon S7.
6. Thought marking stays consistent manuscript-wide (typography deferred K28).

### Calibration examples
> **Calibration only. No chapter modified.** The audit specifically named the pattern where a plain observation is followed by slogan-like narration.

- **BEFORE:** *"The machine had hesitated."* then *"He filed it." / "That was the whole conversation."*
- **AFTER (calibration):** "The machine had hesitated before it returned the reading. He wrote the time down, because he did not have a word for it yet, and a file needed a word." *(connected English; the observation is followed by a concrete action and an in-voice reasoning clause — no slogan)*

- **BEFORE:** "Two readings and a stamp. It was, he thought, a fair system."
- **AFTER (calibration):** "Two readings and a stamp. He supposed it was as fair as any system that had to decide something about him."

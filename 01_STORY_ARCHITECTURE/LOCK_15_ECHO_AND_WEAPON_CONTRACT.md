## 2. Early Echo

The Uncarved Seal begins producing **Echo fragments before Daichi understands what the artifact is** and before he enters the Academy.

Echo is deliberately limited. It may present as:

- a brief silhouette;
- a one-second visual fragment;
- a partial scene;
- a position or movement that disappears immediately;
- a short sensory impression;
- another small fragment of information relevant to a phenomenon.

Echo **does not explain itself** and does not give Daichi a complete solution. Daichi may initially interpret an Echo as a strange memory, fatigue, coincidence, or an unexplained visual event.

The reader should understand that the event happened literally, while Daichi does not yet understand its mechanism.

### Echo trigger — Recognition + Proximity + Investigative Intent

Echo is not a passive scanner and does not activate merely because Daichi holds the Uncarved Seal.

The intended trigger requires three conditions to converge:

1. **Recognition:** Daichi notices a meaningful inconsistency or anomaly that ordinary explanation does not adequately resolve.
2. **Proximity:** the Uncarved Seal is sufficiently close to the relevant SP/anomalous trace, object, phenomenon, or contaminated record.
3. **Investigative intent:** Daichi actively tries to understand what is wrong rather than merely noticing it.

When these conditions align, the Seal **may** produce an Echo. The trigger is not guaranteed, and Daichi cannot simply command the artifact to provide an answer.

This makes Echo reward observation and investigation rather than curiosity alone or random activation.

### Echo side effect — Residual Sensory Distortion

Every meaningful Echo carries a temporary sensory aftereffect. After the fragment ends, Daichi may experience:

- headache or pressure behind the eyes;
- ringing or muffled hearing;
- visual afterimages;
- brief disorientation;
- temporary difficulty separating the Echo's residual impression from the present scene.

Stronger or more intrusive Echoes may produce a more pronounced aftereffect. The effect is temporary and is not a Depth payment, Contract toll, or automatic permanent injury.

The most important danger is **false certainty**: for a short period after an Echo, residual sensory information can make Daichi feel that an Echo fragment is still present. He must therefore verify what he perceived rather than treating every fragment as unquestionable reality.

### Echo boundary

Echo never supplies a complete explanation. It can provide a fragment relevant to a phenomenon, but the fragment still requires observation, hypothesis, testing, and interpretation.

## 3. Pre-Academy introduction

The Echo hook should appear **before Academy entry**, ideally in the opening chapters.

Preferred sequence:

1. Daichi receives/possesses the Uncarved Seal before Academy entry.
2. He experiences a brief unexplained Echo.
3. He does not understand it and does not immediately receive an answer.
4. After entering Records & Archives, he encounters an old archive reference describing an SP/anomalous artifact capable of producing fragmentary impressions or equivalent information.
5. Daichi recognizes that the archive description matches what happened to him.
6. He tests the artifact rather than simply believing the book.
7. He gradually learns that **the phenomenon is not the same thing as a conventional Esper Ability**.

This creates an early mystery with a fair-play explanation: the reader sees the Echo first; the archive later gives the reader and Daichi enough vocabulary to reinterpret it.

## 4. Relationship to Contract #1

Echo is the **early artifact hook and diagnostic/provisional information function**.

THE POLITE KNOCK remains the first formal Contract and retains its locked grammar. Echo must never silently become an alternative version of the Knock.

The progression is therefore:

**Uncarved Seal → Echo fragments → Academy/archive investigation → understanding of SP artifact behavior → genuine solving → THE POLITE KNOCK (Contract #1) → later Weapon Contract (Contract #2).**

## 5. Contract #2 — Weapon Contract

Contract #2 is a **weapon amplification and mastery contract**.

### Core function

When Daichi selects a weapon, the Contract temporarily grants:

- enhanced operational understanding/mastery of that weapon;
- amplification of the weapon's effective performance;
- a maximum normal active window of approximately **10 minutes per weapon selection**.

The locked target concept is **up to approximately 2× amplification** under the Contract's valid conditions.

### The ten-minute rule

The normal amplification window is approximately ten minutes for the currently selected weapon.

Forcing the same active window beyond its limit produces a clear physical consequence in the body part acting as the weapon medium.

For ordinary weapon use, this is the hand and associated arm:

- substantial localized pain;
- worsening control/coordination;
- temporary inability to use the affected limb effectively if he continues to force the state.

This consequence is literal and should be stated clearly in prose. It is not a vague "price" that readers must infer. It is also distinct from Echo's sensory aftereffect and Contract #1's Depth cost.

### The weapon-switch interaction

The important strategic discovery is:

**The ten-minute limit applies to the current weapon selection, not automatically to the entire battle.**

Daichi can switch to another weapon and establish a new active weapon state, subject to the Contract's other conditions and future fatigue limits.

This is the Contract's hidden strategic advantage.

It rewards the fact that Daichi has studied many weapons instead of making him a single-weapon specialist.

## 6. Why Daichi studies Weapons early

Daichi's initial Weapons education is **not** a secret attempt to become a weapons expert.

He studies Weapons because Operations Support requires him to understand:

- classification;
- identification;
- authorization;
- deployment records;
- maintenance records;
- damage reports;
- chain of custody;
- weapon terminology.

This is the ordinary institutional reason shown to the reader.

Later, Contract #2 turns that apparently ordinary study into a major strategic advantage.

### Weapon Knowledge → Familiarization → Mastery → Contract

The early Academy arc must establish that **Weapon Mastery is a real academic/practical concept before Contract #2 exists**.

- **Weapon Knowledge:** understanding the weapon, terminology, classification, limitations, and records.
- **Weapon Familiarization:** identifying and correctly handling the weapon and understanding its ordinary operating requirements.
- **Weapon Mastery:** demonstrating effective use under practical conditions, including control, handling, timing, movement, and adaptation under pressure.
- **Weapon Contract:** temporarily elevating Daichi's practical mastery of the selected weapon and adding supernatural amplification.

Daichi should initially be stronger in knowledge than in practical mastery. His early progress is legitimate training, not hidden supernatural power. This distinction must remain visible so that the later Weapon Contract feels like a payoff to accumulated preparation rather than a retroactive revelation that Daichi was already overpowered.

## 7. Academy social interpretation

Daichi's growing collection of weapon knowledge should produce conflicting literal opinions:

1. **Show-off interpretation:** some cadets believe he carries/studies many weapons because he wants attention.
2. **Weapons-master interpretation:** some cadets conclude he must be an exceptional weapons specialist because he can handle many weapon types.
3. **Weak-cadet interpretation:** others correctly point out that weapon knowledge does not change his F Rank, low conventional Esper output, or physical vulnerability.

All three interpretations may coexist.

The story must not secretly confirm that Daichi is a conventional superhuman weapon genius. His advantage is preparation, knowledge, Contract interaction, adaptability, and switching strategy.

## 8. Required early payoff

The early Weapons-study scenes must receive a direct later payoff.

The intended reader reaction is:

**"So that is why he learned all those weapons earlier."**

The story should not rely on subtle visual callbacks alone. When the payoff occurs, the narration/dialogue should explicitly connect the earlier Weapons education to the current advantage.

## 9. Anti-power-creep firewall

Contract #2 does **not**:

- increase Esper Rank;
- awaken Daichi;
- increase conventional Esper output;
- erase his physical vulnerability;
- make him automatically superior to trained specialists;
- give unlimited simultaneous weapon mastery;
- remove the ten-minute consequence;
- make every weapon equally suitable in every situation.

A stronger cadet with superior raw output remains dangerous. Daichi wins through preparation, information, weapon choice, timing, and rule interaction rather than hidden conventional power.

## 10. Literal-presentation rule

For this progression, important information must be stated plainly on the page.

When Daichi is praised, the reader should be told that he is praised.

When another cadet is contemptuous, the dialogue or narration should make the contempt understandable.

When a weapon switch creates the strategic advantage, the prose should explain the reason.

When the ten-minute limit causes strong localized pain in the weapon-bearing hand/arm, the prose should state the consequence directly.

When early Academy scenes use the term **Weapon Mastery**, the prose should make clear that this is an ordinary training concept, not the Contract itself.

Subtle gestures may enrich a scene, but they must not carry the only load-bearing information.

## 11. Chapter placement target

- **CH001–CH003:** seed the Seal/Echo and establish the literal Academy/Records/Weapons-study logic.
- **Early Academy:** Daichi discovers the archive description and tests the Echo hypothesis.
- **CH004 onward:** Weapons familiarization is an ordinary Operations Support requirement and produces direct competence/payoff beats.
- **Contract #1:** remains on its existing locked sequence.
- **Contract #2:** later formation is now committed to the Weapon Contract; exact formation phenomenon, formal name, slot timing, and detailed acquisition sequence must be planned in a dedicated Contract #2 implementation pass, but the identity and core function above are locked.

## 12. Final design statement

The intended progression is **mystery → fragment → investigation → understanding → information Contract → broad preparation → weapon Contract → adaptive weapon switching**.

Daichi does not become strong because the story secretly discovers that he was strong all along. He becomes dangerous because the story repeatedly rewards what he actually does: **observe, learn, test, prepare, and choose correctly under pressure.**

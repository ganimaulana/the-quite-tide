# LOCK 20 — MOTION SENSE → CONTRACT #1 → BENKEI SEAL

> **STATUS: AUTHOR-LOCKED — 2026-09-24**
> This lock supersedes the early Echo/Uncarved-Seal architecture for Daichi's innate ability and early Contract presentation. It does not erase historical QA records; those records describe the superseded draft state.

## 1. Core decision

- **Echo is removed from Daichi's innate ability architecture.** Daichi does not possess an Echo ability.
- **The Uncarved Seal stone artifact is removed from the live early-story canon.** It is not Daichi's source of power and does not sit in his pocket as an inherited supernatural artifact.
- Daichi's innate Esper Ability is **Motion Sense**.
- The anomalous **book** is the early supernatural medium for Contract records/offers/binding information. It is an SP item, not an omniscient system or RPG interface.
- **THE POLITE KNOCK remains Contract #1** and retains its locked grammar unless a later author lock explicitly changes it.
- The later weapon Contract is **BENKEI SEAL**. Its core function and 10-minute mechanics are locked below; its acquisition phenomenon must be earned through a genuine SP solve and must not appear as random loot.

## 2. Motion Sense — innate Esper Ability

### Core definition

**Motion Sense** gives Daichi an abnormal perception of movement. He can detect and track moving objects, bodies, and changes of motion with unusual clarity within his effective sensory range.

The ability provides **movement information**, not interpretation.

### It can detect

- direction of movement;
- changes in trajectory;
- acceleration/deceleration;
- changes in body position while the body is moving;
- repeated movement patterns;
- multiple moving objects when the sensory load remains manageable.

### Practical skill applications

These are **applications of the same perception**, not separate powers:

- rapid approximate counting of people or moving objects when enough movement is available;
- tracking a moving target's position and trajectory;
- timing a physical response against an observed moving target;
- improving the accuracy of thrown objects by accounting for the target's observed movement, relative position, timing, and trajectory;
- catching or intercepting moving objects when Daichi's physical ability is sufficient;
- detecting movement patterns, repeated routes, rhythm, synchronization, or changes in an established movement pattern;
- noticing movement anomalies when one movement does not fit the surrounding observed pattern;
- noticing changes in spatial occupancy caused by people or objects moving into or out of an area;
- comparing the timing and trajectory of multiple moving objects.

These applications do not create new information categories. They are consequences of Daichi becoming skilled at interpreting Motion Sense data and combining it with ordinary observation, training, and physical ability.

In particular, **accurate throwing is a learned application, not an automatic targeting function**. Motion Sense can help Daichi estimate where a moving target will be relative to the thrown object, but he must still perform the throw correctly.

Rapid counting is likewise an observation advantage, not a supernatural absolute count. Stationary or occluded people may not be detected through Motion Sense alone.

### It cannot do

Motion Sense is **not**:

- precognition;
- intent reading;
- mind reading;
- x-ray vision;
- future sight;
- automatic tactical analysis;
- automatic weapon mastery;
- a strength/speed/reflex multiplier.

If an opponent moves, Daichi receives better information about the movement. **Daichi still has to interpret the movement and physically respond to it.**

### Stillness boundary

Motion Sense is weakest or provides no special information when the relevant object/person is completely stationary.

Therefore it does not automatically expose:

- stationary traps;
- hidden mechanisms that are not moving;
- a person's intention before physical movement begins;
- an object simply because it is anomalous.

### Physical ceiling

Motion Sense does not make Daichi fast enough to dodge everything.

His ability becomes practically useful because he also trains his body: reaction, footwork, balance, endurance, positioning, and recovery.

A stronger or faster opponent remains dangerous. Daichi may **see** an attack and still be unable to evade it if his body, position, or stamina is insufficient.

## 3. Academy interpretation

The Academy may classify Motion Sense as a **low/negligible direct-combat ability** because it produces little or no conventional offensive output.

That assessment is not wrong. The ability is genuinely weak as a direct power.

Daichi's unusual advantage comes from combining:

**Motion Sense + physical conditioning + observation + hypothesis testing + terrain awareness + strategy.**

His combat identity is therefore **pure evasion and tactical survival before commitment**, not hidden raw power.

## 4. Motion Sense and observation

The ability must never solve a case by itself.

The recurring grammar remains:

**OBSERVE → HYPOTHESIZE → TEST → RESULT → UPDATE → TEST AGAIN → SOLVE / FAIL**

Motion Sense supplies one category of observation. Daichi supplies the interpretation.

The ability may reveal that a movement is unusual without revealing why it is unusual. An anomaly is therefore a reason to investigate, not an answer.

For example:

> Motion Sense: the opponent's shoulder moved and the center of mass shifted.
>
> Daichi's reasoning: the next strike is probably a right-side attack.

The second statement is Daichi's inference and can be wrong.

## 5. Combat identity before Benkei Seal

Daichi is not expected to defeat stronger cadets through Motion Sense alone.

His early combat advantage is:

**SEE → EVADE → REPOSITION → OBSERVE → WAIT → COUNTER OR ESCAPE**

This supports survival against stronger opponents without secretly making Daichi stronger than the Academy's combat specialists.

He may win a duel through attrition, terrain, opponent frustration, fatigue, pattern recognition, and a well-timed counter. He may also lose when the opponent's speed, area control, coordination, or environmental strategy exceeds what Motion Sense can compensate for.

## 6. Contract medium — the anomalous book

The book is an SP item that initially appears ordinary.

- It is not a status screen.
- It does not display levels, XP, HP, percentages, cooldowns, or a complete skill description.
- It does not automatically explain phenomena.
- Other people may see an ordinary or blank book where Daichi sees anomalous writing.
- The book records Contracts and offers minimal binding information.

The governing principle is:

> **The book tells Daichi what exists. It does not tell him what it means.**

When a genuine SP solve qualifies for a Contract, the book may record an offer. Daichi may accept or refuse. Refusal carries no punishment; the offer/history may disappear from the anomalous writing.

Acceptance can reveal a simple binding method. After binding, the Contract's deeper mechanics are discovered through trial rather than delivered as a system screen.

THE POLITE KNOCK remains the first formal Contract.

- genuine solve/RESonance relationship;

## 8. Contract #2 — BENKEI SEAL

**BENKEI SEAL** is the weapon Contract reserved for a later genuine SP solve related to Benkei and the phenomenon's demonstrated rule.

It must not be presented as random loot or as a reward for merely discovering a historical name.

### Core principle

> **The hand remembers what the mind has not learned.**

Benkei Seal does **not** teach Daichi a weapon from nothing.

It amplifies a weapon capability Daichi has actually learned through Academy training and practical experience.

### Weapon definition

For Benkei Seal, **a weapon is defined by use, not by shape**.

An object may serve as a weapon when Daichi deliberately uses it as one and possesses genuine learned competence with that use. The object does not need to be a conventional weapon.

Therefore an improvised object may qualify as a weapon state when Daichi has actually learned how to weaponize it effectively. Examples may include a card, coin, stone, cord, pen, or other ordinary object when the relevant technique has been genuinely learned.

This does **not** mean that Benkei automatically turns any object into a powerful weapon. If Daichi has no genuine learned competence with an object's weaponized use, Benkei cannot create that competence from nothing.

The governing distinction is:

**object + deliberate weapon use + learned competence → eligible weapon state**

**object + mere belief that it is a weapon → not sufficient**

### Weapon definition

For Benkei Seal, **a weapon is defined by use, not by shape**.

An object may serve as a weapon when Daichi deliberately uses it as one and possesses genuine learned competence with that use. The object does not need to be a conventional weapon.

Therefore an improvised object may qualify as a weapon state when Daichi has actually learned how to weaponize it effectively. Examples may include a card, coin, stone, cord, pen, or other ordinary object when the relevant technique has been genuinely learned.

This does **not** mean that Benkei automatically turns any object into a powerful weapon. If Daichi has no genuine learned competence with an object's weaponized use, Benkei cannot create that competence from nothing.

The governing distinction is:

**object + deliberate weapon use + learned competence → eligible weapon state**

**object + mere belief that it is a weapon → not sufficient**

### Discovery progression

The objective rule above is **not initially known to Daichi in full**.

When Benkei Seal is first acquired, Daichi should understand only the practical baseline established by his training: Benkei can amplify his learned competence with conventional weapons he already knows how to use.

His later understanding that non-conventional objects can qualify as weapon states must emerge through **trial, failure, observation, and experience**. He should not immediately conclude that any object can become a weapon merely because he thinks of it as one.

The intended progression is:

**conventional weapon → comparison between weapon types → improvised-object experiment → repeated testing → functional weapon definition**

This discovery follows the same reasoning identity used elsewhere in the novel:

**OBSERVE → HYPOTHESIZE → TEST → RESULT → UPDATE → TEST AGAIN**

The Contract does not hand Daichi the complete rule. The rule exists; Daichi has to discover what it means.

### Amplification

The target concept remains approximately **2× Daichi's existing effective capability/understanding**, not a flat absolute power multiplier.

Therefore:

- beginner → approximately 2× beginner capability;
- intermediate → approximately 2× intermediate capability;
- expert → approximately 2× expert capability.

The Academy training that builds the baseline is therefore structurally necessary.

### Deliberate activation

Training alone does **not** activate Benkei Seal.

The Contract requires a deliberate activation trigger discovered through binding/trial. The activation window starts only when Daichi consciously invokes the Contract.

No RPG-style cooldown is displayed. The practical limitation is bodily strain.

### Ten-minute window

The normal active window is approximately **ten minutes for the current weapon state**.

The ten minutes are not ten minutes of invulnerability or automatic victory. Daichi can still be injured, outmaneuvered, exhausted, or defeated.

### Post-limit strain

Forcing the same active weapon state beyond its normal window produces escalating physical consequences:

- substantial localized pain;
- worsening coordination/control;
- temporary inability to use the affected limb effectively if he continues forcing it.

### Weapon switching

The Contract wording does not automatically impose a single global ten-minute battle limit.

Daichi may discover through trial that switching to another weapon can establish a new active weapon state. **Accumulated bodily strain does not reset when the weapon changes.**

This creates a strategic loophole rather than an infinite-power exploit.

## 9. Benkei Seal combat philosophy

Daichi's preferred use is:

**SURVIVE → PREPARE → EXECUTE.**

Against a stronger opponent, he can spend the early fight using Motion Sense to evade, reposition, study movement, and force the opponent to spend stamina. He activates Benkei Seal only when the conditions make the limited window valuable.

In survival exercises, he can use concealment and terrain to rest, recover from accumulated strain, search for suitable weapons, and observe other cadets before committing to a ten-minute execution window.

The Contract rewards preparation rather than replacing it.

## 10. Required anti-power-creep rules

Neither Motion Sense nor Benkei Seal may:

- secretly raise Daichi's Rank;
- erase his physical limitations;
- make him unbeatable;
- grant knowledge he has not earned;
- provide precognition;
- read intent;
- create mastery from an untrained weapon;
- make every weapon equally effective;
- eliminate fatigue or injury;
- replace Academy training.

## 11. Author North Star

> **Motion Sense lets Daichi survive the attack.**
>
> **Benkei Seal lets him make the opening count.**
>
> **Daichi's real weapon is deciding when to use either.**

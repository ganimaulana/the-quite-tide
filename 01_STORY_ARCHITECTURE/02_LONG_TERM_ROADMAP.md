## §3. Detailed roadmap, ch121–200 (Arcs IV–V; macro-phase 2 begins)

**Macro-phase 2: "The Priced Man" (~ch121–250)** — the world has priced him; now it shops.

### ARC IV — "The Paper Auction" (~ch121–160) [ADAPT] of canon Arc IV

- **Driving question** [PROPOSED]: Who owns the *knowledge* of the artifact?
- **Antagonist force**: the auction machinery — three factions bidding on the researcher's propagated note + the institute's sealed appraisal (canon Arc IV's auction kind, [ADAPT]ed: the lot is *information about* the artifact, not the torn page).
- **Midpoint** (ch140): the handwriting check analogue — the leaked note's *annotations* are contemporary; someone is annotating the artifact's record *now* (canon Arc IV's midpoint beat, [ADAPT]ed).
- **Climax** (ch152–158): **Contract #4 forms at CLIMAX** (Q364). Daichi's authentication — only the holder can authenticate — sets the auction's terms (V2; canon Arc IV's climax, [ADAPT]ed). **Slot 5 unlocks** (ch155).
- **Undervaluation cycles** (3, new actors/structures): (1) **Auction-house** (economic contempt — "the lot is worthless" → he sets the terms; structure: economic); (2) **Bureaucrat auditor** (skeptical audit of his filing method → the manual cites him; structure: skeptical audit, new actor); (3) **Rival's senior** (respectful-but-dismissive at guild level → the senior's deployment plan needs his witness; structure: protective condescension, new actor). Conversions per guardrails.
- **Gains/loses**: gains C4, Slot 5, the authentication reputation (now *priced* — forgers and buyers both want him); loses the note's containment (the knowledge is out — exposure: PEAK), and the last of his deployment cover.
- **Raises Arc V**: he is now a *priced asset* — which means he can be *bought*. The institute's acquisition budget becomes a **recruitment offer**.

### ARC V — "The Golden Handcuffs" (~ch161–205) [ADAPT] of canon Arc VI

- **Driving question** [PROPOSED]: What is he worth — and to whom?
- **Antagonist force**: the recruitment pitch — a guild/Court offer: rank, money, status, protection *if* he submits to study and deployment. Real, tempting, economically rational (he is still a clerk; ordinary-life economics [CANON]).
- **Midpoint** (ch183): the offer's *price* itemized — what "submit to study" means (the marks catalogued; the artifact handled by others — it goes dormant; the dormancy *demonstrated* to the buyers). **Second failure** (ch185): a forced solve under observation fails — priced harder than ch19 (the watchers see him fail; exposure PEAK). The failure is *useful*: it shows the buyers what the artifact is not.
- **Climax** (ch198–203): **Contract #5 forms at CLIMAX** — from the *refused* offer's fallout phenomenon (the phenomenon the buyers wanted him for, solved on his terms). The **priced refusal** (Q481 solution 3's shape at arc scale): he refuses the offer, and the refusal *costs* (protection withdrawn; a new enemy — the buyers don't like being refused). **Slot 6 unlocks** (ch200).
- **Undervaluation cycles** (3): (1) **Recruiter** (economic contempt — "we're being generous to an F-rank" → the refusal reprices the negotiation; structure: economic, new actor); (2) **Elite buyer** (polite erasure at the signing table → he names terms they can't meet; structure: polite erasure, escalated with variation); (3) **Skeptical press** (social pile-on redux, new angle — "fraud or plant?" → the failure's honesty converts some; structure: skeptical audit/social). 
- **Gains/loses**: gains C5, Slot 6, *independence* (priced); loses protection, gains a *grudge-holding* institutional enemy (the first true antagonist-with-a-budget — not a villain, a *buyer refused*).
- **Raises Arc VI**: the refusal is public enough to become *theology*. Two factions read his existence oppositely.

## §5. Endgame shape: what "outside conventional classification" means (options, not a locked truth)

Per Q480 (NEEDS REDESIGN) and Q397, premise 10's phrase must be given demonstrable meaning without premature locking. Three live options [PROPOSED], with this worker's operating recommendation:

- **Option A — metaphysical** (Q480 solution 1): his power operates on an axis the Vector doesn't detect — **information-position, not output**. Demonstrable only at the finale (a demonstration scene, designed late — Q486 solution 2's participatory shape: the final Contract doesn't *tell* him the truth, it *puts him in* it).
- **Option B — institutional** (Q480 solution 2): he *is* classifiable (T7/Unknown drawer exists — Q480's warning honored); the endgame is **political** — what institutions *do* with an unclassifiable asset (the Asylum Protocol, the Tribunal's deferred docket). "Outside classification" as *legal* status, not power tier.
- **Option C — ironic** (Q480 solution 3): the system had a drawer all along; "outside classification" was his (and the reader's) misunderstanding — the institutions were three steps ahead. The skeptics' in-world position; dramatic-irony fuel.
- **[RECOMMENDATION]**: operate **B** for ch700–930 (the political endgame is the most old-canon-faithful and the most plannable); reserve **A** as the finale's *demonstrated-but-never-asserted* deep truth (Q486's law: closed by phenomena, never by assertion); keep **C** alive as the in-world skeptical counter-thesis. **Do not lock** until the Approach band's planning (pre-ch850 checkpoint per CHAPTER_ENGINE §31's open-parameter discipline).
- **Endgame stakes** (Q400 solutions 1+2+3, combined): epistemic + infrastructural, never power-level. What he stands to lose: **understanding itself** (the Contracts' price comes due — the entities' attention, the L5 fence); the *infrastructure* of his power (the artifact, his filing category, his cover — never his rank); and the identity thesis as the final exam: *"Everyone else grows stronger by awakening powers. Daichi grows stronger by understanding things that should not exist"* — the finale tests **whether understanding is enough**, staged as a confrontation no power can win (Q400 solution 3). The three candidate finale shapes (Q481) — the personal test, the refusal, the participatory demonstration — stay live options until pre-ch850.

## §6. Serialization budgets (the loop inside the budgets — Q494 solution 1)

The Contract loop does not float free (Q494: NEEDS REDESIGN): **the loop is the mystery budget's instrument** — one engine, two faces. The loop paces *chapters*; the old budgets pace *arcs*:

- **Phenomenon budget** [PROPOSED; Q496 solution 1]: per arc — minor phenomena (texture, 1–3 ch, uncapped but pre-paid), **1 major phenomenon** (the arc's Contract engine, 10–20 ch), catastrophic phenomena budgeted per macro-phase (≤1 per 3 arcs). Anti-filler: the old filler test ports — an arc is filler if it touches neither Daichi's stakes, nor the mystery layer, nor the power layer (Q496).
- **Chaining rule** [PROPOSED; Q496 solution 2]: every Contract's information output must open ≥1 new phenomenon lead — the treadmill becomes a *chain* (each cycle *causes* the next). Enforced at batch planning (§22 step 9).
- **Breakthrough budget** [PROPOSED]: full Contract successes ≤1 per arc (Arcs I–XV); the *density* rule (Q383 solution 1): a breakthrough must move ≥3 §5 axes (mystery + faction + personal cost, minimum) — this is what "more meaningful" *means*, auditable per chapter.
- **Reveal budget** [ADAPT]: ~1 major reveal per 25 chapters (P2–P3 equivalent), ~1 per 40 (P4 equivalent) [CANON] — every major reveal reprices ≥2 factions [KEEP].
- **The 1000-chapter viability verdict** (old AUDIT/03 §18) is **not inherited** (Q494): the reboot needs its own viability verdict once the contract-design worker's ledger and the phenomenon budget are both ratified. This roadmap is the *claim*; the verdict is a later deliverable. [UNKNOWN] until then.

## ARC I — "The First Contract" (~ch1–30)

- **Driving question** [PROPOSED]: What is the Mori family artifact, and what does it want with Daichi?
- **Antagonist force** (not a villain) [PROPOSED]: the **Rank system itself** — a bureaucracy that files ERROR as broken (Q495); plus the artifact's first phenomenon (PHENOMENON-01/02, phenomena-scale, no malice).
- **Midpoint reversal** (ch15): the Contract's **first true cost** — over-invocation drains Current Depth to near-zero, the artifact marks him (mark #1), and a faction instrument logs the spike. The power is not free; the camouflage cracks.
- **Climax payoff** (ch24–25): phenomenon-02 solved *with* the ranked team as instruments ("read the file, find the Rule, spend someone else's strength" — Q399 [KEEP]); the team takes the credit, he takes the file — and the appraisal record names a **previous holder**.
- **What the reader learns**: the gauge's economics (visible meter, priced use, failure's four creditors); the twelve hollows are binding points (slot-cap as L3 fact); Rank F is a *true* reading of his Esper ability — the contempt has a foundation (Q388).
- **What Daichi gains**: Contract #1 (investigative utility); Slot 2 (ch30); *access* (not rank); the cultivated-undervaluation strategy (ch13); the researcher's conversion (political interest).
- **What Daichi loses**: the safety of being unfiled (his ERROR gets a second look, ch28); one failure survived (ch19, priced); anonymity's *cheapness* — camouflage now costs active management.
- **How it raises Arc II's stakes**: the sealed file on the previous holder opens one page (ch30) — the artifact has a *history*, and history has creditors. Exposure moves latent→compounding (the researcher's paper cites "the Mori artifact").
- **Undervaluation→reversal cycles** (3 reversals; 5 distinct actor×structure cells — see PACING file §3):
  1. Bureaucratic clerk (ch3, procedural misfiling) — contempt stands as *condition*, no reversal (the world's baseline).
  2. Researcher (ch4→ch16→ch26, intellectual dismissal → "malfunction" frame → **conversion** to "unpriced asset"; the conversion *pays* — citation + access owed).
  3. Teammate (ch8, protective condescension → quiet vindication, V6 — right when no one watched).
  4. Official (ch13→ch22, polite erasure → **procedural reversal** V1 — the system needs what it can't file).
  5. Skeptic/auditor (ch29, skeptical audit → exposing-a-lie V7 — proof delivered sideways; conversion to watchful neutrality).

## ARC II — "The Appraisal" (~ch31–70)

- **Driving question** [PROPOSED]: Who else knows about the artifact — and what did the generations conclude about it?
- **Antagonist force** (not a villain) [PROPOSED]: **the appraisers** — a research institute (entitled curiosity, not malice) that wants to *borrow* the artifact, plus the sealed file's owner (institutional self-protection). Nobody wants him dead; everybody wants him *filed*.
- **Act structure**:
  - **START** (ch31–40): Daichi investigates the previous holder's identity (Arc I's hook paid toward). The institute makes contact — politely, through the converted researcher (ch26's conversion *compounds*: her interest now has institutional weight). **Contract #2's phenomenon** surfaces, chained from C1/C2 information outputs (Q496 chaining rule): a harder Rule, adversarial (it misleads — Q493).
  - **Undervaluation cycle A** (ch36–44): **Rival** — a young D-rank Esper (the benchmark character; Q380 solution 1: the reader tracks his visible rank-ups against Daichi's slower, denser breakthroughs). Structure: **public ranking spectacle** (a demonstration day; Daichi's ERROR read aloud as comedy). Daichi's response: tactical (not hurt — the cultivated cover is now conscious). **Reversal** (ch44): the rival's team is assigned the phenomenon; their output *cannot touch* its Rule. Daichi's file can. Reversal type V4 (demonstration — non-combat) + V3 (documentation triumph). The rival converts to *grudging professional respect* (guardrail: 2-beat cap — ch36 contempt, ch44 conversion).
  - **MIDPOINT** (ch48–52): the appraisal concludes — the artifact is "useless" **except** that it chose him. The institute requests a *loan* (the succession/transfer problem — Q463/Q464): the attempt to separate artifact from holder **fails informatively** (it goes dormant in other hands — Q372's dormancy as demonstration, not assertion). Midpoint reversal: the appraisal's verdict inverts — the artifact isn't useless, the *appraisers' instruments* are. The institute's posture shifts from curiosity to **acquisition interest** (political, not violent — yet).
  - **Undervaluation cycle B** (ch54–60): **Elites** — a private appraisal gala (canon Arc V's appraisal echo, [ADAPT]ed). Structure: **polite erasure** (not introduced, talked over, the artifact called "the paperweight"). Daichi attends as the researcher's "+1" (cultivated cover: he *lets* them misfile him). **Reversal** (ch60): only the holder can authenticate — he authenticates what their appraisers couldn't (V2, authentication triumph; canon Arc IV's "Daichi's authentication" beat, [ADAPT]ed to the reboot). One elite converts to *fear-tinged interest*; the rest to watchfulness.
  - **Undervaluation cycle C** (ch55–63, interleaved): **Enemy** — a phenomenon-touched hostile actor (not a mastermind: a desperate, Erosion-burned ex-Esper) picks Daichi as the weak link. Structure: **predatory targeting**. **Reversal** (ch63): surviving the impossible — evasion, endurance, Rule-use; the ex-Esper's strength *feeds* the phenomenon (Q399 interference, staged at small scale). Victory is documentation-shaped (V5, endurance vindication). The enemy is *not* converted — removed as a threat, kept as a warning (guardrail: predatory actors don't get cycle-partner status).
  - **CLIMAX** (ch64–70): phenomenon-03 solved; **Contract #2 forms at CLIMAX** (Q364: gated). Its price is the arc's thesis: the counterparty's phenomena-scale want is **continuation** — binding it means *housing* its remainder (the Contract's information output is heavy: it opens the Arc III lead — deployment-grade phenomena are proliferating). The scheduled event becomes available once the required conditions are met → **Slot 3 unlocks** (ch70, paid on-page).
  - **END** (ch70): the previous holder's **identity** revealed (payoff of Arc I's hook — partial: the *cost* of holding is still withheld for Arc III/IV). The researcher's published note propagates beyond her control (exposure: compounding). The institute's acquisition interest is now a *budget line* (faction consequence).
- **What the reader learns**: the artifact's "historically useless" assessment — *who* assessed it, *with what instruments*, and why they were wrong (Q495 solution 2 paid); the transfer rule (it cannot be taken — only chosen-to); the benchmark rival's visible progression vs. Daichi's consequence-density (Q380/Q383: his breakthroughs move ≥3 §5 axes — mystery + faction + personal cost — vs. the rival's single-axis rank-ups).
- **What Daichi gains**: Contract #2 (harder-won; its remainder-housing is a standing obligation — Q497's obligation-price, narrative form); Slot 3; the authentication triumph (a *reputation* among appraisers — the wrong kind of fame); the rival's respect.
- **What Daichi loses**: the researcher's *trust* (she publishes — her conversion's price; their relationship repriced from ally to *interested party*); a family relationship strained (his mother asked him to stop digging into the holders — ordinary-life cost); the artifact's dormancy is now *witnessed* (one more camouflage layer spent).
- **How it raises Arc III's stakes**: the institute's acquisition budget + the proliferating deployment-grade phenomena + the C2 remainder he now *houses* = the system has both motive and pretext to **use** him. The question is no longer "what is he?" but "what is he *for*?" — asked by people with deployment authority.
- **Cycle accounting**: 3 reversals (rival, elites, enemy) + 1 conversion-arc (researcher/institute: curiosity → acquisition interest). Actors: rival / elites / enemy — all distinct from Arc I's set. Structures: public spectacle / polite erasure / predatory targeting — all distinct from Arc I's columns (C10, C4, C5, C6, C8) and from each other. **No column repeats within 15 chapters of its last use.**

  - **Undervaluation cycle B** (ch84–104, slow burn): **Civilians** — the operation's footage leaks (the deepfake dividend *fails*: too many witnesses, too consistent). Structure: **social pile-on** (meme-ification: "F-rank guy," the ERROR as punchline).

### Q451 — Causality
**Question:** 02.I says anomaly Rules "may exceed the Attuned channeling caps — and inherit proportional costs." If a contract *is* a portable anomaly Rule, does Daichi inherit the exceed-caps clause — and what denominates the cost?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** The clause exists for site-bound anomalies (02.I: "larger Tide pressure, wider Seismograph visibility, faster Undertow attention, harder containment"). The directive never states whether contracts are "anomaly Rules" in this sense. If yes, a contract could legally exceed every Attuned cap — the causal-bubble limits, the output ceilings — with the bill "denominated differently." If no, contracts are capped at channeling scale and the clause is irrelevant.
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient — this one clause decides whether the contract system is a *new tier of power* or a *new delivery mechanism for old power*. Every "can the contract do X" question (Q418–Q429) secretly depends on it.
**Possible solutions:**
1. Yes, with the old bill: contracts can exceed Attuned caps, and the cost is the anomaly's own — Seismograph visibility, Undertow attention, containment pressure. Consequences: contracts become strategic assets with strategic prices; Daichi holding one is a geopolitical event — the story scales up fast, which may not be wanted in the early arcs.
2. No: the artifact throttles every contract to Attuned-channeling scale (the mediation bottleneck, Q418 solution 1). Consequences: the system stays street-level; "GREATER BREAKTHROUGH" (premise 12) means *cleverer*, not *bigger* — which is actually the old novel's thesis ("a better-prepared nobody," 30.3).
3. Case-by-case, priced in story: the artifact *can* exceed caps, but doing so triggers the old containment response (Sweepers, Tribunal, Seismograph) — the cap is enforced by the world, not the metaphysics. Consequences: the most serialized (the world pushes back); but it makes the metaphysics permissive and the institutions the only brake — institutions can be outwitted, metaphysics can't.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — 02.I's exceed-caps clause must state its contract-era scope; it currently assumes site-bound anomalies.

### Q452 — Causality
**Question:** 02.I (CANON-LOCKED): "The past cannot be changed" — ever. The directive's core verb is SOLVE. Does "solving" a causal anomaly ever mean rewriting its cause?
**Status:** [CONTRADICTION]
**Current answer:** The past's unchangeability is CANON-LOCKED (02.I): "Any ability, ritual, or anomaly claiming to alter the past is either viewing Tide-marks, creating a local causal bubble, or lying." The directive (premise 6) requires Daichi to "UNDERSTAND and SOLVE the phenomenon/anomaly/task." For a causal phenomenon (a replay, a loop, a prophecy-fulfillment), the natural-language meaning of "solve" includes *fixing* it — and fixing a past-caused loop reads as editing the past.
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient — the collision is linguistic but the consequences are metaphysical. The first causal-phenomenon draft will need a ruling on whether "solved" means "understood," "ended," or "rewritten."
**Possible solutions:**
1. "Solving ≠ rewriting," as reboot law: to solve a causal phenomenon is to understand its Rule and *end its recurrence* (close the loop, discharge the Tide-mark) — never to edit what happened. Consequences: preserves 02.I untouched; gives "solve" a precise technical meaning (closure, not revision) that drafts can apply uniformly.
2. Allow rewriting inside causal bubbles only (the old exception, preserved): a contract can create a line-of-sight, minutes-long bubble at ruinous cost — the phenomenon's own scale, not Daichi's will. Consequences: the old exception ports; but "ruinous Erosion" needs a contract-era equivalent (Q411's cost question again).
3. Forbid causal phenomena as contract sources entirely: loops, replays, and prophecies can be *witnessed*, never *solved* — they're the L5-adjacent class the artifact refuses (Q427 solution 1's refusal list). Consequences: simplest; but it removes some of the richest story material (the Furumachi replay was EVENT-092, a load-bearing chapter).
**Canon impact:** [KEEP] — 02.I's past-unchangeability must be re-locked with a contract-era gloss on "solve"; the lock is CANON-LOCKED and cannot be softened by implication.

### Q453 — Causality
**Question:** 02.IV (CANON): "Power is legible... If Daichi cannot eventually figure out *why* something happened, it should not have happened." Are acausal contract effects permitted — effects with no legible chain from cause to result?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** The directive assumes understanding precedes the contract — which implies legibility. But entity-derived effects (premise 6's "Entities") may follow Undertow grammar, which "has no concept of human relevance" (03.1) and in which "causality... do[es] not hold" (03.1). An effect whose cause is in a substrate without causality is, from the Membrane side, *acausal by construction*.
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient — legibility was the old novel's narrative contract with the reader (33_MYSTERIES.md: "the distinction is the whole game"). If contracts can be acausal, the reader can't play fair; the mystery architecture's "budgeted mystery" rule (02.IV) loses its enforcement mechanism.
**Possible solutions:**
1. No acausal effects, ever: the artifact only stores what Daichi understood *as a Rule*, and Rules are causal by definition in the Membrane — anything acausal is un-storable (another refusal-list entry, Q427 solution 1). Consequences: 02.IV survives intact; the cost is forbidding the weirdest (and most containment-fiction-flavored) phenomena as contract sources.
2. Acausal effects allowed but *marked*: the contract carries its acausality as a known property (the phenomenon's file says "cause unknown"), and the story treats it as a budgeted mystery (02.IV's INTENTIONAL MYSTERY category), not a bug. Consequences: preserves the mystery budget's discipline while allowing genuine weirdness; requires the author to actually budget each acausal effect as a promise.
3. Acausal *appearance*, causal *reality*: every contract effect has a cause, but the cause may be in the counterparty's substrate — Daichi's understanding is always incomplete *in principle*, and the series' deepest engine is closing that gap (the old L5-2 shape, generalized). Consequences: the most ambitious; makes "understanding" asymptotic rather than binary — which actually matches premise 12's "GREATER BREAKTHROUGH" better than a pass/fail solve.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — 02.IV's legibility rule needs a substrate-causation clause; the old wording assumes Membrane-side causes.

### Q454 — Identity
**Question:** The directive assumes entities can be counterparties — but old canon says the Undertow is a substrate "in which causality, time, and identity do not hold" (03.1). If the counterparty has no stable identity, what exactly did Daichi contract *with*?
**Status:** [NEEDS REDESIGN]
**Current answer:** This is a metaphysical hole under the entire contract system. A contract — even a fantastical one — presumes a counterparty: something that persists, can be bound, and can perform. Old canon's substrate explicitly denies identity to Undertow structures. The directive (premise 6) lists "Entities" alongside Monsters, Anomalies, and Phenomena without defining entity-identity, persistence, or the difference between an entity and a phenomenon.
**Sufficiency:** Structurally insufficient — every downstream question (consent Q460, ownership Q458/Q463, death Q403, ethics Q461) inherits this hole. The system needs an ontology of counterparties before it needs anything else.
**Possible solutions:**
1. Define entities as *stable patterns*: the contract binds a pattern, not a person — patterns persist (that's what makes them contractable), and "identity" is pattern-continuity, not personhood. Consequences: gives the system its ontology (pattern-law instead of contract-law); but it makes "contract" a metaphor for "pattern-binding," and the directive's legalistic framing needs rewriting.
2. Restrict counterparties to Membrane-side phenomena: Daichi contracts with *Seeps* (which have fixed Rules, 03.1), never with Undertow structures directly — "entities" means "sapient Seeps" (the Asylum Protocol's class, GOV-010). Consequences: the counterparty problem dissolves (Seeps have Rules; Rules are stable); but it caps the system's weirdness at the Membrane — no direct Undertow bargains, ever.
3. Embrace the instability: counterparties *are* unstable, and the contract's function is to *stabilize* them — signing imposes Membrane-identity on an Undertow structure (the artifact as an identity-forge). Consequences: the most original and the most dangerous — Daichi *makes* persons by contracting; the ethical fallout (Q458–Q462) becomes the series' core rather than its edge.
**Canon impact:** [REWRITE] — premise 6's counterparty list must be given an ontology; "Entities" as currently used is a placeholder, not a category.

### Q455 — Identity
**Question:** Old Daichi is "exactly what they look like" — ordinary, forgettable, "unremarkable by design and by nature" (29 Part A); "lanky from night shifts rather than exercise"; loses futsal games to Saitō. New Daichi is "extremely high intelligence; physically gifted, athletic, disciplined, combat-capable; strong leadership potential" (premise 2). Is this the same person?
**Status:** [CONTRADICTION]
**Current answer:** Direct collision on the protagonist's defining attributes. Old canon's weakness was *load-bearing*: "Daichi is never secretly omnipotent" (01); "genuinely weak: no combat training, no power, no money, no connections" (01); the STORY_ENGINE's internal arc ("the same clerk, answerable; not the strongest person in the room"). MEMORY.md's standing preference: his arc "must not be a simple WEAK → STRONG power climb" and "must not suddenly become a completely different person."
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient — the directive reimagines the protagonist's body, mind, and social capacity simultaneously. "Reimagined" is doing a lot of work; the question is whether anything of the old Daichi survives besides the name.
**Possible solutions:**
1. Hard reboot, honestly labeled: the new Daichi is a new character; the old bible becomes reference material (setting, factions, metaphysics), not continuity. Consequences: cleanest intellectually; but it discards 20 finished chapters, the 12-arc spine, and the reader's attachment — the highest-cost option, requiring author-level sign-off.
2. Bridge narrative: the new attributes are *developed*, not innate — the reboot's early arcs show an ordinary Daichi *becoming* disciplined/athletic/combat-capable through training (the artifact's demands as a training montage with costs). Consequences: preserves continuity and MEMORY.md's constraint ("earned, not granted"); but "extremely high intelligence" can't be trained into existence — that one needs a separate justification (or a downgrade to "very sharp").
3. Keep the old body, upgrade only the mind: Daichi stays lanky, untrained, and forgettable — the reboot's "gifted" is cognitive only (functional intelligence, premise 13), and "combat-capable/leadership" are *developed* skills, never innate gifts. Consequences: the smallest delta from canon; the undervaluation premise (premise 14) survives intact; but premise 2 must be rewritten — "physically gifted, athletic" is the part that breaks the old camouflage.
**Canon impact:** [REWRITE] — premise 2 or 29 Part A must yield; "reimagined" cannot coexist silently with "exactly what they look like."

### Q456 — Identity
**Question:** The directive allows contract effects including "silhouettes, sensory impressions" (premise 8). Can a contract grant borrowed identity — impersonating the living or the dead — and how does that interact with old canon's recognition-based Slipping?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** Old canon 02.I: Slipping arrival requires *recognition* — "somewhere held strongly and accurately in the living memory of *another living person*" who knows *you*. Identity is load-bearing in the metaphysics: patterns, recognition, "not yourself, not an echo, not a recording." The directive's "silhouettes" effect is undefined — disguise? impersonation? identity-theft? Nothing addresses whether a borrowed face fools recognition, wards, or the Veil.
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient — identity-theft is the natural exploit of a "silhouette" contract, and the old system's identity-anchored mechanics (Slipping, pattern-capture, Census registration) all assume faces are honest.
**Possible solutions:**
1. Silhouettes are sensory only, never identity: the contract grants *appearances* (a face, a voice) that fool eyes but not metaphysics — wards, recognition, and Lantern senses see through them. Consequences: the old identity mechanics survive; the silhouette is a disguise, not a transformation — useful, not load-bearing.
2. True identity-borrowing, priced in the old currency: wearing another's pattern risks *pattern-bleed* (the old 40–60 Fathom "thinning" symptoms, 04.4 — "remembering too much, including things that never happened to them"). Consequences: the horror writes itself (he comes back *wrong*); the price is identity-instability, which the old bible already mechanized.
3. Forbid identity effects: "silhouettes" means the *entity's* silhouette (seeing the counterparty's shape), never a wearable face. Consequences: the narrowest reading; kills the exploit at the cost of one of the directive's more evocative effect types.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — 02.I's recognition/identity clauses assume faces are honest; the reboot must state what a borrowed face fools.

### Q457 — Soul / Consciousness
**Question:** Old canon has no "soul" — it has *pattern* (collapses at death), *echo* (degraded remnant), *Drowning* (dissolution), and *the thinning* (personality erosion). Does the reboot import a soul-concept, or are "Entities" just patterns/echoes/Seeps?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** The directive uses "Entities," "Monsters," "Soul / Consciousness" (in the task's category list) without defining any of them. Old canon's vocabulary is deliberately materialist-informational: patterns are neural/informational structures, not souls (03.1, 36_TERMINOLOGY.md — "soul" returns zero hits in the core files). Importing "soul" would be a metaphysical regime change; not importing it leaves "entity" undefined (Q454).
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient — the category list for this audit *names* Soul/Consciousness, which means the reboot team is thinking in those terms. The bible's silence on souls was a choice (informational metaphysics, no dualism). That choice needs re-ratifying or revising.
**Possible solutions:**
1. No souls, explicitly: the reboot's metaphysics stays informational — "entity" means stable pattern (Q454 solution 1), "consciousness" means pattern-complexity, and anyone saying "soul" in-world is speaking religion (the Choir's vocabulary), not physics. Consequences: preserves the old metaphysics' hardest edge; requires policing the word "soul" out of the system's technical language.
2. Souls as a *discovery*: the reboot's deep lore reveals that patterns *are* what religions called souls — the informational metaphysics was incomplete (the old L2 "70% accurate" model, 33). Consequences: the most dramatic; recontextualizes without invalidating (33's rule for L5 resolutions) — but it must be *earned* as a late-arc revelation, not assumed in the premise.
3. Dual vocabulary, kept separate: physics says "pattern," people say "soul," and the story never adjudicates — the gap is a permanent intentional tension (like the old no-adversary axiom, 33 L5-3). Consequences: the most novelistic; but the contract system needs *operational* definitions (can you contract a soul?), and deliberate vagueness at the operational level is just a bug.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — 36_TERMINOLOGY.md must define or explicitly exclude "soul"; the old glossary's silence was load-bearing.

### Q458 — Soul / Consciousness
**Question:** If a contracted entity is conscious/sapient, old canon's Asylum Protocol (GOV-010) grants sapient anomalies sanctuary — "cannot be extradited, seized, or examined without OEP consent — including by the Tribunal" (06_GOVERNMENTS.md). Can Daichi "own" a being the hidden world's law says cannot be owned?
**Status:** [CONTRADICTION]
**Current answer:** Direct collision. The Asylum Protocol is the hidden world's only secret supernatural jurisdiction (06_GOVERNMENTS.md §231; 07_INTERNATIONAL_RELATIONS.md §32, EVENT-073). A sapient contracted entity is arguably a *seized* sapient anomaly — Daichi holding it in a slot looks like exactly what the Protocol forbids. The directive never mentions sapience, consent, or the Protocol.
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient — this isn't an edge case; "Entities" as counterparties *is* the system. If any contracted entity qualifies as sapient, the protagonist is committing a standing jurisdictional crime from the first signing.
**Possible solutions:**
1. The Protocol applies: sapient entities cannot be contracted — the artifact refuses (refusal list, Q427 solution 1), or the contract is void under hidden-world law and the Tribunal can prosecute. Consequences: clean legally; but it makes "Entities" mean "non-sapient entities," which needs stating — and it makes the most interesting counterparties off-limits.
2. Contracts aren't seizure: a consensual contract is *employment*, not captivity — the entity can leave (the Protocol protects against *non-consensual* holding). Consequences: converts the legal question into a consent question (Q460) — the system's legality hinges on entity-consent being real, which Q454's identity hole must first close.
3. The Protocol becomes the antagonist: Daichi's contracts *are* a gray-zone seizure, the OEP objects, and the series litigates it (EVENT-073's sequel — the Sulu organism as precedent). Consequences: the most serialized; converts a contradiction into the middle-third legal arc — but it makes the protagonist a test case for supernatural civil rights, which is a *very* different novel unless handled carefully.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — 06_GOVERNMENTS.md's Asylum Protocol needs a contract-era opinion: are stored sapient entities "held" under the Protocol?

### Q459 — Soul / Consciousness
**Question:** If Daichi contracts with Drowned-derived phenomena, is he trafficking in the setting's equivalent of the dead — and do the Choir, the Court, and the factions treat that as desecration?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** Old canon: the Drowned "usually leave a new Seep where they stood" (03.1); the Choir's theology holds "Drowning is salvation" and the Undertow is "the Mother Below, dreaming" (33 L3). A Drowned-derived contract is, theologically, *bottling salvation* — or *enslaving the saved*. The directive never addresses the religious/social reception of contract sources. Old canon's rule (33): "respect the praxis, doubt the cosmology" — the Choir's phenomenology is "irritatingly close" (29 §B.10).
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient — the old bible's factions had *opinions* about everything metaphysical, and those opinions drove plot (the Choir canonizing the tether, the Court collecting bloodlines). The reboot's contract sources will have the same reception — unpriced.
**Possible solutions:**
1. Price it socially, per faction: the Choir calls Drowned-contracts desecration (or *theft from the Mother*); the Compact calls them salvage; the Tribunal calls them unregulated pattern-commerce. Consequences: the old faction-opinion engine does the new work; each contract source comes with a reception table — highly serialized.
2. The artifact launders provenance: stored contracts carry no trace of their source's nature (a Drowned-phenomenon contract is indistinguishable from any other). Consequences: avoids the reception problem technically — but "the file has no provenance" is itself a horror (Daichi may not know what he's holding), and it undercuts the "understanding" premise (did he *really* understand it?).
3. Make it the central ethical arc: Daichi discovers a contract's source was someone's Drowned loved one, and the series asks what "not by killing" means when the dead can't consent (Q460's coercion question, sharpened). Consequences: the most human; converts metaphysics into the novel's moral center — but it needs the echo-consent rule (Q402) settled first.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — 10_RELIGIONS.md / 33 L3 need contract-source reception doctrines; the old factions' opinions don't auto-port.

### Q460 — Contract Ethics
**Question:** "Solve, don't kill" (premise 6) can be coercive: draining a phenomenon's purpose, binding an entity against its will, "understanding" as a euphemism for capture. Does a coerced contract count — and what adjudicates willingness?
**Status:** [NEEDS REDESIGN]
**Current answer:** The directive's ethic is stated as a *method* (understand/solve vs. kill) with no *consent* requirement. Old canon's closest analog: the tether "cannot be engineered" — staged transfers dissipate (29 §B.8); "the universe does not choose holders" — a metaphysical anti-coercion rule. The new system has no equivalent: a sufficiently brilliant, sufficiently ruthless Daichi could force-solve phenomena the way the old Factor force-bought anomalies.
**Sufficiency:** Structurally insufficient — without a coercion rule, "not by killing" is a *technique*, not an ethic. The anti-cliché identity rule (premise 3: "Daichi grows stronger by understanding things that should not exist") collapses if understanding can be extracted at gunpoint.
**Possible solutions:**
1. Metaphysical coercion-lock: coerced solving yields coerced contracts — fragment-only, unreliable, degrading (the phenomenon's Rule resists misunderstanding the way the old tether resisted engineering). Consequences: the old §B.8 anti-engineering rule reborn as contract law; "understanding" becomes un-fakeable by construction — the strongest possible foundation for premise 3.
2. The artifact adjudicates: it refuses coerced signings (the Q406 solution-2 / Q415 solution-2 pattern — the medium as moral agent). Consequences: consistent with a pattern of artifact-refusals, but concentrates a lot of moral agency in an object — Q454's identity question gets heavier.
3. No lock — and the story is about the temptation: Daichi *can* coerce, the contracts *work*, and the price is everything the old bible priced (attention, exposure, the entity's enmity, faction response). Consequences: the most adult; the ethic becomes a *choice* with consequences rather than a *law* with enforcement — but the author must then show the consequences, every time, without flinching.
**Canon impact:** [REWRITE] — premise 6's "not by killing" must be upgraded from method to law (with a coercion rule) or downgraded honestly to preference; as written it promises an ethic the mechanics don't enforce.

### Q461 — Contract Ethics
**Question:** Old brake #2: "the entity never intervenes on Daichi's behalf" (AUDIT/03). New premise: contracts ARE entity-derived capabilities. Is a signed contract "intervention"?
**Status:** [CONTRADICTION]
**Current answer:** This is the single most load-bearing clash in the reboot. Old canon's brake was absolute and *thematic*: the Laggard "never makes Daichi strong. It makes him *informed*" (29 §B.12); the entity's attention was a *cost*, never a favor (29 §B.6.3); ARC XII's entire endgame (the test — "whether it answers," DATABASE/02_STORY_AND_ARC.md) assumed the entity had never acted *for* him. The new system is *built* on entity-derived power: every contract is the entity doing something for Daichi, formalized.
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient — and binary. Either the brake survives (and the contract system is redesigned around non-intervention) or it falls (and the old endgame's hinge is spent in chapter one). There is no middle that isn't wordplay.
**Possible solutions:**
1. Redefine intervention, precisely: the brake forbade *unilateral, unpriced, unasked* entity action *for* Daichi. A contract is *bilateral, priced, asked* — a bargain, not a favor. The brake survives as "no *free* intervention." Consequences: the most surgical; preserves the old endgame's logic (the test is still unanswered *favor*) while permitting bargains — but "bargain vs. favor" needs a bright line or every draft will blur it.
2. Keep the brake absolute and redesign contracts as non-entity-derived: the artifact stores *Daichi's understandings*, not entity favors — the counterparty is the phenomenon's Rule, which is mindless (like the old tether's "window, not a being"). Consequences: dissolves the contradiction by removing entities as agents — but guts premise 6's "Entities" and the consent/ethics questions with it.
3. Drop the brake openly, with a changelog: the reboot's thesis *is* that the old restraint was wrong — entities can be bargained with, and the story is about what that costs. Consequences: honest; but it retires the old ARC XII hinge ("whether it answers" — it's been answering since contract one) and the "attention flows both ways" horror — the deepest old mysteries need replacements (Q483, Q499).
**Canon impact:** [REWRITE] — brake #2 must be redefined or retired explicitly; it cannot coexist silently with entity-derived contracts. This is the reboot's highest-priority metaphysical decision.

### Q462 — Contract Ethics
**Question:** The artifact "chooses/recognizes Daichi" (premise 4) after generations of uselessness. Old canon: "The universe does not choose holders. It *leaves* the tether where a mind can hold it... Daichi was not chosen. He was *present*." (29 §B.8, CANON). Chosen-vs-present is a direct thematic contradiction.
**Status:** [CONTRADICTION]
**Current answer:** The old sentence was the novel's thesis — the STORY_ENGINE's "arc of presence" (§3: "He was not chosen; he was *present*... presence is a choice he keeps making"), the answer to "what does presence obligate" (§2). The new premise replaces contingency with election: the artifact waited generations and *recognized* him. These are not two mechanics; they are two theologies.
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient — the author must choose the novel's theology. "Recognition" can be dressed as contingency ("it recognized the pattern it was made for"), but a hostile reader will call it chosenness with extra steps (Q476).
**Possible solutions:**
1. Keep contingency, demote recognition: the artifact didn't *choose* — Daichi was the first holder whose mind could *hold* what it stores (Loudness + the specific intelligence the medium requires); "recognition" is the family's romantic word for a mechanical fit. Consequences: preserves the old thesis verbatim; the artifact's "choice" becomes lock-and-key, not election — but premise 4's emotional beat ("finally recognized") deflates.
2. Keep election, retire the thesis: the reboot's theme is *chosenness examined* — what does it mean to be selected, what does it obligate, can you refuse it? Consequences: honest thematic reboot; but the STORY_ENGINE's "arc of presence" (UNFILED→ANSWERABLE) must be rewritten — "presence" no longer answers "why him."
3. Split the timeline: the artifact *was* contingent for generations (nearest capable mind, like the tether) — and Daichi is the first it *responded* to, which is a new event the story investigates (why now? what's different?). Consequences: converts the contradiction into the central mystery (the artifact's change of behavior *is* the plot); the old thesis holds for the backstory, the new theology is the story — the most serialized option.
**Canon impact:** [REWRITE] — 29 §B.8's "not chosen, present" or premise 4's "chooses/recognizes" must yield; the novel's thesis sentence cannot contradict the premise on page one.

### Q463 — Contract Ownership
**Question:** 02.II (CANON-LOCKED): "Anomalies are non-fungible... There is no commodity market in 'anomaly units'." Stored contracts in slots look fungible, countable, and tradable. Does the reboot keep non-fungibility?
**Status:** [CONTRADICTION]
**Current answer:** The old non-fungibility rule was economic load-bearing: "This single fact prevents most infinite-wealth schemes: you cannot scale what you cannot reproduce" (02.II). The directive's slots + gauge + outcome grades (success/partial/fragment) describe a *systematized* inventory — graded, slotted, countable. A graded inventory is a commodity inventory with better marketing. The Red Ledger (SUP-004) traffics in exactly this kind of asset (29 §B.9, EVENT-093).
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient — if contracts are fungible, the Ledger can price them, the Red Ledger can auction them, and Daichi is a mint (Q467). If they're non-fungible, the slot/gauge systematization needs explaining (why do unique things come in standard slots?).
**Possible solutions:**
1. Non-fungibility preserved, slots as metaphor: each contract is unique (bound to Daichi's specific understanding of a specific phenomenon); "slots" count *relationships*, not *units* — they can't be transferred, priced, or compared. Consequences: 02.II survives; but the gauge/slot language must be rewritten to stop sounding like an inventory UI.
2. Fungibility embraced, market designed: contracts *are* tradable (with the transfer rules Q466–Q467), and the reboot designs the contract market (Ledger listings, Red Ledger auctions, Tribunal regulation) as a major setting system. Consequences: the most worldbuilding-intensive; converts the contradiction into the economic spine of the middle arcs — but it retires 02.II rather than adapting it.
3. Split: the *understanding* is non-fungible (Daichi-bound), the *invocation rights* are fungible (lendable, Q466) — the old "anomaly vs. anomaly-adjacent service" distinction (cf. 08_ECONOMY.md's conjuration-contracting trade). ### Q494 — Serialization

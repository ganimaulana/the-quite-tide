## Contract System

### Q101 — Contract System
**Question:** Is the Artifact the only contract medium in the world, or can other mediums exist (dormant, rival, or manufactured)?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** Neither canon nor the directive addresses medium plurality. Canon has Ledger-registered anomaly assets and object seeps but no supernatural contract mechanism at all — "contract" appears only in the commercial sense (08_ECONOMY, 06_GOVERNMENTS).
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient. If other mediums exist, Daichi's uniqueness collapses and the anti-cliché identity ("not the chosen one with the unique artifact") needs re-anchoring. If his is a true singleton, every one of the 28 factions hunts one object, which the plot must price honestly.
**Possible solutions:** (1) Singleton artifact, irreplaceable. Consequences: makes Daichi the sole target of all factions permanently; requires either constant flight or protection-plot armor, and strains the "indifferent world" tone (01_CORE_PREMISE). (2) A medium *class* exists but others are dormant/dead (other families hold inert ones). Consequences: preserves his uniqueness while allowing future rivals; requires inventing dormant-medium lore and risks diluting the identity rule. (3) Anyone can attempt a medium, but only his bloodline's is "awake" — and no one knows why. Consequences: invites DIY-medium exploits; needs a hard rule for why copies fail or the Red Ledger (SUP-004) mass-produces them.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — INTL-003's Ledger registry (07_INTERNATIONAL_RELATIONS) would need a new asset class for media.

### Q102 — Contract System
**Question:** Who or what adjudicates that a Contract is valid — the Artifact, the entity, or Daichi's own belief that he understood?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** The directive says Daichi must "understand and solve"; no arbiter is named. Canon's nearest institution is the Classification Office's ratification process (05 §5.3), which adjudicates *vectors*, not contracts.
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient. Without an arbiter, self-certification lets Daichi declare anything "solved" — the entire progression loop becomes confidence-based.
**Possible solutions:** (1) The Artifact verifies (it "recognizes" genuine understanding). Consequences: the artifact becomes a judge with unknowable criteria; risks deus-ex-machina and undermines "understanding" as human work. (2) The entity's acceptance is the seal (Sapient-style acknowledgment). Consequences: non-sapient anomalies (INTEL None/Reactive) arguably can't contract — shrinks the pool to higher-intel entities. (3) Daichi self-certifies, but the Resonance gauge only moves on genuine solves, judged by whether the effect *works* when tested. Consequences: elegant but needs a diegetic failure signal; a silently-failing contract is a reader-frustration risk.
**Canon impact:** [UNKNOWN] — depends on whether INTEL:Unknown entities can consent; 05 §5.1's Sapient-presumption protocol is the nearest canon.

### Q108 — Contract System
**Question:** Does the old "entities never intervene for Daichi" brake survive contracting?
**Status:** [CONTRADICTION]
**Current answer:** Standing audit guidance is absolute: "the entity never intervenes on Daichi's behalf — a single rescue would collapse the entire cost structure" (AUDIT/03_CHARACTER_STORY_AUDITS; also listed in the brakes that must never be released). The directive's contracts grant "NEW CAPABILITY" from entities — at minimum utility/information effects that aid him in crises.
**Sufficiency:** The clash is direct. A contract that helps Daichi in a crisis is intervention by another name; relabeling it "earned capability" does not dissolve the DEM risk the old brake was built to prevent.
**Possible solutions:** (1) Keep the brake absolutely: contracts grant only information/records, never direct aid. Consequences: preserves the old cost structure but guts the directive's "new capability" promise; combat-adjacent utility becomes nearly impossible. (2) Redefine the brake: no *unearned* intervention; contracted effects are earned and priced, therefore allowed. Consequences: honest reconciliation via HxH-style equivalent exchange; but the line between "earned aid" and "rescue" will be litigated every arc — needs a bright-line test. (3) Drop the brake for contracted entities only. Consequences: simplest; but AUDIT/03's DEM-immunity verdict was structural, and removing it reopens the deus-ex-machina door the old bible nailed shut.
**Canon impact:** [REWRITE] — AUDIT/03's standing guidance ("brakes that must never be released," item 2) must be rewritten, not patched.

### Q114 — Contract Conditions
**Question:** Who sets the conditions — Daichi, the entity, or the Artifact — and can Daichi negotiate them?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** The directive cites HxH-style "restrictions/conditions/specialization/trade-offs" as inspiration; no setter is named.

Consequences: truest to the inspiration; but two-sided condition-setting doubles the rules surface the reader must learn.
**Canon impact:** [UNKNOWN] — no canon contract mechanic; the Ledger's auction terms (INTL-003) are the nearest commercial analog.

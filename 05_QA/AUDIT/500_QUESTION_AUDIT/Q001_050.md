### Q001 — Core Premise
**Question:** The reboot reimagines Daichi as extremely high intelligence, physically gifted, athletic, disciplined, and combat-capable. Old canon (29 Part A) defines him as 172 cm and "lanky from night shifts rather than exercise," with "no combat training (he loses futsal games to Saitō, let alone Sweepers)," an unremarkable student ("present, adequate, forgettable"), and "genuinely weak." Which specific old-canon facts about his body and history are being overwritten, and what replaces his "lanky night-shift clerk" camouflage?
**Status:** [CONTRADICTION]
**Current answer:** New directive states the reimagined traits; 29 Part A states the opposite in detail (physique, futsal losses, transcript). Nothing in the directive explains where the new traits come from.
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient — the contradiction is direct and load-bearing: his weakness is "the bible's promise, kept in every file" (29).
**Possible solutions:**
1. Retcon his youth: hidden athletic/training history (e.g., undisclosed discipline). Consequences: breaks "unremarkable by design and by nature" and the 29 daily-life inventory; requires rewriting his school history and family memories.
2. Latent-potential framing: traits exist but were never developed until the story starts (high IQ masked by apathy, athletic base never trained). Consequences: cheapest, but "combat-capable" cannot be latent — capability requires training; keeps a soft contradiction on the combat point.
3. Keep old body, upgrade only cognition: intelligence was always there (a clerk's eye is canon), athleticism stays out. Consequences: sacrifices the "physically gifted" pillar of the proposal; keeps 29 intact.
**Canon impact:** [REWRITE] — 29 Part A's physical/character definition must be rewritten whichever way this goes.

### Q002 — Core Premise
**Question:** The directive's identity rule is "Daichi grows stronger by understanding things that should not exist" (not by a secret OP artifact). But 02.IV already makes this the universal method: "Power is legible. Every ability and anomaly follows a Rule that can, in principle, be discovered through observation." Ilsa Brandt studied the Laggard for seven years; Cartographers map the unmappable; a prepared T2 beats a T4 by reading the file (30.3). What is exclusive about Daichi's understanding that a competent Lantern examiner or Cartographer cannot replicate?
**Status:** [LOOPHOLE]
**Current answer:** The directive names understanding as the differentiator, but canon distributes that competence everywhere (02.IV "Competence is distributed"). No exclusivity mechanism is stated.
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient — without an exclusivity mechanism, any clever Attuned with the same files should match him, and the premise collapses into "he's just smarter."
**Possible solutions:**
1. The Artifact is the exclusivity: only it can *store* what understanding yields (contracts), so understanding alone is necessary but insufficient. Consequences: the identity rule becomes "understanding + artifact," weakening the anti-chosen-one claim (premise 4's "chooses/recognizes Daichi" then does the real work).
2. Loudness + tether-adjacency gives him data no one else can get (canon: Loud memory resists Veil; Laggard Use 10 clean recordings). Consequences: keeps old canon's actual differentiator but makes the reboot dependent on the Laggard, which the reboot may be trying to sideline.
3. Accept non-exclusivity: rivals *can* replicate his method, and the story's tension is that they do (competence distributed). Consequences: strongest narratively, but the "identity rule" becomes a theme, not a power system — the contract mechanics then need their own gating.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — 02.IV already supports method-based power; the reboot must name what the Artifact adds.

## Daichi's Personality (continued)

### Q251 — Daichi's Personality
**Question:** What is his secrecy posture — does he lie about the Artifact, to whom, and how good is he at it?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** Old canon surrounded him with files and colleagues (the NQA desk) but never fixed a secrecy doctrine for the reboot's Artifact; the directive is silent on who knows.
**Sufficiency:** Every relationship in the story runs on the secrecy gradient — who knows what determines every scene's tension — and an unchosen posture means inconsistent lying scene to scene.
**Possible solutions:** (1) Concentric secrecy: family knows something, Saito knows nothing, institutions suspect. Consequences: clear gradients; each ring's breach is a plot event. (2) Radical honesty with a few, total lies to the world — the inner circle is real. Consequences: strong bonds; the circle's membership becomes high-stakes. (3) He is a bad liar and knows it — so he avoids rather than deceives. Consequences: characterful; constrains his social plotting.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — the series bible needs a "who knows what" ledger from chapter one.

### Q252 — Daichi's Personality
**Question:** What does he want on an ordinary Tuesday — does the reboot preserve ordinary-life continuity?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** Old canon's chapter planning explicitly preserved ordinary-life continuity (DATABASE/04_BLUEPRINTS_AND_STATE.md); the directive's faster pacing and higher stakes threaten to crowd it out.
**Sufficiency:** Without ordinary Tuesdays, the extraordinary has no contrast — and the user's ordinary-background constraint quietly dies.
**Possible solutions:** (1) Quota: every movement contains ordinary-life scenes with real content (money, food, family calls), not just breathers. Consequences: pacing cost; identity payoff. (2) Fuse them: the ordinary life is the cover and the constraint (see Q211). Consequences: efficient; risks making every ordinary scene secretly about the plot. (3) Let ordinariness erode visibly as the price of the double life — and mourn it. Consequences: the loss becomes theme; the constraint is spent deliberately, not leaked.
**Canon impact:** [KEEP] — ordinary-life continuity was a planning rule in old canon; re-ratify it as one.

## Daichi's Motivation

### Q253 — Daichi's Motivation
**Question:** Why doesn't he sell the Artifact and retire?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** The directive gives the Artifact immense value and Daichi no reason not to monetize it; old-canon Daichi's Laggard was unsellable by nature (a window, not an object). If the Artifact is an object, "sell it" is the rational move.
**Sufficiency:** The central motivation question — every reader will ask it by chapter five, and "he just doesn't want to" is not an answer.
**Possible solutions:** (1) He can't: binding mechanics (see Q255) make sale impossible or fatal. Consequences: cleanest; but can't is weaker than won't. (2) He won't: the Artifact is family (see Q214 custodian-framing) — selling it is selling the Moris' trust. Consequences: motive with heart; needs the family written well enough to carry it. (3) He tried to price it and discovered the market pays in control, not cash — every buyer wants him with it. Consequences: the attempted sale becomes an early plot that teaches him the lesson.
**Canon impact:** [UNKNOWN] — blocked on binding/transferability (Q220, Q255).

### Q254 — Daichi's Motivation
**Question:** Why doesn't he live quietly and never touch anomalies again?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** Old canon's Laggard was involuntary in the strong sense (a window he couldn't close, transfer only at death); the directive's Artifact has no stated opt-out mechanics. If he can quit, "why doesn't he" needs answering; if he can't, the mechanism needs stating.
**Sufficiency:** The quiet-life option must be visibly closed or visibly refused — otherwise his continued risk-taking reads as authorial convenience.
**Possible solutions:** (1) He can't quit: the Artifact draws anomalies, or resonance decays dangerously if unused. Consequences: the treadmill is mechanical; his agency then lives in how he walks it. (2) He won't quit: curiosity/obsession (Q248) plus a concrete reason (debt, family, a promise). Consequences: motive with a face; needs the reason established early. (3) He tries to quit — and the story shows what quitting costs. Consequences: the refusal is earned on-page; spends early chapters on the attempt.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — the old involuntariness needs a reboot-native equivalent, stated, not assumed.

### Q255 — Daichi's Motivation
**Question:** Is the Artifact bound to him — and is the binding a designed mechanism or an assumption?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** The directive says "inherited family Artifact" and "repository of Contracts" but defines no binding: can he give it away, lose it, have it stolen? Old canon's engineered-transfer-fails rule governed the Laggard, not this.
**Sufficiency:** Binding decides the answers to Q253 (sell), Q220 (transfer), Q289 (fencing), and Q296 (hostages) — it is the single most load-bearing undecided mechanic in the reboot.
**Possible solutions:** (1) Soul-bound: it cannot leave him while he lives; attempts fail or backfire. Consequences: closes sale/theft routes; makes him the eternal target instead. (2) Custodian-bound: it stays with whoever holds it legitimately, and legitimacy has rules. Consequences: theft becomes possible-but-governed; the rules must be airtight. (3) Unbound: it's an object, and the story is about keeping it. Consequences: maximum jeopardy; the "why doesn't someone take it" questions become permanent.
**Canon impact:** [UNKNOWN] — must be fixed before any market, crime, or government plot is finalized.

### Q256 — Daichi's Motivation
**Question:** What does he want that only anomaly-solving gives him?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** The directive defines how he grows (understanding) but not what he hungers for; old-canon Daichi's wants were ordinary (stability, answers, protecting his small circle). A protagonist needs a positive want, not just an inability to quit.
**Sufficiency:** "He solves anomalies because he can" sustains ten chapters; a want sustains a series.
**Possible solutions:** (1) He wants the map: a complete understanding of the hidden world — the cartographer's hunger. Consequences: elegant with the identity rule; needs the map to stay incomplete forever. (2) He wants safety for his people, purchased one solved anomaly at a time. Consequences: concrete and sympathetic; risks making every arc a protection racket. (3) He wants to know what the Artifact is — the inheritance is a question, not an answer. Consequences: the central mystery becomes personal; the answer must then be worth it.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — the identity rule needs a want attached; "understanding" is a method, not a motive.

### Q257 — Daichi's Motivation
**Question:** If contracts are power, why doesn't he farm safe, low-risk anomalies quietly for slots instead of taking real risks?
**Status:** [LOOPHOLE]
**Current answer:** The directive's solve-to-earn economy has no stated risk/reward curve; old canon had no farming because there was nothing to farm. Rational Daichi grinds the safe spawns — and the story never starts.
**Sufficiency:** Every progression system needs its anti-grinding governor, or the protagonist's risk-taking is irrational.
**Possible solutions:** (1) Diminishing returns: the Artifact (or resonance) discounts repetition — safe solves stop paying. Consequences: forces escalation mechanically; the discount curve must be specified. (2) Safe anomalies are claimed: the easy ones belong to institutions, hunters, or criminals — access is the risk. Consequences: ties the economy to factions beautifully. (3) He does farm — and the story is honest that early growth is grinding, until a safe solve goes wrong. Consequences: subverts the montage; the "gone wrong" must be earned, not sprung.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — the contract economy needs a written risk/reward curve; see also Worker B's farming audit.

### Q258 — Daichi's Motivation
**Question:** Does anyone he loves need the Artifact — is his motivation externalized to family, and does that make the Moris hostages?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** The directive's inherited Artifact implies family history but assigns the Moris no needs; old canon kept family largely off-board. An Artifact that could protect, heal, or save a loved one is the strongest motive available — and the most exploitable.
**Sufficiency:** If the family needs nothing, the inheritance is decorative; if they need something, every faction's optimal move is the hostage play (see Q296).
**Possible solutions:** (1) Yes, and the story owns it: a Mori's condition is the engine, and protection plots are central. Consequences: powerful; the family must then be written as people, not levers. (2) No: the Moris are fine, and his motive is his own (see Q256). Consequences: cleaner; the inheritance then needs another reason to matter. (3) Ambiguous: the Artifact might help, and the maybe is what drives him. Consequences: hope as fuel; the answer must eventually come.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — the Moris need a defined narrative function before the inheritance plot advances.

## Daichi's Emotional Arc

### Q259 — Daichi's Emotional Arc
**Question:** Old arc: UNFILED → ANSWERABLE (not WEAK → STRONG). What is the new arc's name and shape?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** Old canon named the arc precisely (DATABASE/03_TRACKERS.md: seven stages end in knowledge and options, never raw output). The directive proposes power-outside-ranks but names no emotional trajectory.
**Sufficiency:** An unnamed arc cannot be tracked — and "gets stronger outside the system" is a power arc wearing a trench coat unless the emotional shape is specified.
**Possible solutions:** (1) UNKNOWN → UNDERSTOOD: the arc is epistemic — he becomes someone who can bear what he knows. Consequences: coherent with the identity rule; needs the cost of knowing staged. (2) ALONE → ANSWERABLE-FOR: the arc is about responsibility — from solo solver to someone others depend on. Consequences: ties to leadership (Q241–Q246); the old "answerable" is kept and deepened. (3) HIDDEN → LEGIBLE: the arc is about being seen — ERROR becoming a name. Consequences: ties to the rank plot; risks making the arc about status.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — the old UNFILED → ANSWERABLE should be the starting point of the renaming, not discarded.

**Current answer:** Old-canon Daichi's restraint had roots in ordinariness and caution; the directive's family Artifact implies family history that could hold a wound — or not. Nothing names it.

### Q262 — Daichi's Emotional Arc
**Question:** What breaks him — the low point the arc requires?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** Neither canon nor directive stages his nadir; old canon's progression is a ratchet of knowledge, not a valley. Every long arc needs the night he quits, fails, or loses someone.
**Sufficiency:** An undesigned low point will be improvised under deadline — and improvised nadirs are usually either too cheap (he bounces back in a chapter) or too dark (the story can't recover).

Consequences: the misreading principle given flesh; the recovery arc writes itself. (2) Betrayal by someone he led (see Q245 solution 3) — the leadership price. Consequences: ties the low point to theme; costs a relationship permanently. (3) The Artifact's price comes due — resonance, memory, or humanity, collected all at once. Consequences: the system's teeth shown; the cost schedule must be real.

### Q263 — Daichi's Emotional Arc
**Question:** How does the public ERROR/UNDEFINED label shape his self-image?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** The directive makes the label a plot device (undervalue→reversal) but not a psychological fact; old-canon Daichi had no public label at all. Being officially unclassifiable does something to a person.
**Sufficiency:** The story will either use the label's interior cost or the character will feel oddly untouched by his own premise.

### Q264 — Daichi's Emotional Arc
**Question:** The directive's undervalue→reversal beats imply a protagonist who enjoys the reveal — does the reboot keep the old restraint or replace it with shonen confidence, and what is lost either way?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** Old-canon Daichi is restrained, careful, cost-conscious; the directive's satisfaction cycles structurally reward a protagonist who sets up and springs reversals — a showman's move. The tension is unaddressed.

Consequences: code-switching as character; risks feeling manipulative.

### Q269 — Romance
**Question:** How does romance avoid becoming transactional — "solve anomaly, win affection" on repeat?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]

## Academy

### Q271 — Academy
**Question:** In a world with official F–S Esper ranks, what is the academy for — training, sorting, or harvesting?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** The directive proposes ranks but no academy; old canon's conflict engine has academy-harvesting generators built for Attuned students and Fathoms. The institution's purpose decides every scene set inside it.
**Sufficiency:** A training academy produces very different plots from a sorting academy or a harvesting one — and "all three" is an evasion unless the mix is designed.
**Possible solutions:** (1) Sorting first: the academy exists to classify Espers for institutional use — Daichi's ERROR breaks its machinery. Consequences: the ERROR plot gets an institutional home; the academy's menace is bureaucratic. (2) Training first: a genuine school — his attendance (or intrusion) is then about learning, not evasion. Consequences: warmer; needs a reason a 24-year-old attends. (3) Harvesting first: the old generators' darkest reading — students as resources. Consequences: horror-adjacent; the academy becomes an antagonist institution.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — the academy's purpose must be declared before any academy plot is outlined.

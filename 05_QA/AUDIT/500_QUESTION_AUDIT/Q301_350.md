### Q301 — Economy
**Question:** Who pays public, officially ranked (F–S) Espers, given that canon's supernatural economy is covert — roughly $140–200B in annual supernatural-adjacent activity whose ~200,000 direct employees mostly believe they work in insurance, pharma, logistics, or telecom?
**Status:** [CONTRADICTION]
**Current answer:** Canon `08_ECONOMY.md` prices the hidden economy (bounties, salaries, licensing, anomaly auctions) entirely inside covert channels: ordinary fiat for 99%+ of transactions, soundings as closed settlement units (`09_CURRENCIES.md`), and employment camouflaged as civilian jobs (`18_CIVILIAN_LIFE.md` §18.4: "The org chart is the camouflage"). The proposal's "public/official Espers" [PROPOSED] requires an overt payroll — guild salaries, rank-based pay bands, public hunter contracts — for which no canon funding stream exists.
**Sufficiency:** The contradiction is structural, not numerical: canon's economy is designed so that supernatural labor is never publicly priced. A public Esper labor market cannot be bolted on without a visible fiscal base.
**Possible solutions:**
1. [RECOMMENDATION] Keep Espers "official" (state-registered, salaried through threshold agencies like the SMD) but not "public" — ranks F–S become internal civil-service bands, preserving the covert payroll. Consequences: The proposal's public-Esper premise must be downgraded; the F–S system survives as hidden bureaucracy, and the undervaluation cycle must be re-anchored to institutional dismissal rather than public contempt.
2. [RECOMMENDATION] Declare the masquerade partially lifted as the reboot's premise change, and build the overt Esper economy from scratch (guild charters, public bounty boards, rank-indexed insurance products). Consequences: `08_ECONOMY.md`, `09_CURRENCIES.md`, `17_MEDIA_AND_INTERNET.md`, and `26_INFORMATION_CONTROL.md` require full rewrites; the secrecy categories of this audit collapse into a single premise decision.
**Canon impact:** [REWRITE] — Either the proposal's "public" premise or the canon economy's covert-labor foundation must be rewritten; they cannot coexist.

### Q303 — Economy
**Question:** What is the price of a Contract, when canon law and economics agree that Seeps are non-fungible and person-attached tethers cannot be transferred at any price?
**Status:** [CONTRADICTION]
**Current answer:** `09_CURRENCIES.md` and `02_WORLD_RULES.md` establish anomaly non-fungibility: every Seep is bespoke, which is why the Gray Market auctions *Rules and documentation*, never the phenomena themselves (`25_TIMELINE.md` EVENT-046, EVENT-084 — the Koi Network stole documentation mid-transfer, not the Seep). `14_LAW_ENFORCEMENT.md` §14.3(2) plus `25_TIMELINE.md` EVENT-093 hold that brokering a person-attached tether is attempted trafficking and the transfer itself cannot be engineered. A Contract — a solved anomaly bound to Daichi via the artifact — is therefore unpriceable in every market canon recognizes.
**Sufficiency:** Canon fully answers this by ruling out every pricing mechanism: not property (Mori doctrine), not transferable (EVENT-093), not fungible (non-fungibility rule). The proposal's Contract economy has no legal object to price.
**Possible solutions:**
1. [RECOMMENDATION] Price the *documentation and services around* Contracts (investigation reports, Rule transcriptions, containment consults) while the Contracts themselves remain unpriceable — mirroring EVENT-084's Rule-auction model. Consequences: Daichi can earn money as an investigator without selling his Contracts; `09_CURRENCIES.md` needs only a "Contract-adjacent services" schedule, not a new asset class.
2. [RECOMMENDATION] Establish by reboot premise that Contracts, unlike tethers, are separable from the person and licensable — a deliberate exception to the Mori doctrine. Consequences: `14_LAW_ENFORCEMENT.md` §§14.1–14.3 must be rewritten with the Contract exception, and EVENT-093's trafficking logic must be re-scoped; the artifact becomes the most valuable licensable object in the setting, which rewrites faction incentives around Daichi.
**Canon impact:** [REWRITE] — Either canon's non-fungibility/trafficking law gains a Contract exception, or the proposal abandons Contract pricing.

### Q305 — Economy
**Question:** If a Contract is person-bound to Daichi but its *knowledge* (the solved Rule) can be written down, what stops the Koi Network from stealing the documentation and selling the solution — exactly as they did with the T4 Object Seep's Rule in 2021?
**Status:** [LOOPHOLE]
**Current answer:** Canon precedent is direct: `25_TIMELINE.md` EVENT-084 — the Koi Network (CRIM-001) stole Rule documentation mid-transfer at the Trieste auction, and Gray Market prices tripled. The proposal says Contracts arise "through understanding and solving" [PROPOSED] — but understanding, once achieved, is transcribable. Nothing in the proposal distinguishes the Contract (bound) from its underlying solved Rule (stealable information).
**Sufficiency:** The loophole is canon-shaped: the setting already contains professional Rule-thieves, and the proposal hands them a new product line without a defense.
**Possible solutions:**
1. [RECOMMENDATION] Rule that the artifact encrypts the solution: Daichi *understands* the anomaly, but the Contract's operational content is stored in the artifact in non-transcribable form (his notes describe effects, never the mechanism). Consequences: Preserves the Koi as a threat to his *notes* while protecting the Contracts; needs one hard rule added to the artifact's spec and a `26_INFORMATION_CONTROL.md` note on Contract-information hygiene.
2. [RECOMMENDATION] Accept the theft vector as a story engine: stolen Rule-documentation creates "counterfeit Contracts" — buyers who know the Rule but lack the artifact get partial, dangerous effects. Consequences: Turns the loophole into plot fuel (fragment-only piracy); requires defining what a Rule-without-artifact does, which is new canon but bounded.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — Canon's documentation-theft precedent stands; the proposal must specify what exactly is bound vs. copyable.

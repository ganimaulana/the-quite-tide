## Combat

### Q201 — Combat
**Question:** How does a Rank F with no combat Esper ability survive a direct fight against a ranked combat Esper without the scene collapsing into "genius talks, enemy waits"?
**Status:** [CONTRADICTION]
**Current answer:** Old canon gives Daichi no combat training, zero Fathoms, no direct matter effect, no general defense, and the standing rule "a bullet still works"; the conflict doctrine is to read files, identify Rules, and spend others' strength rather than fight (31_CONFLICT_ENGINE.md). The directive proposes an athletic/combat-capable Daichi, which contradicts the no-training clerk.
**Sufficiency:** The two designs point opposite ways — old canon says he must never be in a fair fight; the directive implies he can stand in one. Until the contradiction is resolved, every combat scene has two incompatible rulebooks.
**Possible solutions:** (1) Keep old-canon frailty and define "combat-capable" as fit, evasive, and good at running — never fight-winning. Consequences: preserves "a bullet still works" and the underdog identity; but the directive's "combat-capable" promise to readers is then mostly cosmetic. (2) Grant real fighting skill via a defined, modest past (school sports club, self-defense courses), kept strictly at trained-human level. Consequences: honest retcon of the clerk past; costs some ordinariness but stays inside the user's "ordinary background" constraint if kept modest. (3) Let contracts supply combat effects under hard caps (one slot, resonance cost, no passive defense). Consequences: fights become resource puzzles; but every combat contract must be audited against the "never secretly OP" rule or the guardrail rots.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — keep the old conflict doctrine (win by Rules, not force) and define "combat-capable" as survivability, not parity.

### Q202 — Combat
**Question:** If contracts are earned by understanding, what does Daichi do mid-fight when the observe→hypothesize→test loop has seconds, not days?
**Status:** [LOOPHOLE]
**Current answer:** Old canon's method requires observe → hypothesize → smallest falsifiable test → pay cost; combat compresses that loop to zero. The directive names understanding as the prize but specifies no accelerated procedure.
**Sufficiency:** Nothing in either source gives Daichi a legal mid-combat move — so either fights must always be pre-solved (predictable) or he improvises off-book (unverifiable genius).
**Possible solutions:** (1) Rule: no new contracts mid-combat — fights are fought with prepared contracts only, making preparation the real battle. Consequences: clean and verifiable; but removes improvisation, the most fun part of a genius protagonist. (2) Allow "flash hypotheses" — untested reads the Artifact records as fragment-grade or that fail loudly on error. Consequences: preserves improvisation with a price; needs the fragment/failure mechanics (Worker B's area) to be airtight. (3) Make mid-combat understanding someone else's job — Daichi directs allies who execute. Consequences: reinforces leadership-without-authority; but solo scenes have no answer.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — port the old falsifiable-test method into a combat-time variant with explicit error pricing.

### Q203 — Combat
**Question:** Does the ERROR/UNDEFINED scan plus undervalue→reversal cycles turn every fight into the same beat (mocked → revealed → won), and how many repetitions before readers pattern-match it?
**Status:** [LOOPHOLE]
**Current answer:** The directive proposes undervalue→reversal as a reader-satisfaction device; nothing in the directive or canon limits its frequency or varies its shape.
**Sufficiency:** A satisfaction device with no governor becomes a formula — and a formula the *characters* should notice (why does everyone keep underestimating the ERROR guy?). The device needs a governor or it eats the story.
**Possible solutions:** (1) Cap reversals per arc and force some undervaluations to end badly — he stays underestimated and loses. Consequences: keeps the device honest; costs some wish-fulfillment. (2) Let the underworld learn — after early arcs, smart enemies overprepare for him, flipping the dynamic. Consequences: best long-term fix; requires the setting to have institutional memory. (3) Vary the reversal currency — sometimes the reversal is escape, information, or a third party's intervention, not victory. Consequences: preserves surprise within the pattern; needs discipline in chapter planning.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — keep the device but write the governor into the series bible, not just chapter plans.

### Q204 — Combat
**Question:** If contracts can produce combat-usable effects, what distinguishes Daichi from "secretly OP" — where exactly is the line?
**Status:** [NEEDS REDESIGN]
**Current answer:** The directive's identity rule ("never secretly OP, never instantly dominating stronger characters") is a slogan, not a mechanism; no contract in the proposal ships with a defined output ceiling.
**Sufficiency:** A slogan cannot be audited. Without a numeric or categorical ceiling on what one contract (and one slot) can do in a fight, every combat scene is one generous ruling away from hidden-OP.
**Possible solutions:** (1) Hard categorical ban: no contract may grant direct damage, defense, or stat-boosts — only information, access, and conditional triggers. Consequences: the cleanest guardrail; but shrinks the design space for the "10 candidate Contract #1" work. (2) Numeric ceiling: contract output capped below D-rank Esper output, permanently. Consequences: verifiable; but "permanently" fights the progression fantasy and needs enforcement. (3) Cost ceiling: combat-useful contracts cost resonance/lifespan at rates that make each fight a visible sacrifice. Consequences: preserves strong effects with honest pricing; requires the resonance economy to be fully specified first.
**Canon impact:** [REWRITE] — the directive's combat-contract permissions need a written ceiling before any Contract #1 candidate is selected.

### Q205 — Combat
**Question:** How do Daichi's enemies stay credibly intelligent when the protagonist's whole advantage is being smarter than them?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** Nothing in canon or the directive addresses antagonist competence preservation; old canon's conflict generators assume Daichi exploits *anomaly* Rules, not that he out-thinks *people*.
**Sufficiency:** If every smart enemy loses to a smarter protagonist, the setting's institutions look decorative. The audit needs a rule for when Daichi's read of a person can simply be wrong.
**Possible solutions:** (1) Import 29 §B.5 ("misreading is always possible") from anomaly-reading to people-reading: his social deductions carry explicit error bars. Consequences: keeps enemies credible; costs some protagonist infallibility readers expect. (2) Give institutions counter-intelligence doctrine specifically for "clever F-ranks" — they don't need to out-think him, just to compartmentalize. Consequences: realistic; makes the world feel competent. (3) Restrict his genius to anomalies/things, not people — he reads Rules, not minds. Consequences: sharp identity ("understands things that should not exist"); but limits political-plot participation.
**Canon impact:** [KEEP] — 29 §B.5's misreading principle should explicitly cover people, not just anomalies.

### Q206 — Combat
**Question:** Does the directive's one starting slot plus hidden Resonance gauge actually prevent prep-time stockpiling (solving easy anomalies in advance and entering every fight loaded)?
**Status:** [PROPOSED]
**Current answer:** The directive proposes one Contract Slot and a hidden Resonance gauge as the stockpiling governor; slot-growth rules and resonance costs are not specified.
**Sufficiency:** The governor exists in name but has no numbers — one slot caps breadth, but a single perfectly-chosen combat contract plus resonance could still trivialize arcs. The frame is sound; the calibration is missing.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — keep the slot/gauge frame; the numbers are the missing design, not the concept. Specify slot growth and resonance costs before Contract #1 selection.

### Q207 — Combat
**Question:** How does Daichi survive attacks that allow no observation at all — ambush, sniper, poison?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** Old canon is explicit: no general defense, and "a bullet still works" (DATABASE/03_TRACKERS.md); the conflict doctrine therefore avoids fair fights entirely. The directive's "physically capable" does not stop a sniper.
**Sufficiency:** Neither source gives him a legal answer to surprise violence — which means either the story must never surprise him (contrived) or he must sometimes nearly die (unpriced).
**Possible solutions:** (1) Make mortality the point: he survives ambushes the way ordinary people do — sometimes he doesn't, and allies/scars accumulate. Consequences: highest stakes; but a serialized protagonist needs plot armor by another name. (2) Allow one narrow passive: the Artifact warns (never shields) — information, not defense. Consequences: preserves "bullet still works" while giving him a dodge chance; the warning's reliability must be capped or it's spider-sense. (3) Institutional answer: he is valuable enough that someone assigns protection — which becomes a leash (see Q215). Consequences: realistic; trades independence for safety.
**Canon impact:** [KEEP] — "a bullet still works" must survive the reboot as the anti-hidden-OP guarantee.

## Government

### Q215 — Government
**Question:** Why doesn't the government simply conscript, detain, or disappear a man who can solve anomalies on demand?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** Old canon shows faction attention converging in 2024 (25_TIMELINE.md EVENT-092–100) and has government-exploitation generators (31_CONFLICT_ENGINE.md), but those were built for a weak clerk worth watching, not an Artifact-holder worth owning. The directive gives the government no doctrine for him.
**Sufficiency:** "Because the plot needs him free" is not an answer — and every week he operates openly, the non-detention needs an in-world reason, or the state looks incompetent.
**Possible solutions:** (1) Jurisdictional fog: no agency has clear authority over an ERROR-ranked civilian, and agencies block each other. Consequences: realistic; but fog lifts the moment he becomes undeniable. (2) He is more useful free: his solves are public goods the government harvests without owning him. Consequences: elegant — detention would kill the golden goose; needs the government to be smart enough to see it. (3) Mutually assured exposure: he holds dead-man documentation of what he knows. Consequences: gritty; turns every government scene into a standoff.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — port the old exploitation generators, but rewrite their leverage math for an asset-class Daichi.

### Q217 — Government
**Question:** He has no general defense and a bullet still works (old canon) — what stops a government lab from simply seizing him and the Artifact?
**Status:** [LOOPHOLE]
**Current answer:** Old canon's hard limits make him physically seizable; the directive adds Artifact value without adding any anti-seizure property. The incentive (priceless asset) and the cost (a van and four agents) are wildly mismatched.
**Sufficiency:** This is the reboot's cheapest exploit — any rational state actor tries the van first and asks questions later. The design must close it or stage it.

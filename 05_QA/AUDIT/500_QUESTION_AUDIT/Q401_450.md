### Q401 — Death
**Question:** If Daichi dies, what happens to his stored contracts — do they die with him, transfer with the Artifact, persist independently, or dissipate?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** Neither source addresses it. Old canon has a death rule only for the tether: on the holder's death or Drowning it passes to the nearest Loud witness; engineered transfers dissipate; with no Loud witness present it dissipates (`29_PROTAGONIST_ANOMALY.md` §B.8). The new directive (premise 4–7) defines the Artifact as a medium/repository with slots and a resonance gauge, but gives no succession, inheritance, or death rule for stored contracts.
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient — this is the first question every faction planner, insurer, and hostile reader will ask, and the old canon's answer was load-bearing (it made assassination pointless and engineered inheritance impossible). Silence here re-opens both.
**Possible solutions:**
1. Contracts are bound to the living holder's understanding and die with him; the Artifact resets to one empty slot for the next holder. Consequences: keeps assassination pointless (old canon's protection preserved), but makes every stored contract a wasting asset and punishes long arcs where Daichi banks capabilities.
2. Contracts persist in the Artifact and transfer with it under the old tether rule (nearest Loud witness, unengineered). Consequences: preserves old-canon continuity cheapest, but turns the Artifact into inheritable military hardware — every faction's optimal move becomes positioning a Loud witness at Daichi's deathbed.
3. Contracts dissipate on death, always, like an engineered transfer. Consequences: simplest and most consistent with "the universe does not choose holders," but removes all succession drama and makes the multi-generational artifact premise pointless (nothing accumulates across generations).
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — old §B.8 transfer rule must be explicitly extended or replaced for the contract repository; it cannot stay silent.

### Q402 — Death
**Question:** Can Daichi contract with a dead entity — a Drowning-born phenomenon, a Tide-mark, or an echo wearing a dead person's shape?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** The directive says contracts come from "Monsters, Anomalies, Phenomena, Entities" (premise 6) with no life/death qualifier. Old canon: death collapses a person's pattern; anything returning is an echo — not the person — and echoes degrade (`02_WORLD_RULES.md` §I); patterns can only be captured during collapse, and only by a T3+ Lantern examiner with consent or Tribunal warrant (02.I).
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient — "Phenomena" arguably includes Drowning sites and Tide-marks (the old bible's most common post-death phenomena), so a hostile reading permits contracting with the dead by default. That needs an explicit yes/no.
**Possible solutions:**
1. Forbid contracts with post-death phenomena entirely: the dead cannot be "solved" because there is no living Rule to understand, only residue. Consequences: clean, preserves the resurrection brake's perimeter, but closes off an obvious story vein (Drowning-site contracts) the High Tide setting would otherwise supply.
2. Allow contracts with Drowning-born phenomena but never with echoes/identities: you can contract the Seep the Drowned left, never the person. Consequences: preserves "echoes are not people" while keeping the story vein; requires a bright-line test for "phenomenon vs. identity" that drafts will constantly blur.
3. Allow echo contracts but define them as information-only, degrading, and non-binding (the echo cannot consent, so the contract is void as a bargain and functions as a recording). Consequences: philosophically rich (a contract with something that cannot agree), but "contract" becomes a misnomer and the ethics section needs rewriting.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — 02.I's pattern-capture rule needs a contract-specific corollary.

### Q403 — Death
**Question:** If a contracted entity is destroyed or killed after the contract is signed, does the contract survive, weaken, or terminate?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** Nothing addresses post-signature counterparty death. Old canon analogies: borrowed matter reverts ≤72h (02.I); Seeps are unique and non-fungible (02.II); the tether cannot be severed by wards (29 §B.5). The directive allows temporary, conditional, or permanent durations (premise 8) without tying duration to the counterparty's survival.
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient — a "permanent" contract with a killable counterparty is either false advertising or an immortality backdoor for the entity (killing it does nothing). Factions will test this immediately.
**Possible solutions:**
1. Contract terminates when the counterparty's pattern collapses; "permanent" means "for the counterparty's natural duration." Consequences: honest and consistent with pattern-collapse metaphysics, but makes every permanent contract hostage to assassination — factions will kill entities to strip Daichi's capabilities.
2. Contract survives as a stored "recording" of the solved Rule, independent of the entity (the understanding, not the being, is what's stored). Consequences: makes Daichi assassination-proof at the cost of making entities disposable — directly undercuts the "not by killing" ethic, since killing the entity after signing costs him nothing.
3. Contract degrades into fragment-only on counterparty death (premise 8's "fragment-only" outcome repurposed as a death state). Consequences: elegant — death has a defined mechanical meaning inside the existing outcome taxonomy — but requires the taxonomy to be rewritten as states, not just signing outcomes.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — premise 8's duration taxonomy must be extended with a counterparty-death rule.

### Q404 — Death
**Question:** The Artifact is "inherited for generations" (premise 4); the old tether passes by proximity + Loudness to the nearest Loud witness, never by inheritance, and engineered transfers dissipate (29 §B.8). Which rule governs the Artifact's passage at a holder's death?
**Status:** [CONTRADICTION]
**Current answer:** Direct clash. Old canon: "The transfer mechanism is proximity + Loudness, not inheritance, not worthiness, not fate. The universe does not choose holders." (29 §B.8, CANON). New directive: ancient, inherited for generations within a family, eventually chooses/recognizes Daichi (premises 2–4, [PROPOSED]).
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient — these cannot both be true of the same object. One of them must yield, and the choice rewrites either the old theme ("not chosen, present") or the new premise ("family artifact chooses").
**Possible solutions:**
1. Keep the old transfer rule and demote "inherited for generations" to in-world family mythology: the Moris *believe* it was inherited, but each passage was actually proximity+Loudness coincidence within a Loud-prone household. Consequences: preserves old canon's load-bearing theme at the cost of deflating the new premise's emotional engine (the artifact's "choice" becomes superstition).
2. Keep the new inheritance rule and rewrite §B.8: the Artifact is a different object-class than the tether was, with its own succession law. Consequences: clean break, but §B.8's "not chosen, present" was the old novel's thesis sentence — removing it orphans ARC II, ARC XII, and the STORY_ENGINE's "arc of presence."
3. Split the difference: the Artifact passes by inheritance *among the Loud* — family inheritance works only because the Mori line runs Loud, and a non-Loud heir would fail to hold it (nearest-Loud-witness fallback). Consequences: mechanically reconcilable, but "chooses/recognizes Daichi" still needs a selection criterion distinct from "was the Loud one who was there."
**Canon impact:** [REWRITE] — either §B.8 or premise 4 must be rewritten; a footnote cannot reconcile "not inheritance" with "inherited for generations."

### Q405 — Resurrection
**Question:** Does the absolute no-resurrection brake survive the reboot — specifically, can a contract ever "solve" a death?
**Status:** [CONTRADICTION]
**Current answer:** Old canon: "No true resurrection" is CANON-LOCKED (02.I); brake #3 "the resurrection prohibition holds absolutely" (AUDIT/03_CHARACTER_STORY_AUDITS.md). The new directive is silent on resurrection but its core verb is SOLVE — and death-phenomena (Drowning sites, echoes, Tide-marks of the dead) are phenomena. A "solved" death-entity walks like resurrection.
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient — the brake's absoluteness was written for a world without contracts. The directive doesn't challenge it, but also doesn't ring-fence it. Silence is not preservation when the new verb ("solve") applies to the forbidden object ("death").
**Possible solutions:**
1. Restate the brake in contract terms: no contract may restore a collapsed pattern to living personhood; "solving" a death-phenomenon can only ever yield information, never return. Consequences: preserves the brake at the cost of one explicit carve-out that must be policed in every death-adjacent draft.
2. Allow echo-contracts framed as non-resurrection (Q402 solution 3): the dead can be consulted, never returned. Consequences: keeps the letter of the brake while creating a permanent resurrection-adjacent temptation the story must never indulge — a loaded gun on the mantelpiece for 1000 chapters.
3. Drop the brake and let a late-arc contract genuinely resurrect someone, paying a defined price. Consequences: breaks the single most load-bearing metaphysical rule in the bible (02.I CANON-LOCKED, confirmed "at terrible cost" by every faction); the setting's theology, Tribunal law, and Choir doctrine all need rewriting. Highest cost, not recommended without author-level sign-off.
**Canon impact:** [KEEP] — with an explicit contract-era restatement; the brake must be re-anchored, not assumed.

### Q406 — Resurrection
**Question:** If a contract effect merely resembles resurrection — a phenomenon that replays the dead so convincingly that witnesses cannot tell — who adjudicates whether the brake was violated: the Tribunal, the artifact, or Daichi?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** Nothing addresses adjudication of edge cases. Old canon: the Tribunal prosecutes pattern-theft absolutely (02.I); echoes "degrade" but no timeline or test is given for distinguishing echo from return. The directive has no adjudication layer at all.
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient — "resembles resurrection" is exactly where hostile readers and in-world factions will probe. Without an adjudicator, every such scene becomes an authorial assertion.
**Possible solutions:**
1. The Tribunal's existing pattern-theft jurisprudence extends to contracts: any contract that presents a collapsed pattern as a living person is prosecuted as resurrection-attempt. Consequences: gives the old legal system new work and a clear enforcement hook, but requires the Tribunal to detect contract use it may not be able to see.
2. The artifact itself refuses: the medium rejects death-phenomena contracts at signing (a built-in brake, like wards that cannot be severed). Consequences: clean and self-enforcing, but makes the artifact a moral agent — which collides with Q454 (entity identity) and the "medium, not mind" framing.
3. No adjudication — the resemblance cases are left as in-world controversy, with factions disagreeing (Choir: miracle; Compact: echo-fraud; Tribunal: deferred docket). Consequences: most realistic and most serialized (a running legal-theological dispute), but risks looking like the author dodging the question.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — 14_LAW_ENFORCEMENT.md's Tribunal jurisdiction needs a contract-resurrection clause.

### Q407 — Resurrection

**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]

### Q408 — Resurrection
**Question:** Is contracting with an echo — a degraded pattern wearing a dead person's shape — a loophole around the resurrection brake: indefinite interrogation of the dead without technically resurrecting anyone?
**Status:** [LOOPHOLE]
**Current answer:** 02.I: "Echoes are not people... Echoes degrade." Nothing forbids *talking to* an echo or *contracting for* an echo's memories. The directive's allowed contract effects explicitly include "information, memory fragments" (premise 8). An echo-contract is therefore permitted by the letter of both systems — and it is resurrection-adjacent power (access to the dead's knowledge) without resurrection.
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient as a design matter: this is the first genuine exploit in the new system. A Daichi who contracts echoes becomes the setting's necromantic intelligence agency, and no brake prices it.
**Possible solutions:**
1. Close it: echoes cannot be contracted because a degrading pattern cannot give the stable "understanding" a contract requires (the Rule dissolves as the echo does). Consequences: clean metaphysical closure; costs the story a rich vein but protects the brake.
2. Price it: echo-contracts are allowed but degrade with the echo — the contract's duration is the echo's remaining coherence, and using it accelerates the degradation (you spend the dead to question them). Consequences: morally freighted, self-limiting, and very much in the old canon's "everything is priced" spirit; requires a degradation mechanic.
3. Leave it open as a deliberate temptation arc: Daichi discovers the loophole, uses it, and the story prices it socially (the Choir calls it desecration, the Tribunal calls it pattern-theft-adjacent). Consequences: best serialized fuel, but the author must commit to never letting it become routine — a discipline problem, not a design problem.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — 02.I's echo clause needs a contract corollary: echoes are not people *and* cannot be stable counterparties (or can, at a stated price).

### Q409 — Healing

Consequences: cleanest; keeps 16_MEDICINE.md intact; but removes an obvious utility the readers will expect the system to eventually grant.
3. Allow healing but make it the story's most expensive contract class: each healing permanently spends a contract slot (the slot "scars"). Consequences: creates a genuine triage economy (who does Daichi spend a slot on?), but invents a new cost currency that needs full specification.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — 04.6's healing limits must be restated as contract-law; they cannot be assumed to carry over.

### Q410 — Healing

### Q411 — Healing
**Question:** When a contract heals (or does anything costly), who pays — Daichi's body, the resonance gauge, the contracted entity, or nobody?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** Old canon: every use is priced (29 §B.6: "Every use of the Laggard is priced"); 02.I (anomaly-rules clause): "Nothing gets the effect for free; the bill is just denominated differently." The directive describes a gauge that *fills* (solving increases resonance) and *unlocks slots* — a currency that only accumulates. No per-use cost is defined.
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient — this is the new system's missing cost curve. A gauge that only grows is not a price; it is a score. Without a payer, the "everything is priced" canon is dead on arrival.
**Possible solutions:**
1. The gauge is spent, not just filled: invoking a contract drains resonance; solving refills it. Consequences: creates a genuine economy (earn-and-spend) that mirrors Erosion without copying it; requires defining drain rates per contract class.
2. Daichi pays physiologically per use (the old §B.6 currencies: nosebleeds, exhaustion, exposure, attention). Consequences: preserves old canon's body-horror pricing and keeps Daichi fragile; but then what is the gauge *for* — it risks becoming a redundant second currency.
3. The contracted entity pays: each invocation spends the entity's coherence/pattern-stability. Consequences: ethically explosive (using a contract consumes the counterparty — the "not by killing" ethic erodes by degrees) and self-limiting; needs an entity-depletion mechanic or entities become infinite batteries.
**Canon impact:** [REWRITE] — the directive's gauge must be rewritten from a progression meter into (or alongside) a cost currency; "resonance only grows" cannot stand.

### Q412 — Memory
**Question:** Can a contract grant memory manipulation — reading, altering, or erasing memories?
**Status:** [CONTRADICTION]
**Current answer:** Old canon 02.I CANON-LOCKED: "No direct mind control. A mind is a Membrane phenomenon; the Undertow cannot grip it directly." Suggestion seeding only: resistible, backlash-priced, geometrically unreliable at scale (brake #4). The directive's allowed effects include "information, memory fragments" (premise 8) — memory-*reading* is arguably permitted; memory-*alteration* is mind control by another name.
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient — the directive does not distinguish reading from altering, and the old lock was written against channeling, not contracts. A memory-altering contract is the most-regulated act on earth (Choir law, 140+ countries) with a back door.
**Possible solutions:**
1. Permit memory-reading contracts, forbid memory-altering ones: the artifact can store what was understood, never rewrite a living mind. Consequences: preserves 02.I's lock with one bright line; memory-reading alone is still enormously powerful (Q413).
2. Permit alteration but import the full old price: resistible, backlash on Daichi, geometrically unreliable — i.e., contracts don't bypass Choir law, they just deliver it through a new pipe. Consequences: legally consistent; but then the contract is a worse Choir license, raising the question of why the system includes it.
3. Forbid both: the artifact cannot mediate minds at all (it stores understandings of *phenomena*; a mind is not a phenomenon). Consequences: simplest, most consistent with "the Undertow cannot grip it directly" — but Q414's Loudness question then needs its own answer, and the directive's "memory fragments" effect must be redefined as the entity's own memories, not the target's.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — 02.I's mind-control lock needs a contract-era restatement distinguishing reading from altering.

### Q415 — Mind Control

### Q416 — Mind Control
**Question:** If Daichi "solves" a mass-suggestion Seep, does he gain a mass-influence contract — i.e., exactly the compounding the bible defines as impossible?
**Status:** [LOOPHOLE]
**Current answer:** Brake #4: compounding is *defined* as impossible because per-link unreliability compounds geometrically (AUDIT/03_CHARACTER_STORY_AUDITS.md §1024; 02.I). The directive's solve-to-contract pipeline has no scale cap: a solved phenomenon yields a contract, and mass-suggestion is a phenomenon. Consequences: the lock becomes deterrence rather than physics; works only if the author never lets him get away with it twice.

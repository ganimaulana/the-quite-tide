## Contract Failure (continued)

### Q151 — Contract Failure
**Question:** Can Daichi *force* a contract on an unwilling entity (coercion, blackmail, hostage-taking)?
**Status:** [LOOPHOLE]
**Current answer:** The directive says contracts come from understanding/solving, "NOT by killing" — but killing is only the extreme end of the coercion spectrum. Canon's power-theft requires murder (02.I), and suggestion seeding carries backlash (02.I) — the setting already prices forced influence.
**Sufficiency:** "Not by killing" leaves blackmail, hostage-taking, and leverage fully open — and blackmail is cheaper than murder and very much the Red Ledger's idiom (SUP-004).
**Possible solutions:** (1) Coerced contracts are void (the artifact rejects them). Consequences: needs the artifact to judge coercion — black box; but preserves the "understand, don't dominate" identity (directive §3). (2) Coerced contracts form but are *brittle* (the entity sabotages from inside). Consequences: dramatically honest — the coerced counterparty is a saboteur; but every coerced contract becomes a betrayal arc — repetitive at scale. (3) Coercion works and is *legal-ish* in the Gray Markets. Consequences: darkest timeline; Daichi becomes what the old bible's factions are — the moral-event-horizon guardrail must be explicit and permanent.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — 02.I's backlash principle (every forced influence has a return path) should apply: coerced contracts carry automatic backlash.

### Q152 — Contract Failure
**Question:** What if the anomaly is solved *accidentally* (Daichi trips over the solution without understanding it)?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** The directive's identity rule: "Daichi grows stronger by understanding things that should not exist" (§3). Accidental solving bypasses the entire progression fantasy — luck is not understanding.
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient. Accidents happen in investigation fiction; the system must either reward, ignore, or punish them — "the artifact knows you didn't mean it" is intent-detection by black box.
**Possible solutions:** (1) Accidental solves don't count (no understanding, no contract). Consequences: cleanest; but requires intent-detection. (2) Accidental solves count at fragment grade only. Consequences: rewards luck partially; preserves the understanding premium — the preferred middle. (3) Accidental solves count fully. Consequences: fastest power growth, weakest identity; the directive's §3 becomes decorative.
**Canon impact:** [KEEP] — 02.IV "power is legible" + 29 §B.5 (misreading is always possible) imply understanding is *verifiable*, not incidental; accidents are data, not mastery.

## Partial Contracts

### Q153 — Partial Contracts
**Question:** Can Daichi deliberately leave an anomaly partially unresolved to farm fragments repeatedly?
**Status:** [LOOPHOLE]
**Current answer:** The directive allows fragment-only outcomes; nothing prevents solve → fragment → leave → return → fragment cycling. This is the purest farming exploit in the brief — a renewable fragment orchard.
**Sufficiency:** A renewable resource the protagonist can harvest at will, with no stated counter, collapses the scarcity the whole progression loop assumes.
**Possible solutions:** (1) Fragments are one-per-anomaly (the anomaly is "tapped"). Consequences: kills the farm; but needs a diegetic reason the second visit yields nothing. (2) Diminishing returns curve (each revisit yields less). Consequences: permits farming but asymptotes; needs the curve published. (3) The anomaly *notices* being farmed and hardens (Q142/Q143). Consequences: the farm fights back — best narrative answer; the orchard grows teeth.
**Canon impact:** [ADAPT] — 02.II "anomalies are non-fungible" supports one-tap logic: you can't harvest the same uniqueness twice.

### Q154 — Partial Contracts
**Question:** Do fragments stack, merge, or combine into full contracts?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** The directive lists "fragment-only" as an outcome; no fragment economy is defined.
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient. Fragments are either a currency (combinable — then they're just slow contracts) or souvenirs (inert — then why track them); the design must choose.
**Possible solutions:** (1) Fragments of the *same* entity merge toward a full contract. Consequences: gives partials a progression path; but then Q153's farm becomes a slow full-contract printer — needs the one-tap rule. (2) Fragments are inert intel (clues, sensory impressions — directive §8). Consequences: matches "feeds later Contracts" without becoming currency; safest. (3) Fragments are tradable (Red Ledger commodities). Consequences: creates a fragment market the Ledger (INTL-003) would regulate — deep worldbuilding cost for a side mechanic.
**Canon impact:** [UNKNOWN] — no canon fragment concept; 08's sounding (SND) is the nearest currency analog.

### Q155 — Partial Contracts
**Question:** Does a fragment occupy a contract slot?
**Status:** [UNRESOLVED]
**Current answer:** Directive: starts with ONE slot; fragment slot-status unaddressed.
**Sufficiency:** Insufficient. If fragments are slot-free, the slot cap is bypassable by collecting fragments instead of contracts — the economy's load-bearing wall has a door in it.
**Possible solutions:** (1) Fragments are slot-free (they're not contracts). Consequences: the slot economy leaks — fragment-hoarding becomes the optimal portfolio; needs a separate fragment cap. (2) Fragments occupy a slot at reduced weight (e.g., three fragments = one slot). Consequences: unified economy; but fractional slots add bookkeeping. (3) Fragments don't use slots but *decay* (Q159). Consequences: time, not slots, balances fragments — elegant; the decay rate must be stated.
**Canon impact:** [UNKNOWN] — slot/fragments are new; the Laggard's storage rule (29 §B.11 use 7: "the capacity *is* the ratchet") is the tonal model — capacity always costs.
